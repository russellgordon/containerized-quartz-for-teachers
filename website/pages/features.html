What it does

Course notes become a real website — one that is quick, searchable, readable on a phone, and yours to take with you.

The point of it

Most systems for sharing material with a class ask for the same thing twice: once when the material is written, and again when it is posted. The second pass is where the time goes — a form here, an upload there, a dozen clicks to publish a single handout.

Plantoir removes the second pass. The notes are the website. Writing a file and saving it is the whole act of authoring; publishing is a button, and the result is a site that belongs to the teacher rather than to a platform.

What a teacher gets

Files that outlive the software

Everything is plain text in ordinary folders. Change schools, change tools, or stop using Plantoir altogether, and the material is still readable, still organised, and still yours.

One course, several classes

A course is written once and published separately for each class section, so two groups moving at different speeds can each see only what they have reached.

A course ready on the first day

Dozens of Ontario course codes come with a complete working course already written, including the Ministry's expectations as pages that can be linked to. Every other code opens with a starting outline shaped for the subject.

Nothing to administer

No server, no accounts to manage, no administrator password. The app brings everything it needs the first time it runs.

Private until published

Notes stay on the teacher's own computer. Nothing leaves it until a section is published on purpose.

Free, and open

The app costs nothing and the source is public, so nothing about a course depends on a company's plans for its product.

Adding a course

A course code is enough to begin. Where ready-made material exists for that code, the pages, folders and curriculum expectations are set up in one step; where it does not, the app suggests an outline that suits the subject — a drama course opens with conventions and warm-ups, a chemistry course with investigations and lab safety.

Looking like your course, not like an app

Each class section chooses its own colour scheme, typeface and footer, so two sections of the same course can be told apart at a glance. The choices are made in the app and shown as a live sample before anything is built.

Seeing it before anybody else does

A preview builds the real site and shows it inside the app window. It is the same site the class would get, built the same way — so what appears in the preview is what appears when it is published.

{{shot:preview}}

On the page, for students

  • Search across the whole course, so a student looking for a definition finds it without scrolling through a term of material.
  • Mathematics set properly — equations are typeset rather than pasted in as pictures, chemistry notation included.
  • Diagrams drawn from the notes. A flowchart or a timeline is written as a few lines of text and drawn when the site is built, so correcting it means correcting a sentence.
  • Code with syntax highlighting, along with images, video, and PDFs.
  • Pages that link to each other, so a lesson can point at the concept behind it and the concept can list the lessons that used it.
  • Readable on a phone, in either light or dark, without a separate mobile version to maintain.

Publishing

A finished section can go to a free hosting service, to a school's own web space by way of an ordinary folder, or to a paid host — whichever a school allows. The address stays the same from one publish to the next, so a link handed out in September still works in June.

Publishing can also be scheduled: a section prepared on Sunday evening can appear on Monday morning without anyone opening a computer.

What it needs

A Mac

macOS 15 or later, on Apple silicon or Intel. A Windows version is built and will be released separately.

An internet connection, at the start

The first run downloads what the site builder needs, which takes a few minutes. After that, building and previewing work offline; publishing needs a connection again.

Somewhere to put the site

A free hosting account, or a folder on a school web server. Neither costs anything to try.
