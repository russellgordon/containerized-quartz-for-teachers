$$
\text{efficiency} = \frac{E_{\text{useful}}}{E_{\text{total}}} \times 100\%
\qquad P = VI \qquad E = Pt
$$

**1.** A motor is supplied $500\ \mathrm{J}$ and delivers $425\ \mathrm{J}$ of
motion. Find its efficiency and the energy wasted.

> [!success]- Answer
> $\frac{425}{500} \times 100\% = 85\%$, with $75\ \mathrm{J}$ wasted — mostly
> as heat in the windings, some as sound.

**2.** A bulb converts $1\,200\ \mathrm{J}$ into $60\ \mathrm{J}$ of light.
Efficiency? What became of the rest?

> [!success]- Answer
> $\frac{60}{1200} \times 100\% = 5\%$. The other $1\,140\ \mathrm{J}$ became
> heat. An incandescent bulb is a space heater that happens to glow.

**3.** A $1500\ \mathrm{W}$ kettle runs for 3 minutes. How much energy, in
joules and in kilowatt-hours?

> [!success]- Answer
> $E = (1500\ \mathrm{W})(180\ \mathrm{s}) = 270\,000\ \mathrm{J}$
>
> In kilowatt-hours: $1.5\ \mathrm{kW} \times 0.05\ \mathrm{h} = 0.075\ \mathrm{kWh}$

**4.** At $0.13\ \mathrm{\textdollar /kWh}$, what does question 3 cost?

> [!success]- Answer
> $0.075\ \mathrm{kWh} \times 0.13 \approx \textdollar 0.01$ — about one cent. Worth
> knowing before anyone tells you the kettle is why the bill is high.

**5.** A 60 W incandescent bulb and a 9 W LED give the same light. Over 1000
hours, how much energy does each use, and what is saved?

> [!success]- Answer
> Incandescent: $60\ \mathrm{kWh}$. LED: $9\ \mathrm{kWh}$. A saving of
> $51\ \mathrm{kWh}$, or about \$6.63 at 13 cents.
>
> One bulb, one year of evenings. Multiply by every socket in a school.

**6.** A solar panel receives $1000\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$ and produces
$200\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$. Efficiency? Where does the rest go?

> [!success]- Answer
> $20\%$. The rest is reflected, passes straight through, or becomes heat in
> the panel — which is itself a problem, because a hot panel is a less
> efficient one.
