Science deals with the very large and the very small, so we write numbers in a
form that keeps them readable.

$$
3\,450\,000 = 3.45 \times 10^{6} \qquad 0.000\,072 = 7.2 \times 10^{-5}
$$

> [!tip] How to use this page
> Every answer is hidden until you click it. Work the question first — reading
> a worked answer feels like learning, and mostly is not. The feeling of
> recognising an answer is not the same as being able to produce one.

## Write in scientific notation

**1.** $149\,600\,000\ \mathrm{km}$ — the distance from Earth to the Sun.

> [!success]- Answer
> $1.496 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{km}$

**2.** $0.000\,000\,000\,1\ \mathrm{m}$ — roughly the width of an atom.

> [!success]- Answer
> $1 \times 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m}$

**3.** $8\,640\ \mathrm{s}$

> [!success]- Answer
> $8.64 \times 10^{3}\ \mathrm{s}$

## Write as ordinary numbers

**4.** $6.02 \times 10^{23}$

> [!success]- Answer
> $602\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000$ — six hundred and two sextillion.
> This one has a name: Avogadro's number.

**5.** $1.6 \times 10^{-19}$

> [!success]- Answer
> $0.000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,16$ — the charge on a single electron, in
> coulombs.

## Convert

**6.** $2.5\ \mathrm{km}$ to metres.

> [!success]- Answer
> $2500\ \mathrm{m}$

**7.** $450\ \mathrm{mL}$ to litres.

> [!success]- Answer
> $0.450\ \mathrm{L}$

**8.** $3\ \mathrm{h}$ to seconds.

> [!success]- Answer
> $10\,800\ \mathrm{s}$ — $3 \times 60 \times 60$.

**9.** $1500\ \mathrm{W}$ to kilowatts.

> [!success]- Answer
> $1.5\ \mathrm{kW}$

> [!note] Significant digits, briefly
> Your answer cannot be more precise than your least precise measurement. If
> you measured $12.4\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $3\ \mathrm{cm}$, do not report
> $37.2\ \mathrm{cm^2}$ — report $40\ \mathrm{cm^2}$.
