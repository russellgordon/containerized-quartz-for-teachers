After each major investigation, add an entry. Four questions, briefly:

1. **What did you actually do?** Two sentences.
2. **What surprised you?** A result, a difficulty, something about your group.
3. **What would you change?** Be specific — "measure at three distances instead
   of one", not "be more careful".
4. **What does this connect to?** Another lab, a concept, something outside
   school.

## An example worth imitating

> In the photosynthesis lab our 40 cm discs never floated at all, and we assumed
> we had done something wrong. Comparing with two other groups, everyone's 40 cm
> trial was slow — ours was just slowest because our lamp was dimmer. What
> surprised me is that I nearly threw away a correct result because it did not
> match what I expected. Next time I will check with another group **before**
> deciding my data is wrong.

That entry is worth more than a perfect report, because it shows thinking about
thinking.
