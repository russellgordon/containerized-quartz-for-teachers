## The idea

**Power** is the rate of using energy. **Efficiency** is how much of that energy
did the job you wanted.

$$
P = VI \qquad E = Pt
$$

$$
\text{efficiency} = \frac{E_{\text{useful}}}{E_{\text{total}}} \times 100\%
$$

## Worked example

A lamp draws $0.50\ \mathrm{A}$ at $120\ \mathrm{V}$:

$$
P = VI = 120\ \mathrm{V} \times 0.50\ \mathrm{A} = 60\ \mathrm{W}
$$

Left on for 3 hours:

$$
E = Pt = 60\ \mathrm{W} \times 3\ \mathrm{h} = 180\ \mathrm{Wh} = 0.18\ \mathrm{kWh}
$$

## Where the rest of the energy goes

| Device | Useful output | Wasted as | Rough efficiency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Incandescent bulb | Light | Heat | ~5% |
| LED bulb | Light | Heat | ~40% |
| Electric kettle | Heat in water | Heat elsewhere | ~80% |
| Electric motor | Motion | Heat, sound | ~85% |

> [!note] Nothing reaches 100%
> Energy is conserved, but not all of it stays useful. In an incandescent bulb,
> 95% of the electricity becomes heat — it is a space heater that glows. That
> single number is why they have largely disappeared.

Measure this for yourself in [[Efficiency of a Light Bulb]].
