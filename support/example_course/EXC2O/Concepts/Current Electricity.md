## The idea

Static electricity is charge sitting still. **Current** electricity is charge
flowing steadily — and that flow can do useful work.

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit | Measures |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current | $I$ | ampere (A) | Rate of charge flow |
| Potential difference | $V$ | volt (V) | Energy given per unit charge |
| Resistance | $R$ | ohm ($\Omega$) | Opposition to flow |
| Charge | $Q$ | coulomb (C) | Amount of charge |

$$
I = \frac{Q}{t}
$$

If $12\ \mathrm{C}$ of charge pass a point in $4.0\ \mathrm{s}$:

$$
I = \frac{12\ \mathrm{C}}{4.0\ \mathrm{s}} = 3.0\ \mathrm{A}
$$

> [!note] The water analogy, and where it breaks
> Voltage is like pressure, current like flow rate, resistance like a narrow
> pipe. It is a good analogy — but water can spill out of an open pipe, while
> charge cannot flow at all unless the circuit is complete. Every analogy has an
> edge; know where yours is.

## Conventional current

By convention, current is drawn flowing from **+ to −**, even though electrons
actually drift the other way. Franklin guessed the sign before anyone knew
electrons existed, and the convention stuck.
