## The idea

Energy enters almost every ecosystem on Earth as **sunlight**, gets captured by
producers, and then moves up through the organisms that eat them. At every step,
most of it is lost as heat.

## The ten percent rule

Roughly **10%** of the energy at one trophic level ends up stored in the next.
The rest was spent staying alive, or never eaten in the first place.

$$
E_{\text{next level}} \approx 0.10 \times E_{\text{this level}}
$$

Start with 10 000 kJ captured by plants in a field:

| Trophic level | Energy available |
| --- | --- |
| Producers (grass) | 10 000 kJ |
| Primary consumers (grasshoppers) | 1 000 kJ |
| Secondary consumers (frogs) | 100 kJ |
| Tertiary consumers (hawks) | 10 kJ |

> [!question] Why are there no ten-level food chains?
> Work the arithmetic down another few rows and you will answer this yourself.
> There is simply nothing left to support the tenth level.

That is also why a pyramid is the right shape for this diagram, and why eating
lower on the food chain feeds more people from the same land.
