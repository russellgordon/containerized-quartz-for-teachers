## The idea

In a **physical change**, the substance is still the same substance afterwards.
In a **chemical change**, new substances exist that did not before.

## Evidence of a chemical change

No single sign is proof. Two or more together is convincing.

- A colour change that will not reverse
- A gas produced (bubbles in a solution that was not boiling)
- A precipitate — a solid appearing from two clear liquids
- Heat or light given off, or a noticeable chill
- A new odour

> [!warning] The classic trap
> Boiling water bubbles vigorously and produces a gas. It is a **physical**
> change: those bubbles are water. Dissolving sugar looks dramatic and is also
> physical — evaporate the water and the sugar is still sugar.
>
> The question is never "did something happen?" It is "is it still the same
> substance?"

## Conservation of mass

Mass is conserved in any change, physical or chemical. Burn steel wool in a
sealed container and the total mass is unchanged — burn it in the open and it
appears to *gain* mass, because oxygen from the air joined it.

$$
m_{\text{reactants}} = m_{\text{products}}
$$
