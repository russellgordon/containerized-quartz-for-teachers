## The idea

Atoms gain or lose electrons to reach a full outer shell. A charged atom is an
**ion**, and oppositely charged ions stick together.

- Lose electrons → fewer negatives → **positive** ion (**cation**). Metals do this.
- Gain electrons → **negative** ion (**anion**). Non-metals do this.

$$
\mathrm{Na} \rightarrow \mathrm{Na^+} + e^- \qquad
\mathrm{Cl} + e^- \rightarrow \mathrm{Cl^-}
$$

Put them together and the charges must cancel exactly:

$$
\mathrm{Na^+} + \mathrm{Cl^-} \rightarrow \mathrm{NaCl}
$$

## Balancing charges

Magnesium forms $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$; chlorine forms $\mathrm{Cl^-}$. One
magnesium needs **two** chlorides:

$$
\mathrm{MgCl_2}
$$

| Cation | Anion | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathrm{Na^+}$ | $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ | $\mathrm{NaCl}$ |
| $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ | $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ | $\mathrm{CaCl_2}$ |
| $\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$ | $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$ | $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$ |

> [!tip] The crossover trick — and why it works
> Swapping the charges to become subscripts works because it guarantees the
> total positive and total negative charge are equal. Use it, but be able to
> explain *why* it works; that is what a test question will ask.
