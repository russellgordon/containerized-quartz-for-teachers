## Before you touch the dial

A multimeter measures several things, and each needs a **different connection**.
Choosing wrong can destroy the meter, or the circuit, or both.

| Measuring | Dial setting | How it connects | Circuit state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Potential difference | V⎓ | **Parallel**, across the component | Powered on |
| Current | A⎓ | **In series**, in the path | Powered on |
| Resistance | Ω | Across the component **alone** | Powered **off** |
| Continuity | 🔊 | Across the two points | Powered off |

## Steps

1. Set the dial **before** connecting anything.
2. Black lead in COM, always.
3. Red lead in **V/Ω** for voltage and resistance; in **A** or **mA** for
   current — and yes, moving that lead is the step everyone forgets.
4. Start on the highest range if your meter is not auto-ranging.

> [!danger] The expensive mistake
> Leaving the leads in the current sockets and then measuring voltage puts a
> near-short across your source. Best case, the meter's fuse blows. Make it a
> habit: **check the sockets, then the dial, then connect.**

> [!tip] Resistance must be measured out of circuit
> Other components in parallel give the meter alternative paths, and you will
> read a value lower than the real one. Disconnect at least one end first.
