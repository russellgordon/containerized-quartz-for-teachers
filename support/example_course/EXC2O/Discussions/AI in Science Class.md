## The question

Where is the line between using a tool and not doing the work?

## Scenarios — allowed, not allowed, or "it depends"?

1. Asking an AI to explain photosynthesis in simpler words
2. Asking it to write your lab conclusion
3. Asking it to check your arithmetic
4. Asking it to find sources on climate impacts in Nunavut
5. Asking it to rewrite your paragraph more clearly
6. Asking it to generate data because your lab failed

Number 6 is unambiguous. The rest are worth arguing about.

## My position, for the record

Tools that help you **understand** are welcome. Tools that **replace** your
thinking defeat the purpose of being here. Scenario 1 helps you think; scenario
2 skips it entirely. Scenario 5 sits in the middle, and depends on whether you
could have written it yourself.

Whatever you use, **say so and say how**. See [[Finding and Citing Sources]].

> [!warning] The practical problem
> AI systems produce confident, fluent, wrong answers — including invented
> citations that look completely real. Checking is not optional. In this class,
> an unchecked citation is treated the same as a made-up one.
