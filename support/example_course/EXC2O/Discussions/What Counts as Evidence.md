## The question

How do you decide what to believe, when you cannot check everything yourself?

## Cases to argue about

1. A single well-run study finds a result nobody has replicated yet.
2. Ten thousand people report that a remedy works for them.
3. A model predicts something twenty years out, matching past data closely.
4. A practice used successfully by a community for centuries, never formally
   studied.
5. A result you can reproduce yourself on a lab bench in twenty minutes.

Rank them. Defend your ranking. Then argue with someone who ranked differently.

> [!note] There is no answer key here
> Case 4 in particular deserves care. Long-standing practices often encode real
> observation over timescales no study could match, and dismissing them as
> "anecdote" has caused science to miss things repeatedly. Weighing that against
> the value of controlled testing is a genuinely hard question — which is why we
> are discussing it rather than me telling you.
