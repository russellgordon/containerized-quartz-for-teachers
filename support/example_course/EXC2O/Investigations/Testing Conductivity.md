## Purpose

Classify materials as conductors, insulators, or somewhere in between, and
relate the result to atomic structure.

> [!danger] Safety
> Battery packs only — **never** wall current. If a wire or material gets warm,
> disconnect immediately.

## Materials

- Battery pack, lamp or buzzer, alligator leads
- Test materials: copper wire, aluminium foil, graphite pencil lead, steel nail,
  glass rod, plastic ruler, rubber band, tap water, salt water, distilled water,
  wood, a coin

## Procedure

1. Build a simple circuit with a gap in it.
2. Bridge the gap with each material and record how brightly the lamp lights.
3. For liquids, use two electrodes a fixed distance apart — keep it identical
   for every liquid.

## Analysis

1. What do the conductors have in common structurally?
2. Distilled water conducts poorly; salt water conducts well. The water is the
   same — so what is carrying the charge? See [[Ions and Ionic Compounds]].
3. Graphite is a non-metal that conducts. Look up why, and say what that shows
   about "metals conduct, non-metals do not".
