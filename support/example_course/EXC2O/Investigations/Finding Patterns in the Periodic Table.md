## Purpose

Use provided data to discover the organisation of the periodic table for
yourself, rather than being told it.

## Materials

- Data cards for 20 elements: atomic number, atomic radius, melting point,
  reactivity notes — **element names removed**

## Procedure

1. Working in pairs, sort the cards into groups by whatever pattern you find.
2. Write down the rule you used. Be specific enough that another pair could
   apply it.
3. Trade rules with another pair and sort **their** cards by **your** rule.
4. Only now, reveal the names and compare your arrangement to the real table.

> [!tip] This is what Mendeleev did
> He had no idea electrons existed. He sorted cards by properties and trusted
> the pattern enough to leave gaps for elements nobody had found. You are
> repeating the method, not the memorisation.

## Sample data

| Card | Atomic number | Radius (pm) | Melting point (°C) | Reacts with water? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 3 | 152 | 181 | Vigorously |
| B | 11 | 186 | 98 | Violently |
| C | 19 | 227 | 63 | Explosively |
| D | 9 | 42 | −220 | — |
| E | 17 | 79 | −102 | — |
| F | 10 | 38 | −249 | No |

## Analysis

1. What happens to atomic radius as you go down a column? Explain it using
   shells — see [[The Bohr-Rutherford Model]].
2. Cards A, B, and C get more reactive going down. Cards D and E get *less*
   reactive going down. Why do the two families behave in opposite directions?
3. Predict the melting point and radius of the element below C. Then look it up.
