> [!abstract] At a glance
> Groups of three · **three class periods** of work time, then presented ·
> **Format:** a written proposal and a five-minute pitch

## The task

You are a mission scientist. Propose a mission, and defend it against the
argument that the money would be better spent on Earth.

## Your proposal must include

1. **The destination and the question.** Not "go to Europa" but "determine
   whether Europa's subsurface ocean has the chemistry to support life".
2. **The instruments.** What do you need to measure, and what would measure it?
3. **The journey.** Distance, rough travel time, and the main hazard.
   [[Distances in Space]] has the numbers.
4. **The benefit on Earth.** What comes back besides knowledge? Point to real
   precedents from [[Technology From Space Exploration]].
5. **The honest cost.** A number, and what else that number could buy.

## The counter-argument — required

Write the strongest possible case **against** your own mission, then answer it.
Do not choose a weak objection to knock down.

> [!important] This is the actual skill
> Anyone can argue for something they like. Stating the opposing case fairly and
> *then* responding is what separates an argument from an advertisement — and it
> is exactly what
> [[A2.4|the scientific literacy expectation]] asks of you.

## Assessment

| Criterion | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Scientific accuracy | Distances, timescales, and instruments are plausible |
| Depth of research | Real missions and real technologies cited |
| Quality of the counter-argument | The strongest objection, fairly stated |
| Communication | A pitch that a non-scientist would follow |
