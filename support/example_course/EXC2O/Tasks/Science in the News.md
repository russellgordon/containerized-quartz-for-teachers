> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Frequency:** three times this semester · **Curriculum:** [[A2.2]], [[A2.4]], [[A2.5]]

## The task

Find a news article making a scientific claim, and take it apart.

## Every submission answers these

1. **The claim.** In one sentence, in your own words.
2. **The source of the evidence.** Is there a study behind it? Who paid for it?
   Can you find the study itself, or only reporting *about* it?
3. **The sample.** How many? Of what? Over how long?
4. **The gap.** What does the headline say that the study does not?
5. **Your verdict.** Believable, overstated, or wrong — and what would change
   your mind.

> [!example] Where headlines usually go wrong
> - **Correlation reported as cause.** "Coffee drinkers live longer" — or do
>   people who can afford coffee have better health care?
> - **Sample too small to matter.** Twelve participants is a pilot study.
> - **Relative risk without absolute risk.** "Doubles your risk" means little
>   if the risk was 1 in 2 million.
> - **A single study treated as settled.** One result is a hypothesis with
>   evidence, not a conclusion.

## For the third submission

Choose an article about **artificial intelligence in science**, and additionally
address: what work is the AI actually doing, and what claim is being made about
it that the evidence does not support? See [[A2.2]].
