One passage, several approaches. Method on [[Using a Critical Lens]].

**1.** Ophelia, 4.5, singing after her father's death. What does a
**feminist** reading ask?

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Who is permitted to speak, and under what condition? Ophelia's only
> unsupervised speech in the play arrives when it can be dismissed as
> madness — and its content is about sex and betrayal, which is exactly
> what she was forbidden to discuss when sane.

**2.** The same scene. What does a **materialist** reading ask?

> [!success]- Answer 2
> What is Ophelia's position in a household economy? She is an asset
> whose value is her marriageability, managed by a father and a brother
> and priced by a prince. Her collapse follows the removal of the man
> whose status secured hers.

**3.** The same scene. What does a **formalist** reading ask?

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Why songs, and why fragments? The scene moves from verse into snatches
> of ballad — a form that belongs to nobody and everybody — and the
> broken metre is the only place in the play where a line's collapse is
> not a character's choice.

**4.** Which reading would you defend, and what does it cost?

> [!success]- Answer 4
> No fixed answer, and the cost sentence is the point: the feminist
> reading can flatten the scene's strangeness; the materialist can
> underweight the language; the formalist can make it a technical
> exercise. Naming the cost is what makes an essay credible.

**5.** Take a passage from *The Handmaid's Tale* and do the same with two
approaches.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Test yourself with the question from
> [[Does the Lens Make the Reading]]: what passage would refute the
> reading you just produced? If none could, you have described the lens.
