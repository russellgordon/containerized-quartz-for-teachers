Turn each observation into a claim with a mechanism, per
[[Thesis and Argument]].

**1.** Observation: Laertes acts immediately in the same situation.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> *Laertes functions as the play's control: by giving a second son a
> murdered father and no hesitation, Shakespeare makes any account of
> Hamlet's delay that appeals to the situation alone untenable, and
> forces the explanation into language rather than circumstance.*

**2.** Observation: Hamlet's soliloquies use the vocabulary of proof.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> *The play stages delay as an epistemological problem rather than a
> temperamental one: Hamlet's language is saturated with legal and
> evidentiary terms, and every postponement follows a demand for proof
> that a ghost, by definition, cannot supply.*

**3.** Observation: Offred keeps correcting her own narration.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> *The corrections are not unreliability but method: Atwood stages the
> production of a testimony under conditions that make accuracy
> impossible, so that the reader has to decide what a compromised account
> is worth — a decision the Historical Notes then make for them, badly.*

**4.** Observation: the novel ends two centuries later, at a conference.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> *The Historical Notes convert testimony into material, and the joke
> made at Offred's expense implicates the reader who has just consumed
> her account as a story — which makes the frame the novel's final
> argument rather than its epilogue.*

**5.** Take three observations from your own annotations and write a
claim for each. Then mark the one you could disprove.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> The one you could disprove is the one worth writing about. A claim that
> cannot fail cannot be argued, and an essay proving something
> unfalsifiable is a summary with quotations in it.
