> Individual · five class periods · 1,200 words · assessed on Thinking,

## The task

One argument that needs both *Hamlet* and *The Handmaid's Tale*.

## The test your thesis must pass

Delete one text from your thesis sentence. If the claim survives, you do
not have a comparative argument yet — see [[Comparative Argument]].

## Questions that have worked

- Both texts make testimony depend on a hostile or indifferent audience.
  What does each conclude about whether the account survives?
- Both are obsessed with proof. What counts as evidence in each, and who
  gets to decide?
- Both give a protagonist a role written by somebody else. What does each
  text think refusal costs?
- Both end by handing the story to somebody who was not there.

## Requirements

- At least three passages from each text.
- Point-by-point organisation unless you can defend another shape in your
  outline.
- One paragraph where the comparison complicates your own claim.
- Attention to form: a play and a novel do not make arguments the same
  way, and that difference should appear in the essay.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Both texts known precisely, including the parts that resist the comparison |
| Thinking | One argument requiring both texts; the comparison does work in every paragraph |
| Communication | The reader always knows which text they are in and why |
