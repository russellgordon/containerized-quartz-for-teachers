> [!abstract] At a glance
> Groups of three · one 35-minute seminar led by your group, plus
> participation in the others · assessed on Communication, Thinking, and
> Knowledge

## The task

Lead a discussion of one act of *Hamlet* that the room could not have had
without your preparation.

## What your group brings

1. **A question the play does not settle**, approved two classes ahead.
2. **Four passages** with act, scene, and line.
3. **A lens**, named: which critical approach your question comes from,
   and why that one. See [[Critical Approaches]].
4. **A plan for the silence** and a closing move.

## What leading looks like at this level

You speak less than a fifth of the time, and your job is to make other
people's contributions collide productively. The moves are on
[[Seminar Skills]]; the one that separates a strong seminar from an
adequate one is **inviting the objection you fear**.

## Participation elsewhere

Assessed on the same terms: evidence, building, conceding. In this course
a concession is worth more than a clever point, because it is the only
move that proves listening happened.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The act is known closely; the passages are the right ones for the question |
| Thinking | The discussion tests something; your lens is used rather than announced |
| Communication | You listen, respond precisely, and make room |

> [!tip] Questions that have worked here
> "Does the Mousetrap prove anything?" · "What does the play do with
> Ophelia's silence in Act 3, and who is responsible for it?" · "Is
> Horatio a character or a device?"
