> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · October to April, four checkpoints · a text you choose, a
> 2,000-word essay, and a ten-minute oral defence · part of the final 30%

## The task

Choose a text, read it alone, research it, build an argument, write it at
length, and then defend it out loud to me and to two classmates who have
read your essay.

[[Choosing Your Independent Study Text]] has the list and the rules.

## The checkpoints

| Checkpoint | When | What is due |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. The choice** | Mid-October | Text approved, plus a paragraph on why this one |
| **2. The reading** | Early December | Annotations, a passage log of at least sixteen entries, three candidate questions |
| **3. The argument** | Mid-February | Thesis with a mechanism, a full outline, three secondary sources logged with your own sentence on each |
| **4. The draft** | Late March | A complete draft, workshopped in class |

Each is assessed. They exist because the failure mode of a six-month
project is invisible until April, and completely fixable in December.

## The essay

Two thousand words, evidence from across the whole text, at least three
secondary sources used to extend or complicate rather than to agree, a
counter-reading section, and a conclusion that says what follows. Method
on [[The Extended Essay]] and [[Research Writing]].

## The defence

Ten minutes. I ask, and so do your two readers:

- Why that passage and not the one before it?
- What is the strongest objection to your reading, and why does it fail?
- Which source changed your mind, and how?
- What would you need to find in the text to abandon your thesis?

Being asked those in front of somebody is the fastest way to find out
whether an argument is genuinely yours.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The whole text; evidence from all of it, not the first hundred pages |
| Thinking | An argument that could only come from reading this text closely |
| Communication | 2,000 words a reader can hold, and a defence that answers what was asked |
| Application | Research integrated and cited; conventions correct throughout |
