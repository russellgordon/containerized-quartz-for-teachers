## The task

One argument about *Hamlet*, proved from across the play, with the
strongest opposing reading given a full section and a real chance.

## Choosing the question

From your annotations. Questions that have produced strong essays:

- Is the delay temperamental or epistemological? What does the play's
  vocabulary of proof suggest?
- Laertes acts immediately in the same situation. What does the play
  argue by including him?
- What does the play do with Ophelia's speech — and with the fact that
  her madness is the only time she is allowed to say anything unedited?
- Why does the play end with Fortinbras?
- Is the ghost reliable, and does the play care?

## Structure

Thesis with a mechanism; four or five moves in a defensible order; a
counter-reading section; a conclusion that says what follows. The detail
is on [[The Extended Essay]].

## How it is built

| Period | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Pattern from annotations; question drafted |
| 2 | Conference; passages gathered and paraphrased |
| 3 | Outline, including the counter-reading you will engage |
| 4 | Draft |
| 5 | Workshop: partner marks unsupported claims and the weakest section |
| 6 | Rewrite; quotations and citations checked |

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The play known accurately, including where it inconveniences you |
| Thinking | A mechanism, not a theme; the counter-reading is the real one |
| Communication | Sustained structure; a reader can hold the argument across 1,500 words |
| Application | Conventions used correctly, including verse quotation and act-scene-line citation |
