> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · five class periods · 1,000 words · assessed on Thinking,
> Knowledge, and Communication

## The task

Read one text from [[Short Texts and Essays]] through **two** critical
approaches, and say what each one makes visible and what it costs.

Not "here is a feminist reading, here is a Marxist reading". One
argument, in which the second lens complicates the first.

## What it must contain

1. **A claim about the text** — not about the theory.
2. **The first reading**, argued from passages.
3. **The second lens applied to the same passages**, showing something
   the first could not see.
4. **A paragraph on the cost**: what each approach has to ignore.
5. **A conclusion that says which reading you would defend and why** —
   "both equally" is available only if you can show why the text requires
   it.

## How it is built

| Period | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Text chosen; read without a lens; annotations |
| 2 | Lens one applied; the questions it produces written down |
| 3 | Lens two applied to the same passages; conference with me |
| 4 | Draft |
| 5 | Workshop: partner marks anywhere theory replaced evidence |

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Both approaches understood well enough to be used rather than named |
| Thinking | The second lens genuinely complicates the first; the cost paragraph is honest |
| Communication | A reader who knows neither approach can still follow the argument |

> [!warning] The failure this task exists to prevent
> The essay that could have been written without the text. If your
> paragraphs work with the quotations removed, you have written about
> criticism rather than about literature — see [[Using a Critical Lens]].
