A critical lens is a set of questions that makes some things in a text
visible and other things harder to see. Using one well is a skill; using
one badly produces an essay that could have been written without reading
the book.

## The method

1. **Read the text first, without a lens.** Annotate normally.
2. **Choose the lens the text keeps handing you.** If half your marks are
   about who has money and who works, that is a signal.
3. **Ask the lens's questions of the text**, and write down the answers
   that surprise you.
4. **Test the reading against a passage that resists it.** Every lens has
   a blind spot; naming yours is what makes the essay credible.
5. **Argue from the text**, never from the theory. The lens produced the
   question; the passage has to produce the answer.

## What each lens asks

| Lens | Its questions |
| --- | --- |
| **Formalist** | How is this made? What do structure, imagery, and diction do? |
| **Feminist** | Who acts and who is acted upon? Whose interiority do we get? What does the text assume about gender? |
| **Postcolonial** | Who is centred, who is othered, whose land and language is this, and who is speaking for whom? |
| **Marxist / materialist** | Who works, who owns, who benefits, and what does the text treat as natural? |
| **Psychoanalytic** | What is repressed, displaced, or repeated? What does the text refuse to say? |
| **Reader-response** | What does the text ask a reader to do, and which reader does it assume? |

## The failure modes

- **The verdict.** "This text is patriarchal." Possibly — but a verdict
  is not a reading, and it usually stops the essay at the moment it
  should start.
- **The costume.** Naming a lens in the introduction and never using its
  questions again.
- **The text ignored.** If your paragraph works without the quotation,
  you have written about the theory, not the text.

> [!tip] The test that predicts the mark
> Would somebody who has read the text but not the theory find your
> reading persuasive? If yes, the lens is doing its job — making
> something visible that was always there.
