> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Margaret Atwood** · *The Handmaid's Tale* (McClelland and Stewart,
> 1985) · winner of the Governor General's Literary Award · read across
> six weeks

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> A regime built on the control of women's bodies: state-organised rape,
> executions, forced separation of families, and the policing of
> literacy. It is not gratuitous and it is not comfortable.

## The premise

In the Republic of Gilead, a theocracy that has replaced part of the
United States, fertile women are assigned to households as Handmaids. One
of them tells us what happened, on tape, afterwards.

## What to notice

- **The tense.** Present tense for the Handmaid's life, past for the
  before. Ask what that does to a reader's sense of what is inevitable.
- **The corrections.** "I made that up. It didn't happen that way." The
  novel keeps showing the seams of its own testimony — see
  [[Narration and Testimony]].
- **The before.** The flashbacks contain the argument: the accounts
  frozen, the jobs ended, the friend who says it will pass.
- **Serena Joy.** A woman who campaigned for the arrangement that now
  silences her.
- **Language.** Gilead's renaming — Ofglen, Ofwarren, the Ceremony, the
  Particicution — and what a regime does by controlling nouns.
- **The Historical Notes.** Read them twice. The second time, ask what
  the professor's jokes are doing to the testimony you have just
  finished.

## Questions we will argue about

1. Is Offred a resister? Does the novel want her to be, and does it
   punish the reader who does?
2. Atwood has said she included nothing that had not already happened
   somewhere. What does that claim do to how we read it — and is it
   entirely true?
3. What is the Historical Notes section for, and whom does it indict?
4. Read through a feminist lens and then a materialist one — what does
   each show, and what does each miss? See [[Critical Approaches]].

[[The Lens Essay]] and [[The Comparative Essay]], where it sits beside
*Hamlet* on the question of what testimony needs in order to survive.
