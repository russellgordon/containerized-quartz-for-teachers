> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Format:** two pages. **Individually.** Due at the end of Unit 2.
> **Assessed on:** Knowledge, Application, Communication.

Take one real situation and analyse the forces in it properly — the way
an engineer would, not the way a textbook question does.

## Choose one

- A car braking hard on a wet road.
- A climber hanging from a rope on an overhang.
- A tow truck pulling a car up a ramp.
- A cyclist rounding a corner. (Ambitious — the physics is Grade 12, but
  the free-body diagram is not.)
- Your own, cleared with me first.

## What to produce

1. **A free-body diagram** for the object of interest, with every force
   labelled by what exerts it.
2. **A calculation**: given plausible numbers you state and justify,
   find the acceleration, or the force required, or the minimum
   coefficient of friction — whichever the situation turns on.
3. **A change, analysed**: alter one condition (wet road, steeper ramp,
   heavier load) and say what changes and by how much.
4. **A safety sentence**: what does your analysis imply about how the
   situation should be handled in practice?

## Where to get plausible numbers

[[Coefficients of Friction]] gives you $\mu$ values you measured
yourself. Masses can be looked up and cited. Anything you assume, say so
in one line — an assumption stated is good practice; an assumption hidden
is a mark lost.
