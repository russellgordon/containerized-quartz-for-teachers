> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Format:** a 20-second video plus one page of graphs and analysis.
> **Individually or in pairs.** Due at the end of Unit 1.
> **Assessed on:** Knowledge, Thinking and Investigation, Communication.

Find a real motion in the world, film it, and tell its story three times —
in words, in graphs, and in numbers.

## The brief

1. **Film something that moves** and changes its motion at least once: a
   skateboard rolling to a stop, a lift starting and stopping, a ball
   thrown upward, a bus pulling away. It must be safe, legal, and yours.
2. **Analyse it** frame by frame — see [[Analysing Video of Motion]].
3. **Produce three descriptions** of the same motion:
   - a paragraph in plain words;
   - a position–time graph AND a velocity–time graph, drawn from your
     data;
   - the acceleration during one phase, with units and an uncertainty.
4. **Answer one question**: at what instant was the velocity zero, and
   was the acceleration zero at that instant too?

## Requirements

- The metre stick (or other known length) must be visible in the video.
- Your graphs must be built from your own measurements, not sketched.
- State your positive direction and origin. Hold them.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The graphs match the motion, including the signs |
| Thinking | The analysis handles the phase change, not just the easy part |
| Communication | Someone who did not see the video could describe the motion from your page |

> [!tip] The best submissions
> pick something with a genuinely interesting middle — the moment a
> skateboard stops being pushed, the instant a ball reverses. A car
> driving past at constant speed is easy to analyse and has nothing to
> say.
