> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Format:** three pages plus measurements. **Pairs.** Due at the end of
> Unit 4. **Assessed on:** Thinking and Investigation, Application.

Pick a room in this school and explain what it does to sound — then say
what you would change and what it would cost.

## The brief

1. **Measure** something real about the space: the time a clap takes to
   die away, where the echoes come from, or how the loudness of a fixed
   source changes with distance across it.
2. **Explain** what you measured using [[Wave Properties]],
   [[Interference and Beats]], and [[Resonance and Standing Waves]] —
   whichever actually apply. Do not force all three.
3. **Recommend** one change (soft surfaces on one wall, a different
   speaker position, angling a reflector) and predict its effect.
4. **Cost it**, roughly. A recommendation with no cost attached is a wish.

## Rooms that repay the effort

The gym, the cafeteria at lunch, the stairwell, the music room, and the
corridor outside the library are all acoustically interesting for
different reasons. The physics lab is not.

## What I am looking for

A measurement you actually took, connected to a wave idea that genuinely
explains it, ending in a recommendation someone could act on. Three
paragraphs of general facts about sound, with no numbers, is the failure
mode.
