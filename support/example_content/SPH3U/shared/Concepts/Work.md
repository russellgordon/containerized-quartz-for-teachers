In physics, work is force times displacement in the direction of the
force:

$$W = Fd\cos\theta$$

measured in joules. One joule is one newton-metre.

## What counts, and what does not

Carrying a heavy box across a level room does NO work on the box, in this
sense: the force you exert is upward, the displacement is horizontal,
$\cos 90^\circ = 0$. Your muscles disagree, and they are right about
biology — but the box gained no energy, and that is what work tracks.

Push the box along the floor and you do work. Lift it and you do work.
Hold it still and you do none.

## Work is a transfer of energy

That is the whole reason to define it. Do work on something and you change
its energy — its speed ([[Kinetic Energy]]), its height
([[Gravitational Potential Energy]]), or its temperature, if friction got
there first.

## Negative work

Friction acts opposite to the motion, so $\cos 180^\circ = -1$ and the
work is negative: it takes energy OUT. The area under a force–displacement
graph gives the work, signs included — see [[Graphing Data]].
