A ramp is where free-body diagrams start earning their keep, because
gravity no longer points along the motion.

## Tilt the axes, not the ramp

Choose your $x$ axis ALONG the slope and your $y$ axis perpendicular to
it. Then the object accelerates only in $x$, and the $y$ forces balance.
This one choice turns a hard problem into two easy ones.

Gravity splits into:

$$F_{g,\parallel} = mg\sin\theta \qquad F_{g,\perp} = mg\cos\theta$$

## What follows

- The normal force is $mg\cos\theta$, NOT $mg$ — it shrinks as the ramp
  steepens.
- Since friction is $\mu F_N$, friction shrinks too.
- With no friction, $a = g\sin\theta$: independent of mass, again.

> [!example] The angle where it starts to slide
> Tilt slowly until an object just begins to move, and at that angle
> $\tan\theta = \mu_s$. That is the whole method behind
> [[Coefficients of Friction]] — one angle, no force sensor needed.
