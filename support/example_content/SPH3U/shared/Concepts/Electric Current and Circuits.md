**Current** is charge flowing past a point: one ampere is one coulomb per
second. **Potential difference** (voltage) is the energy given to each
coulomb: one volt is one joule per coulomb.

The plumbing analogy is imperfect but useful: current is the flow rate,
voltage is the pressure difference driving it, resistance is how narrow
the pipe is. Where it breaks down is that charge is never used up — the
same electrons return to the battery. What gets used up is energy.

## A circuit needs a complete loop

No loop, no current. This sounds obvious and is the cause of most
non-working circuits in the lab, usually because a wire looks connected
and is not.

## Reading a schematic

| Symbol | Component |
| --- | --- |
| Long and short parallel lines | Cell (battery) |
| Zigzag or rectangle | Resistor |
| Circle with a cross | Lamp |
| Circle with A | Ammeter, wired IN the loop |
| Circle with V | Voltmeter, wired ACROSS the component |

That last distinction — ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel — is the
practical skill of this unit, and it is why [[Ohm's Law]] can be tested at
all.
