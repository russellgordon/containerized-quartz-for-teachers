When two objects are connected by a rope, they share an acceleration. That
one sentence solves most pulley problems.

## Two ways to attack them

**As a system.** Treat both masses as one object. The tension is internal
and cancels, so only the outside forces matter:

$$a = \frac{F_{net,\text{external}}}{m_{total}}$$

**As two objects.** Draw a free-body diagram for each, write
$F_{net} = ma$ for each, and solve the pair. Slower, but it also gives you
the tension — which the system method hides.

Use the first to get the acceleration, the second when the question asks
about the rope.

## What a light, frictionless pulley does

It changes the direction of the tension and nothing else. The tension is
the same throughout the rope. Real pulleys have mass and friction, which
is one of the honest reasons a measured acceleration comes out lower than
the predicted one.
