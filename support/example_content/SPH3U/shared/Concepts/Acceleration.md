Acceleration is the rate at which VELOCITY changes:

$$a_{av} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{\Delta t}$$

Its unit, $\text{m/s}^2$, is metres per second, per second: how much the
velocity changes in each second.

## Three things acceleration is not

- **It is not speed.** A jet at constant $900\ \text{km/h}$ has zero
  acceleration. A car pulling away from a light at $20\ \text{km/h}$ has
  plenty.
- **It is not always speeding up.** Negative acceleration means the
  velocity is becoming more negative — which is slowing down if you are
  moving forward, and speeding up if you are moving backward.
- **It does not require motion.** At the very top of its flight, a thrown
  ball has zero velocity and an acceleration of $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$
  downward. That is why it does not stay there.

## Free fall

Near Earth's surface, anything falling with air resistance small enough to
ignore accelerates downward at about $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$. This value has
a name, $g$, and it does not depend on mass — the fact that surprises
everyone the first time, and the one [[Measuring g]] is built to test.
