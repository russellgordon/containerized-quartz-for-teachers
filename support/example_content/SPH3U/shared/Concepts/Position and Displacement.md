**Position** is where something is, measured from an origin you chose.
**Displacement** is the change in position: where it ended up, minus where
it started, with a direction attached.

Distance is not displacement, and the difference is the whole point.

> [!example] The walk to the far wall and back
> You walk 8 m to the wall and 8 m back. The **distance** you travelled is
> 16 m. Your **displacement** is zero — you are where you began. A
> pedometer measures the first; physics usually cares about the second.

## Scalars and vectors

A quantity with size only is a **scalar**: distance, speed, mass, time. A
quantity with size and direction is a **vector**: displacement, velocity,
acceleration, force. Writing a vector without its direction is not a
shortcut, it is a missing answer.

In one dimension the direction collapses to a sign, which is why this unit
insists you pick a positive direction and stick to it.

## Why displacement is the useful one

Because it is what the equations of motion are about, and because it adds
sensibly. Two displacements of $+5\ \text{m}$ and $-3\ \text{m}$ give
$+2\ \text{m}$ — the answer a map would give you. Two distances would
give 8 m, which describes your tiredness rather than your location.
