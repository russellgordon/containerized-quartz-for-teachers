$$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$$

The energy something has because it is moving, in joules.

## The square is the important part

Double the mass and the kinetic energy doubles. Double the SPEED and it
quadruples. This is not a detail — it is why speed limits exist, why a
50 km/h crash is not half as bad as a 100 km/h one, and why the last
metre of a roller coaster drop delivers more than the first.

| Speed | Kinetic energy of a 1200 kg car |
| --- | --- |
| 30 km/h | 42 kJ |
| 60 km/h | 167 kJ |
| 120 km/h | 667 kJ |

## Where it comes from

Do work on a stationary object and it speeds up. The work–energy theorem
says the net work done equals the change in kinetic energy:

$$W_{net} = \Delta E_k$$

which is the same physics as $F_{net} = ma$, written in the currency of
energy rather than force. Sometimes one is easier; usually it is this one,
because energy has no direction to keep track of.
