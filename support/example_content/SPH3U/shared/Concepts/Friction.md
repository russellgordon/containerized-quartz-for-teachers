Friction is the force a surface exerts along itself, opposing sliding. It
is the reason Newton's first law feels wrong and the reason you can walk.

$$F_f = \mu F_N$$

The coefficient $\mu$ has no units — it is a ratio of two forces — and it
depends on both surfaces, not one. "The friction of rubber" is meaningless
until you say rubber on what.

## Static and kinetic

**Static** friction acts before sliding starts, and it adjusts itself to
whatever is needed, up to a maximum. **Kinetic** friction acts during
sliding, and it is roughly constant. For most pairs of surfaces
$\mu_s > \mu_k$, which is why a heavy box is harder to start than to keep
moving — and why anti-lock brakes are worth having.

## What it does not depend on

Surface area, and speed. Both surprise people, and both are approximations
that hold well enough for the situations in this course.
