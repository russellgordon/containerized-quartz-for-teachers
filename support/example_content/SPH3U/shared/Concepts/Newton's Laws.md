Three sentences that between them explain every motion in this course.

## First: things keep doing what they are doing

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps its
velocity, unless a net force acts. The everyday feeling that motion needs
a continuous push is a statement about friction, not about motion.

## Second: net force causes acceleration

$$\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a}$$

Twice the net force, twice the acceleration. Twice the mass, half the
acceleration. Note what it does NOT say: it says nothing about velocity.
A large force on a fast object can slow it down.

## Third: forces come in pairs

If A pushes B, then B pushes A, equally hard, in the opposite direction.
The two forces of a pair NEVER act on the same object — which is why they
do not cancel. You push the ground backward; the ground pushes you
forward; you accelerate because of the second one.

> [!warning] The pair that is not a pair
> Gravity pulls you down and the floor pushes you up. Those are NOT a
> third-law pair — they act on the same object, and they happen to be
> equal only because you are not accelerating. Step into a lift and one of
> them changes.
