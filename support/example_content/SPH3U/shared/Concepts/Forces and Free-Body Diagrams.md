A force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons, and it always comes from
something. "The force of motion" does not exist; the force of the floor on
your feet does.

## The free-body diagram

Draw the object as a dot. Draw every force acting ON it as an arrow
pointing away from the dot, in the direction it acts, roughly to scale.
Label each arrow with what is exerting it.

That last rule is the one that fixes most mistakes. If you cannot name
what exerts a force, it is not there.

## The forces this course uses

| Symbol | Force | Exerted by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $F_g$ | Gravity (weight) | The Earth |
| $F_N$ | Normal force | The surface, perpendicular to it |
| $F_f$ | Friction | The surface, along it |
| $F_T$ | Tension | A rope or string |
| $F_a$ | Applied force | Whatever is pushing or pulling |

## Net force is a vector sum

$$\vec{F}_{net} = \sum \vec{F}$$

Add them with their directions. If the net force is zero the object's
velocity does not change — which does not mean it is not moving. See
[[Newton's Laws]].
