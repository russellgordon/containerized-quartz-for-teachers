$$V = IR$$

For many materials, the current through them is proportional to the
potential difference across them. Resistance $R$, in ohms, is the constant
of proportionality.

## It is a finding, not a definition

Ohm's law is a description of how certain materials behave — and plenty do
not. A filament lamp's resistance rises as it heats, so its graph curves.
A diode conducts one way and not the other. Calling those "non-ohmic" is
not an insult; it is a description.

Which is why the way to test it is to MEASURE a graph of $V$ against $I$
and see whether it is a straight line through the origin. A straight line
means ohmic, and its slope is the resistance. That is exactly
[[Ohm's Law and Resistance]].

## Power in a circuit

$$P = IV$$

and, combining with Ohm's law, $P = I^2R$. This is why a kettle draws a
large current, why extension cords have limits, and why the wire that
carries the current gets warm while the appliance does the useful work.
