$$F_g = mg$$

**Mass** is how much matter there is, in kilograms, and it is the same
everywhere. **Weight** is the force gravity exerts on that mass, in
newtons, and it changes with where you are.

A 60 kg student weighs about 590 N on Earth and about 98 N on the Moon.
Their mass is 60 kg in both places.

## Why g is both things

$g = 9.8$ is the free-fall acceleration in $\text{m/s}^2$ AND the weight
per kilogram in $\text{N/kg}$. Those are the same number for the same
reason: $F_g = mg$ and $F_{net} = ma$ are the same equation when gravity
is the only force. That coincidence is exactly why every object falls at
the same rate — [[Measuring g]] tests it.

## Apparent weight

What a scale reads is the normal force, not the gravity. Accelerate
upward in a lift and the scale reads more; accelerate downward and it
reads less; cut the cable and it reads zero, which is what "weightless"
actually means. Nothing about gravity changed in any of those.
