The difference between students who find physics hard and students who
find it manageable is almost never algebra. It is the first ninety seconds.

## The routine

1. **Draw it.** Even for one-dimensional motion. Mark the origin and the
   positive direction.
2. **List what you know**, with units and signs, and what you want.
3. **Name the physics** — which idea is this? Constant acceleration?
   Conservation of energy? Say it out loud before reaching for an
   equation.
4. **Then choose the equation**, and only then. Every equation this
   course uses is on [[Formula Reference]].
5. **Check the answer for sense.** Is it the right size? The right sign?
   Would you believe it about a real object?

## Step 5 is not optional

A car that accelerates at $400\ \text{m/s}^2$, a mass that arrives with
negative kinetic energy, a resistance of $0.0002\ \Omega$ in a classroom
circuit — all of these are arithmetic that "worked". Physics answers are
about the world, and the world constrains them.

> [!tip] When you are properly stuck
> Write down what you would need to know in order to finish. Half the time
> that sentence tells you which equation you are missing; the other half
> it is exactly the question to bring to [[Help Sessions]].
