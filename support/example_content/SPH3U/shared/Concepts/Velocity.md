Velocity is the rate at which position changes:

$$v_{av} = \frac{\Delta d}{\Delta t}$$

Read it as a sentence: displacement per unit of time, with the direction
of the displacement.

## Average is not instantaneous

Average velocity smooths over everything that happened in between. Drive
120 km in 2 hours and your average is 60 km/h even if you stopped for
twenty minutes and drove at 100 the rest of the way.

**Instantaneous** velocity is the velocity right now — the slope of the
tangent to a position–time graph at a single instant. On
[[Position–Time Graphs]] you find it by drawing that tangent; a speedometer
finds it by measuring over an interval short enough not to matter.

## Uniform motion

If velocity never changes, the motion is **uniform**, and the position–time
graph is a straight line. That is rarer than textbooks suggest — a puck on
ice, a car on cruise control, light in a vacuum. Everything else is
[[Acceleration]].

> [!tip] The sign is information
> A velocity of $-4\ \text{m/s}$ is not "slower than $4\ \text{m/s}$".
> It is the same speed in the other direction.
