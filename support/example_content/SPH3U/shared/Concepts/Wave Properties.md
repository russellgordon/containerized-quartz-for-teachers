A wave is a disturbance that travels while the medium stays put. Shake one
end of a long spring and a pulse runs to the far end — but no coil of the
spring makes that journey. Energy moves; matter does not.

## The five quantities

| Quantity | Symbol | Means | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wavelength | $\lambda$ | Distance between repeats | m |
| Frequency | $f$ | Repeats per second | Hz |
| Period | $T$ | Seconds per repeat | s |
| Amplitude | $A$ | How far the medium moves | m |
| Speed | $v$ | How fast the disturbance travels | m/s |

Two relationships tie them together:

$$T = \frac{1}{f} \qquad v = f\lambda$$

## The one that surprises people

**Speed is set by the medium, not by the source.** Shake the spring faster
and you get a shorter wavelength, not a faster wave. That is why $v = f\lambda$
is so useful: with $v$ fixed, $f$ and $\lambda$ trade off exactly.

## Transverse and longitudinal

In a transverse wave the medium moves across the direction of travel (a
spring flicked sideways, light). In a longitudinal wave it moves along it
— compressions and rarefactions. [[Sound Waves]] are longitudinal, which
is why sound needs something to travel through.
