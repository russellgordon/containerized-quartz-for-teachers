Three graphs describe the same motion, and each is built from the one
before it. Being able to move between them — in both directions — is most
of what Unit 1 asks.

## The chain

$$\text{position} \xrightarrow{\ \text{slope}\ } \text{velocity} \xrightarrow{\ \text{slope}\ } \text{acceleration}$$

and back the other way by area:

$$\text{acceleration} \xrightarrow{\ \text{area}\ } \text{velocity} \xrightarrow{\ \text{area}\ } \text{position}$$

## What each graph shows

| On a… | The slope gives | The area gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position–time graph | Velocity | — |
| Velocity–time graph | Acceleration | Displacement |
| Acceleration–time graph | — | Change in velocity |

## Area below the axis counts as negative

A velocity–time graph that dips below zero contributes NEGATIVE
displacement — the object came back. Add the areas with their signs and
you get displacement; add their sizes and you get distance travelled.
That single distinction explains most disagreements about these graphs.

## Sketching without numbers

Being able to sketch the shape — flat, sloped, curved — before calculating
anything is worth more than it looks. It catches sign errors and
impossible answers before they cost you a page of algebra.
