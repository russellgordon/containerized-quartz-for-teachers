## The SI units this course uses

| Quantity | Unit | Symbol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | metre | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Force | newton | N |
| Energy, work | joule | J |
| Power | watt | W |
| Frequency | hertz | Hz |
| Current | ampere | A |
| Potential difference | volt | V |
| Resistance | ohm | Ω |

A newton is $1\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2$ and a joule is
$1\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$. Those two facts resolve most unit
confusion in this course, and they are worth memorising as sentences
rather than symbols.

## Significant figures, briefly

Your answer cannot be more precise than your worst measurement. If you
timed with a stopwatch to $\pm 0.2\ \text{s}$, an answer to five decimal
places is a claim you cannot support.

- Multiplying or dividing: keep as many significant figures as the
  **fewest** any input had.
- Adding or subtracting: keep as many **decimal places** as the coarsest
  input had.
- Round once, at the end. Rounding at every step manufactures error.

> [!example] The one that catches everyone
> $2.0\ \text{m} \div 3.0\ \text{s} = 0.67\ \text{m/s}$, not
> $0.6666667\ \text{m/s}$. The calculator does not know how carefully you
> measured; you do.
