In physics a graph is not an illustration of the answer. It usually IS the
answer: the slope or the area is the quantity you are after.

## The rules that make a graph readable

1. **Manipulated variable on the horizontal axis**, responded on the
   vertical. Time almost always goes horizontal.
2. **Label both axes with the quantity AND its unit** — "Position (m)",
   not "Position".
3. **Scale the axes evenly**, and do not feel obliged to start at zero
   unless the intercept matters.
4. **Plot the points, then draw the trend** — a straight edge for a linear
   trend, a smooth curve otherwise. Never join the dots.

## Slope and area carry meaning

| Graph | Slope means | Area under it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position–time | Velocity | (nothing useful) |
| Velocity–time | Acceleration | Displacement |
| Force–displacement | (nothing useful) | Work done |

That table is worth more than it looks: half of Units 1 and 3 is reading
it in one direction or the other. See [[Motion Graphs]] and [[Work]].

## Straightening a curve

If a graph curves, you have not failed — you have found a relationship
that is not linear. Plotting against $x^2$, or $1/x$, and getting a
straight line tells you what the relationship IS. That is how
[[The Kinematic Equations]] were found in the first place.
