Physics is not a subject you can learn by watching. Almost every class
starts with something happening — a cart rolling, a spring bouncing, a
circuit refusing to work — and the explaining comes after, once we have
something that needs explaining.

## The shape of a class

1. **A question, and a guess.** Usually written on the board before you
   sit down. Guess out loud; being wrong early is cheap.
2. **Do the thing.** Measure it, build it, or watch it carefully.
3. **Name it.** This is where the vocabulary and the equations arrive —
   attached to something you have already seen.
4. **Practise.** Short sets, in class, where I can see what is going
   sideways. See [[Exercises/index|Exercises]].

## What you keep

Everything that explains an idea lives in [[Concepts/index|Concepts]] and
stays there all semester. Lab procedures live in
[[Investigations/index|Investigations]]. Anything I am marking has its own
page in [[Tasks/index|Tasks]], written before you start it.

Your own record is your [[Physics Journal]] — the one place where you
write physics in your own words rather than mine.

## Equipment and the lab

We are in the lab most weeks. Read [[Safety in the Lab]] before the first
investigation, and read it again before anything involving mass at height,
heat, or mains electricity.

## Getting unstuck

The single most useful sentence in this course is "I expected X and got Y."
Bring me that and we can start. See [[Getting Help]] for when and where.
