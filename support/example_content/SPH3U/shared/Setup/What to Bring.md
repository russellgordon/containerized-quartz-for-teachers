| What | When | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A notebook you do not mind ruining | Every class | Lab benches are wet, dusty, and magnetic |
| A calculator that does trigonometry | Every class | Phone calculators are not allowed on tests |
| A ruler and a pencil | Every class | Graphs and vector diagrams are drawn, not typed |
| Closed shoes | Lab days | Falling masses, dropped glass |
| Your own device (optional) | Video analysis days | The school set works if you have none |

A scientific calculator costs less than a textbook and lasts the whole of
high school. If that is a problem, tell me privately and one will appear
in your hands with no discussion.

> [!tip] The graph paper question
> You do not need to buy graph paper. Hand-drawn axes on plain paper are
> fine, and by Unit 2 most of your graphs will be made in a spreadsheet
> anyway — see [[Graphing Data]].
