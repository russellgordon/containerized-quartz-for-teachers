A ball dropped from 1 m does not return to 1 m. Energy is conserved, so
the missing energy went somewhere. Your job is to say how much, and where.

## Safety

Drop, do not throw. Superballs are genuinely dangerous at eye height.

## Method

1. Drop the ball from a measured height $h_1$ beside a metre stick.
2. Film it, or have two people watch the peak of the first bounce and
   agree. Record the rebound height $h_2$.
3. Five trials per ball, three different balls.

## The accounting

Since mass cancels, the fraction of energy returned is just

$$\frac{E_2}{E_1} = \frac{mgh_2}{mgh_1} = \frac{h_2}{h_1}$$

which is a useful surprise: you can measure an energy ratio with a ruler.

## What to record

| Ball | $h_1$ (m) | $h_2$ (m) | Fraction returned |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Analysis

1. Rank your three balls. What is physically different about the ones
   that return the most?
2. Where did the missing energy go? Name at least two destinations, and
   say how you could detect one of them.
3. If you dropped from twice the height, would the FRACTION returned stay
   the same? Predict first, then test.
