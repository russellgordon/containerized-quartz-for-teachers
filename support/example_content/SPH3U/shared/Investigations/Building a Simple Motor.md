> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Pairs.** This is the build behind the
> [[Motor and Generator Report]] task.

A battery, a magnet, some enamelled wire, two paper clips. Twenty minutes
of fiddling and a thing spins on your bench — driven by exactly the
physics on [[The Motor Principle]].

## Safety

The coil and the battery get warm; disconnect between runs. Neodymium
magnets pinch hard and shatter if they snap together — one magnet per
pair, kept away from phones and cards.

## Build

1. Wind about 20 turns of enamelled wire around a AA battery to form a
   coil roughly 2 cm across. Leave 3 cm straight at each end, opposite one
   another, as the axle.
2. **Strip the enamel from ONE side only** on one end, and all the way
   round on the other. This is the step everyone skips and the reason
   most motors do not run.
3. Bend two paper clips into cradles, tape them to the battery terminals,
   rest the coil in them, and put the magnet underneath.
4. Nudge it to start.

## Why the half-stripped end matters

It is a commutator. Current flows for half a turn and is cut for the other
half, so the twist is always in the same direction. Strip both sides fully
and the coil rocks instead of turning — which is worth doing once,
deliberately, to see.

## What to record

Sketch your motor with the current direction, the field direction, and the
force on each side of the coil marked. Then: three things you changed to
make it spin faster, and what each did.
