> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Groups of three.** Bring shoes you can move in.
> Nothing here is marked; what you notice is used in the next four classes.

A motion sensor at one end of the lab, a strip of tape on the floor, and a
graph on the board. Your job is to walk so that the sensor draws the graph
on the board.

## Safety

Walk, do not run. Watch for bags and stools — you will be looking at a
screen, not at the floor.

## What to do

1. Set the sensor at chest height, and stand about 0.5 m from it.
2. For each of the four graphs on the board, decide as a group HOW you
   will walk before anyone walks. Write the plan in one sentence.
3. Walk it. Look at what the sensor drew.
4. If it does not match, say why in one sentence, then try again.

## The four graphs

| Graph | What it demands |
| --- | --- |
| Flat, high | Stand still, far from the sensor |
| Straight, sloping down | Walk toward the sensor at a steady pace |
| Steeper, sloping down | The same, faster |
| Curved, flattening out | Walk toward it, slowing as you go |

## What to record

For each graph: your one-sentence plan, a sketch of what the sensor
actually drew, and — if they differed — the sentence explaining why.

## The question to leave with

Two of those graphs describe motion at constant speed and two do not. How
could you tell, from the graph alone, without having watched the walker?
That question is the whole of [[Position–Time Graphs]].
