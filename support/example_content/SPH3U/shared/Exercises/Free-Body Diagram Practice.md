Draw each one before reading the answer. Label every force with what
exerts it — the rule from [[Forces and Free-Body Diagrams]].

**1.** A book resting on a table.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Two forces: gravity down (Earth on book), normal force up (table on
> book). Equal in size because the book is not accelerating — but NOT a
> third-law pair, since both act on the book.

**2.** A box being pushed at constant speed across a rough floor.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Four: gravity down, normal up, applied force forward, friction backward.
> Applied equals friction, and normal equals gravity, because the
> acceleration is zero in both directions.

**3.** A ball at the highest point of its flight, thrown straight up.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> One force: gravity, down. Nothing else touches it. Its velocity is zero
> and its acceleration is $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ downward — which is why it
> does not stay there.

**4.** A crate sliding DOWN a ramp, slowing as it goes.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Gravity down, normal perpendicular to the ramp surface, friction UP the
> slope (opposing the sliding). Friction must exceed the component of
> gravity along the slope for it to be slowing.
