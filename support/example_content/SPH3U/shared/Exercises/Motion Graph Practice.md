Sketching before calculating. Do these on paper, freehand.

**1.** A cyclist rides east at a steady $6\ \text{m/s}$ for 10 s, stops
for 5 s, then rides west at $4\ \text{m/s}$ for 5 s. Sketch the
position–time and velocity–time graphs, taking east as positive.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Position–time: a straight line rising to $60\ \text{m}$ over 10 s, then
> flat for 5 s, then falling to $40\ \text{m}$ over the last 5 s.
> Velocity–time: $+6$ for 10 s, then $0$ for 5 s, then $-4$ for 5 s —
> three horizontal segments.

**2.** A velocity–time graph is a straight line from
$(0\ \text{s},\ 2\ \text{m/s})$ to $(8\ \text{s},\ 10\ \text{m/s})$.
Find the acceleration and the displacement.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Slope: $a = (10 - 2)/8 = 1.0\ \text{m/s}^2$.
> Area (a trapezoid): $\Delta d = \tfrac{1}{2}(2 + 10)(8) = 48\ \text{m}$.

**3.** A position–time graph curves upward, getting steeper. Describe the
motion in one sentence, and say what the velocity–time graph looks like.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Moving in the positive direction and speeding up. The velocity–time
> graph is a straight line sloping upward, starting at whatever the
> initial velocity was.

**4.** Sketch a velocity–time graph whose area is zero but whose motion
is never zero.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Any graph with equal areas above and below the axis — for example
> $+3\ \text{m/s}$ for 4 s then $-3\ \text{m/s}$ for 4 s. The object
> moved the whole time and finished where it started.
