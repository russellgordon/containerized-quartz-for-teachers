**1.** How much work is done lifting a $12\ \text{kg}$ box
$1.5\ \text{m}$ onto a shelf?

> [!success]- Answer 1
> $W = F d = (12 \times 9.8)(1.5) = 176\ \text{J}$, which is also the
> gravitational potential energy it gained.

**2.** A $0.15\ \text{kg}$ ball is dropped from $2.0\ \text{m}$. How
fast is it moving just before it lands, ignoring air resistance?

> [!success]- Answer 2
> $mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$, so $v = \sqrt{2gh} = 6.3\ \text{m/s}$.
> The mass cancels — which is the same fact as everything falling at the
> same rate.

**3.** That ball rebounds to $1.4\ \text{m}$. What fraction of its energy
came back, and where did the rest go?

> [!success]- Answer 3
> $1.4/2.0 = 70\%$. The other 30% became heat in the ball and the floor,
> and sound — the thud is energy leaving.

**4.** A $60\ \text{kg}$ student climbs a $3.2\ \text{m}$ staircase in
$4.5\ \text{s}$. Find the power developed.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> $W = mgh = 1880\ \text{J}$; $P = W/t = 1880/4.5 = 420\ \text{W}$.
> About the same as five bright old-style light bulbs, sustained.

**5.** How much energy is needed to raise $0.50\ \text{kg}$ of water from
$20^\circ\text{C}$ to boiling? ($c = 4180\ \text{J/kg}^\circ\text{C}$)

> [!success]- Answer 5
> $Q = mc\Delta T = 0.50 \times 4180 \times 80 = 1.7 \times 10^5\ \text{J}$.
