Ontario requires speed limiters on most heavy trucks, capping them at
105 km/h. Cars are not limited. Should they be?

## The physics you need first

Stopping distance has two parts: the distance covered during reaction time
(proportional to speed) and the braking distance (proportional to speed
SQUARED, because it is set by kinetic energy). Doubling your speed roughly
doubles the first and quadruples the second.

| Speed | Reaction (1.5 s) | Braking | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60 km/h | 25 m | 22 m | 47 m |
| 100 km/h | 42 m | 62 m | 104 m |
| 120 km/h | 50 m | 89 m | 139 m |

## The question for the room

If the numbers above are the argument for limiting cars, what is the
argument against? List them, then sort them into three piles: claims
physics can settle, claims data could settle, and claims about values.

## What the discussion is for

Notice which pile is biggest. Most disagreements that feel like arguments
about facts turn out to be arguments about how much freedom a measured
risk buys.
