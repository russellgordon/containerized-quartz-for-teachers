A body is a set of systems that depend on each other, and the clearest
way to see a dependency is to look at what happens when one part of it
stops working. That is what this task is: one condition, explained
properly, for somebody who needs to understand it.

## The situation

A condition affecting a tissue, an organ, or a system has been
explained to somebody badly. They have been given either a wall of
technical language they cannot use, or a reassuring paragraph that
tells them nothing. You are going to do it properly.

Choose **one condition** affecting humans or plants. Some starting
points, and you are not limited to them:

- A condition of the circulatory, respiratory, or digestive system
- A cancer, and what screening actually detects
- A plant disease that damages a crop — a rust, a wilt, a mosaic virus
- A condition where the failure of one system visibly damages another

Then choose a **technological development** connected to it — an
imaging or screening technology, a transplant, a stem-cell therapy, a
genetically modified crop line — and find the ethical question it
raises. Not "is it good or bad", but the specific disagreement between
specific interests.

## Who it is for

Pick one audience and write for it. This choice changes almost
everything about the finished piece, so make it early and tell me who
you chose.

| Audience | What they need from you | What they will not forgive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Grade 8 class visiting the school | The mechanism, in plain language, with one good diagram | Undefined jargon |
| Someone newly diagnosed, or a family member | What is happening in the body, and what the treatment is doing | Vagueness, and false cheer |
| A community grower or gardener | How it spreads, what it costs, what actually works | Advice they cannot act on |
| A school board deciding about a health program | Evidence of effect, and the cost of doing nothing | Confident claims with no source |

## What to hand in

Any **one** of these formats — a two-page written brief, a poster, or a
recorded explainer of four to six minutes. The format changes the
packaging, not the content, and all four sections below are required in
every version.

1. **The biology.** What tissue, organ, or system is affected, and
   **how the failure propagates** to something else. This is the part
   the whole unit was for, so it should be the strongest thing on the
   page. Name the structures involved and use at least one labelled
   diagram of your own, following [[Drawing Scientific Diagrams]].
2. **The public health response.** What screening, prevention,
   vaccination, treatment, or containment programme exists, and — the
   harder part — **assess it**. What does it achieve, who does it
   reach, and who does it miss?
3. **The ethical question.** State the technology, then state the
   disagreement as two positions each held by reasonable people, with
   the strongest version of both. Then say where you come down and
   why. A section that only argues one side has not done the task.
4. **A career.** One paragraph on a real role connected to this work —
   the curriculum's own examples include medical illustrator and
   meteorologist, and yours might be a medical laboratory technologist,
   a plant pathologist, a public health nurse, an epidemiologist. What
   do they actually do all day, and what education and training does it
   take?

Plus a **source list**, in the format we agreed as a class, used
consistently. Minimum four sources, and at least one of them must be
something other than a website — a book, a published report, or a
person you interviewed with their permission.

> [!warning] Where your sources must come from
> Health Canada, Public Health Ontario, a hospital's own patient
> information, a provincial agriculture ministry, a university
> extension service, a peer-reviewed review article, or a professional
> body. Read what the source itself says, not a summary of it.
>
> **Every number in your work must be traceable to something you can
> open in front of me.** If you cannot point at where a figure came
> from, delete it and say "I could not find a reliable figure for
> this", which costs you nothing at all. Inventing one — or repeating
> one from an article that does not say where it got it — costs you the
> section and the trust that the rest of it is accurate. See
> [[What Counts as Evidence]].

## How it will be judged

| What I am looking for | Level 3 sounds like | Level 4 sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The biology | Names the system affected and describes the condition correctly | Explains **how** the failure reaches a second system, structure by structure |
| Fit to audience | Written at roughly the right level | Every choice — a word, a diagram, an omission — is clearly made for this reader |
| The public health section | Describes a programme accurately | Assesses it: what it achieves, who it reaches, who it misses |
| The ethical question | Presents two sides fairly | Names what each side is actually protecting, then takes a position and defends it |
| Sources | Four credible sources, cited consistently | Sources are weighed against each other, and a weak one is named as weak |
| Limits | Mentions that more research exists | States precisely what this case study does **not** establish |

## What sinks an otherwise good case study

- **A wall of copied technical language.** If you cannot say it in your
  own words, you do not have it yet, and your audience will not get it
  from you.
- **The ethical section written as a single side with a token
  objection.** Steelman. Argue against the best version of the other
  case, as in [[Whose Climate, Whose Cost]].
- **A number with no home.** See the warning above; this is the fastest
  way to lose a section.
- **Describing a system without describing an interaction.** The unit
  is about how systems depend on each other. A case study of a single
  organ in isolation has answered a Grade 9 question.

Stuck on choosing? [[Getting Unstuck]] first, then bring me a shortlist
of two rather than a blank page. Format of the write-up:
[[Writing About Science]].
