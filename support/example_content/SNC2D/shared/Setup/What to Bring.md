Every class:

- [ ] Lab notebook — bound, not loose paper, not a binder
- [ ] Pen for writing, sharp pencil and a ruler for every diagram
- [ ] Scientific calculator (a phone will not be permitted on tests)
- [ ] Charged laptop
- [ ] Your [[Science Journal]], if you keep it separately

## Lab days

Check the class page the night before. If it links an investigation, it
is a lab day, and the following are not negotiable:

- [ ] Closed-toe shoes. You will be sent out, and there is nothing I
      can do about it
- [ ] Long hair tied back before you come in, not at the bench
- [ ] Sleeves that can be rolled or fastened; nothing dangling from
      wrists or neck
- [ ] Bags at the front of the room, not underfoot
- [ ] The investigation page read, not skimmed

> [!note] Why the shoe rule has no exceptions
> Everything that goes wrong in a lab goes down. Broken glass, hot
> liquid, and corrosive solution all end up on the floor, and the floor
> is where your feet are. This is a school board requirement and not a
> preference of mine, so please do not spend a lab period negotiating
> it with me.

## About the notebook

Bound, in ink, in order, with nothing torn out. That combination is
what turns a pile of notes into a **record**: readings written as they
happen rather than reconstructed afterwards, mistakes struck through
with a single line and initialled rather than scribbled out, and the
odd observation kept even when you cannot explain it.

The habit is the same at every level of science. Research and industry
notebooks settle patent disputes and safety investigations, and they do
it on exactly these properties — contemporaneous, unaltered, honest.
