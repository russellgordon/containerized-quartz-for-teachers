By the end of this course you will be able to design an investigation, run it
carefully, and say honestly what your evidence does and does not show. Grade 9
asked what happens. This year asks *why* it happens, and what would change if
you altered one thing on purpose.

Three overall expectations set the tone for everything else. The first runs
through every unit rather than living in one:

Put plainly, week to week that means:

- **Design it yourself** — you will be handed a question and some equipment
  more often than a numbered procedure, and you will decide what to control
  and what to measure.
- **Predict before you measure** — writing down what you expect, and being
  wrong in public, is the part that teaches you something. See
  [[Mistakes Are Data]].
- **Explain the mechanism** — not "the mass stayed the same" but *why* it had
  to, as in [[Balancing Equations]]; not "the image flipped" but what the rays
  did to flip it.
- **Judge the evidence** — data has sources, limits, and people behind it.
  [[Reading a Graph Honestly]] and [[What Counts as Evidence]] are where we
  practise this.
- **Show your growth** — your [[Science Journal]] is the record of how your
  thinking changed; see [[How Marks Work]].
