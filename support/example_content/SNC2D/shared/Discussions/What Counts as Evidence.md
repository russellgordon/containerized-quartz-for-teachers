In Grade 9 the question was which sources you trust. This year it is
sharper. **Does this evidence actually distinguish between the
explanations on the table?** Plenty of evidence is true, relevant, and
still useless — because every explanation in the room predicts it
equally well.

## The question

Your group and another group ran the same investigation and got
different results. Neither of you made an obvious mistake. What would
settle it, and what would only feel like settling it?

## Five claims to rank

1. One carefully controlled study, run once, that nobody has repeated.
2. A result you reproduced yourself on the bench in twenty minutes.
3. A pattern in thirty years of measurements, with no proposed
   mechanism.
4. A mechanism that makes sense chemically, with no measurements yet.
5. A practice a community has used and refined over generations, never
   formally studied.

Rank them from most to least convincing. Then find someone who ranked
them differently and work out **why**. Most of the time the
disagreement is not about the evidence at all — it is about what the
claim is being used for.

## The questions we will hold every claim to

| Ask this | Weak answer | Strong answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What was measured? | "The results" | The quantity, the instrument, the uncertainty |
| Compared with what? | Nothing | A control differing in one thing only |
| How many times? | Once | Enough trials to see the spread |
| What else could explain it? | Not considered | Alternatives named, and ruled out |
| What would count against it? | Nothing could | A stated result that would sink it |

That last row is the Grade 10 row. A claim that no possible observation
could contradict is not a powerful claim — it is a claim that has
stopped talking to the world.

## Two traps worth naming out loud

**"Consistent with" is not "supports".** Getting data that fits your
prediction is easy; almost anything fits a vague enough prediction. Data
that is *hard to explain any other way* is the thing that carries
weight.

**A mechanism is not a measurement.** A story about why something ought
to happen is a reason to go and look, not a reason to believe. Claim 4
in the list above is the one people reliably over-rate, and claim 3 is
the one they reliably under-rate.

> [!note] There is no answer key here
> Claim 5 deserves care rather than a quick dismissal. Practices refined
> across generations encode observation over timescales no single study
> can reach, and treating them as "just anecdote" has cost science real
> discoveries. Weighing that against the value of controlled comparison
> is genuinely hard, which is why we are arguing about it instead of me
> handing you a ranking.

Bring your ranking to the discussion in writing. Afterwards, add an
entry to your [[Science Journal]] naming one place your ranking moved
and what moved it. Related: [[Mistakes Are Data]] and
