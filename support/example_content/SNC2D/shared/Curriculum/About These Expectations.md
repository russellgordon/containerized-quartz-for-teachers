Grades 9 and 10: Science, 2008 (revised)**, the academic course **SNC2D**,
published by the Ontario Ministry of Education. SNC2D occupies pages 71 to 81
of that document.

Each page keeps the expectation's official **code** — `B2.3`, `C3.8`, `E2.5`,
and so on — and every lesson, investigation, and task in this course links back
to the codes it addresses. The codes are also tags, so every page tagged `#C3`
can be gathered in one place. The codes here are the document's own: it prints
them beside every overall and specific expectation.

## Grade 10 science is still the 2008 curriculum

This surprises people, so it is worth stating plainly. Ontario replaced the
**Grade 9** science course in 2022 — the de-streamed SNC1W — but left Grades 10
to 12 alone. The 2022 document says so itself:

> Educators should be aware that, with the exception of the Grade 9 Science
> course, 2022 (SNC1W), the 2008 Science curriculum for Grade 10 and the 2008
> Science curriculum for Grades 11–12 remain in effect.

One consequence matters for scheduling. The **prerequisite line printed in the
2008 document is out of date**. It reads "Science, Grade 9, Academic or
Applied", which describes courses that no longer run. The 2022 document's
course chart replaces it with: Grade 9 Science, De-streamed (2022), or Grade 9
Science, Academic (2008), or Grade 9 Science, Applied (2008).

## What the abbreviations in square brackets mean

Most expectations in strands B through E end with something like `[PR, AI, C]`.
Those are printed in the document, and they point back at Strand A. The
document's own footnote explains them:

> \* The abbreviation(s) for the broad area(s) of investigation skills – IP,
> PR, AI, and/or C – are provided in square brackets at the end of the
> expectations in strands B–E to which the particular area(s) relate.

| Code | The skill area |
| --- | --- |
| IP | Initiating and planning |
| PR | Performing and recording |
| AI | Analysing and interpreting |
| C | Communicating |

That is why Strand A is never taught as a unit of its own here. It runs through
every investigation you do — the brackets show which part of it each piece of
work is exercising. Expectations in the "Understanding Basic Concepts" groups
(`B3`, `C3`, `D3`, `E3`) carry no brackets, and neither do Strand A's own
expectations. That is how the document prints them.

## Two things the document does that these pages do not

**Sample issues and sample questions.** Beside nine of the expectations the
printed document sets a *Sample issue* and a list of *Sample questions* —
material written to give teachers a starting point. They are planning support
rather than expectation wording, so they are not reproduced here. If you want
them, they are in the source document at the page range given above.

**Chemical formulas.** The document typesets formulas with real subscripts, and
so do these pages: $\ce{CO2}$, $\ce{H2SO4(aq)}$,
$\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$. One oddity is
reproduced rather than corrected — in `C3.8` the sulfate ion is printed as
$\ce{SO4^-2}$, with the minus sign ahead of the numeral, where the
convention you will be taught writes $\ce{SO4^2-}$. Use the modern form in
your own work; the expectation is quoted as it was printed.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When you ask "why are we doing this?", the answer is
a link. When a department writes a course outline, or a teacher takes over a
class partway through the semester, the codes are what make one person's
planning legible to another.
