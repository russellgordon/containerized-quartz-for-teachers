Two entries about the same afternoon in [[The Law of Reflection]]. One
of them tells me nothing I did not already know.

## The weak version

> [!example]- The entry that took two minutes and was worth about that
> Today we did the reflection lab. We measured the angles and they were
> pretty much equal, so the law of reflection worked. It was fun. Next
> time I will be more careful with the protractor.

Everything in it is true. None of it is information. "Pretty much
equal" hides the only number that mattered, "it worked" is a verdict
rather than a finding, and "be more careful" is a resolution nobody has
ever kept, because it does not name a thing to do differently.

## The strong version

> I predicted the reflected angle would equal the incident angle,
> because light reflecting off a flat surface has no reason to prefer
> one side of the normal over the other — the situation is symmetric.
> Measured pairs, incident then reflected: 30° / 31°, 45° / 44°,
> 60° / 62°.
>
> The first two sit inside the 1° I can read on the protractor. The 60°
> pair does not, and it is off in the same direction as a smaller error
> at 45° would be, which made me think it was systematic rather than
> random. We had drawn the normal by eye instead of setting it square to
> the mirror line, and a normal drawn 1° off tilts every reading the
> same way, with the biggest visible effect at the largest angle.
>
> So the evidence supports equal angles, with the limitation that our
> normal was not constructed accurately. To test that specifically I
> would redraw the normal with a set square and re-run only the 60°
> trial. What I actually learned is that a disagreement getting worse
> as the angle grows is a clue about *which* error you have, not just
> that you have one.

## Why the second one earns what it earns

| Move | Where it shows up |
| --- | --- |
| Prediction stated with a mechanism | "because … the situation is symmetric" |
| Real numbers, not adjectives | the three measured pairs |
| Uncertainty of the instrument named | "the 1° I can read" |
| Disagreement compared against that uncertainty | "does not sit inside" |
| A cause proposed, and a reason to believe it | pattern with angle, normal drawn by eye |
| A specific, testable next step | set square, re-run the 60° trial |
| Something learned about thinking, not just optics | the closing sentence |

Seven moves, about 200 words. Length is not the thing.

> [!tip] The test to apply before you close the notebook
> Could someone who was not in the room reconstruct what you did, what
> you found, and why you believe it? If the answer is no, one more
> sentence usually fixes it — and it is almost always the sentence
> giving the number.
