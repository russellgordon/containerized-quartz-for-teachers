Mid-semester, stop adding entries for one sitting and write one that
looks backwards.

## Do this

1. Re-read [[Your First Entry]], including the sentence about how you
   thought science works.
2. Find an early piece of work and a recent one on the **same skill** —
   two conclusions, two graphs, two attempts to explain a mechanism.
3. Put them side by side and name three specific differences. Quote
   yourself in both directions.

Same skill is the hard part. A messy first lab and a polished final task
are different jobs, and comparing them shows nothing except that the
final task mattered more.

## Not this

> "I have improved a lot and learned many things. I am better at labs
> now and my reports are much stronger than they were at the start."

Nothing in that paragraph could be checked, argued with, or wrong. A
claim nobody could contradict is not evidence of growth — it is a
sentence about wanting to have grown.

## This

> My first conclusion, from the digestion model, was: "The starch broke
> down, so the enzyme worked." One sentence, no numbers, and "worked" is
> doing all the labour.
>
> My conclusion after the reaction-types lab says which of six mixtures
> produced a gas, gives the temperature change for each in degrees, and
> then admits that two of my classifications rest on a single trial and
> could be either of two reaction types on that evidence. I named which
> further test would separate them.
>
> The difference is not neatness. In the first one I was reporting that
> something happened. In the second I was reporting **what the evidence
> can and cannot decide**, which I did not know was part of the job.

## What I am reading for

That last line. Three differences in the work, and then one sentence
about what changed in your head to produce them. The second is worth
more than the first, and it is also the part people leave out.

> [!question] A useful prompt if you are stuck
> What is something you now do automatically that you had to be told to
> do in the first month? Automatic is exactly the evidence — you stopped
> noticing you were doing it.
