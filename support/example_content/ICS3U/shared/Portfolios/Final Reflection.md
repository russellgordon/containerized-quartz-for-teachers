The final reflection is the last piece of writing in this course: one
sustained look **across** your whole [[Code Journal]], written in the
final week. Every other entry looked at a single day. This one reads
the entire record and answers the question a course can actually be
measured by — *what do you leave with that you did not arrive with?*

## Before you write

Reread the journal front to back in one sitting. Expect the earliest
entries to feel written by someone else. As you read, flag:

- one entry that embarrasses you a little; that is usually where the
  growth is buried
- one "what I would try next" that you actually tried, and what
  happened when you did
- one bug that taught you more than any working program did
- one thing your client said that changed what you built

## The shape

Three sections, roughly a page, built the way [[Showing Growth]]
taught: cite the earlier entry, quote your own words, then show the
now.

1. **How my building changed.** The biggest difference between what
   first-week you and current you can *make*, proven with two cited
   entries — from a first program that crashed on a capital letter to
   whatever you handed over on [[Launch Day]].
2. **How my thinking about who I build for changed.** The other axis,
   and in this course just as real. What do you now notice about
   interfaces, error messages, and who gets left out that you did not
   notice at the start? Quote your first entry's "where I stand"
   honestly; it is the baseline.
3. **Where this goes next.** Habits are only real once they have an
   address outside this room: a course you might take, a field where
   these skills already live — see [[Careers in Computer Science]] —
   something you want to build on your own, and one first step you
   could genuinely take in the next month.

> [!tip] Do not write it as a thank-you card
> The temptation is to perform gratitude and round everything up.
> Resist it. The reflections worth reading include the concept that
> never clicked, the feature that shipped broken, the bug that won,
> the client conversation you wish you had had earlier. You have a
> course's worth of evidence that honesty is what makes this writing
> good. Trust it one last time.
