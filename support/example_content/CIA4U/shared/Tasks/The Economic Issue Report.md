**The culminating task. Launched in the second week of Unit 4; presented in
the final week of classes.** Individual.

One current Canadian economic issue, analysed with a model and real data,
ending in a recommendation you defend to an audience that will push back.

## The issue

Something genuinely current and genuinely contested: housing costs,
productivity, tariffs and trade, inflation, the labour market, inequality,
the fiscal track, the energy transition. It must be an issue on which
informed people disagree — if everyone agrees, there is nothing to
recommend.

## What to produce

**A report of about 1,800 words and a twelve-minute presentation with
questions taken.**

| Part | What it must do |
| --- | --- |
| The question | A researchable question, not a topic — see [[The Economic Inquiry Process]] |
| The evidence | Retrieved data from named tables or series, with reference periods |
| The model | One model, chosen and justified, doing actual analytical work |
| The perspectives | What the issue looks like to at least three stakeholders who disagree |
| The recommendation | Specific, costed, and beginning somewhere real |
| The trade-offs | What your recommendation gives up, and who bears that |
| The check | The indicator that would tell you in a year whether it worked |

## How it is marked

- **Synthesis.** A1.6 asks you to evaluate and synthesise findings, which
  means the conclusion follows from the evidence rather than sitting beside
  it. Evidence that cut against you gets named and weighed, not omitted.
- **Communication for the audience.** A1.7 asks for a form fit to purpose:
  a report reads differently from a twelve-minute talk, and both are being
  assessed.
- **The concepts of economic thinking, used.** A2.3 — economic
  significance, cause and effect, stability and variability, economic
  perspective. Not listed in a paragraph; used to structure the analysis.
- **The international dimension.** E2.2 and E3.1 mean your issue has to be
  placed in a world context: what international events or policies bear on
  it, and what the comparable disparities look like elsewhere.
- **Institutions assessed.** E3.2 — where an intergovernmental body has
  responded to your issue, say how well, against criteria you set.

> [!warning] Choose in week one, and choose something with published data
> The strongest reports come from students who spent the first two periods
> checking that the tables they needed exist and are current. The weakest
> come from a topic chosen for interest and abandoned in week three. Do the
> retrieval before you commit.

Everything in the course feeds this. [[The Tariff Year]],
[[The Cost of a Place to Live]], [[The Productivity Question]] and
[[Who Is Missing From the Workforce]] are worked examples of the shape.
