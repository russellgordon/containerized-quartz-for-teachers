A model is a small machine you build on purpose, so that you can reason
about one thing while holding the rest of the world still. Supply and
demand is the smallest useful machine in economics, and most arguments you
meet this year are someone running it in their head without saying so.

## Building it

A demand schedule lists, for each price, the quantity buyers plan to buy
in a period: at \$4 a kilogram, 200 kg a week; at \$3, 300 kg; at \$2,
450 kg. A supply schedule is the same list from the other side — \$4
brings out 400 kg, \$3 brings 300 kg, \$2 only 180 kg, because a higher
price covers the cost of pulling resources off other work.

Equilibrium is the one price at which those two sets of plans are
consistent — here \$3 and 300 kg. It is not a fair price or a good price;
it is the price at which nobody holds a plan they cannot carry out. Above
it, unsold stock builds until someone cuts; below it, buyers bid.

## A movement is not a shift

A change in the good's own price moves you **along** a curve. A change in
anything else **shifts** the whole curve. "The price rose, so demand fell"
is almost always wrong — quantity demanded fell, and demand never moved.

| What changes | Which curve shifts | Example from the curriculum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tastes, information, expectations | Demand | Advertising; a published health finding |
| Input costs, technique, weather | Supply | Shortages of raw materials or labour; new technology; a bad season |
| Taxes | Either, depending on what is taxed | Sales and goods and services taxes; Ontario's land transfer tax |

## What the model leaves out

A tax settles on the less elastic side of the market, not on whoever
writes the cheque — that is [[Elasticity]]. And the model assumes many
buyers and sellers, none able to move the price. Canadian grocery is not
like that: the Competition Bureau (*Canada Needs More Grocery
Competition*, 27 June 2023) found Loblaw, Sobeys and Metro reporting more
than \$100 billion in sales and \$3.6 billion in profits in 2022. Where a
few firms set the price, read [[Market Structures]].
