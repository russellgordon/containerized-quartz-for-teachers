A firm is a decision-making unit, and two questions about it matter here.
What legal form does it take — which decides who bears the loss and who
has a claim on the surplus? And how much should it produce — which turns
on a single comparison made at the margin.

## The form the firm takes

A **sole proprietor** is the firm: the debts are personal, and so is the
loss. A **partnership** spreads that exposure, still personally. A
**private limited company** caps each owner's loss at what they invested,
which is why risky ventures take that form. A **co-operative** belongs to
the members who use it, so the surplus returns to users — a credit union
is one. A **Crown corporation** is owned by government, so losses land on
taxpayers. A **not-for-profit** may earn a surplus but may not distribute
it. A **multinational** works across jurisdictions and can move activity
toward whichever one costs or taxes it least.

## What it costs to produce

Costs come in kinds, and the kinds are not interchangeable. The whole
apparatus exists to support one decision, taken again and again: whether
to produce the next unit at all.

| Term | What it means in plain language | A concrete example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed cost | What you pay this month whether or not you produce | The lease on the bakery |
| Variable cost | What rises and falls with the number of units | Flour, packaging, hours at the counter |
| Total cost | Fixed plus variable at that level of output | The month's whole bill |
| Average cost | Total cost divided by units produced | The bill per loaf |
| Marginal cost | What one more unit adds to total cost | Baking loaf 501 |
| Marginal revenue | What one more unit adds to revenue | What loaf 501 sells for |

The rule follows: produce the next unit while marginal revenue is at least
marginal cost, and stop where the two meet. What the model leaves out is
that it assumes a firm knows its own marginal cost and wants profit above
everything — a co-operative or a Crown corporation may want neither — and
whether marginal revenue equals the price at all depends on
[[Market Structures]]. The habit itself is [[Marginal Thinking]].
