The oldest counter-intuitive result in economics is that a country better
at making everything still gains by importing something. The model that
shows it is small enough to build here, and its limits matter as much.

## Who should make what

Two countries, two goods, one worker-year of effort each. Absolute
advantage is simply who produces more of a good from the same resources.
Comparative advantage is who gives up less of the other good to make it —
and it is comparative advantage, not absolute, that decides who specialises.

| Country | Wheat per worker-year | Coffee per worker-year | Cost of a tonne of wheat | Cost of a tonne of coffee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canada | 6 tonnes | 6 tonnes | 1 tonne of coffee | 1 tonne of wheat |
| Brazil | 2 tonnes | 4 tonnes | 2 tonnes of coffee | 0.5 tonnes of wheat |

Canada holds the absolute advantage in both goods, and it still pays both
countries to trade. Canada gives up one tonne of coffee per tonne of wheat
and Brazil gives up two, so wheat is Canada's. Brazil gives up half a
tonne of wheat per tonne of coffee and Canada a whole one, so coffee is
Brazil's. Trade at any rate between those two and both end up ahead.

The price at which that trade settles depends on the exchange rate regime.
A **fixed** rate is pegged by a government and defended with reserves; a
**managed** float is left alone until it moves too fast; a **floating**
rate is whatever clears, which is Canada's. The regime bears on the terms
of trade — export prices against import prices — because a depreciation
makes your exports cheaper abroad and your imports dearer at home. Never
quote a rate from memory: the Bank of Canada publishes the daily average
as series `FXUSDCAD` each business day by 16:30 Eastern. It stood at
1.3875 on 14 August 2026 — a dated worked example, not "the" rate.

Then the honest limit. The model says total output rises when countries
specialise. It says nothing about how the gains are divided — between two
countries, or between a worker in an exporting industry and one in an
import-competing plant. That silence is the whole modern argument:
[[The Tariff Year]] is the live case, [[The Trade Question]] your turn.
