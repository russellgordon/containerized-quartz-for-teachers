Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level — not one price
going up, but the whole basket drifting. Canada measures it with the
Consumer Price Index, which prices a fixed basket month after month so
that the change you see is a change in price, not in what people bought.

## The number and its parts

The Consumer Price Index rose 2.8% year over year in June 2026, after
3.2% in May, released 20 July 2026 from Table 18-10-0004-01. Excluding
gasoline the rise was 2.2%; gasoline alone was up 20.5% year over year,
shelter 1.5%, and food purchased from stores 3.9%. Always look inside the
headline: a rate driven by one volatile component is a different economic
situation from the same rate spread evenly.

## Why the target is 2% and not zero

The Bank of Canada aims at 2% inflation, the midpoint of a 1–3% control
range. Zero is not the goal, for good reasons. Measured inflation
slightly overstates the true rise in the cost of living, because the
index cannot fully capture substitution and quality improvement. A little
inflation lets relative wages adjust without anyone's pay being cut in
dollar terms. And a positive target keeps nominal interest rates far
enough above zero that the Bank has room to cut when demand falls.

The target is a formal agreement between the Bank and the Government of
Canada. The current one runs to 31 December 2026 and is being renewed;
the Bank published its consultations report in June 2026. Watching that
renewal is where the question of what monetary policy is for gets argued.
