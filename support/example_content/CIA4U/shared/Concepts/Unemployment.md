To be counted as unemployed in Canada you must be without work, available
for work, and actively looking for it. All three conditions matter, and
the third one does most of the damage to the statistic.

## Four kinds, with different cures

**Frictional** unemployment is the time between jobs — people moving,
graduating, or leaving one post for a better one. It is normal, and a
sign of a working labour market. **Structural** unemployment is a
mismatch between the skills or locations workers have and the ones
employers want; it responds to training and mobility, not to spending.
**Cyclical** unemployment rises when demand falls, and is the kind fiscal
and monetary policy aim at. **Seasonal** unemployment follows the
calendar, which is why published figures are seasonally adjusted.

The **participation rate** is the share of the working-age population
either employed or looking. It is the reason the unemployment rate can
mislead: someone who stops searching leaves the labour force entirely and
is no longer counted as unemployed. Those discouraged workers do not
appear in the headline rate at all.

In July 2026 the unemployment rate was 6.4%, down 0.1 percentage points
and a third consecutive monthly decline, with employment up 75,000 and
the employment rate at 60.9% — Labour Force Survey, released 7 August
2026, Table 14-10-0287-01. Read the employment rate alongside the
unemployment rate: together they say whether people found work or stopped
looking.

> [!success]- Can the unemployment rate fall for a bad reason?
> Yes. If enough people give up searching, they leave the labour force,
> the denominator shrinks along with the numerator, and the rate falls
> while fewer people are working. The tell is the employment rate and
> the participation rate: if the unemployment rate falls and the
> employment rate falls with it, the improvement is arithmetic, not
> economic. In July 2026 employment rose by 75,000, so that month the
> fall was the real thing — but you had to check.

loses track of are in [[Who Is Missing From the Workforce]].
