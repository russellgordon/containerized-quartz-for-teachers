Economics has two kinds of statements in it, and most public arguments go
wrong because the two get mixed. A **positive** statement is a claim about
what is, and can in principle be checked: raising this tax reduced
consumption of that good by this much. A **normative** statement is a claim
about what ought to be, and no dataset settles it: that tax was unfair.

You need both. An argument made only of positive claims never explains why
anyone should care; one made only of normative claims cannot be wrong. Say
which kind you are making, and notice when someone slides between them.

## Two honest analyses can still disagree

Two economists can both work carefully, use real data, and reach opposite
positive conclusions. The reason is usually the comparison: to say a policy
caused an outcome you need an account of what would have happened without
it, and that account is never observed.

Minimum wage research is the standard illustration. Card and Krueger's 1994
study compared fast-food employment across the New Jersey–Pennsylvania
border and found no employment loss; Neumark and Wascher re-examined the
same episode with payroll records and found losses; Brochu and Green, using
Canadian data in 2013, found the main effect on hiring rates rather than on
layoffs, with layoff risk for existing workers falling. The disagreement is
about which comparison group is credible — a question of method, not of
values, and not settled by picking the economist you prefer.

> [!note] The four concepts of economic thinking
> The 2015 Ontario curriculum names four for this course, and you will be
> assessed on using them. **Economic significance** — does this matter, to
> whom, and how much? **Cause and effect** — what produced this outcome,
> and what merely accompanied it? **Stability and variability** — what is
> holding steady, what is moving, over what period? **Economic
> perspective** — whose gains and losses does this account leave out?

Run those four over any claim before you repeat it. Applying them to a
specific claim is the work of [[Judging an Economic Claim]]; the research
sequence for major assignments is [[The Economic Inquiry Process]].
