Economics is not one settled body of doctrine. It is a long argument, and
the theorists below are the people who set its terms. You are not asked to
memorise their biographies. You are asked to state each central claim
accurately, and then to judge how far it shapes what governments actually
do — which is a much harder task, and a contested one.

| Theorist | The central claim | Where you can look for it in Canadian policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adam Smith | Specialisation and voluntary exchange coordinate production through prices, with no central direction needed | Competition law and the Competition Bureau's grocery market study of 27 June 2023, which argues the mechanism fails without rivals |
| Thomas Malthus | Population growth presses against the means of subsistence, so resource limits bind | Farmland preservation and resource management debates; the modern form is about land and water, not famine |
| Karl Marx | Returns flow to the owners of capital rather than to labour, and the conflict is structural, not accidental | Labour law, the right to bargain collectively, and every dispute over how a firm's surplus is split |
| John Maynard Keynes | Total demand can sit too low for full employment, and public spending can raise it | Deficit-financed federal budgets — the Spring Economic Update of 28 April 2026 put the 2025–26 deficit near \$67 billion |
| John K. Galbraith | Large firms shape demand rather than simply meet it, and private affluence coexists with public squalor | Advertising rules, and arguments about underfunded public infrastructure beside a strong consumer market |
| Marilyn Waring | National accounts exclude unpaid work, overwhelmingly women's, so GDP misstates what an economy produces | What GDP counts and what it omits; hold the definition of GDP against the work done in your own household |
| Elinor Ostrom | Users of a shared resource can govern it with their own rules, without either privatisation or state control | Co-managed fisheries and Indigenous-led stewardship agreements |
| Thomas Piketty | When returns to capital outrun growth, wealth concentrates | Income and wealth distribution — Statistics Canada's Table 11-10-0134-01 holds the Gini coefficients; look them up rather than quoting one |

## Judging influence, honestly

"Influence" resists measurement. A government may adopt a Keynesian
instrument while rejecting the argument for it, or borrow Smith's language
while regulating heavily. Assess influence by naming a specific policy, the
claim it appears to rest on, and the alternative explanation — that the
policy was chosen for electoral or fiscal reasons and the theory was
attached afterwards. An answer that says "Keynes was very influential"
without a policy attached is not an answer.

Political perspectives cut across the same ground. Neoliberal governments
generally lean on markets and price signals; socialist ones on public
ownership and transfers; and most governments in practice mix instruments
that came from theorists who disagreed with each other. The macroeconomic
machinery behind the demand-side argument is in
[[Aggregate Supply and Demand]], and the question of what all this growth
is for is argued in [[Is Growth the Right Goal?]].
