Come early. The one thing that reliably turns a manageable problem into an
unmanageable one is waiting until the work is due before saying anything.

## When to come

- **Before a task, not after it.** Twenty minutes spent on the shape of an
  argument beats an hour spent repairing one that was built wrong.
- **The moment a figure will not come out.** Data problems are almost never
  worth solving alone; somebody who knows the table finds it in a minute.
- **When you have changed your mind** about your own argument and are not
  sure whether that is progress or panic. It is usually progress.
- **When something outside the course is making the course impossible.**
  You do not have to explain what it is.

## What to bring to the conversation

Bring the actual thing — the draft, the table, the half-drawn diagram —
rather than a description of it. Bring the question you tried to answer and
the last step that worked. "I am stuck" starts a diagnosis; "I have the
July CPI release but I cannot get a real figure out of it" starts a
solution.

> [!tip] Say which kind of stuck you are
> Being stuck in economics is almost always one of two things, and naming
> which saves the first twenty minutes of every conversation.
> **A data problem**: you cannot find the figure, cannot tell which of two
> tables is the right one, or cannot tell whether what you have is real or
> nominal. **A model problem**: you have the numbers and cannot decide
> which model the question wants, or your model is giving you an answer you
> do not believe. Different problems, different fixes, and you can usually
> tell them apart in ten seconds.

## Where to look first

Most data problems answer themselves at
[[Where Canadian Economic Data Lives]] — it names the tables and series
this course uses and where each one is published. Most model problems are
really a judgement problem, and [[Judging an Economic Claim]] is the
checklist for those: what is being claimed, on what evidence, compared with
what, and who is counting.

If you have been through both and are still stuck, that is exactly when to
come and say so. Arriving having tried is not a prerequisite for help, but
it does make the help much faster.
