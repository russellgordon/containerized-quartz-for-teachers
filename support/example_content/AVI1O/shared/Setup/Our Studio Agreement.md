A studio only works if everyone in it feels able to make something badly
in front of other people. That takes agreement, not luck.

## How we talk about work

- **Nobody comments on ability as though it were character.** "I can't
  draw" is a sentence about practice, not about a person, and it is not
  said about anyone else in this room at all.
- **Feedback goes to the work.** "The left side is much busier than the
  right" is useful. "You're better at this than me" is not — see
  [[Giving Feedback]].
- **Describe before you judge.** In a critique the first job is to say
  what is actually there. [[The Class Critique]] sets out how the room
  runs, and [[Describing What You See]] is the skill it depends on.
- **Unfinished is the normal state.** Almost everything you see on
  someone's table is halfway through. React to it that way.

## Other people's materials and work

- Ask before you borrow, and return it cleaned. A brush left in water is
  a brush someone else does not have tomorrow.
- Do not touch, move, or lean on work that is not yours, especially wet
  paint, drying prints, and anything on the rack.
- Shared tools go back where they came from, in the condition you would
  want to find them — [[Clearing Up]].
- Blades, presses, solvents, and dust have rules that protect the person
  beside you as much as you — [[Materials and Safety]].

## Photographs and sharing

We photograph work constantly in this course, because that is how a
portfolio gets built. Images of other people's work belong to the people
who made it.

- **Ask before you photograph someone else's work**, every time, and
  accept no without needing a reason.
- **Ask again before you post it.** Permission to take a photo is not
  permission to publish one.
- Credit the maker when you show it, in class or anywhere else —
  [[Copyright, Ownership, and Credit]].
- If you would rather your own work were not photographed or shown in
  [[The Exhibition]], tell me and we will arrange it.

> [!question] If something in this course is hard for a real reason
> An image you cannot look at, a subject you cannot work on right now, a
> reaction to a material, an accommodation you have not told me about, or
> something outside school that is making all of this heavier — tell me,
> beforehand or afterwards. You do not owe me the reason, and it will not
> change how you are marked. [[Getting Help]] is where to start.
