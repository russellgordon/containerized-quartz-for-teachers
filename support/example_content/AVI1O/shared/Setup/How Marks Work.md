You are marked on what you make, and on how you think about what you
make — not on how much art you had done before September.

## The two parts

| Part | Weight | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Term work | 70% | The seven assessed tasks, studio practice, and critiques |
| Final evaluation | 30% | [[The Portfolio and Reflection]] |

The final evaluation here is your portfolio submitted complete, plus a
written analysis of a work you have not seen before. It is not an essay
alone: a course that spends 110 hours making things should not be settled
by an hour of writing about somebody else's.

## Inside the term work

| Strand of work | Roughly | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creating and presenting | 35% | The studio tasks, the body of work, and [[The Exhibition]] |
| Reflecting and analysing | 20% | Critiques, [[The Interpretation]], [[Art and Society Study]] |
| Foundations | 15% | Terminology, technique, safe and responsible practice |

## What earns a high mark here

- **Process evidence.** The sketchbook shows the thinking that led to the
  piece: sources, false starts, the version you abandoned —
  [[The Sketchbook]].
- **Revision.** The second version is genuinely better and you can point
  at what changed and why — [[Looking Twice]].
- **Honest self-assessment.** You can name a weakness in your own work
  before I do, and say what you would do about it —
  [[Assessing Your Own Work]].
- **Accurate terminology.** The right word for the medium, the tool, the
  technique, and the element — used because it is precise, not decorative.
- **Safe and responsible practice.** Handling, clean-up, sourcing, and
  credit are assessed, not assumed — [[Materials and Safety]] and
  [[Copyright, Ownership, and Credit]].

## Late work

Tell me before the due date and we will set a new one. Work handed in
late without that conversation is still marked, but the feedback arrives
after it is useful to you, which is the real cost. A piece that is
genuinely unfinished on the day is better shown unfinished than not shown
at all — that is a conversation, not a zero.

> [!note] The criteria live on the task
> Every task page ends with a table of what earns the marks for that
> particular piece of work, and [[Learning Goals]] states what the course
> is aiming at overall. Read both before you start, not after.
