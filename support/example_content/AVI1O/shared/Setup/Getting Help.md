Ask early. In a studio course the small problems — a technique you cannot
get, a piece that has gone dead, a material that is fighting you — get
expensive quickly if they wait, because the period ends whether or not you
solved it.

## Ways to reach me

| How | When | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At your table, during studio time | Every period | A technique, a decision, a quick look |
| Before or after class | Every day | Something you would rather not ask in front of the room |
| [[Help Sessions]] | The posted times | Working with me in the room, with space and materials out |
| Email | Any time; replies on school days | Something you want in writing, or a due date to change |

## What to bring

- **The work itself**, if it can be carried. Thirty seconds of looking at
  the actual piece beats five minutes of describing it.
- **A photo, if it cannot.** Wet, large, or fragile work stays where it
  is — take a square-on photo instead, and
  [[Photographing Artwork for Submission]] takes two minutes to read.
- **The sketchbook.** Half the answer is usually already in it.
- **What you have already tried.** "I've glazed it twice and it still goes
  muddy" gets a real answer immediately.

## When a piece is not working

This is the commonest reason to come and find me, and it is not a
confession of failure — it is the ordinary middle of making something.
Bring it before you destroy it. [[Looking Twice]] is the method we use
first, and [[Assessing Your Own Work]] is how to describe the problem to
yourself accurately enough to ask about it. Often the fix is to work
smaller, or from a reference — [[Working From Reference]] — or to make
five more of the same thing until the fault shows itself.

## For a material or a technique

Come to a help session and we will run the trial together with the
materials out, the way we did in [[The Media Trials]]. Anything involving
blades, solvents, dust, or the press we practise supervised before you use
it alone — [[Materials and Safety]].

## Beyond this course

If something outside class is making school hard, say so, to me or to
guidance. It works better when it starts early, and you do not have to
explain why.
