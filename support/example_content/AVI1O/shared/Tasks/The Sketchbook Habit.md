**Checked three times across the term; marked at the end of Unit 1 and
again in Unit 4.** Individual.

The sketchbook is where the work happens before the work. It is marked
on evidence of thinking, not on finished pictures.

## What goes in it

- **Thumbnails** — small, fast, several per idea. Ten bad thumbnails
  beat one careful drawing when you are deciding.
- **Plans and notes**: what you intend, what materials, what you are
  unsure about.
- **Trials** of a technique before you commit it to a piece.
- **What went wrong**, kept rather than torn out.
- **Things you noticed** — a shadow, a shop sign, a texture.
- **The stages named.** Label where you are in the creative process, and
  where you are in a critique — see [[The Creative Process]].

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Frequency | Regular, dated entries — not filled in the night before |
| Thinking | Ideas developed across pages, not single finished drawings |
| Range | Several options generated before one is chosen |
| Terminology | The stages named correctly, in your own words |
| Honesty | Failures kept, with a note on what they taught |

> [!warning] A sketchbook is not a gallery
> The most common mistake is treating it as a portfolio of best work.
> A clean sketchbook with six finished drawings scores lower than a
> messy one with sixty thumbnails, because the mess is the evidence.

[[The Sketchbook]] explains how to keep one; this page is how it is
marked.
