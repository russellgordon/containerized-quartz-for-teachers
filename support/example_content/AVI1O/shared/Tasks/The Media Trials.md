**Due: the end of Unit 2.** Individual. A set of trials, annotated.

Before you can choose a medium, you have to know how it behaves. This
task is a deliberate tour of four, with the evidence kept.

## What you produce

For **each** of drawing, painting, printmaking, and sculpture: a short
trial that tests what the medium does — not a finished picture, a test.

Each trial is annotated with:

- The technique you were testing, named correctly.
- The tools and materials, named correctly.
- What the medium did that you did not expect.
- One effect you would use again, and where.
- The safety and clean-up practice that applied.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Exploration | You genuinely tested the medium rather than making something safe |
| Vocabulary | Techniques, materials, and tools named accurately |
| Effects | You can connect a technique to the effect it produces |
| Safe practice | Handled correctly, without being reminded |
| Reflection | You know which medium suits which of your ideas |

> [!warning] Safety is assessed here, not assumed
> Blades, solvents, spray fixative, dust, and the press all have rules,
> and following them is part of the mark — see [[Materials and Safety]].
> So is what goes down the sink and what does not.

[[Drawing]], [[Painting]], [[Printmaking]], and [[Sculpture]] are the
four; [[Surface and Texture]] is where most of the effects come from.
