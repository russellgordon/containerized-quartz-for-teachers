**Due: the end of Unit 1.** Individual. A set of five small works and a
page of writing.

Five studies, each one built around a single element or principle,
pushed as far as it will go.

## What you produce

Five small works — no larger than a hand-span — each one dominated by
one choice:

1. **Line**, doing all the work.
2. **Value**, from lightest to darkest, no colour.
3. **Texture**, real or implied.
4. **Balance**, symmetrical or deliberately not.
5. **Emphasis** — one thing the eye goes to, and everything else
   serving it.

Then a page of writing: what each study was trying to do, whether it
worked, and the correct terminology for what you used.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| The element | Clearly dominant, and pushed rather than mentioned |
| Craft | Made carefully, at the level Grade 9 can reach in a period |
| Expression | The choices carry a feeling or an idea, not just a demonstration |
| Terminology | Named correctly, in writing and out loud |
| Range | Five genuinely different results, not one idea five times |

> [!tip] Push it further than feels sensible
> A value study with four greys is an exercise; one with twelve is a
> discovery. The studies that go too far are the ones you learn from,
> and nothing here is big enough to waste much material.

[[The Elements of Design]] and [[The Principles of Design]] are what
this is drawn from, and every study belongs in [[The Sketchbook]] first.
