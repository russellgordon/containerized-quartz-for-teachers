**The culminating task. The last eight periods of Unit 4, ending with a
public opening.** Whole class, with individual work.

You select, prepare, install, and label your own work, and stand beside
it while people look.

## The eight periods

| Period | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Selection: which of your works, and why those |
| 2 | Preparation: mounting, edges, backing, cleaning up the object |
| 3 | Photographing everything for the portfolio and the record |
| 4 | Artist statements drafted and workshopped |
| 5 | Curating: how the show is grouped, sequenced, and lit |
| 6 | Installation: hanging, spacing, labels, sight lines |
| 7 | Final checks, and the environmental audit of what we used |
| 8 | The opening: you stand with your work and answer questions |

## What you deliver

- **Work prepared properly for presentation** — finished, clean, mounted
  or plinthed, ready to be handled.
- **A label** for each piece: title, medium, dimensions, year.
- **An artist statement** of about 150 words that a visitor can read in
  a minute — see [[Artist Statements]].
- **A permissions and credit note**: anything referenced, and where it
  came from.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Preparation | The work is genuinely ready for an audience |
| Presentation | Curatorial choices serve the work — spacing, grouping, light |
| Audience | You can say who this show is for and how it reaches them |
| Ethics | Sources credited; nothing shown that you do not have the right to show |
| Environment | Materials and waste handled responsibly, and you can say what you changed |

> [!warning] Hanging is a skill, and it is assessed
> Centre lines, consistent spacing, and labels at a readable height are
> not fussiness — they are the difference between work on a wall and an
> exhibition. We practise it in period 6 and it counts.

[[Copyright, Ownership, and Credit]] governs the ethics, and
[[Art and the Environment]] the last row of that table.
