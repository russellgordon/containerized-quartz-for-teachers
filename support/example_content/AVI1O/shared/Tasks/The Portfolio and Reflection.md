**The final evaluation. The portfolio submitted complete, plus a written
critical analysis in the examination period.** Individual.

A visual arts course cannot be settled by an essay alone. Half of this
is a year of work; half is what you can do with an unfamiliar image.

## The portfolio

Everything, organised, from September — see [[The Portfolio]]:

- The sketchbook, complete
- Photographs of every finished work
- The five elements studies, the information piece, the media trials
- Your written work: [[The Interpretation]], the society study, artist
  statements
- Critique notes, including feedback you received and what you did about
  it

## Written in the examination period

**Part one — an unseen work** (about 90 minutes). You are given a work
you have not studied, and you run the critical analysis process on it in
writing: reaction, description, analysis and interpretation, judgement.

**Part two — your own year** (about 60 minutes):

1. What changed between your September sketchbook and your June one?
2. What has making and analysing art done to how you see things outside
   this room?
3. What skills has this course built that are not about art?
4. What pathway or opportunity have you found — a course, a program, a
   place in the community — that you would actually pursue?

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Completeness | The record is there, organised and dated |
| Analysis | The unseen work is read carefully, with evidence |
| Self-assessment | Honest judgement about your own work, against intention |
| Insight | You notice something not obvious about your own year |
| Communication | Clear writing, correct terminology |

> [!tip] The September sketchbook is the evidence
> Nobody enjoys looking at their first pages. They are also the only way
> to see a year of change, and every strong reflection leans on them —
> which is why [[The Sketchbook Habit]] is marked from week two.
