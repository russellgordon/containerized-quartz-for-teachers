Most of you will not become artists, and this page is not an attempt to
change that. It is about what a year of visual arts leaves behind in
people who go on to do something else entirely — which turns out to be
more than anyone expects, and which you can prove from your own portfolio
rather than take on faith.[^pathways]

## Four things that are not art

**Visual literacy.** You can read an image the way you read a sentence. You
know that emphasis is manufactured, that a colour was chosen, that a crop
excluded something. Every advertisement, news photograph, and application
screen you meet for the rest of your life was designed by somebody using the
principles you have been practising, and seeing the design is self-defence.

**Creative problem-solving under constraint.** Every task this year gave you
limits — this material, this size, this deadline — and asked for an original
answer inside them. That is the shape of most real problems, and little else
in a school timetable rehearses it so directly.

**Tolerance for a bad first attempt.** You have learned that the first
version is supposed to be wrong, and that the useful move is another attempt
rather than an apology. People who never learn this stop at the first draft
of everything.

**Criticism about work, not people.** You can hand something you made to a
group, hear what is wrong with it, and use that instead of defending
yourself. Adults find this extremely difficult.

## Prove it from the portfolio

Do not claim these. Find them. Open your portfolio and point at the page
where the plan changed, the print you re-cut after a proof, the critique
note you acted on, the piece you started over. Specific evidence is the
whole assignment, and it is far more convincing than any list.

## The other half

Making and looking at art also changes what you notice about people. You
have spent a year looking closely at faces, at how bodies are shown, at whose
life gets depicted and whose does not. It is worth asking honestly what that
has done — whether it shifted an assumption, made you slower to judge, or
made you notice which images you scroll past. [[Art and Its Society]] and
[[What Art Is For]] argue this out.
