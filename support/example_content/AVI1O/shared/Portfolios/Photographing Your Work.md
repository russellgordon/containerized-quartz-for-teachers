Nearly everything you make this year will eventually exist as a photograph —
in your portfolio, in an application, in the record you keep after the
original was given away or warped in a basement. A bad photograph of a good
work is how most student work disappears. This takes fifteen minutes to
learn and is worth an hour of anyone's time.

## Flat work, square-on

Put the work on a plain wall or on the floor and centre the camera on the
middle of it, parallel to the surface. If the camera looks up or down even
slightly, a rectangle photographs as a trapezoid, and cropping will not fix
it. Stand further back and zoom in rather than moving close with a wide
lens, which bows straight edges outward. Photograph before framing if you
can — glass reflects everything in the room, including you.

## Light

Two lights, one each side at about forty-five degrees, give even light with
no hot spot. Without lights, go outside on an overcast day or into open
shade: soft, even, and free. Avoid direct sun, avoid the flash, and avoid
mixed light — a window on one side and tubes overhead is the commonest cause
of a colour cast.

| The problem | The cause | The fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edges bowed or leaning | Camera tilted or close | Centre, step back, zoom |
| Bright patch in a corner | One light, or direct sun | Two lights at 45° |
| Colours look wrong | Two kinds of light at once | One light source only |
| Whites look grey | Meter darkens a pale work | Raise exposure a stop |
| Soft everywhere | Hand-held, slow shutter | Brace it, or use a timer |

## Three-dimensional work

Sculpture needs more than one frame. Shoot the front, a three-quarter view,
and any angle where the form does something the others do not. Use a plain
background — a sheet of paper curved up the wall removes the corner line —
and light from one main side so the form casts a shadow.

## Filing

Crop to the edges of the work and nothing else, and keep the largest version
the camera produced — a copy you send can always be smaller, never bigger.
Name files with your name, the title, and the date; no colons or slashes.

While you are here, finish the presentation: sign and date the work and
prepare a label giving your name, the title, and the media used.
[[Artist Statements]] and [[The Exhibition]] pick that up; [[The Portfolio]]
is where the files end up.
