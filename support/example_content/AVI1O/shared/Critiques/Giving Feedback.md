Feedback is a skill, and like every skill it has a technique that can be
taught. The technique is three moves in a fixed order: **describe**, then
**question**, then **suggest** — at most one suggestion, small enough that
the artist could try it before the next class. The order matters more than
the wording, because describing first forces you to look properly.

## Describe

Say what is there, in plain terms, with no verdict attached. It is harder
than it sounds and it is the most useful thing you can offer, because the
artist has stared at the work for so long that they can no longer see it.
Telling them where your eye landed first is genuinely new information.

## Question

A real question, not an instruction in disguise. "Have you thought about
making the background darker?" is a suggestion wearing a costume. "What is
happening behind her?" is a question, and it lets the artist explain a
decision you missed — or discover that they never made one.

## Suggest

One thing. Specific, and about the work. If you have five ideas, the
artist will remember none of them; choose the one you would try first.

| Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- |
| "I love the colours" | "The red at the top pulled my eye first" |
| "It looks kind of empty" | "I looked longest at the bottom corner" |
| "The proportions are off" | "The far arm reads shorter than the near one" |
| "You should add a background" | "What is behind her?" |
| "It's really good" | "The texture on the roof made me lean in" |

The left column is a verdict; the right column is usable. None of the
right-hand versions says whether the speaker liked it, and none is softer —
they are simply about something.

## Two habits to avoid

**The compliment sandwich.** Praise, criticism, praise. Everyone hears the
shape of it, and it teaches people to brace during the nice parts.

**Redesigning it.** If your feedback describes the work you would have made,
you have stopped looking at theirs.

Being on the receiving end is [[The Class Critique]], turning the same three
moves on your own work is [[Assessing Your Own Work]], and
[[Writing About Art]] is where this becomes written.
