Every few weeks the work goes up on the wall and we talk about it. If the
phrase *class critique* makes your stomach drop, you are in the majority,
and most of the dread comes from a misunderstanding. A critique is not a
verdict on you and not where marks are decided. It is a room full of people
telling you what your work did to them, which you cannot learn another way.

## How it runs

1. **The work goes up.** Everyone's, every time. Nobody opts out, which
   is precisely what makes it bearable.
2. **Quiet looking, two or three minutes.** No talking at all. This is
   the part that feels strange and the part that makes the rest work.
3. **Describing.** What is actually there — subject, materials, the
   elements you can point at. No opinions yet.
4. **Interpreting.** What it seems to be about, and what in the work
   gives that impression.
5. **Evaluating.** What is working, and what one thing might be tried.
6. **The maker speaks last**, and asks one question of the room.

## Being the one on the wall

Do not introduce your work and do not apologise for it. "I ran out of
time" and "it's not finished" tell the room to look for faults instead of
looking at the work, and they will oblige. Say nothing, or say one
sentence that asks for what you need: *tell me whether the figure reads as
turning away.*

Then write things down. Do not explain what you meant. If eleven people
did not see it, "what I meant was" changes nothing — their not seeing it is
the finding.

> [!tip] Your first reaction is worth saying out loud
> The most useful sentence in a critique is often the simplest: "my eye
> went straight to the top left corner." You are not required to be
> clever. You are required to report honestly what happened when you
> looked, which is exactly what [[Looking Twice]] trains.

## Being in the room

Describe the work, never the person, and never the person's ability. Speak
about what you saw rather than what you would have done. Applaud, or
whatever this class does instead — a room where showing feels safe produces
braver work, and everyone in it improves faster.

[[The Critical Analysis Process]] is the method underneath, and
[[Describing What You See]] is step three practised on its own.
