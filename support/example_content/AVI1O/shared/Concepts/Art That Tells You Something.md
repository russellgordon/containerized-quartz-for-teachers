Some work exists to express what the artist feels. Other work has a job to
do: get you to the right exit, tell you what the meeting is about, make a
quantity understandable, or carry the meaning of a story you are also
reading. That second kind is everywhere, you have obeyed a great deal of it
today already, and almost none of it was made by accident.

## Four kinds and what each is up against

| Kind of work | The job it has | What it has to survive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signage | Direct someone who is moving | Two seconds, at an angle |
| Poster | Make a passer-by remember one thing | Nine other posters |
| Infographic | Make a quantity or process clear | Being checked against data |
| Illustration | Carry meaning a text cannot | Sitting beside the words |

Every one of those constraints comes from the *audience*, not the artist.
That is what defines the category: the work is judged by whether it did its
job for someone else, in the conditions where they actually met it.

## The message decides the design

Start by writing down, in one plain sentence, what a viewer should walk away
with. If you cannot write the sentence, no amount of good drawing will supply
it. Then the principles do the work — contrast separates the important thing
from everything else, and emphasis puts it first in the order the eye
travels.

Hierarchy is the practical version. Decide what is first, what is second, and
what is merely present, then make those three differences obvious. A design
where everything is loud is a design where nothing is.

## Test it, don't admire it

Work with a job can be tested, a luxury most art does not have. Pin it up and
walk back four metres. Squint until the detail disappears — if the main thing
survives, your value structure is sound. Better still, show it to someone for
two seconds and ask what they saw. Their answer is your result.

That test is exactly what [[The Information Piece]] asks you to run, and the
symbols you reach for while making it are the subject of
[[Conventions in Art]]. If your piece is heading for a screen rather than a
wall, [[Digital and Emerging Media]] has the practical side.
