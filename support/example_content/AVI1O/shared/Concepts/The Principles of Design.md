If [[The Elements of Design|the elements]] are the ingredients, the
principles are what you do with them. They describe arrangement: whether a
work holds together, where a viewer looks first, whether they keep looking. A
piece can contain every element and still be a mess — because a mess is a
principles problem, not an ingredients problem.

## The list this course uses

- **Balance** — how visual weight is distributed, evenly or deliberately not.
- **Contrast** — difference set beside difference: dark on light, rough on
  smooth, tiny beside enormous.
- **Emphasis** — the part you meant to be seen first.
- **Harmony** — parts that agree, because they have something in common.
- **Movement** — the path the eye takes through the work, and in what order.
- **Proportion** — the size of the parts against each other and the whole.
- **Rhythm and repetition** — something recurring, at an interval you control.
- **Unity** — the sense that this is one work and not several.
- **Variety** — enough difference to keep an eye awake.

> [!note] Two printed lists, and they disagree
> The curriculum names the principles twice: once in the visual arts overview
> and again in its glossary at the back — and the two do not match. The
> glossary version leaves out contrast and counts rhythm and repetition as two
> separate principles. This site follows the overview, so that everyone in this
> room is working from the same words. If a list you meet elsewhere differs
> slightly, that is why, and neither list is wrong.

## Most of them come in pairs

Unity and variety pull against each other; so do harmony and contrast. All
unity is wallpaper, all variety is noise, and the interesting work sits
somewhere between — which is why "add more contrast" is good advice exactly
half the time. Emphasis and movement, meanwhile, are about the viewer rather
than the work, which means you can test them on a real person in seconds.

## Using them to fix things

The fastest thing the principles do is turn a vague complaint into a repair.
"It feels boring" usually means variety or contrast. "It feels scattered"
usually means unity or harmony. "I don't know where to look" is emphasis,
every time. Bring that to [[Composition]] and [[The Elements Study]], and you
can give feedback someone can act on — the standard in [[Giving Feedback]].
