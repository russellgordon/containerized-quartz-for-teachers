Somebody designed the packaging on the desk in front of you, the icons on
your phone, the signage that got you to this room, and the credits of the
last thing you watched. What this course teaches — generating ideas, solving
a problem visually, finishing to a standard, taking criticism — turns out to
be portable.

## Routes out of Grade 9

| Route | What it is | What it asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Senior art | AVI2O, then Grade 11 and 12 | Room in your timetable |
| College | Ontario's public colleges | One central application |
| University | Degrees in art and design | A separate application |
| Apprenticeship | Paid training with an employer | An employer to take you on |
| Straight to work | Freelance and commissions | Work you can show |

Ontario's public colleges are applied to through one service,
`ontariocolleges.ca`, which currently covers several program choices across
more than one college. Universities have their own central application, run
through your guidance department. Apprenticeship is certified provincially by
Skilled Trades Ontario, which covers more than 140 trades. And some art and
design programs ask to see a portfolio before admitting you — which ones, and
what they want in it, is printed on each program's own admissions page, worth
reading in Grade 10 rather than Grade 12.

## Where the skills go

The curriculum names advertising, animation, art therapy, fashion design, and
graphic design. Add illustration, photography, product design, architecture,
exhibit and set design, game art, curation, framing, sign work, tattooing,
and teaching — then the far larger group of jobs that are not art jobs at all
but reward anyone who can make an idea visible.

## Opportunities that already exist

You do not have to wait for a pathway. Schools need posters, sets, costumes,
murals, and yearbook layouts. Festivals, libraries, galleries, and local
organisations need help with displays, promotion, and installation, and most
will take a volunteer who asks. All of it is real experience and,
photographed, real portfolio material — which is what [[The Portfolio]] is
for, and what [[What You Take With You]] asks at the end of the course: not
what you made, but what you can now do.
