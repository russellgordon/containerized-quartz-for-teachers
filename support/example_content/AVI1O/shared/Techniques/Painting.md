We paint with acrylic and with watercolour. Both are pigment held in a
binder and thinned with water, and that is where the similarity ends.
Almost every problem a beginner has with paint comes from treating one of
them like the other.

## Two paints, two sets of rules

| Behaviour | Acrylic | Watercolour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dries | In minutes, and darker | In minutes, and lighter |
| Once dry | Waterproof, paint over it | Rewets, lifts, blends again |
| Whites | From white paint | From paper you left alone |
| Built | Dark to light, opaque | Light to dark, transparent |
| Fixes | Cover the mistake | Very few — plan instead |

Read that twice before your first painting period. In watercolour the white
of the paper is the only white you have, so highlights are decided before
you start and protected while you work. In acrylic you can bury anything,
but the colour dries a shade darker than it looks wet — so match colours
against a test patch that has dried, not one that is still glossy.

## Mixing

Work from a small palette. Three primaries, white, and one earth colour
will make more usable colours than a set of twenty tubes. Two rules carry
most of it: mixing a colour with the one opposite it on the wheel dulls it
towards grey, and lightening acrylic with white turns it chalky while
lightening watercolour with water keeps it clear. Mix more than you need.

## Layering

Both paints reward patience. In watercolour, a transparent wash over a dry
wash creates the effect of light passing through the picture, and it only
works if the first layer is genuinely dry — touch it early and it blooms.
In acrylic, a thin underpainting that sets the dark and light shapes makes
every later decision easier, because you are adjusting rather than inventing.

## Why brushes matter

A round holds a point and makes a line that swells. A flat makes an edge
and covers ground. A filbert is a flat with the corners taken off, so it
blends without leaving a hard end. Choosing badly is why a painting fights
you.

Brushes die of neglect, not use. Acrylic that dries in the ferrule is
permanent, so rinse before you set a brush down rather than at the bell, and
never leave one standing in water. Rinse water goes to the settling bucket,
which [[Clearing Up]] explains.
