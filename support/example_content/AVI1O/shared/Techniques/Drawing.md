Drawing comes first, and not because it outranks the other media. It comes
first because it is the fastest — a pencil and twenty minutes will tell you
more about what you are actually looking at than a week of thinking, and
nearly everything else you make this year begins as a drawing of some kind.

## What a pencil actually does

Pencils are graded by how hard the graphite is. H grades are hard and pale,
B grades are soft and dark, and the grade decides what a mark is able to
do. Keeping three or four on the table is not fussiness — it is your range.

| Grade | Behaviour | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2H | Pale, holds a point for ages | Construction lines you mean to lose |
| HB | The middle of the range | Notes, thumbnails, everyday work |
| 2B | Soft, dark, blunts quickly | Most of your tonal drawing |
| 6B | Very soft, smudges, nearly black | Deep shadow, fast large marks |

Graphite never reaches true black; it goes a dark, shiny grey, which is why
charcoal, conté, and ink exist. Paper matters as much: tooth gives a grainy
mark and smooth paper an even one, which is [[Surface and Texture]] already.

## Line

A line is a decision, and it carries far more than an outline. Vary the
pressure and one line describes weight, distance, and speed — press where a
form turns towards you, lift where an edge dissolves into light. Draw long
lines from the shoulder in one committed movement; slow lines look anxious.

## Tone

Tone is light and dark, and it is what makes a thing look solid. Squint
until detail disappears and you see three or four shapes of value instead of
a hundred small things. Block in the darkest dark and lightest light first,
so the rest has a scale to sit on; most beginner drawings are far too pale.

## Measuring by eye

Hold the pencil at arm's length with your elbow locked and measure one part
of the subject against the tip and your thumb. That is your unit; count
everything else in it. Do the same with angles — lay the pencil along an
edge you can see, then carry it to the page without letting it rotate. Then
check by turning the drawing upside down, which stops your eye reading a
face and makes it read shapes.

Fixative is sprayed outdoors or in the booth, never at the table; see
[[Materials and Safety]]. [[Making Marks]] is where this gets looser.
