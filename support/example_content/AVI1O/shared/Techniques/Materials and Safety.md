This is not a poster of warnings. Every material in this room has a
behaviour, and safety is knowing that behaviour before it surprises you —
the same knowledge that makes you better at using it.

## Before you touch anything

Find five things on your first day so you never hunt for them under
pressure: the sink and eyewash, the first aid kit, the fire blanket, the
sharps container, and the switch or hood that actually moves air out of the
room. Propping a door open is not ventilation; air has to leave. Every
product here carries hazard symbols and a safety data sheet, and an
unlabelled container is never used. When you do not know, ask.

## The risks that are real here

| Material | What goes wrong | The precaution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spray fixative | Solvent you breathe | Outdoors or in the hood only |
| Blades, cutters | Slips into the free hand | Cut away, hand behind blade |
| Plaster | Heats as it sets, sets in pipes | Never on skin or in a drain |
| Clay, plaster dust | Silica dust, breathed for years | Damp-wipe, never sweep |
| Kiln | Stays hot for many hours | Teacher loads and opens it |
| Printing press | Roller force, caught fingers | One operator, bed clear |
| Oil-based products | Vapour, rags that self-heat | Water-based media here |
| Pigments | Some are toxic if swallowed | No food at the table, wash hands |

## Dust is the one people miss

Nobody is hurt by clay dust today, which is exactly why it gets ignored.
Fine silica dust from dry clay and plaster damages lungs over years, and the
broom is the worst offender: sweeping lifts settled dust straight back into
the air. Damp sponge on tables, damp mop on floors, and sand plaster wet.

## Where the waste actually goes

Water is a solvent, not a disposal system. Down the drain: hand washing, and
rinse water that has been through a settling bucket. Never down the drain:
plaster or clay slurry, which sets in the trap and needs a plumber; solvent
of any kind; oil-based ink. Acrylic rinse water waits in the bucket until
the solids drop, then the water is poured off and the sludge binned dry.

Cut from the offcuts box before opening a fresh sheet, keep newsprint for
the next print run, and buy nothing you will not finish. Empty ink
containers, spent aerosols, and anything marked hazardous go to a household
hazardous waste depot, not the bin — a can that once held solvent is still a
solvent problem. [[Art and the Environment]] and [[Clearing Up]] follow on.
