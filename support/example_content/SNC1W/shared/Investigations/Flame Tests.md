## Purpose

Identify metal ions by the colour they produce in a flame, and connect that
colour to electron behaviour.

> [!danger] Safety
> Open flame, tied-back hair, goggles on. Nichrome loops are **hot** long after
> they stop glowing. Never point a burning splint at anyone.

## Background

Heat gives an electron enough energy to jump to a higher shell. It falls back
almost immediately and emits the difference as light. Because each element's
shells are spaced differently, each emits its **own** colours.

$$
E_{\text{photon}} = E_{\text{high}} - E_{\text{low}}
$$

## Procedure

1. Clean the loop in acid, then in the flame, until it adds no colour.
2. Dip in the first solution and hold in the hottest part of the flame.
3. Record the colour **immediately** — the good ones last under a second.
4. Clean thoroughly between samples.

## Analysis

1. Identify the unknown. State exactly which observation decided it.
2. Every solution above is a chloride. Why does that matter for the conclusion?
3. Sodium contaminates everything — a trace turns any flame yellow-orange. How
   did you guard against that, and how confident are you?

> [!note] Where else you have seen this
> Fireworks are flame tests with a budget. Strontium for red, barium for green,
> copper for blue. Astronomers use the same principle in reverse to determine
> what distant stars are made of — see
> [[E2.3|the evidence about the universe's origin]].
