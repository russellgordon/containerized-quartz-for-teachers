## The idea

Eight planets, several dwarf planets, and an enormous number of smaller bodies,
all orbiting one star.

| Planet | Type | AU from Sun | Year (Earth years) | Moons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mercury | Terrestrial | 0.39 | 0.24 | 0 |
| Venus | Terrestrial | 0.72 | 0.62 | 0 |
| Earth | Terrestrial | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1 |
| Mars | Terrestrial | 1.52 | 1.88 | 2 |
| Jupiter | Gas giant | 5.20 | 11.9 | 95+ |
| Saturn | Gas giant | 9.58 | 29.4 | 145+ |
| Uranus | Ice giant | 19.2 | 84.0 | 28+ |
| Neptune | Ice giant | 30.1 | 164.8 | 16+ |

## The pattern in the table

Rocky and small close in; gaseous and huge farther out. The dividing line is the
**frost line** — beyond it, water and methane could freeze into solids and be
swept up by growing planets. Inside it, the young Sun blew those gases away.

> [!note] Why Pluto was reclassified
> The definition of a planet requires that a body has *cleared its orbital
> neighbourhood*. Pluto shares its region with countless Kuiper Belt objects.
> Nothing about Pluto changed — the definition did, once we found how many
> similar objects were out there.
