## The idea

Two non-metals cannot both give electrons away — neither wants to. Instead they
**share**, forming a covalent bond and a discrete **molecule**.

| | Ionic | Molecular |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Made of | Metal + non-metal | Non-metal + non-metal |
| Electrons are | Transferred | Shared |
| Melting point | High | Usually low |
| Conducts when dissolved | Yes | Usually no |
| Basic unit | A repeating lattice | A single molecule |

## Naming with prefixes

Molecular names use prefixes; ionic names do not.

| Prefix | Number |
| --- | --- |
| mono- | 1 |
| di- | 2 |
| tri- | 3 |
| tetra- | 4 |
| penta- | 5 |

- $\ce{CO}$ — carbon monoxide
- $\ce{CO2}$ — carbon dioxide
- $\ce{N2O4}$ — dinitrogen tetroxide

> [!warning] One letter, one very large difference
> Carbon dioxide is what you exhale. Carbon **monoxide** binds to your blood 200
> times more readily than oxygen does and will kill you in a closed garage. The
> difference is a single oxygen atom — which is precisely why chemical names are
> so fussy.
