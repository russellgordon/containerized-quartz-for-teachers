## The idea

**Biodiversity** is the variety of life in a place — how many different species,
and how much genetic variety within each one.

## Why variety makes a system tough

Imagine two fields. One is planted with a single variety of wheat; the other
holds forty species of grasses and wildflowers.

A new fungus arrives.

The monoculture has one answer to that question, and if the answer is "no
resistance", the whole field is lost. The meadow has forty answers, and some of
them work. That is **resilience**, and it comes directly from variety.

> [!example] The Irish potato famine
> A single potato variety, grown almost everywhere, met a water mould it had no
> resistance to. Over a million people died. The biology of the failure was
> monoculture.

## Measuring it

Species richness is the simplest measure: count the species. It ignores how
*common* each one is, which is why ecologists also use indices that account for
evenness.

| Plot | Species found | Individuals | Richness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mowed lawn | 4 | 220 | Low |
| Unmowed edge | 17 | 190 | Higher |
| Woodlot floor | 23 | 145 | Highest |
