## The idea

A **physical property** can be observed without changing what the substance is.
A **chemical property** describes how it behaves when it *does* change into
something else.

| Physical | Chemical |
| --- | --- |
| Colour, odour, texture | Flammability |
| Melting and boiling point | Reactivity with acid |
| Density | Ability to rust or tarnish |
| Solubility | Toxicity |
| Conductivity | Stability when heated |

## Density, because it comes up constantly

$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V}
$$

A block with a mass of $54.0\ \mathrm{g}$ and a volume of
$20.0\ \mathrm{cm^3}$:

$$
\rho = \frac{54.0\ \mathrm{g}}{20.0\ \mathrm{cm^3}} = 2.70\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}
$$

That is aluminium. Density is a **characteristic** physical property: it
identifies a substance regardless of how much you have.

> [!question] Why does size not matter?
> Cut the block in half and both mass and volume halve. Their ratio does not
> move. Properties that behave this way are far more useful for identification
> than ones that depend on sample size.
