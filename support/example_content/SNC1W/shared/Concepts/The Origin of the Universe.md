## The idea

The evidence points to a universe that began in an extremely hot, dense state
about **13.8 billion years ago** and has been expanding ever since.

## Three independent lines of evidence

1. **Redshift.** Light from distant galaxies is stretched toward red, and the
   farther away a galaxy is, the more it is stretched. Space itself is
   expanding.
2. **Cosmic microwave background.** A faint glow at the same temperature in
   every direction — the leftover heat of the early universe, cooled by
   expansion to about $2.7\ \mathrm{K}$.
3. **Abundance of light elements.** The proportions of hydrogen and helium we
   measure match what the theory predicts should have formed in the first few
   minutes.

> [!important] Why three matters more than one
> Redshift alone could have other explanations. Redshift **and** a uniform
> background glow **and** the exact element ratios, all predicted in advance by
> the same model, is a different level of evidence entirely.

> [!note] A common misunderstanding
> The Big Bang was not an explosion *into* empty space. There was no outside.
> Space itself expanded — and still does, everywhere, all at once.

## Diverse ways of knowing

Cultures around the world, including First Nations, Métis, and Inuit peoples,
have long traditions of observing and explaining the night sky. Many encode real
observational knowledge — seasonal star positions used for navigation and
timing. Understanding how different knowledge systems record and pass on
observations is part of [[E2.3|what this course asks of you]].
