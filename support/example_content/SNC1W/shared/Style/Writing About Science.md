Scientific writing is not fancy writing. It is writing that cannot be
misunderstood.

## Say what you measured, not what you felt

> ❌ The reaction went really fast and got super hot.
>
> ✅ The temperature rose from 21 °C to 47 °C in 30 seconds.

## Distinguish observation from interpretation

An observation is what your senses or instruments recorded. An interpretation is
what you think it means. Keep them in separate sentences.

> **Observation:** Bubbles formed on the magnesium and the tube became warm.
>
> **Interpretation:** A gas was produced and the reaction was exothermic,
> suggesting a chemical change.

## Claim what your data supports, and no more

| Too strong | Honest |
| --- | --- |
| This proves that light intensity controls photosynthesis. | These results support the prediction that higher light intensity increases the rate. |
| The results were wrong. | The results did not match the prediction; the lamp also warmed the water, which was not controlled. |

> [!important] "Prove" is not a science word
> Evidence *supports* or *fails to support*. One lab period does not prove
> anything, and saying it does makes a reader trust the rest of your report less.

## Units, always

A number without a unit is not an answer. $4.2$ what? Seconds, grams, ohms?

## Passive voice is fine here

"The solution was heated to 40 °C" keeps the attention on the procedure rather
than on who was holding the beaker. This is one of the few kinds of writing
where that is the right choice.
