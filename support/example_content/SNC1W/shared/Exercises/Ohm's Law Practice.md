Show your work: formula, substitution, then the answer with units.

$$
V = IR \qquad I = \frac{V}{R} \qquad R = \frac{V}{I}
$$

**1.** $V = 12\ \mathrm{V}$, $R = 4.0\ \Omega$. Find $I$.

> [!success]- Answer
> $I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{12\ \mathrm{V}}{4.0\ \Omega} = 3.0\ \mathrm{A}$

**2.** $I = 0.50\ \mathrm{A}$, $R = 24\ \Omega$. Find $V$.

> [!success]- Answer
> $V = IR = (0.50\ \mathrm{A})(24\ \Omega) = 12\ \mathrm{V}$

**3.** $V = 9.0\ \mathrm{V}$, $I = 0.30\ \mathrm{A}$. Find $R$.

> [!success]- Answer
> $R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{9.0\ \mathrm{V}}{0.30\ \mathrm{A}} = 30\ \Omega$

**4.** A kettle draws $12.5\ \mathrm{A}$ at $120\ \mathrm{V}$. Find its
resistance.

> [!success]- Answer
> $R = \frac{120\ \mathrm{V}}{12.5\ \mathrm{A}} = 9.6\ \Omega$
>
> Low resistance means large current — which is exactly why a kettle needs its
> own heavy circuit.

**5.** A $60\ \Omega$ resistor carries $0.20\ \mathrm{A}$. Find the potential
difference across it.

> [!success]- Answer
> $V = (0.20\ \mathrm{A})(60\ \Omega) = 12\ \mathrm{V}$

**6.** Two $10\ \Omega$ resistors in **series** across $12\ \mathrm{V}$. Find
the total resistance and the current.

> [!success]- Answer
> $R_T = 10 + 10 = 20\ \Omega$, so
> $I = \frac{12\ \mathrm{V}}{20\ \Omega} = 0.60\ \mathrm{A}$

**7.** The same two resistors in **parallel** across $12\ \mathrm{V}$.

> [!success]- Answer
> $\frac{1}{R_T} = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{2}{10}$, so
> $R_T = 5.0\ \Omega$ and $I = \frac{12\ \mathrm{V}}{5.0\ \Omega} = 2.4\ \mathrm{A}$
>
> Note that the total resistance is **less** than either resistor. Adding a
> second path always makes it easier for current to flow.

**8.** A phone charger supplies $5.0\ \mathrm{V}$ at $2.0\ \mathrm{A}$. Find
its power, and the energy delivered in 30 minutes.

> [!success]- Answer
> $P = VI = (5.0\ \mathrm{V})(2.0\ \mathrm{A}) = 10\ \mathrm{W}$
>
> $E = Pt = (10\ \mathrm{W})(1800\ \mathrm{s}) = 18\,000\ \mathrm{J}$
>
> Seconds, not minutes — a watt is a joule per second.
