Use $1\ \mathrm{AU} = 1.50 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{km}$ and
$1\ \mathrm{ly} = 9.46 \times 10^{12}\ \mathrm{km}$.

**1.** Jupiter is $5.20\ \mathrm{AU}$ from the Sun. How many kilometres?

> [!success]- Answer
> $5.20 \times 1.50 \times 10^{8} = 7.80 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{km}$

**2.** Light takes 8 min 20 s to reach Earth. Show that this gives 1 AU.

> [!success]- Answer
> $8\ \mathrm{min}\ 20\ \mathrm{s} = 500\ \mathrm{s}$, and
> $d = vt = (3.00 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{km/s})(500\ \mathrm{s}) = 1.50 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{km}$ ✓

**3.** Proxima Centauri is $4.24\ \mathrm{ly}$ away. Convert to kilometres,
then to AU.

> [!success]- Answer
> $4.24 \times 9.46 \times 10^{12} = 4.01 \times 10^{13}\ \mathrm{km}$
>
> Divided by 1 AU: about $268\,000\ \mathrm{AU}$. The nearest star is a
> quarter of a million times further away than the Sun.

**4.** Voyager 1 travels about $17\ \mathrm{km/s}$. How long to reach Proxima
Centauri?

> [!success]- Answer
> $t = \frac{4.01 \times 10^{13}}{17} \approx 2.4 \times 10^{12}\ \mathrm{s}$,
> which is roughly **75 000 years**.
>
> Voyager is not headed there — but the number is the point. Our fastest
> departing object would need longer than recorded history, many times over.

**5.** Andromeda is 2.5 million light-years away. How old is the light you see?

> [!success]- Answer
> 2.5 million years old. It left before *Homo sapiens* existed; early humans
> were making the first stone tools. Every telescope is a time machine, and it
> only points one way.

**6.** On a model where the Sun is a 24 cm basketball, how far away is Neptune
($4.50 \times 10^{9}\ \mathrm{km}$)?

> [!success]- Answer
> The scale factor is $\frac{0.24\ \mathrm{m}}{1.39 \times 10^{9}\ \mathrm{m}}$,
> giving about $777\ \mathrm{m}$ — most of a kilometre from the basketball.
