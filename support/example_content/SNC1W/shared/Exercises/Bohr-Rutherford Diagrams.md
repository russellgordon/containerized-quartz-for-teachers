For each atom: state the protons, neutrons, and electrons, then draw the
diagram. Shells hold 2, 8, 8.

| Element | Atomic number | Mass number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Helium | 2 | 4 |
| Carbon | 6 | 12 |
| Oxygen | 8 | 16 |
| Sodium | 11 | 23 |
| Magnesium | 12 | 24 |
| Chlorine | 17 | 35 |
| Argon | 18 | 40 |

**1.** Complete the table with the three particle counts for each element.

> [!success]- Answer
> | Element | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons | Shells |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | Helium | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
> | Carbon | 6 | 6 | 6 | 2, 4 |
> | Oxygen | 8 | 8 | 8 | 2, 6 |
> | Sodium | 11 | 12 | 11 | 2, 8, 1 |
> | Magnesium | 12 | 12 | 12 | 2, 8, 2 |
> | Chlorine | 17 | 18 | 17 | 2, 8, 7 |
> | Argon | 18 | 22 | 18 | 2, 8, 8 |
>
> Neutrons = mass number − atomic number, every time.

**2.** Draw all seven diagrams.

> [!success]- Check yours
> A neutral atom has equal protons and electrons. If yours does not, either you
> have drawn an ion — say so — or there is an arithmetic slip. Count the
> electrons in your shells and compare with the atomic number.

**3.** Which of these have a **full** outer shell? What does that predict?

> [!success]- Answer
> Helium (2 in the first shell, which is full at 2) and argon (2, 8, 8).
>
> Both are noble gases, and both are almost completely unreactive. A full outer
> shell means an atom has no reason to gain, lose, or share anything.

**4.** Sodium loses one electron; chlorine gains one. Draw both ions and give
each charge.

> [!success]- Answer
> $\mathrm{Na^+}$ — 11 protons, 10 electrons, shells 2, 8. One more positive
> than negative, so the charge is $1+$.
>
> $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ — 17 protons, 18 electrons, shells 2, 8, 8. Charge $1-$.
>
> Notice what both now have: a full outer shell. That is the whole reason they
> did it.

**5.** Magnesium-24 and magnesium-26 are both magnesium. What differs?

> [!success]- Answer
> The number of **neutrons** — 12 against 14. Protons stay at 12, which is what
> makes it magnesium at all, and electrons stay at 12, so the chemistry is
> identical. They are **isotopes**.
