Climate data is usually presented as *anomalies* — differences from a
long-term average — rather than absolute temperatures. Understanding why is
half the skill.

**1.** Why plot the **difference** from an average instead of the temperature
itself?

> [!success]- Answer
> Because absolute temperatures vary hugely by place — a station in Iqaluit and
> one in Windsor cannot be averaged meaningfully. Their *changes* can be. Using
> anomalies lets thousands of stations be combined, and makes a trend of a
> degree visible where it would vanish inside a 60-degree range.

**2.** A graph of global temperature rises overall but falls in several
individual years. Does a falling year contradict the trend?

> [!success]- Answer
> No. Climate is the statistics of weather over decades. Individual years vary
> for reasons that have nothing to do with the long-term trend — El Niño, a
> volcanic eruption, ordinary noise. A trend is a statement about the shape of
> the whole record, and no single year can refute it.

**3.** Two graphs of the same data, one with a vertical axis from $-1$ to
$+1\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and one from $-20$ to $+20\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$, look
completely different. Is either dishonest?

> [!success]- Answer
> Neither is dishonest by itself — but the second is misleading in effect,
> because it compresses a real change into a flat line. The reader's job is to
> **check the axis** before reacting to the shape. A graph's slope means
> nothing until you know its scale.

**4.** A graph starting in 1998 — an unusually warm year — shows a flatter
trend than one starting in 1970. Name the technique.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Cherry-picking the start date.** Beginning at an unusually high point makes
> the following years look flat or falling. The test is simple: does the claim
> survive if you move the start date a few years either way? If not, the claim
> is about the choice of start date, not about the climate.

**5.** Which indicator from [[Indicators of Climate Change]] would be hardest
to misrepresent this way?

> [!success]- Answer
> Sea level and ice mass are strong answers: both change slowly and in one
> direction, so a cherry-picked window still shows the trend. Year-to-year
> temperature is the easiest to distort, because it is the noisiest.

> [!important] The skill being practised
> Not "is this graph true?" but "what is this graph *for*, and what has it left
> out?" That question is the whole of [[A2.4]].
