Maps in this course are instruments, not illustrations. Each page here is
a working method you will be asked to use on real ground and real data.

- [[Reading a Topographic Map]] — contours, slope, watersheds, profiles
- [[Using a Web GIS]] — layers, overlay, and what an overlay proves
- [[Mapping a Hazard]] — hazard, exposure and vulnerability as separate
  layers, and how to classify honestly
- [[Remote Sensing and Imagery]] — what a sensor measures, which bands
  matter, and where to get imagery free

Read them in that order. Contours come first because slope is the
variable most of the rest depends on; hazard mapping comes late because
it needs everything ahead of it.

The finished maps are collected and reflected on in [[A Map of Your Own]].
