**Launched late in Unit 1, presented in the final week of the unit.**
Pairs. A visual explanation plus a five-minute presentation to the class.

Take one global physical feature or phenomenon and show what built it —
tracing the causal chain back through the spheres until you reach a
process rather than a place.

## Choose one, and it must be global in scale

The Pacific Ring of Fire. The Himalaya. The Sahara. The Great Lakes. The
Atlantic hurricane belt. The Gulf Stream and the climate of northwest
Europe. The tropical rainforest belt. The Canadian Shield. Take one your
partner does not already understand.

## What you produce

**A diagram** that is genuinely explanatory: a flow of causes with the
spheres identified, not a labelled picture. Hand-drawn is fine; it must
be readable from the back of the room.

**A five-minute presentation** in which each partner speaks. Assume the
audience knows the feature exists and nothing about why.

**A one-page source note** listing what you used, with dates —
[[Judging a Source]] applies here as everywhere.

## What the explanation must do

- Identify which spheres are involved and where they interact —
  [[The Four Spheres]].
- Name the process, and say what supplies its energy.
- Explain the **spatial pattern**: why here and not somewhere else, and
  what the distribution of similar features looks like globally.
- Connect back to the internal Earth where relevant, and forward to
  what living things did or now do about it.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The mechanism is correct and complete enough to hold up |
| Thinking | The pattern is explained, not just asserted, from the mechanism |
| Communication | Diagram carries the argument; both partners can answer questions |
| Application | Connections between spheres are specific, not decorative |

> [!question] The question you will be asked afterwards
> "Where else on Earth would you expect to find this, and why?" Prepare
> an answer. If your explanation is a real mechanism it will generate a
> prediction; if it is a description it will not, and the question will
> find that out. See [[Plate Tectonics and What It Builds]] and
> [[Climate and Its Controls]] for how a mechanism generates a pattern.
