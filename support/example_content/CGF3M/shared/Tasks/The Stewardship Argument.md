**Launched early in Unit 3, due mid-unit.** Individual. A structured
argument of about 900 words, written for a named audience who is in a
position to act.

Choose a real site where human activity has altered a physical system,
and argue what should be done about it — using the environment's own
capacity to renew itself as the measure.

## Choose a site with a record

A closed or operating aggregate pit, quarry or mine site. A river reach
that has been straightened, dammed or diverted. A drained wetland. A
farmed slope losing soil. A shoreline that has been armoured. A woodlot
fragmented by development. Local is better than famous, because you can
get evidence and because the audience is real.

## What the argument must contain

1. **The physical system before**, as far as the record allows, and the
   evidence you are using to establish it — imagery, mapping, soil
   survey, a resident's account.
2. **The modification**, described physically, and the mechanism by which
   it changed the system — [[Human Impact on Physical Systems]].
3. **Natural renewal.** What would this site do on its own, and over what
   timescale? Distinguish processes that will recover in years from
   those that will not recover in a human lifetime —
   [[Renewing What Is Damaged]].
4. **Whether the modification changed hazard**, and for whom.
5. **Your recommendation**, with the criteria you assessed it against,
   its cost, who pays, and what it does not fix.

## Write it to somebody

Name the audience in the first line — a conservation authority, a
municipal council, the landowner, the aggregate producer — and write in
the register that audience reads. A council reads a recommendation with
costs. A landowner reads a practical sequence. Adapting the format to the
reader is part of the mark.

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Renewal processes and their timescales are accurate |
| Thinking | Recommendation follows from stated criteria and admits trade-offs |
| Application | Evidence is specific to this site, not to the general problem |
| Communication | Format and register match the named audience |

> [!note] "Restore it" is usually the weakest recommendation
> Full restoration is rare, slow and expensive, and proposing it without
> pricing it reads as unserious. Rehabilitation to a stated, achievable
> condition — with the criteria for judging it — is a stronger argument
> and a harder one to write.
