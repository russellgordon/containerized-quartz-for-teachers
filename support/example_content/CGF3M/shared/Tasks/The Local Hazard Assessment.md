**The culminating task. Launched at the start of Unit 4 and worked on
through it.** Small groups. A written assessment of about 1500 words, a
hazard map, and a fifteen-minute delivery to an invited audience.

Assess one hazard for this community and recommend what should be done
about it — then deliver that recommendation to somebody who could
actually act on it.

## The hazard, and the audience

Shoreline erosion and storm damage on the waterfront. Riverine or urban
flooding. Ice storm and extended power loss. Wildfire at the woodland
edge. Extreme heat. Choose one, and choose your audience with it: the
conservation authority, the municipality's Community Emergency
Management Coordinator, or the school board's facilities staff. They will
be invited, and they will ask questions.

## What the assessment must contain

1. **The physical hazard**, characterised from evidence: process,
   magnitude range, frequency, and how climate change alters that
   frequency or intensity, with the confidence of that claim stated.
2. **A hazard map** showing hazard extent and exposed people, buildings,
   routes and services as distinct layers —
   [[Mapping a Hazard]].
3. **Field evidence** of your own from [[Your Field Notebook]] — this
   community, this ground, measured by you.
4. **Vulnerability**, said out loud: who here is least able to prepare,
   leave, or recover, and how you know.
5. **What is already in place**, from the municipal emergency plan, the
   conservation authority's mapping, and the school's own procedures.
6. **A recommendation**: one measure, roughly costed, with who pays, what
   it does not solve, and how you would know in ten years whether it had
   worked.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Physical process and change over time both handled correctly |
| Thinking | Findings synthesised into a judgement, with uncertainty carried through |
| Application | Own field and mapped evidence used, not only published summaries |
| Communication | Delivered to the real audience, in their register, and defended |

> [!abstract] The test of the whole course
> A stranger who works on this hazard for a living should finish your
> fifteen minutes knowing one thing about their own community they did
> not know before, and should be able to see exactly where you got it.
> That is a higher bar than a grade, and it is the one worth aiming at.

Rehearse against [[Should People Live There?]] and
[[Who Should Pay to Rebuild?]] — both questions will be put to you.
