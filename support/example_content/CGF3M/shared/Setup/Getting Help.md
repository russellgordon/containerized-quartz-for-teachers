Asking for help early is a skill, not an admission. In this course it is
also efficient: most of what goes wrong here goes wrong quietly, over
several days, and is a ten-minute conversation if caught in the first
one.

## When to ask

- You read the concept page twice and it still does not land.
- You cannot find data for the case you chose. This happens constantly
  and is usually solved by changing where you look, not what you study.
- You are not sure a source is any good — bring it, that is a real
  question and a marked skill.
- You missed a field day. There is a way to get the data; ask.
- Something outside school is making this term hard. Tell me as much or
  as little as you want to.

## How to ask usefully

Say what you tried, and where it stopped. "I don't get hydrographs" is
harder to help with than "I can see the peak moves earlier after paving,
but I can't explain why the recession is different." The second gets you
a real answer in two minutes.

Bring the actual thing. The map, the spreadsheet, the paragraph, the
notebook page. Talking about work in the abstract wastes the time you
came for.

## Ways to reach me

Before class, after class, and at the scheduled help sessions. By message
through the school's system, which I read in the evening but do not
answer at midnight. If you would rather ask something without the room
hearing it, a note on my desk works and always has.

## Beyond me

The library can get you sources this site does not list, including
journal access. Guidance can help with load, deadlines and everything
that is not geography. If you are struggling with something serious, they
are the right door and I will walk you to it.
