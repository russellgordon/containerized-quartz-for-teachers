CGF3M is Forces of Nature: Physical Processes and Disasters, a Grade 11
university/college preparation course in geography. It is a full credit —
one credit, which Ontario's secondary schools policy equates to 110
scheduled hours. In this school that is 86 periods of 75 minutes plus a
three-hour final evaluation.

## The shape of the term

| Unit | What it asks | Roughly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — The Spheres and How They Work | How does the planet actually operate? | 22 periods |
| 2 — Hazard, Disaster, and Risk | When does a process become a disaster? | 22 periods |
| 3 — Living With the Physical Environment | What do we take, damage, and repair? | 22 periods |
| 4 — Systems and Interdependence | How are we all connected, and what now? | 20 periods |

Unit 4 ends with the culminating task, delivered to somebody outside this
room. The final evaluation comes after it.

## What a period usually looks like

You meet an idea by doing something with it before it is named. That
might be a set of measurements, a map to interpret, a data extract to
argue over, or a photograph nobody can explain yet. We then compare what
different groups found, name the idea properly, and practise it. The
Concepts pages on this site are written to be read *after* that class,
not before it.

Some periods are work periods, and they are on the schedule for a reason.
That is when conferencing happens and when the stuck get unstuck, so
being present for them matters as much as being present for a lesson.

## Fieldwork is not an extra

We walk to the waterfront and to the woodlot, and we visit a working
vegetable farm five minutes away. Those days carry data you cannot get
any other way, and several tasks require your own measurements. Read
[[Fieldwork Safety]] before the first one and bring what
[[What to Bring]] lists.

## Reading and writing expectations

Expect to read published data rather than a textbook summary of it, and
to attribute everything you use. Expect to be asked, often, how confident
you are in a number and why. That habit is the course.

Marks are explained in [[How Marks Work]]. What we owe each other is in
[[Our Working Agreement]]. If any of this is not working for you,
[[Getting Help]] says how to say so.
