Six real events, each worked through with figures attributed to the body
that published them. They are here so that arguments in this course rest
on something specific — and so that you can see what a properly sourced
account looks like before you are asked to write one.

**Unit 2 — Hazard, disaster, and risk**

- [[The 2013 Southern Alberta Flood]] — a Canadian flood, and what
  upstream weather did to four rivers at once
- [[The 2023 Canadian Wildfire Season]] — the season that reset the
  national record, and what a preliminary figure means
- [[The Tohoku Earthquake and Tsunami, 2011]] — the best-prepared coast
  in the world, and the limits of preparation
- [[Hurricane Fiona in Atlantic Canada]] — a post-tropical storm, a
  record low pressure, and a town on the water

**Unit 3 — Living with the physical environment**

- [[The 2021 Prairie Drought]] — the hazard with no moment, measured by
  what it removed from the harvest

**Unit 4 — Systems and interdependence**

- [[The Great Lakes Water Level Cycle]] — the slow one, happening at the
  shore you walk to

Every figure on these pages names its source and the date it was
retrieved, because disaster figures are revised for months and sometimes
years afterwards. Where two bodies publish different numbers, both are
given — see [[Hazard and Disaster Records]] for why that happens.
