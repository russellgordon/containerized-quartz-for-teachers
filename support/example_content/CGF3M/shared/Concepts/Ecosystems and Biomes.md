A **biome** is a large region defined by the vegetation that a given
combination of heat and moisture allows. Draw mean annual temperature
against mean annual precipitation and the world's biomes fall into
recognisable zones on the graph — which is a strong claim, because it
says climate predicts vegetation without your having to know anything
about the plants.

The claim holds at continental scale and frays at local scale, and both
halves matter. Tundra, boreal forest, temperate deciduous forest,
grassland, desert and tropical rainforest each occupy a band you can
locate from a climograph. Within any of them, a north-facing slope, a
wet hollow or a shallow soil creates a **microclimate** that supports
something the regional label does not predict.

> [!info] Canada's terrestrial ecozones
> Canada uses ecozones rather than global biome names for its own
> mapping. The school sits in the **Mixedwood Plains** ecozone — the
> smallest by area, the most heavily settled, and the one where the
> original deciduous and mixed forest has been most thoroughly
> converted to farmland and city. The woodlot you will visit is a
> remnant of that forest, not a sample of untouched wilderness.

## Energy in, energy through

Ecosystems run on the energy plants capture, and only a small fraction
of that energy passes to each successive feeding level — which is why
food chains are short and why top predators need large territories.
Nutrients, unlike energy, cycle: carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus move
between soil, organism, water and air and return. An ecosystem is
therefore open to energy and close to closed for matter, and that
distinction explains why removing biomass repeatedly — as harvest, as
logging — is a nutrient question and not only a biomass question.

## Why any of this is a hazard topic

Vegetation is not a passive backdrop to physical processes. Roots hold
slopes against landsliding, canopy intercepts rainfall before it strikes
soil, wetlands store flood water, and dry fuel loads determine whether
an ignition becomes [[Wildfire]]. Change the vegetation and you change
the physical behaviour of the place — the argument developed in
[[Human Impact on Physical Systems]].

Where climate zones shift, biomes must shift or shrink, and they cannot
always move as fast as the climate does. That mismatch is one of the
impacts assessed in [[A Warming Planet]]. Test the general claim against
one small place in [[The Woodlot Study]].
