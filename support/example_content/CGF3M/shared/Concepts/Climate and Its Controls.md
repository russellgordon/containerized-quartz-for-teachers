Weather is what happens; climate is the statistics of what usually
happens. Environment and Climate Change Canada builds its **climate
normals** from thirty-year averages, and the current set covers 1991 to
2020. A normal is not a forecast and not a limit — it is the yardstick
you measure an unusual year against.

## Six controls, and how to use them

| Control | What it does | Test it against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Latitude | Sets the angle and duration of solar heating | Iqaluit against Windsor |
| Ocean currents | Warm or cool the air that crosses them | St John's against Victoria |
| Wind and pressure belts | Deliver moisture or withhold it | The Sahara under descending air |
| Elevation | Cools air roughly as it rises | Banff against Calgary |
| Relief and barriers | Force air up, wringing rain from it | Vancouver against Kamloops |
| Distance from water | Water heats and cools slowly, land quickly | Winnipeg against Halifax |

The controls are not a list to memorise; they are a checklist for
explaining any climate graph you are handed. Work down it and the
anomaly usually appears in one row.

## Why the energy moves

Earth receives more energy at the equator than it loses there, and less
at the poles than it loses. The atmosphere and oceans move the surplus
poleward, and the machinery of that transfer — Hadley cells, the trade
winds, the mid-latitude westerlies, the jet streams — is what produces
the global pattern of wet and dry belts. Rising air cools, condenses and
rains; sinking air warms, dries, and makes deserts. The world's great
deserts near 30° north and south are not a coincidence, they are the
descending limb of a circulation.

**Albedo** ties the system back on itself. Snow and ice reflect most of
the light that reaches them; open water and dark forest absorb most of
it. Melt the snow and the surface absorbs more, warms further, and melts
more — a feedback that operates in one hemisphere every spring and over
decades in the Arctic, which is where [[A Warming Planet]] picks it up.

Earth's tilt of about 23.5° is what makes any of this seasonal, and
[[Ecosystems and Biomes]] shows what grows where the resulting pattern
of heat and moisture allows.
