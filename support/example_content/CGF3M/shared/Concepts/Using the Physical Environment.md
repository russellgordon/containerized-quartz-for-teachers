Physical geography is not only a set of constraints. It is also the
reason anything is anywhere. Every settlement in this country sits where
it does because some physical feature offered something, and the offer is
still readable on the ground.

## What features give

A waterfall gives a head of water, which gave mechanical power to mills
and later hydroelectricity — which is why so many Ontario towns have a
main street beside a drop in a river. A sheltered bay gives a harbour. A
moraine or esker gives sand and gravel, the highest-volume mineral
product in the province and the reason aggregate pits cluster in
particular landforms rather than randomly. A floodplain gives soil that
was renewed by the flooding. A forest gives fibre; a mineralised
subduction zone gives ore.

Notice the tense. Some of these benefits are still collected and some are
historical, and a community whose original advantage has expired is a
distinct kind of geography problem.

## The same feature, costed both ways

| Feature | Benefit | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active volcano | Fertile soils, geothermal energy, tourism | Ash fall, lahars, evacuation, total loss |
| Floodplain | Flat, fertile, easy to build on, near water | Flooding, at a frequency you do not choose |
| Monsoon | The rain a crop year depends on | Flooding, landslides, transport failure |
| Heavy snowfall | Snowpack storing water for summer; winter economy | Roof loads, avalanche, closure costs |
| Coast | Trade, fishing, cooling water, amenity | Surge, erosion, and rising sea level |

The table's point is that "should people live there" is almost never a
question about the hazard alone — see [[Should People Live There?]]. It is
a question about whether the benefits, to these particular people, are
worth the risks they will actually carry.

> [!tip] Ask who receives which column
> The benefit and the cost frequently go to different people. A quarry
> supplies aggregate to a region and delivers dust and truck traffic to
> one road. Splitting the table by who gets what is often the whole
> analysis.

Do this for one real proposal in [[The Land Use Question]], and for the
farm five minutes down the road in [[The Farm Visit]].
