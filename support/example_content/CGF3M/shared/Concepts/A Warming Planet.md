The greenhouse effect is not the problem — without it the planet would
average well below freezing and [[The Four Spheres]] would look very
different. The problem is that adding gases which absorb outgoing
infrared radiation raises the equilibrium temperature, and we have added
a great deal of them in a very short time.

## The numbers, with their uncertainty attached

The IPCC's Sixth Assessment reports that global surface temperature in
2011–2020 was about **1.1 °C** above the 1850–1900 baseline, and that
global mean sea level rose about **0.20 m** between 1901 and 2018, with
the rate accelerating from 1.3 mm per year over 1901–1971 to 3.7 mm per
year over 2006–2018.[^ipcc]

Canada is warming faster. *Canada's Changing Climate Report* (2019) found
annual average temperature over Canada rose **1.7 °C** between 1948 and
2016 — likely range 1.1 to 2.3 °C — roughly twice the global increase,
and **2.3 °C** over northern Canada — likely range 1.7 to 3.0 °C —
roughly three times the global rate. Quote the range, not just the
midpoint; the range is part of the finding.

[^ipcc]: IPCC, *Climate Change 2023: Synthesis Report*, and *Climate
Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis*. The World Meteorological
Organization's *State of the Global Climate 2025* puts that year at
1.44 °C ± 0.13 °C above 1850–1900. Different bodies, different baselines
and different periods — always name which you are using.

## Why Canada is an outlier

Snow and ice albedo. Warming melts reflective surfaces, exposing darker
land and open water that absorb more energy, which produces more warming
— the feedback introduced in [[Climate and Its Controls]], operating on a
continent that has a great deal of snow and ice to lose. Thawing
permafrost adds a second loop by releasing stored carbon.

## What it does to the hazards in this unit

Warmer air holds more water vapour, so the heaviest rainfall events
intensify. Warmer, drier fuel lengthens fire seasons — [[Wildfire]].
Warmer oceans give tropical cyclones more energy and lift the water they
push ashore — [[Storms and Floods]]. Heat itself becomes a direct hazard,
as in [[Drought and Heat]].

Notice the shape of those statements. Climate change alters the
*probability and intensity* of a class of events; it does not "cause" any
single one. Attribution science can now estimate how much more likely a
particular event was made, and that is a quantified claim with a
confidence attached — a much stronger thing to write than "because of
climate change".

Impacts are not shared evenly, and some are positive for some places. Say
who benefits, who loses, and over what timescale, or you have written an
opinion. Start with [[The 2021 Prairie Drought]].
