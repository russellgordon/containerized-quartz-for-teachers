Almost every number this course needs is published free by the agency
whose job is to collect it. These four pages are about getting the figure
from that agency, understanding what it actually measures, and being
honest about how much it can carry.

- [[Where the Data Lives]] — the agencies, the datasets, and the links
- [[Reading Climate Data]] — normals, anomalies, climographs, and the
  slicing that produces false trends
- [[Hazard and Disaster Records]] — disaster databases, their entry
  thresholds, and the four biases they build in
- [[Judging a Source]] — who, why, when, who paid

The four questions in the last page apply to the first three. A federal
dataset is not exempt from being read critically; it is simply a source
whose purpose and limits are usually written down, which is a reason to
prefer it rather than a reason to stop thinking.
