A disaster database is not a record of what happened. It is a record of
what was reported, by someone, to a body that had a threshold for
recording it. Every trend you read out of one is a trend in events and in
observation together, and separating the two is most of the skill.

## Public Safety Canada's Canadian Disaster Database

The Canadian Disaster Database holds over a thousand natural,
technological and conflict events affecting Canadians since 1900, and it
is downloadable as a spreadsheet through the federal open-government
portal. An event enters it if it meets one or more criteria: ten or more
people killed; one hundred or more affected, injured, infected, evacuated
or left homeless; an appeal for national or international assistance;
historical significance; or damage severe enough that the community
cannot recover on its own.

Read that list again as a filter. A flood that ruined forty basements in
a wealthy suburb and a flood that displaced a hundred people from a small
community are not equally likely to appear, and neither is an event from
1912 compared with one from 2012.

## Other registers worth knowing

**Earthquakes Canada** at Natural Resources Canada publishes a searchable
national earthquake catalogue; instrumental coverage improves through the
twentieth century, so early small events are simply absent. The
**Canadian Interagency Forest Fire Centre** and NRCan's national fire
database record area burned and fire counts, with current-season figures
explicitly preliminary. **Insurance Bureau of Canada** publishes insured
losses, which count only what was insured and are therefore a floor, not
a total. Internationally, the **EM-DAT** international disaster database
and the reinsurers' catastrophe reports use their own thresholds again.

## Four biases to name in writing

| Bias | What it does to the record |
| --- | --- |
| Reporting | Events near people, media and insurance are recorded more often |
| Threshold | Small events fall below the entry criteria and vanish |
| Detection | Better instruments find more events without more occurring |
| Exposure | Losses rise as more value is built in harm's way, hazard unchanged |

The fourth is the one that ruins most student trend claims. Damage in
dollars rises over time because there is more to damage and money is
worth less; adjust for inflation at minimum, and say plainly that you
have not adjusted for the growth in exposure unless you have.

> [!tip] Say what a zero means
> An empty cell in a hazard database means "not recorded", which could
> be "did not happen" or "nobody was there". Which one you assume
> changes your map — [[Mapping a Hazard]].
