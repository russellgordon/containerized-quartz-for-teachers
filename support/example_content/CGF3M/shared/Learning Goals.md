What you should be able to do by the end of CGF3M, in plain words first
and in the Ministry's words underneath.

## By the end of the course you should be able to

- **Explain a physical process**, rather than name it — what supplies the
  energy, what moves, and what sets the rate.
- **Read the ground**: take a landscape, a contour sheet or an image and
  say what must have happened, and whether it is still happening.
- **Measure something yourself** and use your own data as evidence, with
  observation and inference kept apart.
- **Separate the hazard from the disaster** — the physical event, who is
  exposed to it, and who is hurt worst by it.
- **Judge a source**: who made it, why, when, and who paid, and say what
  it therefore leaves out.
- **Carry uncertainty into your conclusion** instead of dropping it
  there — quote a range when the source gives a range.
- **Make a map that argues honestly**, with its classification, its date
  and its sources on the face of it.
- **Say who bears the cost** of a decision about land, water or risk, and
  represent fairly the people who disagree with you.

## The self-check that matters

You are handed a hydrograph, a climograph and a contour extract you have
never seen, from a place you cannot name. Can you say what process is
operating, how confident you are, and what evidence would change your
mind? That is the whole course, and it is exactly what
[[The Data Examination]] asks of you.
