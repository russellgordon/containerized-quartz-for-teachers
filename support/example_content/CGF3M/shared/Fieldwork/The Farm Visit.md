A working vegetable farm five minutes away is the clearest demonstration
this course can offer that physical geography sets limits people have to
work inside. Everything the farmer decides — what to plant, when, where
in the field, whether to irrigate, whether to drain — is a response to
soil, water, slope and the length of the growing season.

Read [[Fieldwork Safety]] first. Machinery, yards and buildings: stay
with the group and do exactly what the farmer says, immediately.

## What we are looking at

**Soil.** Texture and structure across the field, and how they change
between the high ground and the low. Ask what the field's soil is called
and whether a soil survey exists for it — most of southern Ontario is
mapped.

**Water.** Where surface water goes after heavy rain, whether the field
is tile-drained, where the water is drawn from for irrigation, and how
close the working edge comes to a watercourse.

**Season.** Frost dates at each end, the length of the frost-free period,
what is started indoors because the season is too short for it outdoors,
and which crops are chosen because they tolerate what this site actually
offers.

**Erosion control.** Residue left on the surface, cover crops, buffer
strips along drains, windbreak rows, and whether cultivation runs across
the slope or down it.

## Questions worth asking a farmer

Bring these written down, and listen to the answer rather than to your
next question.

- Which part of this farm is the hardest to work, and why?
- What has the weather done to you in the last five years?
- What did you change after that?
- What decision here is set by the soil rather than by the market?
- What would you do differently if you were starting this farm now?

> [!quote] Take the answers as evidence
> A farmer's account of their own land is a primary source from an
> expert observer with a direct stake — which is exactly what
> [[Judging a Source]] tells you to record and to weigh, not to
> discount. Write the answers down close to verbatim, with the date.

## What it feeds

The physical constraints go into [[Using the Physical Environment]]; the
soil-conservation practices are assessed as rehabilitation in
[[Renewing What Is Damaged]]; and the whole visit is the evidence base
for [[The Land Use Question]], where a real proposal for this kind of
land has to be argued.
