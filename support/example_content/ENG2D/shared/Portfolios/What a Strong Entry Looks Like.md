Two entries about the same class.

## Thin

> Read Act 3 Scene 5. Capulet yells at Juliet. It was intense. Discussed
> whether Juliet has a choice.

## Strong

> 3.5 — Capulet's speech. He calls her "green-sickness carrion" and
> "baggage" in four lines. What struck me: he never uses her name in the
> whole scene. Lady Capulet does, once, and then leaves.
>
> In discussion I said Juliet has a choice because she could refuse.
> Priya pointed at line 194 — "hang, beg, starve, die in the streets" —
> and said that is not a choice, it is a list of outcomes. I think she is
> right, and it changes what I want to argue in the essay: the play keeps
> offering choices that are not choices.
>
> Question: does Romeo ever get a scene like this one?

Three moves: a **quoted detail**, a **thought that changed** and who
changed it, and a **question left open**.

## The shape to copy

| Move | Prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Notice | One quoted phrase, with act/scene or page |
| Think | What it does |
| Argue | What somebody else said, and where you now stand |
| Ask | What you cannot yet answer |
