The media unit works on real texts rather than examples invented for a
worksheet. These are the four kinds we take apart, and what each is for.

## What we study

| Text | What it teaches |
| --- | --- |
| **Two front pages of the same day** | Selection and emphasis: same facts, two arguments |
| **Three advertisements**, chosen by you | Audience, technique, and the claim underneath the claim |
| **A documentary segment** | That honest and neutral are different words |
| **A feed, screenshotted** | How sequence and algorithm change meaning without changing content |

## The method

Every text gets the five questions from
[[Media Forms and Techniques]], then a technique inventory, then the
question that matters most: **what is left out?**

## The advertisements are yours

You bring three you find effective — not three you find stupid. Analysing
something that works on you is harder and far more useful, and it is what
[[The Media Deconstruction]] assesses.

## Two front pages

We compare the same event as covered by two publications: which
photograph, which headline verb, who is quoted first, what is below the
fold. Nothing in that comparison requires accusing anybody of lying,
which is why it is the clearest introduction to
[[Bias and Perspective]] available.

> [!question] Worth arguing about
> An advertisement that tells the truth, beautifully, to sell you
> something you do not need: manipulation, or craft? Bring your answer to
> [[Who Is This For]].
