> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Waubgeshig Rice**, Anishinaabe writer from Wasauksing First Nation ·
> *Moon of the Crusted Snow* (ECW Press, 2018) · a novel about a northern
> Anishinaabe community cut off during a collapse

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> Isolation, hunger, violence, and death — including the deliberate
> withholding of information from a community. Read
> [[Our Agreements]] before we start.

## The premise

The power fails in a remote Anishinaabe community in northern Ontario.
Then the phones. Then the deliveries. Nobody arrives to explain. Evan
Whitesky and the people around him have to decide, without information,
how to get through a winter — and then two men arrive from the south.

## What to notice

- **How ordinary the opening is.** Rice spends real time on the
  everyday — the hunt, the band office, the store — before anything
  fails. Ask what that buys him later. See [[Setting as Argument]].
- **What "apocalypse" means here.** More than one character points out
  that this community has already survived the end of a world. That line
  is the novel's argument; find it and mark it.
- **Anishinaabemowin in the text.** Watch who speaks it, to whom, and
  what it carries that English does not.
- **The Elders.** Aileen in particular. What kind of authority is she,
  and how does the novel distinguish it from the authority of the band
  council?
- **Scott.** How he arrives, how he speaks, and how long it takes the
  community to name what he is.
- **Withheld information.** The reader knows only what Evan knows. That
  choice is discussed on [[Narrative Perspective]].

## Questions we will argue about

1. Is this a post-apocalyptic novel or a novel about survival that
   happens to be set during a collapse? Does the distinction matter?
2. Aileen says the word "apocalypse" does not fit. Who is right — her, or
   the reader who brought the word with them?
3. What does the novel say about who is prepared, and why?
4. The ending. Read as hopeful by some readers and as ominous by others —
   both with evidence. Which passages carry your reading?
