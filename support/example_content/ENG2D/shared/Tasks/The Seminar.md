> [!abstract] At a glance
> Groups of four · one 25-minute seminar led by your group, plus
> participation in everybody else's · a question, two passages, and a
> written follow-up · assessed on Communication, Thinking, and Knowledge

## The task

Lead a discussion of [[Moon of the Crusted Snow]] that other people want
to be in.

## What your group brings

1. **One question** that cannot be answered yes or no, and that the
   novel does not settle. Approved by me two classes before.
2. **Two passages**, with page numbers, that the room will read aloud.
3. **A plan for the silence.** What you will do when nobody answers:
   a smaller question, a vote, a "read this line again and tell me what
   the verb is doing".
4. **A closing move**: what you will ask the room to leave with.

## What being led looks like

You are not presenting. A seminar leader speaks less than a fifth of the
time. The skills are on [[Seminar Skills]]: opening with evidence,
building, complicating, inviting, steelmanning, and conceding.

## The follow-up

Individually, after your seminar: 400 words on what your question
produced. Not a summary of what people said — an argument about what the
discussion revealed about the novel, including the answer you did not
expect.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The question and the passages show that the whole novel has been read |
| Thinking | The seminar goes somewhere; the follow-up says what the discussion established and what it did not |
| Communication | You listen, respond to what was actually said, and make room for other people |

## Participation in other groups' seminars

Assessed too, and on the same terms: evidence, building on others,
conceding. Attendance with nothing to say is not participation, and
talking over four other people is not either.

> [!tip] The best seminar question this room has had
> "Aileen says the word 'apocalypse' does not fit. Is the novel agreeing
> with her, or with the reader who brought the word?" It is answerable in
> four different ways, all with passages, and no group has ever finished
> it in twenty-five minutes.
