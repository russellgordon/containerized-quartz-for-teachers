> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · five class periods · a short story of 800–1200 words plus
> a 300-word craft note · assessed on Application, Thinking, and
> Communication

## The task

Write a short story. Then write about how you built it.

The story is judged as a story. The craft note is judged as analysis of
your own work — and it is the part that makes this an English task
rather than a creative-writing exercise.

## The story

Constraints, because constraints are what make a short story a short
story:

- **One central character** who wants something.
- **A time frame under a week.** Preferably under a day.
- **A turn** — something changes: a decision, an understanding, a
  refusal.
- **No more than three named characters.**
- **A last sentence you chose**, not the one you stopped at.

## The craft note

Three hundred words on the decisions you made and why:

- Where did you start the story, and what did you leave out?
- Whose perspective, and what did that choice cost you? See
  [[Narrative Perspective]].
- One technique you took from a story we read — name the story and what
  you adapted.
- What did the ending do that the beginning set up?

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Application | The story uses the techniques from the unit deliberately, and the writing is controlled |
| Thinking | The craft note explains choices, not intentions — what is on the page and why |
| Communication | The story holds a reader; the note is precise and readable |

> [!tip] Where these usually go wrong
> Too much time. A story covering two years in 1000 words becomes a
> summary with dialogue in it. Choose the afternoon everything turned on,
> and let the reader infer the rest — that is what
> [[Short Stories]] shows five different writers doing.
