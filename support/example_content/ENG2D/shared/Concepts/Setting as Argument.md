Setting is not background. In a good text the where and when are doing
part of the arguing.

## Four jobs

1. **Pressure.** Winter, distance, curfew, a plague quarantine that stops
   a letter arriving.
2. **History.** A place has a past, and in Canadian writing that past is
   often the subject: whose land, whose road, whose reserve, whose town.
3. **Character.** What a person notices about a place tells you what they
   fear.
4. **Meaning.** Verona's public square, a tomb, a generator shed — the
   places a text returns to are the places it is thinking about.

## A worked case

In [[Moon of the Crusted Snow]] the community is already remote before
anything fails, and the novel makes that ordinary — the store, the band
office, the road out. When the power goes, the setting stops being
scenery and becomes the plot: distance, cold, and a community that has
survived a collapse before.

Waubgeshig Rice is Anishinaabe, from Wasauksing First Nation, and the
novel's argument about who is prepared for an apocalypse depends entirely
on the place he chose to set it in.

## In your own writing

Cut any description that does not do one of the four jobs. A paragraph
about weather that is not pressure, history, character, or meaning is a
paragraph a reader will skim.
