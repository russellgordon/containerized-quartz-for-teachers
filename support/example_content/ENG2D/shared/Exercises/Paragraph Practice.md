The shape is on [[The Paragraph]].

**1.** Write the three sentences that should follow this claim:
*The Prologue removes suspense in order to create a different kind of
pressure.*

> [!success]- Answer 1
> One version: *Fourteen lines tell the audience that both children die
> and that the feud dies with them. Every hopeful line afterwards —
> Juliet's "I have no joy of this contract" included — is heard by people
> who already know how it ends. The play therefore asks not whether they
> will be saved but why nobody saves them, which is a question about the
> adults on stage rather than about fate.*

**2.** Find the sentence that does not belong:
*Evan's hunting trips organise the first third of the novel. Rice writes
them in detail: the route, the cold, the division of the meat. Rice is
Anishinaabe and grew up in Wasauksing First Nation. When the power fails,
those routines are the only structure the community has left.*

> [!success]- Answer 2
> The biographical sentence — true, and doing no work in a paragraph
> about narrative structure. It would belong in a paragraph about the
> novel's authority on the community it describes.

**3.** This paragraph has two ideas. Split it and write a topic sentence
for each.

*Mercutio's language is a defence. He puns constantly, refuses to take
love seriously, and turns every threat into a joke. After he is stabbed
he keeps punning — "ask for me tomorrow and you shall find me a grave
man" — and the joke arrives at the same moment as the wound. The play
changes after his death: the comedy stops, the pace doubles, and Romeo
becomes somebody who acts before speaking.*

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Paragraph one: *Mercutio uses wordplay as a defence against
> seriousness.* Paragraph two: *Mercutio's death converts the play from a
> comedy into a tragedy in the space of one scene.*

**4.** Improve a last sentence that merely restates the first.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> End with a consequence instead: *If the light imagery reverses here,
> then every promise made in the balcony scene was made in the language
> the play is about to take away.*
