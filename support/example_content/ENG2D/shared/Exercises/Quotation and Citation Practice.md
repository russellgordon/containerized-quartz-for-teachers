Conventions on [[Writing About Literature]] and [[Citing Sources]].

**1.** Fix the dropped quotation: *Juliet is trapped. "Hang, beg, starve,
die in the streets." This shows her father is cruel.*

> [!success]- Answer 1
> *Capulet answers his daughter's refusal with a list of outcomes rather
> than an argument — "hang, beg, starve, die in the streets" (3.5.194) —
> and the absence of any conditional in the line is what makes it a
> threat rather than a warning.*

**2.** Quote two lines of verse inside your own sentence, correctly
punctuated.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> *Romeo greets the balcony with a question he does not want answered:
> "But soft, what light through yonder window breaks? / It is the east,
> and Juliet is the sun" (2.2.2–3).*
> Slash for the line break, line numbers in brackets, full stop outside.

**3.** Correct the tense: *Rice showed the community as prepared, and
Evan decided to go hunting.*

> [!success]- Answer 3
> *Rice shows the community as prepared, and Evan decides to go hunting.*
> Present tense for the text; past only for the world outside it.

**4.** This citation is wrong three ways: *(Rice, page 71, Moon of the
Crusted Snow)*.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> *(Rice 71)* — surname and page, no comma, no "page", no title. The
> title belongs in the Works Cited entry.

**5.** Write a Works Cited entry for the novel and for the play.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Rice, Waubgeshig. *Moon of the Crusted Snow*. ECW Press, 2018.
> Shakespeare, William. *Romeo and Juliet*. Edited by Roma Gill, Oxford
> University Press, 2001.

**6.** Take one claim from your current draft and find the shortest
quotation that proves it.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> No fixed answer. The test: if your quotation is longer than your
> analysis of it, the paragraph is not finished.
