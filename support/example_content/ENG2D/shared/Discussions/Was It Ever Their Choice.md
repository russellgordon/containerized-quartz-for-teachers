## The question

How much of *Romeo and Juliet* is chosen by Romeo and Juliet?

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Character | They are impulsive, secretive, and fast; a slower pair survives | Thirteen and sixteen, in a city where adults settle disputes with swords |
| Structure | The feud, the arranged marriage, and the law make the ending nearly inevitable | The play gives them several exits they do not take |
| Accident | A quarantined messenger and forty minutes of timing | Then it is not a tragedy, it is bad luck |
| The adults | The Prologue blames the parents, and so does the Prince | Then why is the play named for the children? |

## The evidence to bring

- The Prologue's word "star-cross'd", and whether the play means it.
- Capulet's threats to Juliet in 3.5 — read them aloud and ask what
  choice she is being offered.
- The Friar's plan, and every point at which telling the truth was
  available.
- Mercutio's death, and whether Romeo could have walked away.

## The age question

Juliet is thirteen. If the play makes her a person capable of choosing,
what is it saying about her father? If it does not, what is it saying
about the ending? You cannot have both, and the attempt to have both is
where the best arguments in this room start.
