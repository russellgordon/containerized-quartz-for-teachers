## The question

In [[Moon of the Crusted Snow]], the leadership decides what the
community is told and when. Were they right?

## The cases from the novel

1. Not announcing the scale of the failure immediately.
2. Rationing the food supply without a public vote.
3. Deciding who is allowed to stay.
4. What is finally done about Scott.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tell everyone everything | Adults can handle truth; secrecy breeds rumour and rumour is worse | Panic is real, and a community with no plan does not benefit from knowing there is no plan |
| Tell them when there is a plan | Information without options is cruelty | Who decides when that moment is, and what if it never comes? |
| The Elders decide | Their authority is not administrative, and it has survived worse | An authority that cannot be questioned is a risk however earned |
| The band council decides | It is the accountable body | Accountable to whom, when there is no outside left? |

## The context the novel insists on

More than one character points out that this community has already
survived the end of a world. That line reframes every question above,
and it is the reason a reading that treats the novel as a generic
apocalypse story misses what it is doing. See
[[Whose Reading Counts]].

## Afterwards

One paragraph: the decision you would have made differently, with the
passage that convinced you. Bring it to your seminar —
[[The Seminar]] rewards a claim somebody can push back on.
