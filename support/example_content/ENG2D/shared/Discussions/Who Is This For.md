## The question

An advertisement that tells the truth, beautifully, in order to sell you
something you do not need: craft, or manipulation?

## Bring

The three advertisements you chose for [[The Media Deconstruction]] —
the ones that work on you, not the ones you find stupid.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It is craft | Persuasion is legitimate; every text wants something | Not every text hides what it wants |
| It is manipulation | Techniques designed to bypass thought are not honest, whatever the claim says | By that standard, so is a good essay |
| It depends on the audience | Advertising to adults differs from advertising to children | Where is the line, and who draws it? |
| It depends on the product | Selling shoes and selling vapes are not the same act | Then the ethics are about the product, not the technique |

## The turn, halfway through

We watch one advertisement with the sound off, then the sound alone, then
both. Almost everybody's reading changes. Then we ask: if a technique
works on you *after* you have named it, what does that tell you about
the "just be aware of it" defence?

## Afterwards

Write the standard you would actually apply, in three sentences: what
advertising should be allowed to do, what it should not, and how you
would tell the difference in a specific case.
