The compound microscope is the instrument of this course, and using it
badly wastes a whole period.

## The routine that works

1. **Start at the lowest power.** Always. Finding anything at 400× is
   luck; finding it at 40× and increasing is method.
2. **Coarse focus only on low power.** On high power the coarse knob
   drives the objective through the slide.
3. **Centre before you increase.** Whatever you want stays in view only
   if it was in the middle.
4. **Adjust the light with the diaphragm**, not by squinting. Too much
   light washes out unstained material completely.

## Magnification and field of view

Total magnification is ocular × objective — usually 10× × 40× = 400×. As
magnification goes up, the field of view goes DOWN, which is why finding
things at high power is so hard.

Measure the field diameter once at low power against a ruler. Then you can
estimate the size of anything by what fraction of the field it fills.

## Wet mounts

A drop, the specimen, then the cover slip lowered at an angle from one
edge — dropping it flat traps air bubbles, and a bubble looks convincingly
like a cell to a hopeful eye.

> [!warning] The two ways slides get broken
> Coarse focus on high power, and carrying a microscope one-handed. Both
> avoidable; both happen every year.
