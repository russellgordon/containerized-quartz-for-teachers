This course works with enzymes, buffers, stains, microorganisms, and
occasionally with your own physiology.

## Before every investigation

- [ ] Read the procedure, including disposal.
- [ ] Know where the eyewash, first-aid kit, sharps container, and
      biohazard bin are.
- [ ] Goggles and apron whenever a chemical or a culture is open.
- [ ] Gloves for stains, cultures, and anything biological.

## The four that matter most here

| Hazard | Where | What we do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microbial cultures | Fermentation, enzyme work | Sealed once inoculated; autoclaved before disposal; nothing reopened |
| Chemicals | Buffers, hydrogen peroxide, indicators | Labelled, capped, and never returned to the stock bottle |
| Glassware and sharps | Everywhere | Broken glass in the sharps bin, reported not hidden |
| Your own body | Exercise and heart-rate work | Anyone may sit out any physiological activity, without explaining why |

> [!danger] Cultures are not opened
> A sealed plate stays sealed. Not for a photograph, not to smell it, not
> because something interesting grew. Interesting is exactly the case
> where it stays shut.

What to have with you on a lab day is on [[What to Bring]].

## Human subjects

Some investigations use your own heart rate or breathing. Participation is
voluntary and private: opting out has no effect on your mark, and nobody
is asked to explain a medical reason to a class.
