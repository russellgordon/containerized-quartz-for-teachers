> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · three hours, in the examination period · written, with a
> calculator · all five strands, weighted as the course spent its time

## What it is for

Everything else this semester was done with a partner, a bench, and time
to look things up. This is the one piece of evidence that is
unambiguously yours — and the closest thing in this course to a
first-year university assessment, met while somebody who knows you is
still marking it.

## What is on it

| Part | Roughly | What it asks you to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Biochemistry | 20% | Macromolecules and their structure–function links; enzymes, inhibition, and kinetics; membranes and transport |
| B. Metabolism | 20% | Respiration and photosynthesis stage by stage; ATP yield; chemiosmosis; what changes when a step is blocked |
| C. Molecular genetics | 25% | Replication and repair; transcription and translation; mutation consequences; gene regulation; the biotechnology toolkit |
| D. Homeostasis | 20% | Loops located precisely — receptor, control centre, effector; nervous and endocrine control; the kidney |
| E. Populations | 15% | Growth models and their assumptions; sampling; interactions between species; sustainability |

Woven through: **the skills strand**. One question hands you data you
have not seen — a rate curve, a gel, a growth series — and asks you to
process it, state a conclusion, and say what its uncertainty allows.

## What to expect, precisely

- **Mechanism, not vocabulary.** "The enzyme denatures" earns little;
  naming what heat does to the bonds holding the active site's shape
  earns full marks.
- **Diagrams are marked** on the conventions in [[Biological Drawing]],
  and pathway diagrams must be yours.
- **Calculations show their route** and carry units and significant
  figures, per [[Uncertainty and Error]].
- **One question is set in an unfamiliar context** — an organism or a
  disorder you have not studied. It is testing whether the principles
  transfer, which is the whole point of a Grade 12 course.

## How to prepare

1. **Redo problems, do not reread them.** Five from each set, cold:
   [[Biochemistry Practice]], [[Metabolism Practice]],
   [[Molecular Genetics Practice]], [[Homeostasis Practice]],
   [[Population Practice]].
2. **Redraw the pathways from memory** — respiration, photosynthesis,
   transcription and translation, one feedback loop. If you cannot draw
   it, you cannot yet explain it.
3. **Re-read your own investigations.** Your [[Biology Journal]] entries
   about what went wrong at the bench are the most efficient revision
   there is.
4. **Practise transfer.** Take a principle and apply it to an organism
   the course never mentioned. That is what the hardest question does.
5. **Bring questions to the review classes.** This page is what is on
   the examination.

> [!tip] In the three hours
> Molecular genetics is the largest part; do it while you are fresh.
> If an explanation stalls, write the mechanism you DO know — partial
> mechanisms earn partial marks and a blank space earns none.

## How this is assessed

Against the same expectations as everything else. Per [[How Marks Work]],
this examination is part of the final 30% of the course mark alongside
[[Population Study]], so that neither one afternoon nor one investigation
decides your grade alone.
