Semi-conservative, bidirectional, and asymmetric — the third of those is
where the interesting machinery lives.

## The enzymes

| Enzyme | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Helicase | Unwinds the double helix |
| Single-strand binding proteins | Keep the strands apart |
| Topoisomerase | Relieves the twist ahead of the fork |
| Primase | Lays down an RNA primer |
| DNA polymerase III | Builds the new strand, 5′ to 3′ only |
| DNA polymerase I | Replaces primers with DNA |
| Ligase | Seals the nicks |

## Why one strand is built in pieces

Polymerase only works 5′ to 3′, and the two template strands run in
opposite directions. One new strand (leading) is built continuously; the
other (lagging) is built backwards in Okazaki fragments, each needing its
own primer, and ligase joins them.

That asymmetry is not a design flaw to be explained away — it is a direct
consequence of the chemistry, and it is why the ends of chromosomes
(telomeres) shorten with every division.

## Proofreading

Polymerase checks each base as it adds it, and mismatch repair catches
more afterwards. The final error rate is about one in a billion — low
enough for stability, not zero, which is where variation comes from.
