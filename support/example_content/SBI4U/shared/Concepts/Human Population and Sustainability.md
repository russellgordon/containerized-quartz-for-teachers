The human population passed eight billion in 2022. Growth rate peaked
around 1968 and has been falling since — both facts are true, and using
only one of them is how population arguments go wrong.

## The demographic transition

1. High birth rate, high death rate, slow growth.
2. Death rate falls (sanitation, medicine), birth rate stays high — rapid
   growth.
3. Birth rate falls (education, urbanisation, child survival) — growth
   slows.
4. Both low; population stable or declining.

Most of the world is in stage 3 or 4. Where it is not, the reliable
predictors of falling birth rate are girls' education and child survival —
which is a finding with policy consequences.

## Ecological footprint

The land and water area needed to supply what a population consumes and
absorb its waste. Canada's per-person footprint is among the highest in
the world, which reframes "overpopulation" as a question about consumption
as much as about numbers.

## Carrying capacity, for us

Unlike other species, we change $K$ — agriculture, sanitation, and energy
have raised it repeatedly. Whether that can continue, and at what cost to
everything else, is what [[What Can This Planet Support?]] is for.
