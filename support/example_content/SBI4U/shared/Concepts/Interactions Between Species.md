## Predator–prey cycles

The Canadian lynx and snowshoe hare record, from Hudson's Bay Company fur
returns, is the textbook case: roughly ten-year oscillations with the
predator lagging the prey. It is also more complicated than the textbook
version — food supply and disease matter too — and saying so is better
science than the clean story.

## Competitive exclusion

Two species cannot occupy exactly the same niche indefinitely; one
outcompetes the other or they diverge. Resource partitioning — warblers
feeding at different heights in the same tree — is what divergence looks
like.

## Why introduced species are so destructive

They arrive without the predators, parasites, and competitors that limited
them at home. Zebra mussels in the Great Lakes are the local example, and
their population curve is exponential until it is not.
