## Carbohydrates

Monomers are monosaccharides — $\ce{C6H12O6}$ for glucose. Two join by a
condensation reaction, losing water, to give a disaccharide; many give a
polysaccharide.

| Polysaccharide | Where | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starch | Plants | Storage, coiled and compact |
| Glycogen | Animals | Storage, highly branched for fast release |
| Cellulose | Plant cell walls | Structure — and indigestible to us |

Starch and cellulose are both glucose polymers. The difference is the
orientation of one bond, and it is the difference between food and fibre.

## Lipids

Not polymers. A triglyceride is glycerol plus three fatty acids, joined by
condensation. Saturated chains are straight and pack tightly (solid at
room temperature); unsaturated chains have kinks from double bonds and
cannot pack (liquid).

Phospholipids have a polar head and two non-polar tails, which is why they
form bilayers in water without anything organising them — see
[[Membranes and Transport]].
