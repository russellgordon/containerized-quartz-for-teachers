The toolkit, and what each tool is actually for.

| Tool | What it does | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restriction enzymes | Cut DNA at specific sequences | Cloning, fingerprinting |
| Ligase | Joins fragments | Building recombinant DNA |
| Plasmid vectors | Carry a gene into bacteria | Insulin, growth hormone |
| PCR | Copies a target sequence exponentially | Diagnostics, forensics |
| Gel electrophoresis | Separates fragments by size | Fingerprinting, checking a clone |
| Sequencing | Reads the bases | Everything, increasingly |
| CRISPR-Cas9 | Cuts at a chosen sequence, guided by RNA | Editing, and the reason the ethics became urgent |

## Recombinant insulin, as the worked example

The human insulin gene is inserted into a plasmid, the plasmid into
*E. coli*, and the bacteria are grown in vats. Before 1982 insulin came
from pig and cow pancreases; now it does not. This is the case where the
benefit is uncontested, which makes it a useful baseline for arguing about
the cases that are not.

## What PCR needs

A heat-stable polymerase — originally from *Thermus aquaticus*, found in a
Yellowstone hot spring. A whole industry rests on an organism collected
because someone was curious about extremophiles, which is worth
remembering in arguments about funding basic research.
