Every property of water that matters to biology comes from one fact: the
molecule is polar, so it hydrogen-bonds to itself.

## What follows

| Property | Cause | Consequence for life |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High specific heat | Bonds absorb energy | Organisms and lakes resist temperature swings |
| High heat of vaporisation | Bonds must break to evaporate | Sweating cools effectively |
| Cohesion | Bonds between molecules | Water columns rise in xylem unbroken |
| Solvent for polar molecules | Charges attract charges | Cytoplasm and blood work at all |
| Ice floats | Bonds hold a lattice open | Lakes freeze from the top, life continues beneath |

## Hydrophilic and hydrophobic

Polar and charged molecules dissolve; non-polar ones do not, and cluster
away from water instead. That clustering is not a force pushing them
together — it is water excluding them — and it is what folds proteins and
builds membranes.

Almost every structure in this unit is water arranging things.
