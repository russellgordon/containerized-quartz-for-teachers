**1.** Explain why ice floats, and give one biological consequence.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules in an open lattice when frozen, so
> ice is less dense than liquid water. Lakes freeze from the top down, so
> organisms survive beneath the ice.

**2.** Starch and cellulose are both glucose polymers. Why can you digest
one and not the other?

> [!success]- Answer 2
> The glycosidic bonds differ in orientation. Human enzymes fit the bonds
> in starch and not those in cellulose — an active-site shape argument.

**3.** A protein is heated to 80 °C and loses its function. Which level of
structure was lost first, and why does the primary structure survive?

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Tertiary (and secondary), held by relatively weak interactions. Primary
> structure is held by covalent peptide bonds, which survive.

**4.** Why does a phospholipid form a bilayer in water without anything
arranging it?

> [!success]- Answer 4
> The polar heads interact favourably with water and the non-polar tails
> are excluded by it, so the lowest-energy arrangement puts tails inward.

**5.** A competitive inhibitor's effect can be overcome by adding more
substrate; a non-competitive inhibitor's cannot. Why?

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Competitive inhibitors compete for the active site, so more substrate
> out-competes them. Non-competitive inhibitors bind elsewhere and change
> the enzyme's shape, so extra substrate does not help.
