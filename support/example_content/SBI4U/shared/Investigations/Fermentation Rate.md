> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Groups of three.** Yeast, sugar, and gas you can
> count.

Yeast respiring anaerobically produce carbon dioxide. Count the bubbles,
or measure the gas, and you have a rate.

## Safety

Cultures are sealed once inoculated and are not opened. Warm water baths
only — no open flames near ethanol production.

## Method

1. Fixed yeast suspension, fixed volume, fixed temperature.
2. Vary the sugar: glucose, sucrose, lactose, starch, and a no-sugar
   control.
3. Measure gas produced in ten minutes, three trials each.

## Analysis

1. Rank the sugars. Which produced no gas, and why not? (The answer is
   about enzymes, not about the sugar being unusable in principle.)
2. Write the equation for alcoholic fermentation and identify what the gas
   is.
3. Compare your yields with what aerobic respiration would give from the
   same glucose — see [[Cellular Respiration]]. Express it as a ratio.
4. Design a follow-up that would show whether temperature or sugar type
   matters more.
