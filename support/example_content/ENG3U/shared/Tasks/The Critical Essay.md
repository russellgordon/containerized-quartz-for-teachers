## The task

One argument about *Macbeth*, proved with four passages, with the
strongest counter-reading engaged for a full paragraph.

## Choosing the question

From your own annotations, not from a list. Questions that have produced
strong essays:

- What does the play do with the fact that Banquo hears the same
  prophecy and does nothing?
- The marriage is grammatical: they finish each other's lines until they
  do not. What does the play argue by ending that?
- Is equivocation a theme in this play or its method?
- Lady Macbeth's last scene is in prose. What does that change?

## Structure

Thesis with a mechanism; body paragraphs ordered by argument rather than
by act; a counter-reading paragraph that takes the other side seriously;
a conclusion that says what follows. The detail is on [[The Essay]].

## How it is built

| Period | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Pattern from annotations; question drafted |
| 2 | Conference with me; passages gathered and paraphrased |
| 3 | Outline, including the counter-reading you will engage |
| 4 | Draft |
| 5 | Workshop: partner marks unsupported claims and the weakest paragraph |
| 6 | Rewrite; quotations and citations checked |

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The play is known accurately, including where your argument is inconvenient |
| Thinking | The thesis has a mechanism; the counter-reading is the real one and is answered |
| Communication | Ordered, cohesive, and readable at speed |
| Application | Conventions of literary writing, including verse quotation, used correctly |
