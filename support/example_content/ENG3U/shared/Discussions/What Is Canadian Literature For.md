## The question

Is "Canadian literature" a useful category, or a marketing label with a
syllabus attached?

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It is a real tradition | Shared preoccupations: land, arrival, survival, the state | Those preoccupations are not unique, and the "tradition" was largely assembled in the 1960s and 70s |
| It is a national project | Literature was funded and taught to build a country | Which country, built by whom, and who was left out of it? |
| It is a container for many literatures | Indigenous literatures, Québécois literature, immigrant literatures, regional writing | Then the label describes an address, not a kind |
| It is useful for teaching and nothing else | It puts specific books in front of students | Which is not nothing — but say so honestly |

## The evidence in this course

Look at [[Reading/index|the reading page]]. A Haisla novel, a Métis poem,
a Nova Scotian short story, an argument by a writer of Cherokee and Greek
descent who lives and works here. Ask what they have in common that is
not simply the border.

## Afterwards

Write the definition you would defend, in two sentences, and one text
that breaks it. Everyone reads their second sentence aloud.
