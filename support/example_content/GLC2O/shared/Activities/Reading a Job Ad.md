One period. Everyone brings a real posting — printed, screenshotted, or
copied out — for a job someone your age could plausibly get. We take
them apart together, because a job ad is written in a code that nobody
ever teaches you, and once you can read it, applying gets much easier.

## The three passes

**First pass — what the job is.** Underline the tasks. Ignore the
adjectives entirely for now. Many postings describe an atmosphere for
four lines and the actual work in one.

**Second pass — what they are worried about.** Every requirement exists
because something went wrong once. "Must be available weekends" means
they cannot staff weekends. "Attention to detail" attached to a cash
role means someone's till was out. Read the requirements as a list of
the employer's problems, and your application becomes a list of reasons
those problems will not recur.

**Third pass — what it would cost you.** Hours, location, travel time,
whether the shifts collide with school or with responsibilities you
already have. This is the pass students skip and then regret.

> [!quote] A posting, and what it is actually saying
> "Busy neighbourhood café seeks part-time team member. Evenings and
> weekends. Fast-paced environment. Must be reliable, work well under
> pressure, and be comfortable with point-of-sale systems. Food handler
> certification an asset."
>
> Translated: they are short-staffed at the times nobody wants; someone
> recently did not show up; the till is not the hard part but errors on
> it are visible; and they will train you on food safety, but if you
> arrive certified you go to the top of the pile.

## The factors that are not about you

Sort your posting's demands into two columns: things about your skills,
and everything else — the hours, the transit route, the uniform cost,
whether you would be the only person your age there, whether it fits
around family responsibilities. Both columns are legitimate reasons to
apply or not apply, and being able to name the second column is part of
making an honest choice.

## Finish with a matching list

Beside each requirement, write the evidence from your inventory that
meets it, or write "gap". Gaps are useful information — some can be
closed before you apply, and some tell you this is not your posting.
Bring your annotated ad to [[The Mock Application]].
