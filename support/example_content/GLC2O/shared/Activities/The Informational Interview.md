You talk to one person who does work you are curious about, for fifteen
minutes, and you are not asking them for a job. That last part is what
makes it work. People who will not hire you will very often talk to you,
and what they say is worth more than any careers website.

## Finding someone

Start with people already connected to you: family, family friends,
neighbours, a coach, someone a teacher knows, a former employer. If
nothing turns up, ask in class — between thirty students there is
usually a route to most occupations in town. Your teacher can help with
the approach, and for some workplaces will make the first contact.

## Asking

Say who you are, what you are curious about, and how long you want.
Fifteen minutes is a real ask; "can I pick your brain" is not. By email,
text, or in person, the whole message is four sentences.

## The questions

Six is plenty, and the good ones are about the actual days rather than
the job title.

1. What did you actually do yesterday?
2. What part of this job did nobody warn you about?
3. How did you get here — what was the route, including the detours?
4. What skills do you use every day that you did not expect to?
5. What is changing in this work right now?
6. If you were fifteen and interested in this, what would you do next?

Leave room for the answer to change your next question. A list read out
in order is a survey, not a conversation.

> [!example] What a fifteen-minute answer sounds like
> A paramedic asked question two said the part nobody warns you about is
> paperwork and waiting, and that the calm you need is not the calm you
> see on television — it is the calm to write accurately at three in the
> morning. That is a transferable skill, named by someone doing the job,
> and it is not on any careers site.

## Afterwards

Within a day, write half a page: what they said, what surprised you, and
what it changes about what you thought you wanted. Send a thank-you
message — three lines, naming one specific thing you took from it.
People remember that, and you may want to talk to them again.
