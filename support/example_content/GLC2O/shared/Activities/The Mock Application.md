Two periods. You apply for the posting you annotated in
[[Reading a Job Ad]], someone else reads your application as the
employer would, and then you find out what actually happened when they
read it. Nothing here is marked; [[The Application]] is where it counts,
and this is the rehearsal that makes that one better.

## Period one — write it

Two documents, both short:

- **A résumé**, one page. Contact details, then experience, then
  education, then skills. Every entry earns its space by matching
  something in the posting.
- **A covering message**, four short paragraphs or less. Why this job,
  what you bring with a specific example, what you would need to learn,
  and how to reach you.

Write for this posting only. A general application reads as general, and
the person reading it has forty others.

## Period two — the employer's read

You get someone else's application and thirty seconds — that is roughly
what a first pass gets in real life. Sort it into "interview", "maybe",
and "no", then write down the one thing that put it where it went.

Then read it properly and answer three questions for the writer:

1. Which claim would you have questioned in an interview?
2. Which line told you something a hundred other applicants could not
   have written?
3. What would you have cut?

## Say why this posting

Before you finish, write three lines that nobody else will see: why
*this* job, honestly. Money, hours that fit around school, a bus route,
a family expectation, someone you know already works there, a sector you
want to test — all of these are real reasons, and they are not
embarrassing. Knowing which ones are driving you is what stops you
taking a job that suits your résumé and not your life.

> [!success]- One sentence, before and after
> Before: "I am a hard-working and dedicated individual who is
> passionate about customer service."
>
> After: "I covered the Saturday morning rush at the arena canteen for
> two seasons, on my own from seven, and the float balanced every week."
>
> The second sentence says hard-working without using the word, and it
> can be checked. That is the whole trick, and
> [[Writing About Yourself Without Cringing]] has the rest of it.
