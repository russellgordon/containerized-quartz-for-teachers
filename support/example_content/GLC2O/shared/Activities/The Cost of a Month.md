One period with a spreadsheet, plus whatever looking-up you do before
it. You cost out a single ordinary month of the life you are planning
for — not a year, not a fantasy, one month — and every number in it has
to come from somewhere you can name.

## The rule about numbers

No number goes in the sheet without a source beside it. A rental
listing, a transit fare page, a grocery receipt, an actual bill, a
program's own fee page. "About four hundred, probably" is not a number;
it is a feeling with a digit attached. Where you genuinely cannot find
out, write down what you would have to do to find out, and keep going.

Prices differ by town and change every year, which is why this page
contains no figures. The skill is the looking up.

## The categories

| Category | Examples | Where the number comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Housing | Rent, residence fee, share of utilities, or \$0 if you are at home — say so | A listing, a residence fee page, or the actual bill |
| Food | Groceries, and eating out counted honestly | Two weeks of your household's real receipts, doubled |
| Getting around | Transit pass, fuel, insurance, a bike that needs fixing | The transit site; an insurance quote if a car is in the plan |
| Phone and internet | Plan, data, the thing you forgot you subscribe to | Your current bill |
| Study or training | Tuition, fees, tools, books, safety boots, a laptop | The institution's or employer's own fee list |
| Everything else | Clothing, haircuts, gifts, going out, medication, savings | Your own spending, tracked for one week |

## The income side

List what would come in, net rather than gross, and mark each line as
certain, likely, or hoped-for. Wages, grants, bursaries, loans, family
support, and money you already have saved all belong here, and the
marking matters: a budget that balances only on the hoped-for lines does
not balance.

## Then subtract

If money is left over, decide now where it goes, because undirected
surplus disappears. If it is short, you have four moves and you should
name which one you would take: earn more, spend less, borrow, or change
the plan. Changing the plan is not defeat — finding out in Grade 10 that
a plan needs a different shape is the cheapest possible time to find out.

Bring your sheet to [[The Budget]], where it becomes a full year.
