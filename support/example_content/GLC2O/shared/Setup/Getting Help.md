Ask early. In a ten-week course, a question left for two weeks has
already cost a fifth of the course, and almost everything that becomes a
problem here started as something small that nobody said out loud.

## Reaching me about the course

- **Before or after class**, any day. Best for quick questions and "can
  I check that I have understood this?"
- **Help sessions**, at the posted times, when you want to work on
  something with me in the room.
- **A booked conversation**, any week, for anything that needs longer
  than five minutes.
- **Email**, any time, with replies on school days. Best when you want
  the answer in writing.

Bring something with you if you can — the draft, however rough, or the
question you cannot answer. A page of writing gets a much more useful
response than a description of the page you intend to write.

## Help with the course-planning parts

Guidance holds things I do not: your credit count, what the graduation
requirements mean for you specifically, which specialised programs this
school actually runs, and how to change a course selection. When a
question in this course turns out to be a guidance question, I will say
so and help you make the appointment.

## Help with things outside the course

If something outside class is making school hard — health, family, work,
money, housing — you can say so without giving me the details. I do not
need them. What I can do is make sure the right person knows and that
deadlines stop being the immediate problem.

For mental health specifically, this school has staff whose job it is,
and there are services and helplines outside school, including ones you
can text and ones that are free and open outside school hours. The
current list — who they are, where they are, how to reach them — is
posted in the room and handed out in the first week, because names,
numbers, and hours change and a page like this one would go out of date.
Ask me, ask guidance, or take the sheet without asking anyone. Any of
those is fine, and no explanation is required.

> [!tip] You can ask on someone else's behalf
> If you are worried about a friend, tell an adult. You do not need to
> be sure, you do not need permission, and you will not get anyone into
> trouble. Being worried and wrong costs nothing; being worried and
> silent can cost a lot.

## If you have fallen behind

Come and say so, and bring your list of what is outstanding even if the
list is long. We will decide what actually has to be done, in what
order, and by when — which is usually a shorter list than it feels like
from the inside.

> [!important] Three lines that work anywhere in Canada
> National, free, and open at any hour:
>
> - **9-8-8 — Suicide Crisis Helpline**: call or text 988. You can ask
>   for services meant for young people, and for Indigenous responders.
> - **Kids Help Phone**: 1-800-668-6868, or text CONNECT to 686868.
> - **Hope for Wellness Helpline**, for First Nations, Inuit, and Métis
>   callers, with responders trained in cultural humility: 1-855-242-3310.
