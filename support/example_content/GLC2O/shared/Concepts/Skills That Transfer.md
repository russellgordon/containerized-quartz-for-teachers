Almost everything you can do right now, you learned somewhere other than
a job. That is not something to apologise for in an interview. A skill
that only works in the room where you learned it is not much use to
anyone; the ones worth naming are the ones that move.

## Moving between school, life, and work

| Skill | Where you built it | Where it turns up again |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adapting to change | A new school, a new timetable, a coach moving you to a different position | The schedule changes, the software changes, the manager changes |
| Persevering | Finishing something after the first attempt went badly | A problem you have to see through to the end of the shift |
| Learning from a mistake | Asking why you lost marks, then doing it differently | A correction from a supervisor in your first week |
| Handling a setback | Not making the team, and going back next year | Not getting the job, and asking what would have helped |
| Asking for help | Going to a teacher after a disappointing result | Telling a manager you have not been trained on something |

Read that table in both directions. The left column is evidence you
already have, and the right column is where an employer will need it.
That is the whole argument for [[The Skills Inventory]] being the first
thing we do.

## What resilience actually means

Resilience gets used as though it means never being bothered by
anything. It does not. It means noticing when something has knocked you
sideways, and having somewhere to go next — a person to ask, a strategy
that has worked before, a smaller version of the task you can start on.

Perseverance is the same idea stretched over time. It is not a
personality you either have or do not have. It is a set of decisions
you make repeatedly, and they get easier with practice, which is exactly
what [[Habits That Actually Help]] is about.

## Some things are not yours to adapt to

There is a limit to this, and it is worth saying plainly. If you are
dealing with racism, harassment, or a workplace that is unsafe, the
answer is not a better coping strategy. Those are things other people
are responsible for fixing, and there are people whose job it is to act
on them. Knowing the difference — between what is inside your control
and what is not — is part of the skill, not a failure of it.
