The jobs your grandparents had, your parents had, and you will have are
not the same jobs, and the differences are not random. Three forces keep
showing up — technology, the economy, and how society is organised — and
between them they change what work exists, how it is done, and what
protections come with it.

## What changes the work itself

Automation, robotics, and artificial intelligence take over tasks rather
than whole occupations, which is why the honest question is not "will my
job exist" but "which parts of it will I still be doing". Demand grows
for people who can do the parts machines are bad at, and for skilled
trades who can work with the new equipment. Manufacturing, agriculture,
and construction are now technology-heavy fields, which surprises people
who last looked at them twenty years ago.

An aging population is a second force, and a large one. More people
retiring means both vacancies and rising demand for health care and
support work.

## What changes how the work is done

Remote and hybrid work made distance less relevant to some jobs and not
at all to others. Platform and gig work made it easier to start earning
and harder to count on the earnings. Flexible hours, compressed weeks,
and parental leave are more available than they were, unevenly.

## What changes the rules

Short-term and gig work has grown faster than the rules covering it.
Fewer people hold one job for a career; more people are technically
self-employed and carry costs an employer used to carry — equipment,
time between contracts, and the gaps in employment standards coverage.
Knowing your rights as a worker is now something you have to look up
rather than inherit.

## Where transferable skills come in

This is the practical reason [[Skills That Transfer]] matters. If the
tasks keep moving, the thing worth building is the set of skills that
move with them — communication, collaboration, critical thinking,
self-directed learning, technological fluency. You build those in
classrooms, teams, clubs, part-time jobs, and volunteering, and they
count as evidence in all four places.[^lmi]

[^lmi]: Ontario publishes current labour market information, including
