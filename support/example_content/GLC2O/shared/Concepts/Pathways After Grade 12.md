There are five destinations named in Ontario policy, and they are named
in a deliberate order that is not a ranking: apprenticeship training,
college, community living, university, and the workplace. This course
treats them that way. A plan to start work at eighteen is a plan; a plan
to apprentice is a plan; a plan to take a year and find out is a plan,
provided you can say what you will do with the year.

## The five, and where to look

| Destination | What the first year looks like | Start by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apprenticeship | Mostly paid work with a sponsoring employer, plus scheduled in-school training | Finding out whether your school runs OYAP, and which trades it covers |
| College | Programs from one year to four, often with placements and specific admission requirements | Reading two program pages in full — [[Researching a Pathway\|properly, not skimmed]] |
| Community living | Supported work, learning, and participation, planned with the supports you already have | Asking who at your school handles transition planning |
| University | Three- to five-year degrees, admission usually based on Grade 12 courses | Checking which Grade 11 courses the Grade 12 prerequisites need |
| The workplace | Employment straight out of school, with training happening on the job | Naming the sector, then the employers in it near you |

Entrepreneurship, volunteering, and a gap year sit alongside these
rather than underneath them, and most people will use more than one over
a lifetime.

## Pathways are what get you there

A destination is where you are going. A pathway is the courses,
programs, and experiences between here and there. In Grade 10 the
pathway question is mostly a course-selection question, because Grade 11
courses decide which Grade 12 courses you can take, which decides what
you can apply to.

Specialised routes are worth knowing about now, not in Grade 12:
cooperative education, the Ontario Youth Apprenticeship Program, a
Specialist High Skills Major, and dual credits taken at a college while
still in secondary school. Which of these your school offers is a
question with a specific answer, and guidance has it.

## Doors you can leave open

Almost every pathway can be changed later, but some changes cost a year
and some cost nothing. The cheap insurance is to keep prerequisite
courses you are close to qualifying for, even if you are unsure, and to
know that colleges and universities both admit mature students and
transfer students. Ask the question "what would I need if I changed my
mind in two years" once, now, and write down the answer.
