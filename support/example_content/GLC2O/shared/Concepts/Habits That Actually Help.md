Nobody decides once to be organised. What people actually have is three
or four small routines that run without being decided on again, and the
routines are doing the work that intentions get the credit for.

## After a result you did not want

The habit is: find out why, within two days. Ask the teacher what would
have moved it up, write the answer down somewhere you will see it, and
change one thing on the next piece of work. Two days matters — after a
week the feedback is abstract, and after a month it is gone.

This is the same move you will make at work when a supervisor corrects
you, and the same one when an application is turned down. Ask what
would have made the difference. Most people never ask, which is why
asking is worth so much.

## Before something is due

Start it badly, early. A rough version of a thing, written on the day it
is assigned, tells you what you do not yet understand while there is
still time to find out. Starting well, later, is how work gets handed in
unfinished.

## When you are stuck

Say so out loud to a specific person, with a specific question. "I do
not get it" is a fine opening, and it goes much further with "here is
the part I tried and here is where it stopped making sense" attached.
Speaking up for yourself — describing what you need clearly enough that
someone can supply it — is a skill you will use at every job you ever
have.

## When you are new

New is the condition you will be in on your first day of every job. The
habits that carry you through it are small: arrive before you have to,
write down names, ask about the thing you were shown once, and take on
the task nobody offered you. Curiosity reads as competence long before
you are actually competent.

> [!question]- Which of these is a habit, and which is a mood?
> "I will care more this term" is a mood — nothing about tomorrow is
> different because you thought it. "I write down what is due at the end
> of every class" is a habit, because it names a time and an action. If
> you cannot say when it happens, it will not happen. Turn one of your
> intentions into that shape before you leave today.
