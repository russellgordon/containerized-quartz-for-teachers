**Unit 2. Individual. Due at the end of week nine.** A complete
application package, plus one page of explanation.

A real posting, a real application, and an honest account of why that
posting and not another one. You do not have to send it. You do have to
write it as though you would.

## Choose the opportunity

Any of these counts, and none is a lesser choice: a job, an
apprenticeship or pre-apprenticeship opening, a co-op or volunteer
placement, a summer program, a scholarship or award, or admission to a
program you would apply to in Grade 12. Pick one you researched in
[[Researching a Pathway]] or found in [[Reading a Job Ad]].

## What to hand in

1. **The posting or the program's own requirements**, copied and dated.
2. **A one-page résumé**, targeted at that posting.
3. **A covering message** — letter, email, or application statement,
   whichever the opportunity actually asks for.
4. **A one-page explanation.** Why this opportunity, what in your
   research made it credible, and — the part that matters — which
   factors other than your skills and interests shaped the choice. Money,
   location, transit, family expectations, who else works there, what
   you can afford to train for. Name them.
5. **Your online presence**, checked against the entry in
   [[Evidence of What You Can Do]]: what an employer would find, and
   what you changed before submitting.

## What earns the marks

- **Targeting.** Every line of the résumé earns its place against this
  posting.
- **Evidence over adjectives.** Specific situations, checkable, in your
  own voice.
- **Research showing.** Something in the covering message could only
  have been written by someone who looked this organisation up.
- **Honesty about factors.** The explanation names real influences
  rather than the flattering ones.
- **Care with information.** Nothing shared that should not be, and a
  presence you would be content for them to find.

> [!example] What "research showing" looks like in one line
> Weak: "I have always been interested in your company." Strong: "You
> have added evening hours since September, and evenings are when I am
> available and when I have worked before." One of those could be sent
> to any employer; the other could only be sent to this one.
