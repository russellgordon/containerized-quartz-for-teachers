**The final evaluation. Worth 30% of the course.** Two and a half hours
of scheduled time in the last week, most of it spent listening to other
people. Your own defence is about twelve minutes.

You present [[The Pathway Plan]] and walk through
[[Your Career Portfolio]], to an adult who is not your teacher.

## Who you present to

A guidance counsellor, an administrator, a teacher from another
department, a co-op employer, a community partner, or a family member
you have arranged in advance. Your teacher will confirm who is
available; if you want to bring someone, ask early rather than late.

The reason it is not your teacher is that your teacher already knows
your reasoning. Explaining a plan to someone with no context is a
harder and much more useful test, and it is the situation you will
actually be in when you apply for something.

## The twelve minutes

- **Four minutes — the plan.** Where you are going, why that, and what
  the next three steps are. Not read aloud.
- **Three minutes — the evidence.** Two or three things from your
  portfolio, chosen because they support what you just claimed. Say what
  each one demonstrates.
- **Two minutes — the money.** What the first year costs and how it gets
  paid for, including where it is tight.
- **Three minutes — questions.** Theirs, not scripted.

## What is being assessed

- **Communication.** Clear, audible, and pitched at someone who has
  never heard any of this. No jargon from this course unless you explain
  it.
- **Selection.** You chose the right evidence for the claim, rather than
  showing everything you have.
- **Reasoning.** The plan follows from what you know about yourself and
  from what you found out, and you can say which is which.
- **Reflection.** You can describe how your goals changed over the ten
  weeks, what changed them, and what you now know about how you set
  goals and make decisions.
- **Composure under a real question.** Not having an answer is fine.
  "I don't know, and here is how I would find out" is a good answer.

## Preparing for it

The last three periods are for this: rehearsals in threes, with the
listener asking one hard question each time. Rehearse the opening
sentence and the answer to "why that and not something else", because
those two carry most of the twelve minutes.

> [!tip] Practise being asked something you did not plan for
> Every rehearsal, have your partner ask a question you have not
> prepared. The point is not to have an answer ready — it is to get used
> to thinking out loud in front of someone, which is what an interview
> is and what most of this is preparation for.
