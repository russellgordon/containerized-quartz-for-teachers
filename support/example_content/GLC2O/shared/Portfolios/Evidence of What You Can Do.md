This entry is about the version of you that exists online, whether or
not you built it deliberately. Employers and admissions staff do look;
what they find is either a blank, something you assembled, or something
assembled by accident out of everything you have ever posted.

## The audit

Search your own name, in a browser you are not signed in to, and write
down what the first page of results contains. Then list every account
you hold, including the dormant ones, and note for each whether it is
public, who can see it, and whether you would be comfortable with a
manager seeing it.

This is not a lecture about deleting your life. It is that a passive
digital footprint — the trail you leave without meaning to — and an
active one you built on purpose are different things, and most people
have only the first.[^footprint]

[^footprint]: Your passive footprint is what accumulates as you browse,
shop, and are tagged by other people. Your active footprint is what you
post deliberately. Both are visible; only one is chosen.

## The build

Then make something small and deliberate. Any of these counts:

- A one-page profile of your skills and experience, in a document you
  control.
- A public profile on a career-oriented network, if you are old enough
  for the platform's terms and choose to use one.
- A folder of work you are willing to show — photographs of things you
  built, code, art, writing, a coaching record.

Complete beats clever. An unfinished profile with two lines and no photo
says something you did not mean to say.

## The privacy half

Managing what you share is part of the same skill, not the opposite of
it. Some things never go online anywhere: your social insurance number,
banking details, PINs, your address, your timetable, and photographs of
documents. Use different passwords for accounts that matter, and check
what a site's own policy says it can do with what you give it.

Be as careful with other people's information as with your own. What you
post about a workplace, a co-worker, or a customer belongs to them.

## The entry itself

Half a page: what your search found, what you changed, what you built,
and one line on the difference between what you want visible and what
you want private. That distinction is the point, and it is what makes
this an argument rather than a settings checklist.
