> [!abstract] At a glance
> Solo or pairs · three desk periods in one week, late in the term ·
> one print front page and one site home screen, built from the
> term's real published stories

## What you are making

The publication itself. You take the term's real stories — bylines,
photographs, and all — and design how readers meet them: a front page
for the paper, and a home screen for the site. Same stories, two very
different containers, and every choice is an argument about what
matters most.

Hierarchy is the heart of it. Something leads and something follows,
and you must be able to say why — that is [[News Values|news
judgement]] made visible in ink and pixels.

## How to work

1. Choose the stories. Not your favourites — the strongest mix for a
   reader arriving cold. Write one sentence defending each choice.
2. Sketch the hierarchy on paper first: what leads, what supports,
   where the eye lands and where it goes next, using
   [[Design Principles]] as your vocabulary.
3. Build the print page in [[Build the Front Page]], applying what
   [[Layout Practice]] drilled — grids, alignment, breathing room —
   and set headlines and body with care, per [[Typography]].
4. Sharpen every headline until it earns its size, and complete every
   caption — no photo runs anonymous or unexplained.
5. Build the home screen variant, then compare the two honestly using
   [[Audiences and Platforms]]: what does print do better, what does
   the screen do better, and what did you change between them?
6. Post both where the newsroom can critique them, and revise once.

The desk periods are class time, and using them well is part of the
task — [[How Marks Work]] explains how.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your layouts |
| --- | --- |
| Argued hierarchy | You can defend what leads, in one breath, out loud |
| Design with intent | Alignment, contrast, and space are choices, not luck |
| Sharpened headlines | Each one is true, specific, and sized to its story |
| Complete captions | Every image says who, what, and when — correctly |
| Honest comparison | Print and site variants differ for stated reasons |
| Accuracy | Names and facts survived the move into the layout |
| Consent | Every face on the page was cleared when first published |
| Credit | Every story and photo carries its maker's name |

## Reflect

Write a [[Newsroom Journal]] entry: which story did you demote, and
what did it cost you to do it? Editing a page means disappointing
someone — usually yourself — for the reader's sake.

> [!success]- If the page feels crowded (click to expand)
> The fix is nearly always subtraction. Remove the weakest element
> entirely — not shrink it, remove it — and let the survivors breathe.
> A front page is what you chose, made visible by what you cut.
