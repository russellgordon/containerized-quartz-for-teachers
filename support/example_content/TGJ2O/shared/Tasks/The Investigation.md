> [!abstract] At a glance
> Pairs or trios · six desk periods plus field reporting, spread over
> four weeks mid-term · one long-form story plus one audio or video
> element, published together

## What you are making

The community beat: an answer to a question your community actually
asks. Where does the recycling really go? Why is the crosswalk timing
so short? What happened to the old rink? You pick the question, define
it precisely, and report it until you can answer it — with interviews,
with documents, and with every claim verified.

You publish long-form plus one audio or video element — an interview
clip, a narrated walk of the site, a short explainer. People affected
by the answer get a voice in it — not a courtesy, the job.

## How to work

1. Pitch the question in a [[One-Minute Pitch]] — the test is that a
   neighbour would want the answer. Define what evidence it takes.
2. Map your sources: who would know, what documents exist, and who is
   affected. Plan more sources than you need — some will fall through.
3. Report. Conduct interviews per [[Interviewing]], gather documents,
   and get [[Releases and Consent|consent]] on the record every time.
   [[Free Press, School Press]] explains where the lines are.
4. Verify everything against [[Misinformation and Verification]] — a
   claim you cannot trace to a source does not go in the story.
5. Give affected parties the chance to respond, and make the version
   you publish beat the convenient version.
6. Write long-form in the voice of [[Writing for Publication]], build
   the audio or video element, and publish them together.

The desk periods are class time for planning, verifying, and editing,
and using them well is part of the task — [[How Marks Work]] explains.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your investigation |
| --- | --- |
| A real question | Defined precisely, and your community actually asks it |
| Evidence, plural | Interviews AND documents, from more than one source |
| Everything verified | Every claim in the story traces to a named source |
| Voices heard | Affected people speak; anyone challenged could reply |
| Accuracy | Names, numbers, and quotes checked before publication |
| Consent | Every interview and recognisable face cleared on record |
| Credit | Documents, images, and help are named where they appear |

## Reflect

Write a [[Newsroom Journal]] entry when you publish: which source
surprised you, and which claim was hardest to verify? An answer that
surprised its reporter is evidence of a question worth asking.

> [!success]- If your question dead-ends (click to expand)
> A closed door is a finding. "Nobody would say where the recycling
> goes" is itself a story about your community — report the silence,
> name who declined, and say what remains unknown.
