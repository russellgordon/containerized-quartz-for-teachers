Every product this newsroom makes passes through software, and the
industry uses more kinds than most people expect. You do not need to
master all of it. You need to know what each category is *for*, so that
when somebody says "we will finish it in the NLE" you know what room
they are pointing at.

## The categories, and what each one is actually for

| Category | What it does | What we use it for here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Photo editing — raster** | Edits images pixel by pixel | Correcting exposure and colour, cropping, retouching within the limits in [[When Is a Photo True]] |
| **Vector graphics** | Draws with shapes and curves that scale without blurring | Logos, mastheads, and anything printed at more than one size |
| **Page layout** | Places text and images on a page grid with precise typography | [[Build the Front Page]] |
| **Non-linear video editing (NLE)** | Assembles clips on a timeline, with cuts, titles, and colour | [[Cut a Highlight Reel]] and every video package |
| **Audio editing (DAW)** | Records and edits multitrack audio, with noise reduction and levels | Interview clean-up, podcasts, and voice-over |
| **Animation and motion graphics** | Moves type and graphics over time | Lower thirds, title cards, explainer graphics |
| **Web publishing / CMS** | Manages stories, media, and publication on the web | [[Publish to the Web]] |
| **Asset management** | Catalogues, tags, and finds media | The archive you will be grateful for in April |
| **Collaboration and scheduling** | Assignments, deadlines, and versions | The newsroom board in [[The Production Pipeline]] |

## Free and paid, and why we use both

The industry standard suites are subscription products, and the free
alternatives are genuinely capable: a raster editor, a vector editor, a
page layout program, an NLE, and a DAW all exist as free software good
enough for professional work. Two honest points:

- Employers advertise for the paid names, so meeting them matters — but
  the *skills* transfer almost completely. Somebody who can grade
  footage in one NLE learns another in a week.
- The free tools mean the work does not stop when a subscription
  lapses, and that a student can keep making things at home. That is not
  a small thing.

## Choosing one for a job

Ask three questions in order: **what does the output have to be**
(print, web, broadcast, social)? **What does the next person in the
pipeline need** (a layered file, a flat export, a specific codec)?
**What will still open in five years**? The third question is why
archives are kept in common formats, and why the working files and the
exports both get backed up in [[Backing Up Your Media]].
