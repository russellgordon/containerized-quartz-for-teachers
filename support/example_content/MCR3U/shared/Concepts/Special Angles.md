Calculators stayed in bags at the boards today, and your group still
produced exact trigonometric ratios — because two triangles you can
draw from scratch contain all of them. Exact values like
$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ are not decimals that got dressed up; they are
what the triangles actually say, before rounding throws anything
away.

## Two triangles, one table

Cut a unit square along its diagonal: a right triangle with two 45°
angles, legs 1 and 1, hypotenuse $\sqrt{2}$. Cut an equilateral
triangle of side 2 down its altitude: a right triangle with angles
30° and 60° and sides 1, $\sqrt{3}$, 2. Every entry below is just
opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse read from one of those two
pictures:

| $\theta$ | $\sin\theta$ | $\cos\theta$ | $\tan\theta$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0°$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $30°$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ |
| $45°$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $60°$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\sqrt{3}$ |
| $90°$ | $1$ | $0$ | undefined |

Do not memorise this table — *rebuild* it. Sketch the two triangles
in your margin at the start of any trig work; thirty seconds of
drawing beats a term of flashcards, and it is exactly the kind of
note your future forgetful self will thank you for.

## Past ninety degrees

Put a rotating arm of length 1 at the origin and let $\theta$ be its
angle from the positive $x$-axis. The arm's tip has coordinates
$(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ — and now every angle from 0° to 360° has
ratios, right angle or not. An angle past 90° borrows its numbers
from a *related* special angle in the first quadrant; only the signs
change, following the coordinates. So
$\sin 150° = \sin 30° = \frac{1}{2}$ (height still positive in the
second quadrant), while $\cos 135° = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ (the arm
now points left).

One consequence worth sitting with: a single ratio value belongs to
*two* angles in a full turn. If $\sin\theta = \frac{1}{2}$, then
$\theta = 30°$ or $\theta = 150°$ — the arm reaches the same height
on the way up and on the way around. That doubling has consequences
for [[The Sine Law]], and it makes lovely [[Estimation Duels]]
material: where must $\sin 50°$ sit relative to $\sin 45°$ and
$\sin 60°$?

Exact-value fluency is the opening act of
[[Trig Ratios and Laws Practice]].
