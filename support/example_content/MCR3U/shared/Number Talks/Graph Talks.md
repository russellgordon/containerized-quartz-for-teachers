A graph appears on the board with no numbers, no labels, no title —
just axes and a shape. Three questions, always in this order: what do
you notice? what do you wonder? what story could this graph be
telling? With no scale to lean on, shape is where the mathematics lives.

## How we play

1. Thirty seconds of silent looking. No hands yet.
2. Notice only what is *there*: a crest, a repeat, a flat approach.
3. Pitch a story — then point to the feature of the graph that
   each part of your story explains.

> [!example]- Three stories for one wave-shaped curve
> - "The height of one seat on a Ferris wheel. Each crest is a pass
>   over the top; the valleys are the loading platform."
> - "Water depth at a dock across two days — tide in, tide out, and
>   the middle line is the depth the water keeps returning to."
> - "It is $y = \sin x$." True to the shape, but a story with no
>   characters. We sent it back for some.

## One variation

A curve that creeps along near the axis, then climbs like it means
it. A savings account left alone? A rumour spreading? The same
picture your group will build in [[Folding Paper to the Moon]] — and
the reason it *never quite touches* the axis is a feature worth
noticing out loud.

> [!tip] Features have names
> Amplitude, period, axis of the curve, asymptote, intercepts — using
> the right name for the right feature is half of every story; that
> vocabulary is the backbone of [[Sinusoidal Functions]] and
> [[The Exponential Function]] alike.
