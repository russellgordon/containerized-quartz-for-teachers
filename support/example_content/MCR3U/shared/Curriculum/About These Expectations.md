Curriculum, Grades 11 and 12: Mathematics** (revised 2007), the Grade 11
university preparation course **MCR3U — Functions**, published by the
Ontario Ministry of Education.

> [!warning] About the codes
> The 2007 document prints the strand letters — its headings read
> "A. Characteristics of Functions" and so on — and numbers each
> strand's overall expectations `1., 2., 3.` and its specific
> expectations `1.1, 1.2, …`. What it does **not** print is the
> combined form: codes like `A1` and `B2.3`, used throughout this
> site, join the printed letter to the printed number in the style
> teachers and course outlines commonly use. They are consistent
> within this site, but you will not find the combined codes printed
> in the Ministry's pages.

Two small honesty notes about the reproduction:

- The heading for group `A3` (Determining Equivalent Algebraic
  Expressions) carries a printed footnote: *"The knowledge and skills
  described in the expectations in this section are to be introduced
  as needed, and applied and consolidated, as appropriate, in solving
  problems throughout the course."*
- The sample problem in `C2.3` is printed beside a diagram (a
  staircase pattern of grey and white linking cubes) that cannot be
  reproduced as text; a bracketed note marks its place. Typeset
  mathematics (stacked fractions, radicals, true superscripts) is
  linearized, wording untouched.

Each page keeps its code, and every lesson, exploration, and task in
this course links back to the codes it addresses. The codes are also
tags, so every page tagged `#A3` can be gathered in one place.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing
this?", the answer is a link. When a department writes a course
outline, or a teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes
are what make one person's planning legible to another.
