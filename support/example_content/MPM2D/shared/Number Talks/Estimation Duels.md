A quantity goes on the board — a distance across a grid, a slanted
area, the angle of the ramp out front — and two duellists commit to
estimates before anyone computes. Each defends a reason. Then the class
brackets: surely too low, surely too high, squeezed until cornered.

## How we play

1. Commit in writing first. A number without a reason scores nothing.
2. Defend: what did you compare it to, and which way did rounding push you?
3. Bracket as a class, then measure or calculate the reveal.

> [!example]- One duel: how far from $(2, 1)$ to $(10, 7)$? (click to expand)
> - "More than 8 — that is just the sideways part of the trip."
> - "Less than $8 + 6 = 14$ — walking the two legs of the L is the
>   long way round."
> - "It is the hypotenuse of an 8-by-6 right triangle — a 3-4-5
>   triangle scaled by 2, so exactly 10."
> - "I said 9.5 — a diagonal never beats its longer leg by much."

## One variation

Angle duels: estimate the tilt of the stair rail or the flag rope
before any tool appears. Your eyes get their verdict later, once
[[The Primary Trigonometric Ratios]] can compute what a protractor
cannot reach.

> [!tip] Bracketing is a life skill
> An answer you cannot bracket is an answer you cannot check. Naming
> "too low" and "too high" first is [[Checking Your Own Work]] done
> in advance — before the mistake instead of after it.
