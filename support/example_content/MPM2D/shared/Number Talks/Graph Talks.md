A graph appears on the board with no numbers, no labels, no title —
just axes and a shape. Three questions, always in this order: what do
you notice? what do you wonder? what story could this graph be
telling? With no scale to lean on, shape is where the mathematics lives.

## How we play

1. Thirty seconds of silent looking. No hands yet.
2. Notice only what is *there*: a peak, a crossing, a flat stretch.
3. Pitch a story — then point to the feature of the graph that
   each part of your story explains.

> [!example]- Three stories for one arch-shaped curve (click to expand)
> - "A basketball's height over time. The peak is the top of its
>   flight; the second crossing is the catch."
> - "Profit against ticket price: too cheap loses money, too dear
>   empties the gym, and the peak is the sweet spot."
> - "It is $y = -(x-2)^2 + 4$." True to the shape, but a story with
>   no characters. We sent it back for some.

## One variation

Two unlabelled lines that cross. A race decided? A [[Break-Even]]
moment? The same picture your group will defend in [[Crossing Paths]].

> [!tip] Features have names
> Vertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts, zeros, maximum — using the
> right name for the right feature is half of every story; that
> vocabulary is the backbone of [[Quadratic Relations]].
