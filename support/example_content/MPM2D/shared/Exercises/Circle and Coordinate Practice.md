These questions follow [[The Equation of a Circle]] — discovered at
the boards in [[The Circle on the Grid]], where the length formula
from [[Midpoint and Length]] got aimed at every point at once. Keep
that origin in mind: $x^2 + y^2$ is a squared distance from $(0, 0)$.

## Questions

1. State the radius of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 49$.
2. Write the equation of the circle centred at the origin that passes
   through $(0, -6)$.
3. Sketch $x^2 + y^2 = 25$, labelling all four intercepts.
4. Write the equation of the circle centred at the origin that passes
   through $(5, 12)$, and state its radius.
5. **Explain why.** Without graphing, decide whether $(4, 5)$ lies on,
   inside, or outside the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 36$ — and explain why
   comparing $x^2 + y^2$ with $r^2$ settles the question.
6. **Find the error.** Jordan says the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 10$ has
   radius $10$. Test Jordan's claim with a point, explain what went
   wrong, and state the actual radius.
7. **Challenge.** Find every point on $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ whose
   $x$-coordinate is $3$. What famous triangle just appeared?

## Answers

> [!success]- Answer 1
> $r^2 = 49$, so $r = 7$. The equation stores the *square* of the
> radius — worth saying aloud every time.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> $(0, -6)$ sits $6$ units from the origin, so $r = 6$ and the
> equation is $x^2 + y^2 = 36$.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> A circle of radius $5$ through $(5, 0)$, $(-5, 0)$, $(0, 5)$, and
> $(0, -5)$. Four intercepts, one compass-worth of symmetry.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> $r^2 = 5^2 + 12^2 = 169$, so the equation is $x^2 + y^2 = 169$ and
> $r = 13$. The 5–12–13 triangle strikes again.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> $4^2 + 5^2 = 41 > 36$, so $(4, 5)$ is *outside*. Why it works:
> $x^2 + y^2$ is the squared distance from the origin and $r^2$ the
> squared distance of points *on* the circle — equal means on, less
> inside, greater outside. Here $\sqrt{41} \approx 6.4 > 6$.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> Test $(10, 0)$: $10^2 + 0^2 = 100 \neq 10$, so that point is not on
> the circle and the claim fails. Jordan read $r^2$ as $r$. Actually
> $r = \sqrt{10} \approx 3.2$ — a much smaller circle.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> Substitute $x = 3$: $9 + y^2 = 25$, so $y = \pm 4$. Two points,
> $(3, 4)$ and $(3, -4)$ — and the 3–4–5 right triangle, drawn from
> the origin to either one.
