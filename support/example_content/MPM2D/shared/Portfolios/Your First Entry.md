The first entry in your [[Math Journal]] is the hardest, because the
page is blank and you do not yet trust what belongs on it. So for week
one only, the entry is guided: answer the three prompts below, a few
sentences each, written the same day as class. Ten minutes, done.

## The three prompts

1. **What we did** — the facts, briefly. The warm-up, the thinking
   task, where your group got to before time ran out.
2. **What surprised me** — one specific moment you did not expect. In
   the mathematics, in a classmate's method, in yourself.
3. **Where I stand with math right now** — honestly. Everyone arrives
   in Grade 10 with a history; naming yours is the first piece of
   data in the record.

## What honest looks like

These fragments are the register to aim for — plain, specific, true:

> "The second differences came out the same every single time, and
> apparently that is *why* the graph curves. I checked two tables of
> my own and it held. I don't see yet what a constant 2 has to do
> with the shape of a parabola, but I want to."

> "I said the figure was 'obviously' a rectangle and my group made me
> prove the diagonals were equal before they would write it down. It
> took the length formula four times. Obvious is apparently not a
> reason."

> "Grade 9 went fine as long as the steps were on the board first.
> Today there were no steps, just the problem. I hated the first ten
> minutes and then, weirdly, did not."

None of these is polished, and none performs for a reader. Each names
a real moment and stays with it — which already beats a page of "math
was fine today, I learned a lot."

> [!important] Nobody reads this but me
> As [[Math Journal]] promises: entries are private. I read them;
> other students never do, and nothing you admit here is quoted or
> used as an example without your explicit okay. The honesty you risk
> in week one becomes your best evidence of growth by spring — but
> only you decide who ever hears about it.
