By the end of this course, you will be able to think mathematically about
parabolas, coordinates, and triangles — and, just as importantly, to work
the way mathematicians work: investigate, conjecture, verify, defend. The
curriculum names seven processes that run through everything we do:

%%curriculum-start%%
![[Mathematical Process Expectations]]

The full set — every overall and specific expectation across the three
strands — lives in the Curriculum folder, and each task page links to
exactly the expectations it addresses.
%%curriculum-end%%

Put plainly, week to week that means:

- **Think on your feet** — work problems you have not been shown how to
  do, at the whiteboards, in [[Our Classroom Norms|a room built for it]].
- **Connect representations** — a relation is a table, a graph, an
  equation, and a story at once; moving between them is the course's
  quiet superpower.
- **Verify, don't trust** — from
  [[Checking Your Own Work|checking a solution]] to
  [[What Makes a Proof Convincing|proving a quadrilateral's identity]] on
  the coordinate grid.
- **Grow visibly** — your [[Math Journal]] is where struggle turns into
  evidence; see [[How Marks Work]].
