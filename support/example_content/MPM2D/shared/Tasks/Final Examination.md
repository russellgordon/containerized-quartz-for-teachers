> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · two and a half hours, in the examination period · written,
> with a calculator and one formula sheet supplied · all three strands

## What it is for

Everything else this semester was worked in groups, at the boards, with
somebody beside you and time to look things up. This is the one piece of
evidence that is unambiguously yours. It also matters more than most
Grade 10 papers, because MPM2D is the gate to the Grade 11 and 12
mathematics courses — what you can do unassisted here is what those
courses will assume.

## What is on it

| Part | Roughly | What it asks you to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Quadratics | 40% | Expand and factor; convert between standard, factored, and vertex form; find zeros, axis, and vertex; solve with the quadratic formula; sketch; solve problems about maximum area, height, and revenue |
| B. Analytic geometry | 35% | Slope, length, and midpoint; parallel and perpendicular; right bisectors; equations of lines; prove a claim about a shape on the grid; circles centred at the origin |
| C. Trigonometry | 25% | Similar triangles; the primary ratios; the sine and cosine laws; problems in two triangles |

Every part asks you to **show the route**. A bare answer earns almost
nothing; a clearly wrong number reached by a correct method earns most
of the marks available.

## What to expect, precisely

- **A sketch question with no table of values.** You will be given a
  quadratic and asked to sketch it, showing the direction of opening,
  the intercepts, the axis, and the vertex.
  [[Factors and Intercepts]] has the three routes; choose one and say
  which.
- **A proof on the grid.** "Show that $ABCD$ is a parallelogram" is
  answered with slopes and lengths, not with a ruler. Stating the
  property you are using — equal slopes means parallel — is part of the
  mark.
- **A right bisector**, which is midpoint plus negative reciprocal plus
  an equation, in that order.
- **A two-triangle trigonometry problem**, where a side shared between
  the triangles is the bridge. Draw it before calculating anything.
- **Exact answers where they exist**, and rounding stated where it does
  not: "12.4 m, to one decimal place" is a complete answer.

## How to prepare

1. **Redo problems, do not reread them.** Five from each set, cold:
   [[Expanding and Factoring Practice]], [[Quadratic Formula Practice]],
   [[Linear Systems Practice]], [[Midpoint and Length Practice]],
   [[Circle and Coordinate Practice]], [[Trig Ratios and Laws Practice]].
2. **Rebuild the connections.** Factored form gives zeros, zeros give the
   axis, the axis gives the vertex — one chain, not three facts. Draw it
   from memory; the gaps are your study list.
3. **Practise the three sketching routes** on the same quadratic, so you
   can pick the fastest one under time.
4. **Re-read your own journal** entries about the errors you fixed. They
   are the most efficient revision in the course, because you wrote them
   at the moment of understanding.
5. **Bring questions to the review classes.** This page is what is on the
   examination.

> [!tip] In the two and a half hours
> Quadratics is the largest part and the one you have practised most —
> do it first while you are fresh, and give the proof question the time
> it deserves rather than the time that is left. If a question stalls,
> write what you know and what you intended to do; a described method
> earns marks and a blank space earns none.

## How this is assessed

Against the same expectations as everything else. Per [[How Marks Work]],
this examination is part of the final 30% of the course mark alongside
[[The Math Symposium]], so that neither one afternoon nor one project
decides your grade alone.
