## What you are making

The course ends the way mathematics actually lives: in conversation.
You curate **one exhibit** — either your best problem from the course,
re-solved better than you solved it the first time, or an
investigation that extends a course idea one step past where class
stopped. Alongside it, a **growth statement** that uses your
[[Math Journal]] as evidence.

On symposium day, half the room exhibits while half visits, then we
swap. Every exhibitor holds a defended conversation: visitors ask real
questions, and you answer from understanding, not from a script.

## How to work

1. Choose. Reread your journal for the problem that fought you
   hardest, or the idea that left a loose thread — what happens to the
   border problem in three dimensions? Does the circle's equation
   survive a centre that moves? Either door leads somewhere.
2. Rebuild. "Re-solved better" means a different method, a cleaner
   argument, or a step toward the general case — never just neater
   handwriting. If you extend an idea instead, your investigation
   needs a question it genuinely answers.
3. Write the exhibit page with [[Writing About Math]] open beside you.
4. Write the growth statement. [[Showing Growth]] shows the shape:
   claims about your growth are cited to dated journal entries, the
   way evidence works everywhere else in this course. Your
   [[Final Reflection]] entry gets its first draft here too.
5. Rehearse the conversation with a partner. The questions will be the
   honest kind — "why does that step work?", "what if the numbers were
   different?" — so practise answering the question behind the
   question.
6. Exhibit, visit, and ask at least one real question at someone
   else's table. Visiting well is part of the task.

The working periods are class time, and using them well is part of the
task — [[How Marks Work]] explains how.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like at your table |
| --- | --- |
| A worthy exhibit | The chosen problem or question carries real weight |
| Genuinely better | The re-solve differs in method, not in tidiness |
| Growth cited | Every claim points to a dated journal entry |
| A live extension | Any investigation answers a stated question |
| A defended conversation | Questions are met with reasoning, not recital |
| Generous visiting | You asked real questions at other tables |

## Reflect

The symposium is the course's reflection made public, and your
[[Final Reflection]] is its written half: where your understanding
started, where it ended, and the evidence in between. Write it after
the conversations — they have a way of showing you what you know.

> [!success]- If nothing feels worthy of an exhibit (click to expand)
> Find the journal entry where you were most wrong. The distance
> between that page and what you can do now *is* the exhibit — and
> re-solving that problem well makes the strongest table in the room.
