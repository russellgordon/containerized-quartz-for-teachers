Prejudice in early America was not only an attitude. It was written down,
enacted by legislatures, enforced by courts, and enforced most heavily on the
people least able to answer back. Naming the statute rather than the feeling
is what turns a complaint into a historical argument, so this page starts
with the rules.

## The rule, and who it was written against

| Rule or practice | What it did |
| --- | --- |
| Virginia, 1662 | Made a child's condition follow the mother's, so slavery was inherited |
| Colonial slave codes | Restricted movement, assembly, literacy, testimony, and self-defence |
| Anti-Catholic laws | Barred Catholics from office and the vote in colonies that had once tolerated them |
| Coverture | Placed a married woman's property and legal identity under her husband's |
| Naturalization Act, 1790 | Limited naturalization to a "free white person" of good character |

That last row is the one to sit with. It was passed by the first Congress of
a republic whose founding declaration had announced that all men are created
equal, and it made whiteness a statutory qualification for becoming an
American. The contradiction is not a later discovery. It was visible at the
time, and people said so.

## Two cases worth studying closely

**Salem, 1692.** Accusations of witchcraft in Massachusetts led to trials in
which spectral evidence — testimony about what a spirit had done, which no
one else could see or contest — was admitted. Nineteen people were hanged,
one man was pressed to death under stones for refusing to plead, and others
died in jail. Most of the accused were women, and a disproportionate number
were poor, widowed, or otherwise on the margin of the community. The colony
later reversed the convictions and paid compensation, which tells you the
society itself came to regard the process as a failure.

**Native land and Native belief.** Dispossession was routinely justified by
the claim that Native peoples did not use land properly and did not hold the
right religion. Missionary work and land seizure were frequently carried out
by the same institutions. Where an argument about souls and an argument about
acres arrive together, ask which one was doing the work.

## The women who put it in writing

In 1776 Abigail Adams asked her husband to "remember the ladies" as the new
laws were drafted, and warned that women would not consider themselves bound
by laws in which they had no voice. He did not act on it, and the laws of the
new republic left coverture in place. Her letter matters not because it
succeeded but because it proves the argument was available and was made.
