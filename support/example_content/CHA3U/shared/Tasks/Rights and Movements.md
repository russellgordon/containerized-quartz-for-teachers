**Due: the end of Unit 3.** Pairs. An inquiry with a public-facing
product.

Rights in the United States were claimed before they were granted, and
the claiming took generations. This task follows one claim across the
Jim Crow decades and into the movements that came after 1945 — long
enough to see that the second half did not begin from nothing.

## The inquiry

Choose **one right** and trace how it was claimed: the vote, schooling,
housing, a fair wage, protection from violence, a place on a jury, the
right to travel unmolested, the right to be counted a citizen at all.
Follow it in two places at once.

- **Before 1945**, in the organising of African Americans under Jim Crow
  — the associations, the newspapers, the churches, the lawsuits, the
  campaigns against lynching, the schools built when none were provided.
- **After 1945**, in the movement that pressed the same claim, and in
  what the law finally said.

Then add **one other group** claiming citizenship in the same decades —
Native American nations, Chinese or Japanese Americans, Jewish
Americans, Mexican Americans, women, or organised radicals — and say
what their claim shared with yours and where the two diverged.

## What you produce

An **inquiry report** of about 1,100 words answering: what was demanded,
by whom, through what organisation, against what resistance and by what
mechanism, what changed in law, what changed in life, and what did not
change at all. And a **public-facing piece** — a panel, a short
recording, a briefing sheet — that a stranger could learn from in five
minutes.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Agency | The people who organised are the subject of your sentences |
| Continuity | The pre-1945 organising is treated as the movement, not its prologue |
| Resistance | Named, with its mechanisms — law, employer, landlord, registrar, mob |
| Evidence | Primary where possible, and cited |
| Communication | The public piece works for someone who was not in this class |

> [!quote] "In 1965, African Americans were finally given the vote."
> Three faults in nine words. *Given* by whom — the sentence has no
> actor, and so the people who did the work vanish. *Finally* makes a
> delay out of what was a defence, mounted by people you can name. And
> it treats that year as a beginning: ask your sources whether Black
> Americans had voted in earlier decades, and if so, what removed them
> and who did the removing. Then rewrite the sentence with a subject for
> every verb.
