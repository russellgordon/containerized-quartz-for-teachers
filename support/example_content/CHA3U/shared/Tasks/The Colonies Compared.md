**Due: the middle of Unit 1.** Individual. About 800 words.

"The Thirteen Colonies" is a convenience. They were founded by different
people, for different reasons, under different charters, and they ran on
different kinds of labour. This task asks you to take two of them apart.

## What you choose

Two colonies, far enough apart that the comparison is worth making. Then
compare them on three things, and only three.

1. **How the place was settled.** Who came, from where, with what
   charter — proprietary, royal, or company — and who was already there.
2. **How it was governed.** Who could vote, who sat in the assembly,
   what the church had to do with it, and who answered to London.
3. **Who did the work.** Wage labour, indentured servitude, enslaved
   labour, family farming, or some mixture — and how that mixture
   changed across the period.

## The fourth thing

Then add one instance of **cooperation across a difference** in one of
your two colonies: an alliance, a confederacy, a trading arrangement, a
joint petition, a boycott, a treaty that held for a while. Say what each
side wanted out of it and what it cost them.

This is not a decorative paragraph. Colonial America is usually taught
as a sequence of conflicts, and the arrangements that worked are
evidence about what was possible.

## The claim you finish with

Close by naming **one regional difference** that outlasted the colonial
period, and saying which of your three comparisons produced it. One
sentence, defended by the evidence you have already laid out.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Comparison | Genuinely comparative, point by point, rather than two descriptions in sequence |
| Labour | Who worked, under what compulsion, treated as central rather than mentioned |
| Cooperation | A real arrangement, with what each side wanted from it |
| Consequence | The regional difference you name follows from your evidence |
| Sources | At least three, at least one primary, cited |

> [!example] What a comparative sentence looks like
> Weak: *One colony grew tobacco for export. The other had smaller
> farms.* Two facts, side by side, doing no work.
> Stronger: *Because the export crop rewarded large holdings worked by
> unfree labour, landholding in the first colony concentrated in fewer
> hands than in the second — and the men who sat in its assembly came
> from that narrowing group.* One sentence, two colonies, a mechanism,
> and a direction of travel.

[[Colonies and the People In Them]] and [[Empire and Its Rules]] are the
ground; [[Religion in Early America]] and
[[Cooperation Across Difference]] are where two of your three
comparisons will send you. For where the charters and assembly records
sit, see [[Government Records]].
