Ask early. Almost everything that becomes a real problem in this course
began as a small question somebody did not ask in week two.

## Ways to reach me

- **Before or after class**, any day — quick questions, and checking your
  thinking before you commit an afternoon to it.
- **[[Help Sessions]]**, at the posted times — working on something with me
  in the room, which is by far the most efficient of these.
- **A booked conversation**, any week — anything that needs more than five
  minutes, including anything you would rather not raise in front of the
  class.
- **Email**, any time, with replies on school days — best when you want
  something in writing.

## What to bring

"I don't get it" is a fine opening, and we get much further with one of
these in front of us:

- The source you are stuck on, open, with the confusing part marked.
- Your draft, however rough. A bad paragraph is far more useful to me than a
  description of the paragraph you intend to write.
- The question you cannot answer, written out, and what you have already
  tried, so that we do not repeat it.

## When the argument is not working

This is the commonest reason students come, and it is nearly always one of
four things:

1. **The question cannot be settled by evidence.** Rewrite the question, not
   the essay. [[Building an Argument]] starts here for a reason.
2. **You have evidence but no claim.** Say what you think, in one sentence,
   then check whether the sources support it.
3. **You have a claim but no objection to it.** If nothing could count
   against your position, you have written a description.
4. **Your sources all agree.** Usually a sign they came from one place, or
   from one another. [[Using Evidence]] and [[Where the Records Live]] fix
   this.

## Beyond this course

Some of this material is heavy, and it does not stop being heavy at the
bell. If a lesson stays with you, or something outside class is making
school hard, say so — to me, to guidance, or to any adult here you trust,
and without having to explain why.

> [!note] Support that is not me
> The guidance office can connect you to counselling and to community
> supports, including ones with no connection to this school, and the crisis
> lines posted there are staffed outside school hours. You do not need my
> permission or a reason to go, and you may leave a lesson to do it —
> [[Our Working Agreement]] says so in writing.
