Movements rarely fail for lack of a grievance. The grievance is usually
decades old and well documented before anything changes, which means the
historical question is almost never "why did people object?" but "why did
objecting work in this year and not in the year before?"

This investigation runs across two periods deliberately. The curriculum
places suffrage, labour, temperance, the social gospel and early civil
rights organising before 1945, and civil rights, antiwar, feminist, Native
rights and environmental movements after it. Comparing across that divide is
where the pattern shows.

## Five conditions worth testing

Copy these into [[Your Inquiry Notebook]] as a list, and record for each
movement you study whether the condition was present, absent, or
partly present, with the source that shows it. This is a research
instrument, not a marking scheme, and nothing on this page can be ticked
off here.

- [ ] **Organisation that outlasts an event** — an association with dues,
      officers, a newspaper, and a name that survives a defeat
- [ ] **Allies with something to lose** — churches, unions, professional
      bodies, or a political party that needs the movement's votes
- [ ] **A visible act that forces a response** — and evidence that it was
      planned to do exactly that
- [ ] **A legal or legislative opening** — a court decision, an election, a
      war, or a treaty obligation that changes what is possible
- [ ] **Coverage that reaches people outside the movement** — and a record
      of how it was framed when it got there

## The evidence set

Organisational records: constitutions, minutes, membership figures,
finances. The movement's own publications, which state its arguments in its
own words. Opposition material, which is equally organised and equally
documented. Government responses, from statutes down to surveillance files.
And press coverage from at least two regions, read as
[[Newspapers and Print Culture]] describes.

## The traps

**Reading backwards from the outcome.** Once a movement has won, its
strategy looks inevitable and its internal arguments disappear. Find the
disagreements inside it — they are in the minutes.

**Crediting the leader with the movement.** Named individuals matter, and
naming only them erases the organisers, the treasurers and the thousands
whose participation was the actual leverage.

**Assuming success is a single event.** A statute passed is not a practice
changed. Say which you are measuring, and check the other one.

## What the class produces

A comparative argument: two movements, one condition, and an explanation of
why the same condition produced different results. Then a paragraph on what
you would need to know to be more certain. It leads into
[[Rights and Movements]], and the disagreement belongs in
[[Who Counts as American|Who Counts as American?]].
