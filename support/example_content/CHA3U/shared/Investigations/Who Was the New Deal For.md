Between 1933 and 1939 the federal government did things it had never done:
paid wages directly, insured deposits, regulated hours, built dams and
schools, and took responsibility for old age. Whether that record counts as
rescue, as reform, or as a settlement that deliberately left some people out
depends entirely on whose experience you measure it by — which makes it an
unusually clean test of [[Historical Significance]].

## The question, taken apart

*For whom was relief available?* Programmes had eligibility rules, and rules
are documents.

*Who administered it?* Many federal programmes were delivered locally, and
local administration in a segregated country produced segregated outcomes
without a single word about race in the federal text.

*What was excluded from coverage?* Ask which categories of work were left
outside new labour and social insurance protections, and then ask who did
that work. This is the single most productive line of inquiry available
here, and it can be pursued entirely through statute and regulation.

*What happened afterwards?* A programme that ends in 1943 and a programme
that still exists are different historical objects.

## The evidence set

The statutes and executive orders themselves. Agency reports and the
photographs commissioned to document the programmes, read with the cautions
in [[Photographs]]. Employment, wage and relief series from
[[Statistics and the Census]]. Contemporary criticism from left and right,
because both existed and both are evidence. And testimony, treated the way
[[Testimony and Narrative]] requires.

> [!question] The question that organises the whole inquiry
> **Take one programme and follow one person through it.** What did they
> have to prove to qualify, who decided, what did they receive, for how
> long, and what happened when it ended? A single documented case, with the
> rule that governed it quoted, is worth more than a page of national
> summary — and it forces the eligibility question that the summary version
> lets you skip.

## The trap

Both available slogans — that the New Deal saved the country, and that it
did far too little for those who needed it most — can be supported by real
evidence, which is why neither is an argument on its own. State your
criteria first: relief delivered, unemployment reduced, inequality changed,
rights created, or durability. Different criteria give different answers
honestly, and saying so is a strength.

## What the class produces

A written judgement with criteria stated in the opening paragraph and at
least one group whose experience cuts against your conclusion, dealt with in
the body rather than conceded at the end. It feeds directly into
[[The Industrial Republic]].
