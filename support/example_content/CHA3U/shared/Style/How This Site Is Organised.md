This site is the course: the schedule, the sources, the arguments, and
the expectations behind them.

| Folder | Holds | You will use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **All Classes** | One page per period | Every day |
| **Concepts** | The ideas and the periods | After the class that met one |
| **Sources** | What American history is made of, by kind | Whenever you are handed a document |
| **Investigations** | A question, evidence, and a judgement to reach | In class, and again in review |
| **Writing** | How an argument is built and cited | Whenever something is due |
| **Discussions** | Seminar questions | The night before a seminar |
| **Portfolios** | Your notebook and your own record | All term |
| **Tasks** | Assessed work, with criteria | Before you start, not after |
| **Tutorials** | Using the site; working in an archive | The first time you need one |
| **Setup** | How the class runs | Week one |
| **Style** | How the site works | Now |

## How the pieces connect

Class pages are the spine. Each links what that period consists of, and
the backlinks at the foot of any page show which classes used it.

Content pages end with a **Curriculum connection** — the Ontario
expectations that page addresses, in the Ministry's own words. The site
builds a coverage map from those links.

## Two conventions

- **Concept pages come after the argument**, not before. They assume you
  have already worked the source.
- **The newest class is on the section landing page.** If you were away,
  start there.
