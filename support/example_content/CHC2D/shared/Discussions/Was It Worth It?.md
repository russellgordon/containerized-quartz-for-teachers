**The question:** Canada entered the First World War as a country whose
foreign policy was decided in London, and finished it signing the Treaty
of Versailles under its own name. Was what the country gained worth what
it cost?

Every word there is doing work. *Worth* assumes a scale. *Gained*
assumes the gains were shared. *Cost* assumes we can count it.

## Prepare

Bring **one soldier**. Not a battle — a person. The Canadian
Expeditionary Force personnel files are digitised and free, and an
attestation paper gives you an age, a birthplace, a trade, and a next of
kin in the enlisting man's own handwriting. Or bring one testimony from
The Memory Project. Record the source as [[Reading a Primary Source]]
says. Bring one figure for Canadian dead as well, with the source that
published it and when.[^totals]

## Three positions, and a fourth that refuses the question

**The war made the country.** Canada raised, paid for, and eventually
commanded its own force; it signed the peace separately and sat in the
League of Nations. Compare 1914 with 10 September 1939, when Parliament
debated for a week after Britain declared and Canada declared on its own
authority. That gap is the measure of what changed.

**Canada had no choice to make.** In 1914 a British declaration bound
the whole Empire. Calling it a national decision reads a later Canada
backwards into an earlier one — the trap [[Historical Perspective]] is
meant to catch.

**The cost was not shared.** [[Conscription and the Country It Split]]
and [[Indigenous Peoples and the First World War]] describe people who
carried the war's weight without holding its citizenship in full. A gain
for the country is not automatically a gain for everyone in it.

**The people who paid cannot vote on it.** "Worth it" is a judgement the
living make about lives that were not theirs. That does not end the
argument, but it should change who you think has standing in it.

Open with your position, name the evidence that would move you, and
carry the disagreement into [[The War Question]].

[^totals]: Published totals differ, and not through carelessness.
Sources vary in whether they count men who died of wounds after the
armistice, the merchant marine, or Newfoundland, which was not yet part
of Canada. Say whose count you are using.
