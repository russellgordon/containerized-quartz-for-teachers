The Holocaust was the systematic, state-organized murder of Europe's Jews by
Nazi Germany and its collaborators. Six million Jewish people were killed.
The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, which maintains the standard
documentation, is explicit that no single German document accounts for every
death: the figure is built from German records, prewar and postwar
demographic studies, records Jews created during and after the war, and
documentation produced by resistance groups. For the killing centres the
records are precise enough to give the toll centre by centre. Elsewhere the
museum says "approximately," and means it.

## It was built in stages

Nazi Germany did not begin with killing. It began with law and exclusion:
boycotts of Jewish businesses, dismissal from professions, removal of
citizenship and civil rights, and the marking of people with badges. On 9–10
November 1938 came the Kristallnacht pogrom — more than 1,400 synagogues
destroyed, about 26,000 Jewish men arrested and sent to concentration camps,
and hundreds of deaths that were never precisely counted. Then ghettos,
deliberate starvation, mass shootings, deportation, and the killing centres.

That sequence matters, because each stage was public and each was survivable
by leaving — if any country would take you.

## Canada would not

Delegates from thirty-two countries met at Evian, France, in the summer of
1938 to discuss Jewish refugees. Apart from the Dominican Republic, none was
willing to accept more. The historians Irving Abella and Harold Troper took
the title of their study of Canadian policy, *None Is Too Many*, from the
answer a Canadian official reportedly gave when asked how many Jews Canada
would admit. Their finding — that Canada took in fewer than five thousand
Jewish refugees between 1933 and 1945, one of the worst records in the
Western world — is the standard account.

The clearest single case is the *St. Louis*, which sailed from Hamburg on 13
May 1939 with 937 passengers. Cuba refused them. The United States and Canada
would not admit them. The ship returned to Europe on 6 June 1939, and of the
620 passengers who landed back in Europe, 254 were later killed in the
Holocaust.

> [!warning] Survivor testimony is evidence, and it has custodians
> Memoirs, filmed interviews and recorded testimony are primary sources of
> the first order, and they were given under conditions. Survivors who spoke
> to museums and archives agreed to specific uses, and institutions such as
> the Montreal Holocaust Museum and the Vancouver Holocaust Education Centre
> set terms for how their holdings may be quoted, reproduced and taught. Read
> those terms before you use a testimony, cite the collection that holds it,
> and never crop a person's account to make a point they did not make.

Canadian soldiers helped liberate camps, and Canadians saw the newsreels.
Refugee policy loosened through the late 1940s, displaced persons were
admitted, and Canada signed the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
Provincial anti-discrimination laws followed, and hate propaganda offences
later still. None of it was automatic; each was campaigned for, often by the
Canadian Jewish Congress. Antisemitism did not end — the security now
standard at synagogues and Jewish schools is a present-day fact.

question of what Canada owed into [[The Wartime Decision]].
