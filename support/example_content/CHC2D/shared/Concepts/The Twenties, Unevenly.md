The decade has a nickname — the Roaring Twenties — and the nickname is the
problem. It describes a real experience of rising wages, new goods, jazz,
cars and confidence. It also describes almost none of the Maritimes, very
little of the countryside, and nothing at all of a reserve. The question
worth asking is not whether the decade roared. It is *for whom*.

## It did not start well

The war economy shut down, soldiers came home looking for work, and prices
that had climbed through the war stayed high. Labour unrest peaked with the
Winnipeg General Strike in 1919, when tens of thousands walked out, the
federal government amended the Criminal Code to reach "unlawful
associations," and strike leaders were arrested. On 21 June — Bloody
Saturday — the Royal North-West Mounted Police charged the crowd and
fired, killing two strikers and injuring many more. Recovery did not
arrive until the middle of the decade.

| Region or group | The 1920s |
| --- | --- |
| Central Canada | Manufacturing, branch plants, a consumer market |
| The Prairies | Wheat exports — profitable, exposed to world prices |
| The Maritimes | Industry collapsing; the Maritime Rights movement |
| The North | Whaling gone, fur prices swinging, Inuit families hit |
| First Nations | Excluded by the pass system and by the *Indian Act* |

That last row deserves a moment. In the decade Canadians most associate with
freedom, Parliament made it an offence for First Nations to raise money to
pursue a land claim. Prosperity and dispossession were happening at once, in
the same country, under the same government.

## Buying it now, paying for it later

Two habits spread in this decade and both matter for what comes next.
Households began buying cars, radios and appliances on instalment credit.
Investors began buying shares on margin, putting down a fraction of the price
and borrowing the rest. Both work beautifully while incomes and prices rise,
and both amplify the fall when they do not.

The Group of Seven painted a Canada of empty northern rock and water, and
that image became a national self-portrait. Ask who is absent from those
canvases: the land was not empty, and the people whose territory it was do
not appear. American films, magazines and radio also arrived in force, which
is when Canadians first began arguing about whether their culture could
survive next door to a much larger one.

Set the decade's unevenness against [[The Crash and the Thirties]], and use
[[Continuity and Change]] to decide what 1929 actually interrupted.
