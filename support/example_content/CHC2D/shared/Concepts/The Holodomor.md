In 1932 and 1933 millions of people starved to death in Soviet Ukraine, in a
country that had food in it. The name for this is the Holodomor, from
Ukrainian words meaning death inflicted by hunger. Canada recognises it in
law as a genocide. Some states dispute that. This page separates what is
established from what is argued, because you will need both.

## What the Soviet state did

Stalin's government forced Ukraine's peasants off their own land and into
collective farms, then set grain quotas the collectives could not meet.
Farmers who resisted were labelled *kulaks*, dispossessed, deported or shot.
When quotas went unmet, brigades searched houses and took not just grain but
all food. Villages that fell short were blacklisted and cut off from supply.
A law of August 1932 made taking even a handful of grain from a collective
field a capital offence. Internal passports issued from December 1932 to city
dwellers, and the sealing of Ukraine's borders in January 1933, trapped
starving families where they were. The government denied that any famine was
occurring and refused offers of international aid.

## The numbers, and why they move

The Holodomor Research and Education Consortium states that demographers
estimate close to four million residents of Ukraine, mostly Ukrainian
peasants, died as a direct result of starvation. Published estimates range
higher, and they vary for a reason you should be able to explain: the state
that caused the deaths also controlled the registration of deaths, suppressed
a census whose results it disliked, and spent decades destroying the record.
Treat any single confident figure with suspicion, and always ask whether it
counts deaths only or also the births that never happened.

| Established by the evidence | Genuinely disputed |
| --- | --- |
| Soviet policy caused the famine | Were Ukrainians targeted *as* Ukrainians? |
| Grain was seized and exported | How far did the leadership foresee it? |
| Escape from famine areas was blocked | Genocide, or crime against humanity? |
| The USSR denied it, refused aid | How does it relate to famine elsewhere? |

Historians arguing for genocide point to measures applied to Ukraine
specifically: the border sealing, the blacklists, and the simultaneous arrest
of Ukrainian cultural and religious leaders. Historians who resist the term
generally accept the mass killing and Soviet responsibility, and argue the
campaign targeted peasants and resistance rather than a nationality. The
Russian government rejects the genocide designation outright, which is a
political position rather than a scholarly one, and should be named as such.

## What Canada recognises, and why Canada

Ukrainians settled in Canada in large numbers before 1914, and their
relatives were among the dead. The Canadian journalist Rhea Clyman travelled
in Ukraine, reported what she saw, and was expelled from the Soviet Union for
it, while better-known correspondents in Moscow repeated the official denial —
which held officially until the late 1980s. Canada's first Holodomor monument
was raised in Edmonton in 1983. Parliament passed the *Ukrainian Famine and
Genocide ("Holodomor") Memorial Day Act*, which received royal assent on 29
May 2008 and sets the fourth Saturday in November as a memorial day; Ontario's
*Holodomor Memorial Day Act, 2009* received royal assent on 23 April 2009.
