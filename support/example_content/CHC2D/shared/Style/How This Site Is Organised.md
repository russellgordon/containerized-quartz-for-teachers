This site is the course: the schedule, the ideas, the documents, the
assessed work, and the expectations behind all of it.

| Folder | Holds | You will use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **All Classes** | One page per period — what we did and what to do next | Every day |
| **Concepts** | The periods and the ideas, stated cleanly | After the class that uncovered one |
| **Sources** | The kinds of evidence this course works from, and how each behaves | Whenever a task needs real material |
| **Investigations** | A question, the evidence, and a judgement, worked through | In class, and again in review |
| **Discussions** | Seminar questions and how to prepare for them | The night before a seminar |
| **Writing** | Thesis, evidence, citation, and the vocabulary | Whenever you are writing something marked |
| **Portfolios** | Your notebook and the reflections built across the term | All term |
| **Tasks** | Assessed work, with what earns the marks | Whenever something is due |
| **Tutorials** | This site, archives, and how an inquiry runs | The first time you need one |
| **Setup** | How the class runs, including how we handle hard history | Week one |
| **Style** | How the site itself works | Now |

## Why Sources and Investigations are separate

One is material; the other is a question put to it.
[[Newspapers and Propaganda]] says what that kind of source can and
cannot show; [[Was Conscription Necessary?]] takes several kinds and
reaches a judgement. Keeping them apart is the difference between having
evidence and using it.

## How the pieces connect

Class pages are the spine. Each links to the concepts, sources, and
tasks that period consists of, so the site reads forward in time even
though the folders are arranged by kind. The backlinks at the foot of
any page show which classes used it — the fastest way to see where an
idea came up, and to notice that one document has served three different
arguments.

## The Curriculum connection

Content pages end with a **Curriculum connection**: the Ministry
expectations that page addresses, in the Ministry's own words. Setup and
Style pages carry none, because they are about how the room and the site
work rather than about history. The site builds a coverage map from
those links, so you can see what the course has actually taught.

## Two conventions

- **Concept pages come after the doing.** They assume you have met the
  idea already, so reading one in advance is less useful than it looks.
- **The newest class is on the section landing page.** If you were away,
  start there.
