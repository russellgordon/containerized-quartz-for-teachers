Ask early. Almost everything that becomes a real problem in this course
began as a small question somebody did not ask in week two.

## Ways to reach me

| How | When | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before or after class | Every day | Quick questions, checking your thinking |
| [[Help Sessions]] | The posted times | Working on something with me in the room |
| A booked conversation | Any week | Anything needing more than five minutes |
| Email | Any time; replies on school days | Something you want in writing |

## What to bring

"I don't get it" is a fine opening, and we get much further with one of
these in front of us:

- The source you are stuck on, open, with the part that confuses you
  marked.
- Your draft, however rough. A bad paragraph is far more useful to me
  than a description of the paragraph you intend to write.
- The question you cannot answer, written out, and what you have already
  tried, so we do not repeat it.

## When the argument is not working

This is the commonest reason students come, and it is nearly always one
of four things:

1. **The question is not answerable by evidence.** Rewrite the question,
   not the essay. [[Building an Argument]] starts here for a reason.
2. **You have evidence but no claim.** Say what you think, in one
   sentence, then see whether the sources support it.
3. **You have a claim but no objection to it.** If nothing could count
   against your position, you have written a description.
4. **Your sources all agree.** Usually a sign they came from one place.
   [[Using Evidence]] and [[Where the Records Live]] fix this.

## Beyond this course

Some of this material is heavy, and it does not stop being heavy at the
bell. If a lesson stays with you, or something outside class is making
school hard, say so — to me, to guidance, or to any adult here you
trust, and without having to explain why.
