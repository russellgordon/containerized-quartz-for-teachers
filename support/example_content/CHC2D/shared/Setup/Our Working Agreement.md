This course studies residential schools, the internment of Japanese
Canadians and of Ukrainian Canadians, the Holocaust, the Holodomor,
anti-Black racism in Canada, and the purge of LGBTQ people from the
public service, the military, and the RCMP. That is not a warning label
attached to the course. It is the course.

Students in this room have family history in some of it, usually
without saying so. Everything below follows from that.

## How we argue about the past

- **Evidence, not assertion.** You are entitled to your own view and not
  to your own facts. Name your source when you state one.
- **Disagree with the claim, not the person.** "That document was made
  by the department being investigated" is an argument; dismissal is not.
- **Let people finish**, particularly when you are certain they are
  wrong, and **change your mind out loud** when the evidence moves you.
- **Nobody speaks for a group they do not belong to**, and you are never
  the class representative of your own background.

## Precision is a form of respect

Where a figure is contested, we use what the responsible body published,
with its own uncertainty attached. On deaths in residential schools: the
Truth and Reconciliation Commission identified 3,201 in its registers of
named and unnamed students, stating that probably not all deaths have
been recorded — for roughly a third no name was written down, and for
roughly half no cause of death. The National Centre for Truth and
Reconciliation reported in 2025 that it had verified 4,141 deaths.
Neither count includes Indian Day Schools and neither is final. Rounded
numbers that travel through social media are not evidence.

## What I commit to

- **Advance notice** before the lessons that deal most directly with
  atrocity, in time for you to prepare or to arrange something else.
- **No surprise images.** Photographs of atrocity are used sparingly,
  named before they appear, and never to produce a reaction.
- **Testimony with its provenance.** Survivors are sources, not
  illustrations: we say who spoke, to whom, when, and on what terms.
