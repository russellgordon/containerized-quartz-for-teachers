**Due: mid-Unit 4.** Individual. About 900 words.

History does not stop at a comfortable distance. This task asks you to
treat something within living memory as history — with sources,
perspective, and judgement.

## The question

Choose a development in Canada since 1982 and explain how it came about
and what it changed:

- A development in the relationship between the federal government and
  First Nations, Métis, or Inuit peoples
- A constitutional or referendum episode, and what followed
- A change in immigration or refugee policy
- An economic shift and its regional effects
- A significant event affecting Black communities in Canada, and the
  response to it

## What makes recent history harder

1. **The sources are abundant and partisan.** More does not mean better.
2. **Participants are alive** and their accounts are shaped by what came
   after.
3. **You know the ending**, which makes contingency hard to see.

Your essay must address at least one of those three explicitly, saying
how it affected your evidence.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Explanation | Causes established, not asserted |
| Sources | Range, with their interests identified |
| Contingency | You show it could have gone otherwise |
| Judgement | A defensible assessment, with grounds |
| Care | Living communities described accurately and fairly |

> [!tip] Ask what was uncertain at the time
> The strongest recent-history writing recovers the moment before the
> outcome was obvious. Contemporary reporting is the best evidence for
> that, precisely because it did not know what happened next.

[[Canada Since 1982]] and [[Patriation, the Charter, and After]] are the
survey this essay goes beneath.
