**Due: mid-Unit 3.** Individual. About 900 words.

Canada built a great deal between 1945 and 1982 — a welfare state, a
health system, a set of rights. This task asks what that building was
for, and whom it reached.

## The question

Choose one programme or policy: universal health insurance, the Canada
Pension Plan, unemployment insurance, family allowances, public housing,
post-secondary expansion, or official multiculturalism.

Then argue: **what problem was it built to solve, did it solve that
problem, and who did it leave out?**

## What the argument must contain

1. **The problem as it was understood then**, with evidence.
2. **The design of the programme** — who was eligible, who paid, who
   administered it.
3. **What changed** afterwards, with figures where they exist.
4. **Who was excluded**, by rule or in practice, and what happened to
   them.
5. **A judgement**, with the standard named.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Design | You explain how the programme actually worked, not just that it existed |
| Evidence | Figures and sources, cited |
| Exclusion | Specific, evidenced, and not treated as an afterthought |
| Continuity and change | You say what genuinely changed and what did not |
| Argument | A claim, defended, with the objection answered |

> [!tip] Eligibility rules are where the history is
> Who qualified, who was means-tested, who was excluded by marital
> status, Indian status, or immigration status — the fine print is where
> a programme's real politics live, and it is usually written down.

[[Building the Postwar State]] is the survey; this task is one piece of
it, in depth.
