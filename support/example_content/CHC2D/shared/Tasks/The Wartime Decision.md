**Due: the end of Unit 2.** Individual. About 1,000 words.

One decision made by Canada or Canadians during the Second World War,
judged.

## The decisions on offer

- The internment of Japanese Canadians and the sale of their property.
- The refusal of the MS *St. Louis* and Canada's refugee policy of the
  1930s and 1940s.
- Conscription, again, and the 1942 plebiscite.
- The recruitment and treatment of First Nations, Métis, and Inuit
  service members.
- The decision to intern Ukrainian, Italian, or German Canadians.

## What the essay must do

1. **Establish what was decided**, by whom, when, and under what
   authority.
2. **Reconstruct the reasoning at the time** — what decision-makers said
   and what they knew. This is historical perspective, and it is not the
   same as agreeing.
3. **Establish what was known and by whom.** Distinguish what could not
   have been known from what was known and set aside.
4. **Judge it**, on stated grounds, and say what standard you are
   judging by.
5. **Say what followed** — the consequence, including any later apology
   or redress.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Dates, authorities, and figures correct and attributed |
| Perspective | The reasoning of the time reconstructed, not caricatured |
| Judgement | Grounds stated; you are not merely disapproving |
| Precision about uncertainty | Where evidence is contested, you say so |
| Citation | Sourced properly throughout |

> [!warning] Understanding a decision is not excusing it
> The point of reconstructing what people believed is to explain how the
> decision became possible — which makes the judgement stronger, not
> softer. That distinction is the whole of
> [[Historical Perspective]].

[[The Holocaust and Canada's Response]] and the investigation
[[Could Canada Have Done More?]] are where several of these decisions
are worked through first.
