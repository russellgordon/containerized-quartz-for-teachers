**Due: the end of Unit 3.** Pairs. An inquiry with a public-facing
product.

Rights in Canada were not granted in one moment. They were claimed,
resisted, litigated, and legislated, over decades, by people who were
often told to wait.

## The inquiry

Choose one movement or campaign between 1945 and 1982 and investigate
how change actually happened:

- Indigenous organising against the 1969 White Paper, and what followed
- Black Canadian organising against segregation and employment
  discrimination
- The women's movement and the Royal Commission on the Status of Women
- Labour organising and the right to strike
- Early gay and lesbian organising, and the state's response
- Disability rights organising

## What you produce

**An inquiry report** (about 1,000 words) answering: what was demanded,
who demanded it, who resisted and how, what changed, and what did not.

**A public-facing piece** — a display panel, a short recorded
explanation, a briefing sheet — that someone outside this class could
learn from in five minutes.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Agency | The people who organised are the subject, not the government that eventually responded |
| Evidence | Primary where possible: pamphlets, testimony, proceedings, reporting |
| Causation | You explain how change happened, not just that it did |
| Honesty | What did not change, and what was lost, are in the report |
| Communication | The public piece works for a stranger |

> [!warning] Watch the passive voice
> "Rights were extended" hides who did the work and who resisted. Name
> both. It is the most common way a rights history quietly becomes a
> story about a benevolent state.

[[Rights, Movements, and Resistance]] and
[[Indigenous Policy and Indigenous Response]] are the ground.
