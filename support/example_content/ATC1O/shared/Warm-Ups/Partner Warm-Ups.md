Working with another body is a separate skill from working with your own,
and it is the one most of your group choreography will depend on. Leaning
back to back, giving and taking weight, moving as a pair through a crowded
floor — none of it works on athleticism. It works on communication.

## Ask, then touch

The rule in this studio is that you ask before you place your hands on
someone, and you name where. "Can I put my hands on your shoulder blades?"
takes two seconds and it is what a professional rehearsal sounds like.

You may decline any partner exercise, at any point, without giving a
reason — and nobody comments on it or asks why. If a partner declines, you
say "no problem" and find another way to do the task. That is not a
disruption to the class; it is the class working.

Some contact needs asking every single time even between people who work
together often: anything near the neck, the waist, or the hips. Assume
nothing is standing permission.

## Weight and trust

Giving weight is gradual. Start at about a tenth of your weight, feel
whether your partner has taken it, and only then give more. Counterbalance —
two people leaning away from each other, each held up by the other —
depends on both people committing equally and neither one changing their
mind quietly. Agree on a word before you start, and say it out loud when you
want to come out: "down."

None of this is about who is bigger. Two people of very different sizes can
counterbalance well by adjusting their angles, and two people of identical
build will fall over if one of them is not really committed. Matched
attention beats matched size every time.

> [!warning] The three hard limits
> Do not take another person's full body weight until you have been taught
> how — that includes lifts, carries, and anything airborne. Never put
> hands on a partner's neck or head in a weight-bearing exercise. Never
> release a counterbalance without saying so first; a silent release drops
> two people, not one, and the one who did not know is the one who gets
> hurt.

Everything here is the physical version of what
[[Our Studio Agreement]] says in words. Carry it into rehearsal, where the
same skills show up as listening rather than lifting — see
[[Devising in a Group]].
