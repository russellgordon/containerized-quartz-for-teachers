"Just move however you feel" is the worst instruction in dance. It gives you
nothing to do, so you stand there deciding whether you look ridiculous —
and you probably do, because deciding whether you look ridiculous is not a
movement. A guided improvisation replaces that with a task, and the task
does the work.

The difference is not subtle. Given a task, your attention goes to the task
rather than to yourself, and movement you would never have chosen turns up
in your body within about ninety seconds. That movement is raw material:
this is where the vocabulary for your own choreography actually comes from.

## The rules that make it safe

- **Everyone moves at once.** Nobody is watching, because nobody has a
  spare moment to watch.
- **There is no wrong answer, but there is a task.** If you are doing the
  task, you are doing it right, and the results are supposed to differ.
- **Repeating is allowed.** You do not owe anyone constant novelty.
- **The teacher will narrate, not correct.** A prompt arriving mid-flow is
  a new door, not a mark against what you were doing.
- **You keep your eyes open unless told otherwise.** With eyes closed, stay
  on the spot or travel very slowly with your hands in front of you, and
  stop when the drum stops.

> [!tip]- Six prompts that reliably produce movement (click to expand)
> Move only one joint, changing which joint every eight counts. Cross the
> room without either foot leaving the floor. Make a shape, then its
> opposite, then something halfway between. Mirror a partner's slow
> movement so closely that a watcher cannot tell who leads. Move only in
> the silences between drum beats. Take one gesture from your morning and
> perform it four times larger, then four times slower.

Notice that every one of those names an element of dance — body, space,
time, energy, or relationship — and changes exactly one thing about it.
That is the whole trick, and [[The Elements of Dance]] is the full list to
raid when you want a prompt of your own.

Keep a note after every improvisation session: two or three movements you
want to keep. Those notes are the start of
[[Making a Movement Vocabulary]], and that vocabulary is what
[[The Movement Signature]] asks you to build a solo from. Improvisation that
is not harvested evaporates by Thursday.
