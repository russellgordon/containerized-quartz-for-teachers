**Due: late Unit 3.** Individual. About 600 words.

One dance work, watched properly and written about using the critical
analysis process.

## The work

A live performance if one is available, or a recorded work your teacher
approves. It must be a whole work, not a clip, and you must be able to
watch it twice.

## The four moves

Follow [[The Critical Analysis Process]] in order, and label the
sections:

1. **Initial reaction** — what you noticed first, before analysing.
2. **Description** — what is actually there: bodies, space, time,
   energy, sound, design. No judgement yet.
3. **Analysis and interpretation** — how the parts work together, and
   what the work seems to be about. Separate the *content* from the
   *expressive and interpretive* choices, and say what each contributes.
4. **Judgement** — is it effective, and by what standard?

Then one more paragraph: if the work addresses a social or environmental
issue, say how the choreography itself carries the argument — not the
programme notes, the movement.

## What is marked

- **Description before judgement.** The most common failure is an
  opinion in paragraph one with no evidence under it.
- **Specific evidence** — moments, not impressions.
- **The distinction** between content and interpretation, used well.
- **Vocabulary** — the terminology of dance, used accurately.

> [!tip] Watch it twice, differently
> First time, watch the whole thing without writing. Second time, watch
> one dancer only, or the space only, or the sound only. Almost every
> strong review in this course came from a second viewing with a narrow
> focus.

[[Content and Interpretation]] is the distinction that carries this, and
[[Dance That Argues]] is where the last paragraph comes from.
