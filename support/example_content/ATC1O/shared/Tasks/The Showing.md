**The culminating task. Eight rehearsal periods in Unit 4, performed for
an invited audience.** Groups of four to six. Three to four minutes.

A finished group work, made by you, run by you, shown to people who are
not in this class.

## The eight days

| Day | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Concept agreed, and the material you already have gathered |
| 2 | Generating: everyone brings movement |
| 3 | Structuring: the shape of the whole piece |
| 4 | First run, however rough — a walking version end to end |
| 5 | Stagecraft: sound, lighting states, costume decisions, spacing |
| 6 | Feedback showing to another group, then revision |
| 7 | Technical rehearsal in the performance space |
| 8 | Dress rehearsal and performance |

## What you deliver

- **The work itself**, performed with commitment.
- **Programme notes** (150 words) your audience reads: what it is, what
  you were working from, who made what.
- **A stagecraft plan** — sound cues, lighting states, entrances and
  exits, marked on a plan of the space.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Reproduction | You perform what was set, accurately, from memory |
| Refinement | The final version is visibly better than day 4 |
| Stagecraft | Choices about light, sound, costume, and space serve the work |
| Projection | Mood reaches the back row — eyes, face, breath, energy |
| Collaboration | The group ran itself; disagreements were handled in the room |

> [!tip] The audience is the point
> A studio showing is a rehearsal with witnesses. A performance is for
> people who did not watch it being made and will read only what your
> bodies tell them. Everything on this page follows from that
> difference.

[[Devising in a Group]] governs the making, [[Stagecraft]] the
production, and [[Projecting Mood]] the performing.
