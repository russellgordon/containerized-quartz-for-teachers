**Due: the end of Unit 3.** Pairs. A short presentation with movement.

Dance is not only a stage art. It is how communities mark occasions,
hold traditions, argue, and gather — and this task asks you to find that
where you live.

## What you do

**Find out** what dance actually happens in your community: schools,
studios, cultural organizations, community centres, powwows, festivals,
places of worship, competitive teams, social dance nights.

**Choose one social or community dance** and answer:

- What is its function — celebration, courtship, mourning, protest,
  belonging, exercise, competition?
- What does it tell you about the people who dance it?
- How is it learned and passed on?
- What would be lost if it stopped?

**Teach the class eight counts of it**, if it is a dance that is
appropriate to teach and share. If it is not — some dances are
ceremonial and not for public performance — say so and explain why
instead. Knowing the difference is part of the mark.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Research | Real local information, from people or organizations, not a search summary |
| Function | You explain what the dance does for the people who dance it |
| Culture | Accurate, respectful, and specific about who and where |
| Judgement | You handled the question of what may be shared |
| Teaching | Clear enough that the class can do it |

> [!warning] Ask before you share
> If the dance belongs to a community you are not part of, find out what
> that community says about sharing it. "I could not teach it, and here
> is why" is a complete and excellent answer.

[[What Dance Does in a Society]] and [[Dance and Community]] are the
ideas behind this.
