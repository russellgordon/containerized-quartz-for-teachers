Picking up choreography quickly is a skill, not a gift. Some people in this
room have been practising it since they were six, which is the only reason
they look fast. The strategies below are what they are doing, whether or not
they could tell you so — and they work just as well the first time you use
them.

You will be given movement three ways, often at once: **verbally** ("step,
step, drop"), **visually** (the teacher demonstrates), and **aurally** (the
counts, the music, the sound of feet). Most people lean hard on one channel.
Deliberately using the other two is the single biggest improvement available
to you.

## What actually works

- **Chunk it.** Nobody memorises thirty-two counts. They memorise four
  chunks of eight, then the joins between them. Learn the joins on purpose —
  transitions are where sequences fall apart.
- **Name it out loud.** Give each move a word: a real term where you know
  one, a nickname where you do not. "Plié, reach, spiral, fall." Words
  survive overnight far better than pictures do.
- **Watch the whole body first, then one part.** First pass, take in shape
  and rhythm. Second pass, pick a single thing — where the weight is, what
  the arms do — and watch only that.
- **Mark it with your hands.** Sitting out or waiting your turn, run the
  sequence small with your fingers on your knee. It is nearly as good as
  full-out for memory and costs nothing.
- **Mind the mirror.** When you face the teacher, their right is your left.
  Learn which side you are actually on, or you will learn the sequence
  backwards and have to unlearn it.
- **Repeat, then correct, then repeat.** Running it wrong ten times teaches
  wrong. Do it, find the one thing that was off, fix that thing, go again.

> [!example]- The three-pass method (click to expand)
> **Pass one:** watch only. Hands still, eyes on the whole body. **Pass
> two:** mark along, saying your words under your breath. **Pass three:**
> full out, and afterwards name the one moment you are unsure of — then ask
> about exactly that moment rather than asking to see it all again.

Filming a run and watching it back is the fastest way to find the gap
between what you think you did and what you did — see [[Filming Yourself]].
Learning the correct names as you go makes the next sequence quicker, which
is what [[Dance Terminology]] is for, and by the time you reach
[[The Showing]] you will be picking up material in a fraction of the time it
takes you now.
