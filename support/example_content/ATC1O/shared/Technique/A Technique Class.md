A technique class looks, from the outside, like the same thing over and over
again. It is meant to. The order of a technique class is a piece of
engineering — each part prepares the tissue and the attention that the next
part will demand, and nothing arrives before your body can meet it. If you
have trained for years, this shape will already be familiar. If you have
never danced, you will know it by the end of the second week, and that is
soon enough.

## The shape of the class

| Part | What it is actually for |
| --- | --- |
| Warm-up | Raise temperature and joint fluid before anything bears load |
| Floor work | Find deep torso support with the floor holding your weight |
| Standing centre | Alignment and balance in one place — slow and exact |
| Travelling | The same material through space, with momentum added |
| Combination | Remembering and performing, not only executing |
| Cool-down | Heart rate down, muscles back towards their resting length |

Rearrange that order and it stops working. Travelling before centre work
means momentum arrives before control, and your knees and ankles pay for it.
Deep stretching before the body is warm is the most common injury in a Grade
9 studio — cold tissue does not lengthen, it tears.

## Repetition is the method, not the filler

A movement is yours when you no longer have to assemble it out of
instructions. Getting there takes far more repetitions than feels
reasonable, which is why the same exercise returns every single day.

Repetition is not neutral, though. You learn whatever you repeat, including
a habit that will cost you later. That is why a teacher stops the class to
correct one shoulder, and why the tenth repetition done carefully is worth
more than the thirtieth done tired. When you are past the point of doing it
well, you are past the point of learning.

## Your class, your body

Part of your job is knowing which movements suit your current fitness and
which ones you are building towards. Marking — running a sequence small,
at low energy, with the shape but not the force — is a working dancer's
tool, not a way of opting out. Use it when you are tired, sore, or learning.

Muscle fatigue is a dull burn that fades. Sharp pain, joint pain, or pain
that gets worse as you continue is a stop signal, and asking for a
modification is technique, not retreat. [[The Safe Dancer]] is where you
write those personal limits down; [[Alignment and Placement]] and
[[The Standard Warm-Up]] are what the first half of every class is quietly
teaching you.
