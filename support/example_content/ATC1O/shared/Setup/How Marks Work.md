You are marked on what you do with movement, and on how you think about
it — not on how much dance you had done before September.

## The two parts

| Part | Weight | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Term work | 70% | The seven assessed tasks, studio practice, and seminars |
| Final evaluation | 30% | [[The Showing]] and [[The Portfolio and Reflection]] |

The final evaluation in a dance course is a performance and a portfolio,
not an essay. That is deliberate: a course that spends 110 hours on your
feet should not be settled by an hour of writing.

## Inside the term work

| Strand of work | Roughly | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creating and performing | 30% | The signature, the phrase, the composition study, the form study |
| Reflecting and analysing | 20% | The dance review, the community task, seminar contribution |
| Foundations | 20% | Safe practice, terminology, technique, collaboration |

## What earns a high mark here

- **Commitment.** Full effort on unfamiliar material beats casual
  competence on familiar material, every time.
- **Specific choices.** You can say what you chose and why — element,
  structure, energy — rather than "it felt right".
- **Revision.** The second version is genuinely better and you can point
  at what changed.
- **Care for others.** Collaboration and safe practice are assessed, not
  assumed.
- **Accurate language.** The right word for the movement, used because
  it is precise.

## If you are injured

Injured dancers are not excused, they are redeployed: notating,
outside-eye feedback, stagecraft, filming, rehearsal direction. Those
are real dance skills and they are marked as such. Tell me early and we
will plan it.

> Every task page ends with what earns the marks for that piece of work.
> Read it before you start.
