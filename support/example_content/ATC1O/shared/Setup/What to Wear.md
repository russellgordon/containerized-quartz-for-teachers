Clothes you can move in, every day. That is the whole rule, and here is
what it means in practice.

| Bring | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Fitted or stretchy clothing you can move in | I need to see your alignment to help you; a hoodie hides everything |
| Bare feet, or the footwear a form needs | Most of our work is barefoot; grip matters, and socks alone are a fall |
| Hair tied back | It is in your eyes on the second turn otherwise |
| A water bottle | You will need it |
| A layer for the cool-down | Bodies cool faster than they warm |

## What to leave off

Jewellery that swings or catches, watches, anything in a pocket, and
loose drawstrings. Nothing that has to be held on with one hand.

## About being looked at

Fitted clothing is a practical requirement, not a fashion one, and the
studio is not a place where anyone comments on anyone's body — see
[[Our Studio Agreement]]. If what you have does not feel workable, come
and talk to me; there is almost always a version that works.

For [[The Showing]] we will decide costume together as part of the
stagecraft, and the plan is that nobody has to buy anything.

> [!tip] Keep a set at school
> The single most common reason a student sits out is that the kit is at
> home. A spare set in a locker fixes an entire semester's worth of that.
