Most people assume a dance starts with a song. It is one option out of four,
and it is the one that does the most of your work for you — which is exactly
why it is worth testing the others. The experiment in this unit is simple:
take one phrase you already own and run it under each of the four conditions
below, back to back, without changing a movement. It will not feel like the
same phrase.

## Music

Music hands you tempo, mood, structure, and a shared count, all free.[^1]
The cost is that an audience will credit the music with anything it can. A
sad song makes almost any slow movement look like grief, including movement
that means nothing at all.

So use music, but audit it: run the phrase once in silence and see how much
of the feeling survives. Whatever survives is yours. And check that you are
allowed to use the track — permission is part of the work.

## Silence

Silence is the hardest and often the most striking. There is nothing to hide
behind, so timing has to come from the body, and the audience starts hearing
your breath and your feet on the floor, which become the score. Silence also
stretches time: eight counts of stillness in silence feels much longer than
eight counts of stillness under music, and you can use that.

## Text

Spoken words give you rhythm and meaning at once. Movement can agree with
text, contradict it, or ignore it entirely — and contradiction is usually
the most interesting of the three. A phrase performed while someone reads a
weather forecast becomes about something. Try it before you dismiss it.

## Pulse

A drum, a clap, a metronome. A pulse gives you structure with almost no mood
attached, which makes it the best condition for *building* — you can hear
whether the phrase is rhythmically clear without a song flattering it.

Running the same material four ways is the clearest example of
experimentation in this course, and it is exactly what
[[Experiment and Revise]] asks you to document. Do it early, while the
phrase is still cheap to change — after [[Phrase Building]] and well before
[[The Composition Study]] is due.

[^1]: Much of the music you will choose is counted in eights, which is why
    dancers count that way. Not all music is: plenty of traditions count in
    other groupings, or follow a rhythmic cycle rather than a bar. If your
    track will not fit into eights, that is the track being interesting, not
    you counting wrong.
