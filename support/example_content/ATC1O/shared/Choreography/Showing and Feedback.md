Every few classes, work in progress gets shown to the room. This is not a
performance and it is not a test. It is the fastest tool available for
making a dance better, and it only works if all three jobs in the room are
done properly: showing, watching, and responding.

## Showing

Walk on, take your starting position, wait until the room is quiet, and
begin. No introduction. No "sorry, we didn't finish the ending" — the room
already knows it is unfinished, and an apology tells everyone to watch for
the flaws you just named instead of the work you did.

Do say one sentence before you start, and make it a request: "watch whether
the canon reads." A specific request gets you specific answers. Then commit
fully, even to the parts you are unsure of, because half-marked movement
cannot be assessed by anybody, including you.

Afterwards, listen and write. Do not explain what you meant. If the room
did not see it, "what I meant was" does not change that — the feedback *is*
the information.

## Watching

Watch the whole thing before deciding what you think. Phones down. Notice
what you actually saw — a moment your eye went to, a place you got lost —
rather than searching for something clever to say. And applaud, every time,
for everyone. A room where showing is safe produces better dances than a
room where it is not.

## Responding

Describe what you saw, ask a question, then offer at most one suggestion.
Keep it about the work and small enough to act on before the next class.

| Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- |
| "It was really good" | "The unison drop made me sit up" |
| "It was confusing" | "I lost you when the two of you split" |
| "You should add music" | "What if the last eight counts were silent?" |
| "That bit was awkward" | "The join into the floor took longer than the rest" |

The left column is a verdict; the right column is usable. Notice that none
of the right-hand versions say whether the responder liked it, and none of
them are softer — they are just about something.

Feedback only counts once you have done something with it, so leave every
showing with one change you will try. [[The Critical Analysis Process]] is
the longer method behind this, and [[Experiment and Revise]] is the habit of
actually acting on what you heard.
