A phrase is the sentence of dance: a handful of movements that belong
together and finish somewhere. It is the unit everything larger is built
from, and learning to make one that holds together is the single most useful
compositional skill in this course.

Eight to sixteen counts is plenty. Beginners nearly always make phrases that
are too long, because length feels like effort — but a sixty-count phrase is
usually four unfinished ideas wearing a trench coat. Make it short, make it
hold, then make another.

## What makes a phrase hold together

- **A motif that returns.** One shape, gesture, or rhythm that comes back at
  least twice. This is what lets an audience recognise your phrase as one
  thing rather than a list.
- **A consistent quality — or one deliberate break in it.** If the energy
  changes four times at random, the phrase reads as four fragments.
- **Somewhere to go.** A phrase that ends where it started can work, but
  only if returning is the point. Otherwise, travel: in level, in
  direction, or in effort.
- **Transitions you have actually chosen.** Most of a phrase is the joining
  between the movements you were excited about. Unchosen transitions are
  where phrases go soft, and they are the first thing an audience sees.

## Knowing when it is finished

Not when you are tired of it. A phrase is finished when it passes three
tests, and you can run all three in ten minutes.

> [!tip] The two-repetition test
> Perform the phrase twice in a row without stopping. If the two versions
> differ in any way you did not intend, it is not a phrase yet — it is an
> improvisation you have partly memorised. Fix the difference, then test
> again. This one test catches more unfinished work than anything else in
> the course.

Test two: teach it to somebody in five minutes. Anything you cannot explain
is a decision you have not yet made. Test three: does the last movement
arrive somewhere the first movement could not have started? If yes, the
phrase has a shape.

Once you have two or three phrases that pass, arranging them is a completely
separate job — that is [[Structures for Making]]. Material to build from is
closer than you think; [[Warm-Up as Choreography]] shows the shortest route
to it. [[The Phrase]] is where this gets assessed.
