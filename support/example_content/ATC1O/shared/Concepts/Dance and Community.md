So far everything you have made has been for this room. The people watching
knew the assignment, knew you, and were about to be watched themselves. That is
a generous audience, and a limited one.

Dance changes when it leaves the studio. A piece performed at an assembly, in a
foyer, at a fundraiser, or in a feeder school's gym does something it cannot do
here — it puts people in a room together around something other than
themselves. That is what "contributing to a sense of community" means.

## What a dance can do for a group of people

- **Gather them.** Someone has to organise the room, and a performance is a
  reason to. Half of what an event does happens before it starts.
- **Mark an occasion.** Earth Day, a graduation, a remembrance, an opening.
  Occasions need form, and dance supplies form.
- **Direct attention.** A cause a poster cannot make anyone care about can be
  made unavoidable for four minutes.
- **Raise something practical.** Funds, awareness, volunteers, a conversation
  in the hallway afterwards.
- **Include people who do not dance.** Crew, front of house, filming, and
  whoever sets out the chairs.

## Knowing who the community is

"The community" is not one thing, and a piece made for one part of it may be
invisible to the rest. Before making work for an audience outside this class,
find out who they are — their ages, why they came, whether they chose to be
there, what they know about dance. A piece made for peers and shown to a room
of eight-year-olds does not fail because it is bad.

> [!tip] Ask the community rather than assuming it
> If you are performing for a cause or a group, talk to somebody from it first.
> One conversation tells you what they actually want said about them, and it
> prevents the most common failure in this kind of work: a well-meaning piece
> its subjects would not recognise. This applies with particular force when the
> community is not your own.

Community performance also has requirements a studio showing does not: a floor
you have never danced on, sightlines from the third row, no wings, sound you
did not test, an audience that may talk. Choose material that survives a bad
floor, and rehearse the entrance and the exit as carefully as the dance.

[[Dance and Community Task]] asks you to find what dance is already doing where
you live and to make something for a real audience beyond this room. Whether
such work changes anything is a genuinely open question, and
[[Can a Dance Change Anything?]] is where we argue about it.
