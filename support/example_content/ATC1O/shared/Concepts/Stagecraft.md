Stagecraft is everything around the dancing that changes how the dancing is
read: light, sound, what you wear, and the space you are in. A school studio
is not a theatre, and you will not have a two-hundred-channel lighting rig.
You do not need one. Four decisions made deliberately will do more for your
piece than any amount of equipment used carelessly.

## Light

The most useful fact in dance lighting is that **light from the side reveals
the body's shape and light from the front flattens it**. Front light is
what a classroom has, and it is why work looks less interesting under the
house lights than it did in your head. If you can get two fixtures onto the
wings, even work lights, do that before anything else. Light from behind
gives you silhouettes and no faces — powerful, and useless if the piece
depends on expression. A blackout is a full stop; use one, not five.

## Sound

One person operates sound, from a written cue sheet, and rehearses those
cues as many times as you rehearse the dancing. Set levels in the actual
room with people standing in it — bodies absorb sound, and an empty gym lies
to you. The seconds of silence before and after the track belong to the
piece, so decide what happens in them.

## What you wear

Costume has one requirement before any others: you must be able to do every
extreme of your choreography in it, tested on the floor rather than in a
mirror. Footwear changes grip, and grip changes what is safe — a shoe that
slides on your studio floor is a decision, not an accident. Beyond that,
costume tells the audience whether you are a group or a set of individuals,
and it either shows the line of the movement or hides it. Regalia worn in
Indigenous dance is not costume and is not ours to borrow; that argument
belongs in [[Does a Dance Belong to a Culture?]].

## Space

Decide where the front is and mark spacing on the floor with tape. Entrances
and exits are part of the dance — a piece that ends with dancers wandering
off has ended twice. Check your best moment from the far end of the front row.

> [!warning] Before anyone dances in the dark
> Tape down every cable, clear props off the floor, and walk your entrance
> and exit paths in the actual lighting state before you run the piece. No
> travelling in a full blackout. Quick changes happen in a lit space, not
> in a dark wing.

Every one of these choices should serve what the dance is about — see
[[Projecting Mood]] — and all of them get planned properly during
[[The Showing]].
