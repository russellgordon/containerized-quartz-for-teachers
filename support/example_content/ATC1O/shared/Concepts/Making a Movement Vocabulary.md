Every dancer has a vocabulary — a set of movements their body reaches for
first. Some of yours arrived from training. Some arrived from a sport, or a
job, or the way you carry a bag. It is already there. The work of this unit is
to notice it, widen it, and use it on purpose. That is different from learning
steps: a step is something you are given, and a vocabulary is something you
build. Yours will not look like anyone else's in the room.

## Where new material comes from

Movement has to come from somewhere, and "make something up" is the least
helpful place to start. In this course it comes from a **stimulus** — a source
outside your own habits that gives you something to respond to: an everyday
gesture, a piece of music, a rhythm with no music attached, an image, an
object, a place, or a theme that actually matters to you.

Five gestures from ordinary life — checking a phone, shielding your eyes,
pulling a sleeve down — will yield a phrase, if you are willing to distort
them. Speed one up eight times. Do one with the wrong body part. Make one
enormous. That is generation, and it is a skill, not a mood.

## Guided improvisation

The other source is improvisation, which sounds like the opposite of structure
but works best with a little of it. A guided improvisation gives one rule —
eyes closed, or stay below knee height, or move only when your partner stops —
and the rule does the thinking so you do not have to.

> [!tip] The rule is the point, not the obstacle
> When an improvisation feels impossible, resist changing the rule. The
> awkward, unfamiliar movement a restriction forces out of you is exactly the
> material you could not have invented on purpose. Our
> [[Improvisation Warm-Ups]] exist to put you in that state quickly, before
> your habits wake up.

Improvising near other people changes what you produce, too. You will find
yourself avoiding, echoing, or waiting. That is not interference — it is
relationship, one of [[The Elements of Dance]], generating material for you.

## Keeping what you make

Improvised movement disappears. If you do not record it, it is gone by the next
period, and you will spend that period remembering instead of building. Write
it down, film it, or both. [[The Movement Signature]] asks you to perform
material that is yours, and the students who find that task easy are the ones
who kept notes.
