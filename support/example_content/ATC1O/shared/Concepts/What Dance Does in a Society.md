A **social dance** is one that people do rather than watch — at a wedding, a
party, a gathering, a hall on a Friday night. There is no audience, or the
audience is also the dancers. Almost every society has them, and what a
society's social dances look like tells you a startling amount about the
society.

Consider the practical questions any social dance must answer. How many people
can do it at once? Do you need a partner, and may you choose them? How much
space does it take? Can a beginner join halfway through? The answers are never
only about movement.

## Four dances and what they were for

| Dance | Where and when | What it was for | What the form reveals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Court dance under Louis XIV | The French court, 17th century | Displaying rank and mastery of etiquette | Order of precedence — the hierarchy made visible |
| The sailor's hornpipe | Britain, widely danced in the 19th century | Exercise and entertainment in confined quarters | Danced largely on the spot, arms held close |
| Powwow dancing | Nations across North America, then and now | Gathering, honouring, keeping relationships | Living protocols; dancers wear regalia, never costume |
| Club and party dance | Cities everywhere, continuously | Belonging, courtship, showing off | Learned by watching; changes and travels fast |

Two of those were danced by people with power and two mostly by people without
it. The difference shows in the movement — in how much floor a dance assumes
it is entitled to, and in whether it needs anyone's permission.

## Dance as a record of conditions

Forms carry the circumstances of the people who made them. A dance made in a
small space stays small. A dance made where drums were banned finds its rhythm
somewhere else. A dance made for after twelve hours of work will not be
strenuous, and a dance made to prove your fitness to a crowd certainly will be.

*What were the conditions?* is the most reliable question you can ask of an
unfamiliar form. It gets you further than asking what the dance means, because
meaning is contested and conditions usually are not.

## The ones you are in

You are inside social dances right now, which makes them hard to see. Consider
what a school dance requires of you: who dances, who stands at the edge, what
is embarrassing and what is not, and who decided any of that. Then ask what a
stranger would conclude about us from watching. [[Who Is Dance For?]] takes
that up in seminar, and [[Forms From Around the World]] gives you something to
compare against.
