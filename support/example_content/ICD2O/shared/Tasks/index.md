Every task here spans multiple classes — the working periods are part
of the course, and how you use them is assessed. Each task page lists
its success criteria before you start, phrased as things an observer
could see in your work. Process counts — [[How Marks Work]] explains
how, and every criteria table traces back to our [[Learning Goals]].

## The five tasks

- [[The Algorithm Field Guide]] — document three real algorithms from
  your life the way a naturalist would, then design a new one precise
  enough for a classmate to execute.
- [[The Quiz Machine]] — build a Python quiz program on a topic you
  care about, then hand it to a play-tester and face the feedback.
- [[The Remix Project]] — take a working program from the Programs
  folder and make it do something meaningfully new.
- [[The Innovation Brief]] — investigate one current or emerging
  innovation and report who benefits, who bears the costs, and what
  question is still open.
- [[Launch Day]] — the culminating task: a computational artifact
  built for a real user, launched, defended, and documented for reuse.
