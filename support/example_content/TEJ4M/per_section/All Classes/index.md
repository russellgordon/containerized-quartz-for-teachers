One page per class, newest at the top of the list: the agenda we followed
and anything to do before next time. Away? Start here, run the warm-up
yourself, and get to a bench before you read anything about it — the page
holds the plan, but in this shop the hardware still comes first and the
ideas still earn their names afterwards.

What has changed since Grade 11 is what counts as finished. Last year the
question was at what current, within what tolerance, and how you know.
This year it is whether the thing will still work in a year, whether
somebody else could maintain it, and what happens when it fails — so
every agenda below runs in the same order: measure, discuss, name it,
practise, and write down the decision you made and why.

Our semester runs in four units:

- **Unit 1 — Systems and Specifications**: failure, requirements, block
  diagrams, tolerances, regulation, and margin, ending in
  [[The Specification]].
- **Unit 2 — Advanced Circuits and Interfacing**: transistors, op-amps,
  filters, sampling, and buses, ending in [[The Interface]].
- **Unit 3 — Control, Robotics, and Embedded Systems**: feedback, motor
  control, state machines, and timing, ending in [[The Control System]].
- **Unit 4 — Networks, Security, and the Profession**: network design,
  services, security, standards, and pathways, ending in
  [[The Engineering Design Project]] and the defence at
  [[The Engineering Review]].
