The lab provides the instruments, the components, and the bench. You
provide the thinking, dressed for a workshop where things get hot and
small pieces of metal occasionally leave at speed.

## Wear

- Closed-toe shoes on every lab day, without exceptions. A dropped
  soldering iron does not negotiate with sandals.
- Long hair tied back, and tied back properly, whenever an iron is on
  or anything is turning. Hair reaches a hot tip faster than you can
  react, and a motor shaft does not stop to consider.
- Sleeves secured, and dangling jewellery, lanyards, and hoodie
  drawstrings off at the bench. They find hot tips and spinning shafts
  without being asked.
- Nothing you would mourn if it met flux, solder, or a small burn.
  Cotton behaves better around heat than synthetics do.

## Bring

- [ ] Your journal or notebook — see [[Tech Journal]]. Bench notes on
      paper survive a circuit that has been dismantled, which is the
      entire point, and this year they also carry the reasons behind
      your decisions.
- [ ] Something to write with, and something to sketch a schematic
      with. Pencil beats pen for a schematic, and you will draw more of
      them this year than in the last two combined.
- [ ] A calculator you can actually use. Your phone stays in your bag
      during bench time, so a physical calculator is genuinely the
      practical option, and it works in every assessment.
- [ ] Your school login, memorised, and — once the capstone starts —
      wherever your firmware history lives, so you are never the person
      whose only copy is on a laptop at home. See
      [[Version Control for Firmware]].

## The lab provides

Safety glasses, soldering stations and extraction, meters,
oscilloscopes, logic analyzers, bench supplies, hand tools, straps and
mats, breadboards, components, and the boards themselves. Personal
tools are welcome to stay home: matched lab kit means every bench can
follow the same procedure, and nothing walks off by accident.

> [!warning] Eye protection is provided and it is not optional
> Safety glasses live at the bench and go on before any lead is
> clipped, any joint is soldered, and any spring-loaded anything is
> released. Clipped component offcuts travel fast and are exactly the
> right size to be a serious problem. If you wear prescription glasses,
> the lab has over-glasses that fit on top — ask, do not improvise.

> [!important] The phone rule
> Phones live in bags during bench time. A phone above an open circuit
> with a hot iron nearby is one fumble away from an expensive lesson,
> and the notifications are not helping your prediction. The one
> exception: photographing *your* bench's own work for your journal or
> your build documentation, which is encouraged — ask before
> photographing anyone else's, per [[Our Classroom Norms]].
