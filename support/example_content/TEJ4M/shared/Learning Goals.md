By the end of this course you will be able to take a problem stated in
words, turn it into a specification you could hand to somebody else,
and build the thing that meets it — then defend every choice you made
along the way. Grade 11 asked whether your circuit worked. This year
asks whether it will still work in a year, whether a stranger could
maintain it, and what happens when it fails. In the curriculum's own
words, three of the goals are:

Put plainly, week to week that means:

- **Specify before you build** — constraints, acceptance tests, and a
  block diagram come before a single component is chosen; see
  [[Writing a Specification]].
- **Design with margin** — a part is chosen against its tolerances and
  its derating curve, not just its headline rating
  ([[Reliability and Derating]]), and you can say what worst case you
  designed for.
- **Close the loop** — sense, decide, actuate, and hold a setpoint,
  with the timing behaviour to prove it — see
  [[Feedback and Control Systems]].
- **Hand it over** — firmware somebody else can read, a build log
  somebody else can follow, and a defence that survives a design
  review; see [[Writing Documentation Somebody Can Build From]] and
  [[The Engineering Design Project]].
