Five minutes at the start of class, every class. Third year, same
routines, same room, same five minutes — and if you were here in Grade
10 and Grade 11 you can already run most of them without being told
how. What has changed is the standard, again. In Grade 10 a good answer
described what would happen. In Grade 11 it carried a number and a
unit. This year a good answer carries a number, the condition it is
true under, and the margin you left — and it names what would prove it
wrong.

| Warm-up | What it trains |
| --- | --- |
| [[Name That Part]] | Reading a part you cannot pick up, from its markings |
| [[Predict the Circuit]] | Committing to a behaviour, not just a value |
| [[Read the Schematic]] | Following a signal across a drawing that has pages |
| [[Read the Waveform]] | Getting five honest numbers off a scope trace |
| [[Spot the Hazard]] | Catching danger on the bench *and* in the design |
| [[Which One Doesn't Belong]] | Defending a choice on a property that matters |
| [[Tech Headlines]] | Telling a specification from an advertisement |

None of these needs a powered bench, and none of them is filler. Each
keeps warm a habit the capstone will demand later, when you are behind
schedule, tired, and holding a board you designed yourself: precise
identification, quantitative prediction, reasoning you can defend to
somebody who disagrees, and an eye that catches trouble while it is
still cheap to fix.

Six of the seven are routines you have run before. [[Read the Waveform]]
is new, and it is new because the instrument is new to your daily
practice: this year an oscilloscope trace goes up on the board and you
read it the way you have been reading schematics since Grade 11.

> [!tip] Never a race, and now never a solo
> The first hand up was never the win condition and still is not. What
> is new this year: once you have your own answer, the routine is not
> finished until you have heard somebody else's and can say precisely
> where the two of you disagree. That is a design review in miniature,
> and it is the skill [[The Engineering Review]] will ask you to do in
> public.
