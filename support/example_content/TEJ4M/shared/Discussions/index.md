For two years these arguments were about technology that other people
made: who pays for it, who can repair it, who gets called a technology
worker. Those questions have not gone anywhere. But this year the
person choosing is you. You pick the margin. You decide what gets
tested before you call something finished. You decide whether the
device you designed can be opened, understood, and repaired by
somebody who is not you — and whether its security is real or
decorative.

Three rules now, and the third is new this year.

The first comes straight from [[Our Classroom Norms]]: we argue with
ideas, never at people, and coming in ready to change your mind is a
strength rather than a lack of one.

The second was added in Grade 11 and still holds: a claim about a
quantity needs a source. "Batteries are terrible for the environment"
is a mood; a figure with its units, its conditions, and where it came
from is an argument somebody can engage with, or refute, which is
better still.

The third belongs to Grade 12. **A design opinion needs its rejected
alternative.** Saying what you would do is half an argument. Saying
what you would *not* do, and what it would have cost, is the other
half — and it is the half that tells everyone whether you have thought
about it or simply picked a side.
