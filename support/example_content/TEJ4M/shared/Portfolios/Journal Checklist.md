Your [[Tech Journal]] is collected at the end of each unit. Before you
hand it in, run this check — it is the same lens I read with, so
anything you catch here is something neither of us needs to discuss
later. Ten minutes with this list is usually worth more than one extra
entry.

## The check

**Coverage**

- [ ] There is an entry for every bench day in the unit
- [ ] Entries were written close to the day, not reconstructed the
      night before collection — reconstructed entries always show,
      because they have no numbers and no decisions in them
- [ ] All six prompts appear across the unit: what I built, what I
      predicted, what the instrument said, what fought back, what I
      learned, what I decided
- [ ] Most circuit entries carry a sketch of what I actually wired,
      and any scope work carries a trace sketch with both scale
      settings written beside it

**Precision**

- [ ] Every measurement has a value, a unit, and the condition it was
      taken under — supply setting, probe points, load state,
      temperature, what else was running
- [ ] Prefixes are right and consistent: milliamps as mA, kilohms as
      kΩ, microseconds as µs, nothing recorded as a bare number
- [ ] Every fault is named and located, not just felt — "the regulator
      in the 12 V branch reached 61 °C after twenty minutes", not
      "something got hot" (see [[What a Strong Entry Looks Like]])
- [ ] Where a prediction and a measurement disagree, the entry says by
      how much and offers at least one cause

**Decisions**

- [ ] Every design choice I made this unit appears somewhere, with the
      alternative I rejected named
- [ ] At least one entry says what a choice *cost* me, not only what
      it bought
- [ ] Any margin I chose is written as a number against a limit — "I
      am at 45 % of the part's rating", not "it should be fine"
- [ ] Anywhere I picked something because it was the only one in the
      drawer, I said so instead of inventing a reason afterwards

**Honesty**

- [ ] At least one entry records something that genuinely fought back
      or confused me, not just the parts that worked
- [ ] My own contribution is named rather than hidden inside "we" —
      what *I* wired, measured, specified, coded, or misread
- [ ] Help is credited where it happened: a partner, a datasheet, an
      application note, a forum post, an AI assistant — the way
      [[Our Classroom Norms]] asks, and for the same reason a
      professional cites a source

**Growth**

- [ ] Each entry ends with one concrete "what I would try next"
- [ ] At least one earlier "try next" shows up later as a thing I
      actually tried, with a sentence about how it went
- [ ] At least one earlier *decision* shows up later as one I revisited
      — kept, changed, or overturned by a measurement

> [!tip] The last three boxes are the whole game
> Anyone can write "I will leave more margin next time." The journal
> becomes evidence on the day a later entry says "went back to the
> 12 V input I chose in Unit 1 and dropped it to 7.5 V; regulator now
> runs at 34 °C instead of 61 °C, and the fan I had budgeted for is
> no longer needed" — a complete loop from decision to measurement to
> revision. That loop is exactly what [[Showing Growth]] will ask you
> to point at, so spend the semester leaving yourself things to point
> at.
