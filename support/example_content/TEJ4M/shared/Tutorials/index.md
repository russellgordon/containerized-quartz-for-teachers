Some skills in this course are not concepts and not labs — they are
*ways of working*. Driving an instrument so that what it shows you is
true. Finding the limit in a datasheet that the datasheet did not put
in a table. Keeping a history of your firmware so that "which version
was on the board?" has an answer. Writing a document somebody could
build from without you in the room.

Each tutorial below teaches one of those properly, once, so that every
lab and task page can link here instead of re-explaining it in a hurry.

Skim them all in the first week, then return to each the moment it
becomes urgent. Two become urgent almost immediately:
[[Bench Power Supply Habits]] earns its keep before the first supply is
switched on rather than after, and
[[Reading a Datasheet Like an Engineer]] earns its keep the first time
a task asks you to defend a component choice rather than follow a
parts list.

If you took the Grade 11 course, two names are familiar and two are
familiar-but-grown. [[Using an Oscilloscope Properly]] assumes you can
already drive a scope and goes after the ways a scope can be confidently
wrong. [[Reading a Datasheet Like an Engineer]] assumes you can find the
absolute maximum ratings and goes after the graphs at the back, the
thermal numbers, and the errata sheet nobody mentioned. The rest are
new, because this year the deliverable is a design rather than a build.
