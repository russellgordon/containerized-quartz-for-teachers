Three notes about the reproduction, because being trustworthy about
small things is the point of a page like this:

- **This curriculum is current.** The Ministry replaced the Grades 9
  and 10 technological education curriculum effective 2024–25, but
  Grades 11 and 12 technological education courses — including this
  one — continue to be based on the 2009 document reproduced here.
- **The document prints no teacher prompts.** What you see on each
  page is the whole of what the Ministry prints beneath that
  expectation.
- **Two printing quirks are reproduced rather than tidied up.**
  `D3.1` prints "electronics(e.g.," with no space before the
  parenthesis, and the document writes "electronics technology" in
  `C1.1` and `C2.2` but "electronic technology" in `C2.1`. Both are
  the Ministry's own text. Correcting them here would make this
  folder disagree with the document it claims to quote.

Each page keeps its code, and every lab, concept, and design decision
in this course links back to the codes it addresses. The codes are
also tags, so every page tagged `#B4` can be gathered in one place.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing
this?", the answer is a link. When a department writes a course
outline, or a teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes
are what make one person's planning legible to another.
