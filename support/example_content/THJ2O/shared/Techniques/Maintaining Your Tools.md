Here is the sentence to take out of this page: the blunt tool is the
dangerous one. A sharp pruner slices through a stem where you aimed it. A
blunt one skids, so you push harder, and when it finally goes through you
have no control over where it goes next. Most cuts in a shop come off a
tool somebody had stopped looking after.

## Cleaning, every time

At the end of a period, before anything goes back on the wall:

- **Scrape and brush the soil off** blades, tines, and treads.
- **Dry it.** A rag is thirty seconds; rust is permanent.
- **Wipe metal with a lightly oiled rag** — a thin film, not a slick.
- **Check the handle** for splits, splinters, and a loose head. A cracked
  handle is out of service, not "careful with that one".
- **Disinfect pruners and saws between plants** when you have been
  cutting anything diseased, so you are not carrying the problem around
  the woodlot — [[Pests and Diseases in Our Woodlot]] explains why.

## Sharpening

Sharpening restores an edge; it does not reshape a tool. Work with the
tool clamped or braced, never held in your lap, and keep both hands
behind the edge. Push the file or stone in one direction, along the bevel
the manufacturer already ground — chasing your own angle rounds a good
edge off within a few sessions. Spades and hoes take a coarse file edge
at a shallow bevel on one face only; pruners and loppers take a fine edge
on the bevelled blade only, and the flat blade stays flat. Saws are set
and sharpened by somebody trained, or replaced.

> [!tip] The edge tells you when
> Rest the tool on a piece of scrap and make one cut. If it needs force,
> it needs sharpening. Waiting until it will not cut at all means you
> have been forcing it for weeks.

## Powered equipment, and storage

Powered equipment runs on a written schedule, not on how it feels: fuel
and oil to the manual's spec, air filters cleaned, lines and belts
inspected, blades sharpened *and balanced*, fuel drained before storage.
An unbalanced mower blade shakes a machine apart and throws debris
unpredictably. All of it happens with the machine off, the spark plug
lead pulled or the battery out, and your teacher's authorisation — see
[[Power Tools and Equipment]].

Tools go back to the same marked place, dry, edges down or covered, long
handles hung rather than leaned. Anything damaged is tagged and reported
the same period, not left for the next person to find with their hand.
Your maintenance log is evidence — see [[The Evidence File]].
