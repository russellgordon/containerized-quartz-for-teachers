Here is the sentence to take out of this page: the blunt tool is the
dangerous one. A sharp pruner slices through a stem where you aimed it. A
blunt one skids, so you push harder, and when it finally goes through you
have no control over where it goes next.

## Cleaning, every time

At the end of a period, before anything goes back on the wall:

- **Scrape and brush soil off** blades, tines, and treads.
- **Dry it, then wipe metal with a lightly oiled rag** — a thin film, not
  a slick. A rag is thirty seconds; rust is permanent.
- **Check the handle** for splits, splinters, and a loose head. A cracked
  handle is out of service, not "careful with that one".
- **Disinfect pruners and saws between plants** after cutting anything
  diseased — [[Pests and Diseases in Our Woodlot]] explains why.

## Sharpening

Sharpening restores an edge; it does not reshape a tool. Work with the
tool clamped or braced, never held in your lap, and keep both hands
behind the edge. Push the file or stone in one direction, along the bevel
the manufacturer ground — chasing your own angle rounds a good edge off
within a few sessions. Spades and hoes take a coarse file edge at a
shallow bevel on one face only; pruners and loppers take a fine edge on
the bevelled blade only. Saws are sharpened by somebody trained.

> [!tip] The edge tells you when
> Rest the tool on a piece of scrap and make one cut. If it needs force,
> it needs sharpening. Waiting until it will not cut at all means you
> have been forcing it for weeks.

## Powered equipment, and storage

Powered equipment runs on a written schedule: fuel and oil to the
manual's spec, air filters cleaned, lines and belts inspected, blades
sharpened *and balanced*, fuel drained before storage. An unbalanced
mower blade shakes a machine apart and throws debris unpredictably. All
of it happens with the machine off, the spark plug lead pulled, and your
teacher's say-so.

Tools go back to the same marked place, dry, edges down or covered.
Anything damaged is tagged and reported the same period, not left for the
next person to find with their hand — [[The Evidence File]].
