Almost everything this course builds or plants begins with a hand tool.
They look simple, and that is exactly the problem — nobody reads
instructions for a spade, so nobody finds out how a spade injures people
until it happens to them.

## What each one is for

| Tool | The job it is built for | How it injures people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spade | Slicing sod, edging a bed, cutting roots | The blade skids off a root into a boot; backs strain when you lift with your arms |
| Round-point shovel | Moving loose soil, compost, gravel | Overloading it — a full blade of wet soil is far heavier than it looks |
| Garden fork | Loosening soil, lifting root balls | Tines through a foot when it is left lying prongs-up |
| Hand pruners | Green wood about the thickness of a pencil | Deep cuts to the hand holding the branch |
| Loppers | Wood up to roughly the thickness of your thumb | Branches that spring back; fingers between the handles |
| Pruning saw | Anything thicker than the loppers can take | It cuts on the pull stroke and does not stop at skin |
| Rake and hoe | Levelling, clearing, weeding between plants | Standing on the head and taking the handle in the face |
| Wheelbarrow | Moving heavy things once instead of twice | Tipping sideways on a slope; fingers crushed against a wall |
| Hammer, saw, chisel, screwdriver | The shop half of this course | Struck thumbs, saw teeth, chisels driven towards a holding hand |

## How to hold one

- **Two hands, body behind the tool.** Power comes from your legs and
  your weight. A spade you stand on is under control; one you swing
  is not.
- **Cut and push away from yourself**, and from whoever is beside you.
  Look at where the blade goes if it slips, and move your other hand.
- **Nothing is a pry bar except a pry bar.** Prying snaps handles and
  turns blades into projectiles.

## Where the injuries actually come from

Not from the cutting — from everything around it. Tools laid on the
ground vanish into grass and get stood on. Tools carried loose in a
wheelbarrow with the edges up become a hazard the moment it tips. A tool
passed blade-first invites someone to grab the wrong end.

So carry long-handled tools at your side, head down and forward where you
can see it, pass anything with an edge handle-first, and put tools down
in the same place every time — that is what the tool wall is for.

And the counter-intuitive one: the blunt tool is the dangerous one,
because it needs force, and force removes your control. See
[[Maintaining Your Tools]], read [[Personal Protective Equipment]] first,
and expect all of it to be assessed during [[The Shop Build]].
