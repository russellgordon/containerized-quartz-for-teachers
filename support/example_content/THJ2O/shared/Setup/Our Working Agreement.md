A shop with power tools in it and a site with other people's property on
it only work if everyone can be relied on. This is what we agree to, and
it binds everyone in the room, including your teacher.

> [!warning] Stopping work is always the right call
> If something feels unsafe — a tool, a job, a slope, a machine, a person
> not paying attention, or a task you have not been trained for — you
> stop, and you say so. You will never be in trouble for stopping and you
> will never lose a mark for it. If you turn out to have been wrong, the
> answer is "thanks for saying", and we go back to work.
>
> This is not a classroom kindness. Under Ontario law a worker may refuse
> work they believe is unsafe, and you are practising that right here so
> that it is a habit before you are paid to have it —
> [[The Law on the Job]].

## Looking after each other

- **Know where everyone is** before you swing, cut, lift, or reverse, and
  announce what you are about to do.
- **No horseplay near tools, machines, water, or vehicles.** There is no
  version of this that is funny afterwards.
- **Nobody works alone** on a job that needs two people, and nobody works
  alone at the water's edge at all.
- **Correction is information.** A note from me, or from a classmate who
  spots a hazard, is not an insult.
- **Everyone gets to learn the skill.** Nobody hogs the good jobs, and
  nobody is left with the wheelbarrow all term because of who they are.

## Tools, and other people's property

Tools are shared, expensive, and a mistreated tool becomes a dangerous
tool. Clean it, return it to its marked place, and report damage the same
period — reporting is never trouble, but hiding it is, because the next
person's hand finds the crack. See [[Maintaining Your Tools]].

We work on the school's grounds, on a public shoreline, in a woodlot, and
on a farm that is somebody's business. We are guests, and permission is
specific: it covers the bed we were asked to work on, not the next one.
Touch nothing at the farm without asking — equipment, animals, produce,
gates, switches. Leave gates as you found them, collect nothing you were
not asked to, and dig nothing you were not sent to dig. What people tell
you about their business goes in your notes, not online, and photographs
follow the rules in [[Photographing Your Work]].

## When a rule is broken

The first time, we stop, sort it out, and you carry on — most breaches
are somebody forgetting, and the fix is a reminder. If it repeats, or if
it put someone in danger, you step out of the practical work for that
period and complete the learning another way; you will still have to
demonstrate the skill before you are signed off. Anything serious follows
the school's process, and your family hears it from me first.

None of that is a mark penalty. Safety is not a deduction in this course
— it is a stop, and then a conversation. [[How Marks Work]] explains why.
