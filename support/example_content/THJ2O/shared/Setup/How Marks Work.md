You are marked on what you can do, on how well you can account for it,
and on how you conduct yourself on a site. Not on how much you already
knew about plants in September.

## The two parts

| Part | Weight | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Term work | 70% | The assessed tasks, practical periods, and your records |
| Final evaluation | 30% | [[The Evidence Portfolio]] and a practical demonstration |

The final evaluation in a shop-and-site course is not an essay. It is
your portfolio, defended in conversation, plus a practical demonstration
in the examination period where you set up a task, choose and check the
tools, work safely, and explain what you are doing while you do it.

## Inside the term work

These are shares of your whole course mark, and together they make up the
70%.

| Strand of work | Roughly | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practical skills | 35% | Building, propagating, planting, maintaining, measuring — the work itself |
| Knowledge and planning | 20% | Plans, drawings, calculations, identification, reports, and the thinking behind a job |
| Safety and professional practice | 15% | [[The Safety Ticket]], your habits on site, records, reliability, and how you work with other people |

## Safety is not a deduction

This is the part that surprises people. **A safety failure is not marked
down. It is a stop-work.** The job halts, we deal with it, and you
demonstrate the skill safely before you are signed off on it — see
[[Our Working Agreement]].

That is exactly how it works on a real site, and it is why the 15% above
is not "marks for being careful". It is marks for the professional
practice that the stop-work protects: knowing the hazards, running the
check, keeping the records, speaking up.

## What earns a high mark here

- **The work stands up.** A joint that is square, a planting that
  establishes, a bed that drains. Outdoor work is marked by whether it
  survives.
- **You can account for your choices.** Why that fastener, that spacing,
  that species, that method — see
  [[Design and Problem Solving in the Green Industries]].
- **Your numbers are right**, checked before you order rather than after.
- **The record is current and honest**, including what went wrong —
  [[Writing a Work Record]] and [[The Evidence File]].
- **You are someone the crew can rely on.** Ready on time, tools back,
  hazards reported, teammates looked after.

## If you miss practical time

Practical skills have to be demonstrated, so missed periods get made up
rather than averaged away. Tell me early and we will find the time.

> [!tip] The criteria live on the task
> Every task page ends with what earns the marks for that specific piece
> of work. Read it before you start, not after you hand it in.
