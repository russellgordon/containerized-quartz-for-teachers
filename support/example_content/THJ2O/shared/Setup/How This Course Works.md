Green Industries is learned by doing green industry work. You will spend
most of this course on your feet — in the shop, on the school grounds, in
the woodlot, at the waterfront, and at a working vegetable farm five
minutes down the road. The pages on this site are what you read before
and after that work, not instead of it.

## A normal period

1. **Briefing, about ten minutes.** What today's job is, where it
   happens, what the hazards are, and who is doing what.
2. **Kit and check.** Boots, gear, tools, and the check in
   [[Before You Start Work]]. This is not optional and it is not quick
   on the days it should not be.
3. **The work, about fifty minutes.** Building, planting, propagating,
   measuring, maintaining, or surveying.
4. **Clean, store, and record.** Tools cleaned and back on the wall,
   site left safe, and your work record written while it is fresh —
   [[Writing a Work Record]].

## The four places

- **The shop and the school grounds.** Tools, construction, propagation,
  and the beds we actually maintain all year.
- **The woodlot**, a walk away. Tree and shrub identification, forest
  health, pests and disease.
- **The waterfront**, also a walk. Shoreline planting, erosion, and
  water quality — the site of [[Planting a Shoreline]].
- **The farm.** A working vegetable operation, visited as a visit. It is
  somebody's business and somebody's living, and we behave accordingly.

> [!note] Roughly two periods in three are outdoors or in the shop
> Which means the weather is part of the course, and the kit list in
> [[What to Wear and Bring]] is the difference between a good day and a
> miserable one. We work in rain and in cold. We stop for lightning,
> extreme heat, and unsafe ground, and you will know by the morning.

## What the course asks of you

- **Turn up ready to work.** Boots on, water in the bag, phone away.
- **Ask for the training before you touch the tool.** Nobody expects you
  to already know — see [[Power Tools and Equipment]].
- **Stop when something is wrong**, and say so. That is the most
  professional thing you will do all year.
- **Keep the record current.** [[The Evidence File]] starts in week one
  and becomes your final evaluation.
- **Look after the person working next to you.**

## The shape of the year

Four units: how living things grow; tools, measurement, and making;
growing and producing; and the industry around us. Along the way you will
build, propagate, run a small production crop, and report on a real pest
problem in our woodlot.

It ends in [[The Site Project]] — a genuine green-industry job on one of
our sites, carried from survey and plan through to a maintenance schedule
the next class can follow. Marks are explained in [[How Marks Work]], and
[[Using This Site]] shows you around these pages.
