Closed boots, every practical period, no exceptions. That is the one rule
that will send you to the library if you get it wrong, because there is
no safe job in this course for someone in canvas shoes. Everything else
here sits underneath it.

## Every practical period

| Bring | Why, specifically |
| --- | --- |
| Closed leather or synthetic boots, laced, ideally over the ankle | A spade, a paver, or a fork tine goes straight through canvas and mesh. No sandals, no open backs, no slip-ons |
| Safety footwear with a green triangle patch for load work | The green triangle means a protective toe and a puncture-resistant sole — required for pavers, deliveries, and shop machines |
| Long trousers | Poison ivy, thorns, string trimmers, and sun |
| A top with sleeves, nothing loose or dangling | Loose cuffs, hood strings, and jewellery catch in rotating machinery |
| Work gloves that fit — leather or leather-palmed | A loose glove is worse than none. Bring your own pair and keep them dry |
| A water bottle, at least one litre, filled before we leave | Heat sneaks up on people, and there is no tap in the woodlot |
| A hat with a brim, and sunscreen | Full sun on the grounds and the shoreline from April to October |
| A rain shell and a warm layer, seasonally | We work in rain. We do not work wet and cold |
| Notebook and pencil in a pocket | Measurements, plant lists, and your work record |
| Hair tied back, long jewellery left at home | The commonest catch hazard in a shop |

## What the school supplies

Eye protection, hearing protection, dust masks, high-visibility vests,
the first aid kit, and every tool and machine you will use. Those live in
the shop and are issued for the period, so you do not need to buy them.
We also keep spare gloves and a few pairs of loaner boots in common
sizes.

## If the kit is a problem

Boots cost money. If getting a pair is difficult, tell me privately
before the first practical period and we will sort it out quietly — there
is a way, there always is, and nobody else needs to know. The same goes
for a rain jacket or a water bottle. What does not work is turning up
hoping it will be fine: you will sit out, and you will have to make the
period up.

## Leave at home, and keep a spare set

Earbuds and headphones stay home, because you cannot hear a shout, a
machine, or a reversing vehicle through them. Phones live in a pocket and
come out for photographs and measurements only —
[[Photographing Your Work]].

The commonest reason a student misses a practical period is that the kit
is at home, so a spare pair of boots and a set of work clothes in a
locker solves an entire semester of that. The reasoning behind each item
is in [[Personal Protective Equipment]], and the hazards they exist for
are in [[Hazards on a Green Industry Site]].
