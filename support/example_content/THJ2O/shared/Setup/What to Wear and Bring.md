Closed boots, every practical period, no exceptions. That is the one rule
that will send you to the library if you get it wrong, because there is
no safe job in this course for someone in canvas shoes. Everything else
on this page is detail underneath it.

## Every practical period

| Bring | Why, specifically |
| --- | --- |
| Closed leather or synthetic boots, laced, ideally over the ankle | A spade, a paver, or a fork tine goes straight through canvas and mesh. No sandals, no open backs, no slip-ons |
| Safety footwear with a green triangle patch for any load work | The green triangle means a protective toe and a puncture-resistant sole — required for pavers, deliveries, and shop machines |
| Long trousers | Poison ivy, thorns, string trimmers, and sun |
| A top with sleeves, nothing loose or dangling | Loose cuffs, hood strings, and jewellery catch in rotating machinery |
| Work gloves that fit you — leather or leather-palmed | A loose glove is worse than none. Bring your own pair and keep them dry |
| A water bottle, at least one litre, filled before we leave | Heat sneaks up on people; there is no tap in the woodlot |
| A hat with a brim, and sunscreen | Full sun from April to October on the grounds and the shoreline |
| A rain shell and a warm layer, seasonally | We work in rain. We do not work wet and cold |
| Notebook and pencil in a pocket | Measurements, plant lists, and your work record |
| Hair tied back, long jewellery left at home | The single commonest catch hazard in a shop |

## What the school supplies

Eye protection, hearing protection, dust masks, high-visibility vests,
the first aid kit, and every tool and machine you will use. Those live in
the shop and are issued for the period — you do not need to buy them. We
also keep a small stock of spare gloves and a few pairs of loaner boots
in common sizes.

## If the kit is a problem

Boots cost money. If getting a pair is difficult, come and tell me
privately before the first practical period and we will sort it out
quietly — there is a way, there always is, and nobody needs to know about
it. The same goes for a rain jacket or a water bottle.

What does not work is turning up hoping it will be fine. It will not be
fine; you will sit out, and you will have to make the period up.

## Leave at home or in the locker

Earbuds and headphones. You cannot hear a shout, a machine, or a
reversing vehicle through them, and that is the whole reason. Phones go
in a pocket and come out for photographs and measurements only —
[[Photographing Your Work]].

## Keep a set at school

The commonest reason a student misses a practical period is that the kit
is at home. A spare pair of boots and a set of work clothes in a locker
solves an entire semester of that, and it means a wet day does not cost
you a period.
