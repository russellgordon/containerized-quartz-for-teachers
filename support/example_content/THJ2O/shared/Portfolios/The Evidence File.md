Nobody in this industry is hired on a mark. They are hired on evidence —
what you can show you have actually done, and how well you can account
for it. Your evidence file is where that gets collected, starting in the
first week and finishing as [[The Evidence Portfolio]], which is your
final evaluation.

Collect as you go. Rebuilding a semester's evidence in June from memory
produces a thin file and an honest teacher can tell.

## What counts as evidence

Not everything you touch. A piece of evidence shows a skill, and it lets
someone else check the claim.

- **Work records.** What the job was, what you did, what went wrong, what
  you changed. This is the backbone of the file —
  [[Writing a Work Record]] is the format.
- **Photographs.** Before, during, and after, of a build, a bed, a bench,
  or a planting — taken the way [[Photographing Your Work]] describes.
- **Drawings, plans, and cut lists.** The site plan for
  [[The Site Project]], the quantities you worked out, the revision you
  made when the first version did not fit.
- **Data.** Germination counts from [[The Growing Trial]], yields from
  [[The Production Run]], the observation log behind
  [[The Pest and Disease Report]].
- **Safety credentials.** [[The Safety Ticket]], and anything else you
  earn — WHMIS training, first aid, a certificate from outside school.
- **Maintenance and tool logs.** Unglamorous, and employers read them
  closely, because they show whether you look after equipment.
- **What other people said.** A note from a client, a supervisor, or a
  teammate. Ask for it while the job is fresh.
- **Your skills record**, kept honestly — [[Your Skills Record]].

## What makes a piece strong

A photograph of a finished bed proves somebody built a bed. The same
photograph, alongside the plan it came from, the quantities you
calculated, a note on the drainage problem you hit at the north end, and
the fix — that proves *you* built it and understood it.

So every item gets a caption: what it is, when, what your part was, and
what you would do differently. Two sentences. Without it the item is
decoration.

> [!success]- What a strong entry looks like (click to expand)
> **Raised bed, north grounds — 14 October.** Built from my own 1:20 plan
> with one other student. I cut and squared the frame and calculated the
> soil volume at 0.72 m³, which came out one bag short because I measured
> the depth after the frame settled. Next time I would order the soil
> after setting the frame, not before. Photographs: plan, framing,
> finished. Related: the cut list and the corrected volume calculation.

## Keeping it

One folder, one place, backed up, named consistently, filed the same day.
A file you have to search for is a file you stop adding to. If yours is
falling behind, that is a normal thing to bring to [[Help Sessions]].
