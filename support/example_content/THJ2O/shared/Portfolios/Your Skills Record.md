Employers in the green industries hire for two things they rarely put on
the job posting. The first is a set of Essential Skills — the general
skills every job uses. The second is work habits — how you behave when
the work gets tedious, wet, or late. The Ontario Skills Passport names
both, and this record is where you track yours with evidence.

## Essential Skills, in this course

| Essential Skill | Where you use it here |
| --- | --- |
| Reading text | A safety data sheet, a seed packet, a manufacturer's fact sheet, a maintenance manual |
| Writing | Work records, a maintenance schedule handed over to the next class |
| Document use | Reading a working drawing, a plant list, a WHMIS label, a production log |
| Computer use | Recording trial data, sizing and filing photographs, looking up a species |
| Oral communication | Briefing a partner before a lift, asking a farmer a real question |
| Numeracy | Soil volume, plant spacing, unit conversions, costing a job |
| Thinking skills | Diagnosing why one tray germinated and the next did not |

## Work habits

Working safely comes first, and it is the one habit that is not
negotiable — see [[Safe Shop and Site Practice]]. Then: teamwork,
reliability, organisation, working independently, initiative,
self-advocacy, and customer service. On [[The Site Project]] every one of
those gets exercised, because there is a real site, a real deadline, and
somebody who has to live with the result.

Two of them are worth spelling out because students undersell them.
**Self-advocacy** is asking for the training, the equipment, or the
explanation you need — it is a skill, not a nuisance. **Customer
service** is what happens when the person whose garden it is turns up and
asks a question while you are covered in soil.

## Recording it honestly

An entry has three parts: the skill, what you actually did, and where the
evidence is.

**Numeracy — 3 November.** Calculated the growing medium for the
propagation bench at 0.4 m³ and ordered it. Evidence: the calculation
sheet and the delivery note in [[The Evidence File]].

That is the shape. Notice what it is not: it is not "I am good at
numeracy". A claim without an example is worth nothing to a reader, and
they can tell instantly.

Be honest about the weak ones too. "Reliability — I was late setting up
twice in October, so I now pack the tools the period before" is a
stronger entry than a row of unbroken excellence, because it shows you
can see your own work clearly. Nobody believes a record with no rough
edges in it.

## When to write it

Weekly, in about ten minutes, while you still remember what happened.
Pair it with the work record for the same period —
[[Writing a Work Record]] — and it stops being a chore. This record
becomes a section of [[The Evidence Portfolio]], and it is the part
employers read first.
