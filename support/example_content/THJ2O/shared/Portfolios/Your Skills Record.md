Employers in the green industries hire for two things they rarely put on
the job posting: a set of Essential Skills, and work habits. The Ontario
Skills Passport names both, and this record is where you track yours with
evidence.

## Essential Skills, in this course

| Essential Skill | Where you use it here |
| --- | --- |
| Reading text | A safety data sheet, a seed packet, a manufacturer's fact sheet, a maintenance manual |
| Writing | Work records, and a maintenance schedule handed to the next class |
| Document use | A working drawing, a plant list, a WHMIS label, a production log |
| Computer use | Recording trial data, sizing and filing photographs, looking up a species |
| Oral communication | Briefing a partner before a lift; asking a farmer a real question |
| Numeracy | Soil volume, plant spacing, unit conversions, costing a job |
| Thinking skills | Working out why one tray germinated and the next did not |

## Work habits, recorded honestly

Working safely comes first and is not negotiable —
[[Safe Shop and Site Practice]]. Then teamwork, reliability,
organisation, working independently, initiative, self-advocacy, and
customer service. Every one of them gets exercised on
[[The Site Project]], because there is a real site, a real deadline, and
somebody who has to live with the result.

An entry has three parts: the skill, what you actually did, and where the
evidence is.

**Numeracy — 3 November.** Calculated the growing medium for the
propagation bench at 0.4 m³ and ordered it. Evidence: the calculation
sheet and the delivery note in [[The Evidence File]].

That is the shape, and notice what it is not — "I am good at numeracy" is
a claim without an example, which is worth nothing to a reader who can
tell instantly. Be honest about the weak ones too: "I was late setting up
twice in October, so I now pack tools the period before" is stronger than
a row of unbroken excellence.

## What the record is for

Written weekly, in about ten minutes, it becomes a section of
[[The Evidence Portfolio]] and it is the part employers read first. Add
certifications as you earn them — first aid, CPR, WHMIS — because green
industry postings ask for them, and note the industry associations,
mentoring programs, and college or apprenticeship routes you come across,
so the path is written down before you need it.
[[The Industry Nearby]] is where that search starts.
