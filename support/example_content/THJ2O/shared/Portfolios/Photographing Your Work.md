Most of what you make in this course cannot be handed in. A bed stays in
the ground, a bench stays on the site, a crop gets eaten. The photograph
is the only thing that comes with you, so it has to carry the evidence —
and a photograph taken casually carries almost none.

## Before, during, after — and the before is the one people miss

Take the *before* photograph the moment you arrive, from a spot you can
stand in again. Nobody believes a shoreline planting improved anything
without the picture of what it looked like first, and by the time you
realise that, the bank is already planted.

- **Before.** Wide, showing the whole area and something fixed at the
  edge — a post, a corner, a tree — so you can line up the later shots.
- **During.** Your hands doing the actual skill: the cut, the joint, the
  root ball going in, the sharpening. Get someone else to take it.
- **After.** Same spot, same framing, same height as the before. Then one
  close shot of the detail you are proud of and one of the whole thing in
  its setting.

## Getting a usable frame

Stand so the light comes from behind or beside you, and check your own
shadow is not lying across the subject. Overcast days are the easiest;
bright midday sun blows out a light gravel path and buries a bed edge in
black shadow. Fill the frame — a build photographed from across the yard
is a photograph of a yard.

Hold the phone level, especially for a *before and after* pair, and shoot
square-on to the face you want measured. If scale matters — a bed depth,
a stem thickness, a paver — put a tape or a ruler in the shot. That one
habit turns a picture into data.

Turn the flash and any filters off. Wipe the lens; a phone that has been
in a work pocket has soil on it. Take three of everything, because a
photograph you cannot retake is one you should not have taken once.

## Naming and filing

Rename the same day, always in the same shape, so the file is findable in
June without opening it:

```
lastname-sitename-stage-yyyy-mm-dd
gordon-shoreline-before-2026-05-04
gordon-shoreline-after-2026-06-11
gordon-raisedbed-during-cutting-2026-10-14
```

Then write the caption while you still remember: what it shows, when,
and what your part was. Photographs without captions become anonymous
within a month.

## Photographing people and other people's places

Photograph places, not people, unless the person has agreed. At the farm
and on any client's site, ask before you photograph anything — it is
somebody's business, and what you see there is theirs. Nothing goes
anywhere public without permission, from them or from your teacher.
