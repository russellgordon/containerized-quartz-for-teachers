Most of what you make in this course cannot be handed in. A bed stays in
the ground, a bench stays on the site, a crop gets eaten. The photograph
is the only thing that comes with you, so it has to carry the evidence —
and a photograph taken casually carries almost none.

## Before, during, after

The *before* is the one people miss. Take it the moment you arrive, from
a spot you can stand in again — nobody believes a shoreline planting
improved anything without a picture of what it looked like first, and by
the time you realise that, the bank is already planted.

- **Before.** Wide, showing the whole area and something fixed at the
  edge — a post, a corner, a tree — so you can line the later shots up.
- **During.** Your hands doing the actual skill: the cut, the joint, the
  root ball going in, the sharpening. Get someone else to take it.
- **After.** Same spot, same framing, same height as the before. Then one
  close shot of the detail you are proud of, and one of the whole thing
  in its setting.

## Getting a usable frame

Stand so the light comes from behind or beside you, and check your own
shadow is not lying across the subject. Overcast days are easiest; bright
midday sun blows out a gravel path and buries a bed edge in black shadow.
Fill the frame — a build photographed from across the yard is a
photograph of a yard.

Hold the phone level, especially for a before-and-after pair, and shoot
square-on to the face you want measured. If scale matters — a bed depth,
a stem thickness, a paver — put a tape in the shot. That one habit turns
a picture into data. Flash and filters off, lens wiped, three of
everything, because a photograph you cannot retake is one you should not
have taken only once.

## Naming and filing

Rename the same day, always in the same shape, so a file is findable in
June without opening it:

```
lastname-sitename-stage-yyyy-mm-dd
gordon-shoreline-before-2026-05-04
gordon-raisedbed-during-cutting-2026-10-14
```

Then write the caption while you still remember: what it shows, when, and
what your part was. Photographs without captions go anonymous within a
month.

Photograph places, not people, unless the person has agreed. At the farm
and on any client's site, ask before you photograph anything — it is
somebody's business, and what you see there is theirs. Nothing goes
anywhere public without permission. It all feeds [[The Evidence File]]
and, in the end, [[The Evidence Portfolio]].
