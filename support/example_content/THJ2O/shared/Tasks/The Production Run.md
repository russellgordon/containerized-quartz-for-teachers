**Launched mid-Unit 3, delivered at the end.** Small groups.

A batch of something, produced to a specification, on a schedule, for a
real recipient — the school, a community garden, a plant sale, or the
site project in Unit 4.

## The work

1. **Specify.** What, how many, to what standard, by when.
2. **Design the process.** Steps in order, who does what, what equipment
   is needed, and where the bottleneck will be — using the design and
   problem-solving process from
   [[Design and Problem Solving in the Green Industries]].
3. **Run it**, recording time, materials, and losses.
4. **Deliver**, on the date promised, to the standard specified.
5. **Review.** What the run actually cost in time and materials, what
   you would change, and what an innovation in this sector — automation,
   controlled environment growing, a new propagation technology — would
   do to your process, your costs, and the people doing the work.

## What is marked

| Criterion | The standard |
| --- | --- |
| Process design | A sequence that works, with the bottleneck identified in advance |
| Execution | The batch meets the specification |
| Delivery | On time, in a state the recipient accepts |
| Data | Real numbers for time, materials, and losses |
| Analysis | The innovation question answered with reasoning, including who gains and who loses |

> [!warning] A production run is judged by the last unit, not the best one
> Anyone can make one good pot. Consistency across the batch is the
> actual skill, and it is what a grower is paid for.

[[Systems That Do the Work]] covers the equipment; the review section
draws on what recent innovations have done to this sector's work and
employment.
