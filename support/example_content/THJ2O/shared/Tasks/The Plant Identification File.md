**Built through Unit 1, handed in at the end of it.** Individual.

Twenty plants you can identify on sight, from our own woodlot, shoreline,
and school grounds — with the evidence that you actually looked.

## What goes in the file

For each plant:

| Field | What it needs |
| --- | --- |
| Common name | The one people actually use |
| Botanical name | Genus and species, spelled correctly |
| Group | Tree, shrub, herbaceous perennial, annual, grass, vine |
| Distinguishing features | What tells it apart from the plant beside it |
| Where you found it | Site, date, and roughly where |
| Evidence | Your own photograph or a pressed, labelled sample |

At least eight from the woodlot, four from the shoreline, and four from
the school grounds.

## What is marked

- **Accuracy** — the identification is right, and the features you name
  are the ones that actually distinguish it.
- **Vocabulary** — the correct terms for leaf arrangement, margin, bark,
  and habit, used properly.
- **Your own evidence** — photographs and samples you collected, dated.
- **Care** — nothing rare or protected was picked, and you took only
  what you needed.

> [!warning] Collect responsibly, and know what you are touching
> Take a photograph rather than a sample where you can. Never collect
> from a protected area or a private site without permission, and learn
> poison ivy and wild parsnip before the first woodlot visit — see
> [[Hazards on a Green Industry Site]].

[[What Makes a Plant a Plant]] and [[The Parts and What They Do]] are the
vocabulary; the file is what proves you can use it in the field.
