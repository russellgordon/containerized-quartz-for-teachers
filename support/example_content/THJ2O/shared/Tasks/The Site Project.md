**The culminating task. Eight periods in Unit 4, ending with a
handover.** Groups of three or four.

A real green-industry job on one of our sites, carried from survey to
handover.

## The jobs on offer

- **A shoreline planting** to hold a bank, using species suited to the
  site — see [[Planting a Shoreline]].
- **A woodlot job**: a trail section, a health assessment with a
  management recommendation, or an invasive species plan.
- **A production bed** on the school grounds, built and planted for the
  next class to harvest.

## The eight periods

| Period | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Survey: measure the site, record conditions, photograph it |
| 2 | Design: a scaled plan and a plant or materials list |
| 3 | Costing and approval: quantities, prices, and sign-off from me |
| 4 | Preparation: materials, tools, and the site itself |
| 5 | Build or plant, day one |
| 6 | Build or plant, day two |
| 7 | Finish, clean up, and photograph |
| 8 | Handover: the maintenance schedule, presented |

## What you deliver

- **A scaled site plan** a person could work from.
- **A costed materials list** with quantities you calculated.
- **The finished work**, to standard, on the site.
- **A maintenance schedule** for the next twelve months, specific enough
  that next year's class can follow it without you.
- **Before, during, and after photographs**, filed.

## What is marked

| Criterion | The standard |
| --- | --- |
| Survey and plan | Measurements accurate; the plan matches the ground |
| Systems | Drainage, irrigation, access, or structure considered and justified |
| Environmental judgement | The work reduces harm rather than causing it, and you can say how |
| Workmanship | It is built or planted to last |
| Community effect | You can say what this does for the people who use this place |
| Handover | The schedule is usable by a stranger |

> [!warning] It stays after you leave
> This is on a real site that other people use, and it will be there in
> five years — or it will have failed there in five years. That is the
> difference between a school project and a job.
