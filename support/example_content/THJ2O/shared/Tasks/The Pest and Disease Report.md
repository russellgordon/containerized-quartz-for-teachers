**Due: late Unit 3.** Pairs. About 700 words plus photographs.

One pest or disease that is actually present, or actually a threat, in
our woodlot, our beds, or the farm — identified, monitored, and reported
with a management recommendation.

## What you produce

1. **Identification**, with your own photographs and the features you
   used. Say how confident you are and what would confirm it.
2. **The damage**: what it does, at what stage, and how to tell it from
   something else.
3. **Monitoring**: how you would check for it, how often, and what
   threshold would mean action.
4. **Management**, using integrated pest management — cultural,
   physical, biological, and chemical controls in that order, and what
   Ontario's rules allow. **Recommend a course of action, not a product.**
5. **Sources**, with dates — government, extension, or industry, judged
   for reliability.

## What is marked

| Criterion | The standard |
| --- | --- |
| Identification | Correct, with evidence, and honest about uncertainty |
| Monitoring | A method someone could actually follow weekly |
| IPM reasoning | Prevention and non-chemical options considered first and seriously |
| Sources | Current, authoritative, and cited |
| Ethics | You address the trade-offs — non-target species, water, workers, and cost |

> [!warning] This course does not teach pesticide application
> Applying pesticides is regulated work with licensing requirements, and
> Ontario restricts cosmetic use. Your report identifies, monitors, and
> recommends an approach; it names no product and no rate. That is the
> professional boundary for a Grade 10 course, and it is assessed.

[[Pests and Diseases in Our Woodlot]] is the fieldwork behind this and
[[Where to Find Out]] is how you judge a source.
