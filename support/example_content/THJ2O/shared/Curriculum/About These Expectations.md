The expectations in this folder are reproduced from **The Ontario
Curriculum, Grades 9 and 10: Technological Education** (revised 2009),
the Grade 10 Open course **THJ2O — Green Industries**, published by the
Ontario Ministry of Education. © Queen's Printer for Ontario, 2009.
Reproduced for educational use.

Each page here holds one expectation, word for word, with the code the
2009 document prints — `A1.3`, `B2.4`, and so on. The codes are also
tags, so every page tagged `#D1` can be gathered in one place.

## Four strands, and none of them runs "throughout"

Unlike the Canadian and world studies courses, this document gives THJ2O
no *throughout this course* strand. Every one of the four stems reads
**"By the end of this course, students will:"** — A on the biology and
the design process, B on the hands-on skills, C on environment and
society, D on safety and careers.

That leaves the weaving to you. Safety in particular is written as
strand D, near the end, and taught from the first day a student picks up
a tool. This course puts D1 in the second class and returns to it every
time a new tool appears.

## The examples are the Ministry's, and they are italic

The 2009 document sets each expectation's *(e.g., …)* list in italics,
and the pages here keep that. It is a useful distinction to hold onto
while planning: the roman text is what a student must be able to do, and
the italic list is the Ministry illustrating what that could look like —
suggestions, not a required checklist.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing this?",
the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline, or a
teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes are what make one
person's planning legible to another.
