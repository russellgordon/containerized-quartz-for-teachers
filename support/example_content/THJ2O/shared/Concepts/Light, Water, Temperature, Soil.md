Two plants of the same species, from the same tray, planted the same
day: one thrives and one sulks. The difference is almost never the
plant. It is the site. Environmental factors are the conditions a plant
did not choose, and reading them before you plant is cheaper than
replacing what dies.

## The five you can actually assess

| Factor | What to look for on our sites | Too little | Too much |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Hours of direct sun; which way a slope faces; canopy overhead | Stretched, pale stems; no flowers or fruit | Scorched leaf edges; midday wilting |
| Water | Where puddles sit after rain; how fast a hole drains | Wilting, small leaves, early leaf drop | Yellowing, root rot, a sour smell |
| Temperature | Frost pockets at the bottom of a slope; heat off a south wall | Growth stops; frost damage on soft tissue | Flower drop and poor fruit set |
| Soil and nutrients | Texture, depth, compaction, drainage, pH | Pale, stunted growth; poor colour | Weak floppy growth; runoff into water |
| Wind | Exposure at the shoreline; a gap between buildings | Little effect | Dried leaves; broken stems; toppled plants |

## Reading a site instead of guessing

Stand still for five minutes and write down what you can see. Which way
does the slope face? Where does water leave, and where does it stay?
What is already growing well here, and what is struggling? A place where
moss thrives and grass will not is telling you about shade and moisture
in plain language.

The shoreline and the woodlot are useful because they are extremes. The
shoreline is open, windy, bright and often dry at the top of the bank;
the woodlot floor is shaded, sheltered, cool and damp. Nothing that
thrives in one will be happy in the other, and no amount of care fixes a
plant put in the wrong place.

## The factors interact

They are never independent. Wind and heat together dry a new transplant
faster than either alone. Compacted soil holds water and starves roots
of oxygen at the same time. High light with too little water gives you
scorch that looks like disease. Check all five before you name a cause —
most "pest problems" turn out to be a site problem that weakened the
plant first.

Soil gets its own study in [[Soils and Growing Media]], and water is a
skill rather than a fact, which is why [[Watering Properly]] exists.
Vary exactly one factor at a time in [[The Growing Trial]] and the
result will mean something. What the plant itself contributes is
[[The Plant's Own Limits]].
