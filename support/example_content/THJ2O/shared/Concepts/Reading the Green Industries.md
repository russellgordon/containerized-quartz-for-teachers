"The green industries" is one phrase covering five trades that share
plants and not much else: agriculture, forestry, horticulture, floristry
and landscaping. Each has its own vocabulary, and using a word loosely
marks you as an outsider faster than anything else you could do. A
supplier who hears you ask for "some of that mulch stuff" quotes you
differently than one who hears you ask for shredded bark mulch by the
cubic yard.

## Words that are used precisely

| Term | What it actually means | The common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardwood | Wood of a broadleaf tree, such as maple or oak | Assuming it always means physically hard |
| Softwood | Wood of a conifer, such as pine or spruce | Treating it as low quality; it frames most buildings |
| Propagation | Deliberately producing new plants, from seed or from a parent | Using it for any kind of planting at all |
| Topsoil | The upper, biologically active layer, dark with organic matter | Calling any bagged dark material topsoil |
| Cultivar | A named variety selected and maintained by growers | Swapping it for "species" — a cultivar is narrower |
| Line flower | A tall flower on a strong stem that sets an arrangement's shape | Confusing it with a mass or filler flower |
| Vine | A plant that climbs using another structure for support | Applying it to any long trailing stem |
| Nursery stock | Plants grown for sale and transplanting | Using it for cut flowers or produce |

## Same word, different trade

Some words shift as you cross from one sector to another. To a forester,
a **stand** is a group of trees of similar age and species managed as
one unit. **Crown** is the leafy top of a tree in forestry, but the
point where stem meets root in horticulture. **Grade** is a quality
classification for lumber and for produce, and also the finished level
of the ground on a landscape site. None of these are errors — they are
context, and you learn context by listening on site.

## Getting it right in your own writing

Three habits make your language reliable. Write the full name the first
time, including the cultivar if you know it. Put a unit on every number
— cubic yards, litres, millimetres, degrees Celsius — and say which
system you used. Describe what you observed in plain terms, and keep it
separate from what you concluded.

Practise the vocabulary in [[The Plant Identification File]], and apply
it every time you fill in a record following [[Writing a Work Record]].
