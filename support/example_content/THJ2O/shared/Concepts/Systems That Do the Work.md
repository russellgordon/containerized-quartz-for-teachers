One person with a watering can can keep about a hundred pots alive. A
grower with fifty thousand pots does not hire five hundred people — they
install a system. Every structure and mechanical system in the green
industries exists to make a job that is possible by hand practical at
scale, and knowing what each one does is how you read an operation the
first time you walk into it.

## What to look for on any site

| System | What it is for | What you will see | What fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Irrigation | Delivering water where and when the crop needs it | Drip line along a row; overhead booms; a timer and a pump | Clogged emitters, split lines, a timer nobody reset |
| Drainage | Getting excess water out so roots can breathe | Perforated tile below grade; graded surfaces; swales | Crushed or silted tile, low spots that pond |
| Greenhouse environment | Holding light, heat and humidity steady | Heaters, roof vents, circulation fans, shade cloth, benching | A vent motor that sticks on the first hot day |
| Storage | Slowing everything down after harvest | Coolers, humidity control, fans, crates on pallets | Warm spots, blocked airflow, produce stacked too high |
| Mechanical handling | Moving material without moving people | Conveyors, graders and sorters, tractors, loaders | Guards removed, worn belts, nobody trained on it |

## Structures are the same idea at four scales

A greenhouse, a hoop house, a cold frame and a shade structure all
change the environment over a small area so you can grow something the
outdoor season would not allow. Add a storage barn, a headhouse and a
potting bench and you have most of a production nursery. Each structure
is a capital cost that the crop has to earn back, which is why
greenhouse operations tend toward high-value plants.

## Systems have consequences

Nothing here is free. Irrigation uses water and electricity. A heated
greenhouse in a Canadian February burns a great deal of fuel. Every
motor is noise and every engine is exhaust. Those costs are the effects
listed in [[What We Do to the Environment]], and choosing a system with
them in mind is the practice described in [[Doing It Better]].

At the farm, pick one system and follow it end to end — where the water
enters, where it goes, what controls it, and what happens when it fails.
Bring the irrigation back to [[Watering Properly]] and the cooling to
[[Harvest and What Happens Next]].

Anything with a motor, a blade or stored energy is also a hazard. Work
through [[Power Tools and Equipment]] and
[[Hazards on a Green Industry Site]] first, and never approach machinery
on a working site without the operator knowing where you are.
