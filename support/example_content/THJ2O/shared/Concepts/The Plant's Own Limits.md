You can give a plant perfect light, water, warmth and soil and it will
still refuse to do some things. A radish will not become a tree. A
shade-tolerant woodland shrub will not fruit heavily in full sun. Those
limits are written into the plant itself, and a grower who ignores them
loses money being surprised.

## What the plant brings to the job

**Plant type** sets the ceiling. An annual has one season to germinate,
grow, flower, set seed and die, so everything about it is fast. A woody
perennial spends years building structure and may produce nothing
saleable for several seasons. They are different businesses.

**Genetics** sets the detail. Within one species, cultivars differ in
days to maturity, disease resistance, hardiness, size, colour and how
well the crop holds after picking. That is why seed catalogues list
numbers rather than adjectives, and why the cultivar name belongs in
your work record beside the species.

**Photosynthetic capacity** sets the rate. Leaf area, how efficiently
that leaf uses light, and how much income goes straight back out in
respiration together decide how fast a plant can grow at all. Strip half
the leaves off a transplant and you have halved its income at the moment
it most needs to rebuild roots.

**Stage of life** sets what is possible now. Prune at the wrong stage
and you remove next year's flowers; harvest at the wrong stage and the
crop will not keep. Match the job to the stage the plant is in.

## After harvest, the biology keeps running

The moment you cut a lettuce or pick a tomato, it stops earning and
keeps spending. Respiration continues, using up sugars, water and
firmness, and the warmer it is the faster that happens. Some crops go on
ripening after picking; sweet corn and most leafy greens only get worse.

> [!warning] A harvested crop is still alive
> Produce left in the sun in a bin at the end of a row can lose more
> quality in one hot afternoon than in a week of proper storage. The
> clock starts at the cut, not at the loading dock.

The environmental half is [[Light, Water, Temperature, Soil]]; what to
do once the crop is off the plant is [[Harvest and What Happens Next]].
