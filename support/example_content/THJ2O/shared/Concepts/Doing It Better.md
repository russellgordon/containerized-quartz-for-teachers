A best management practice is not a slogan. It is a specific, ordinary
change to how a job is done that reduces harm without stopping the work
— and most of them save money within a few seasons, which is why they
spread. Here is the set you will actually use on our sites.

## Soil, water and fuel

Test the soil before you fertilize, so you add what is missing rather
than what is habitual. Keep the ground covered — with a crop, a cover
crop, mulch or plant litter — because bare soil is the soil that leaves.
Leave an unmown, planted strip between any working area and the water;
that buffer traps sediment before it reaches the lake. Water the root
zone rather than the aisle, in the morning, and mulch to cut
evaporation. Choose plants suited to the site, because the cheapest
maintenance is the maintenance you designed out. Keep engines tuned and
blades sharp, shut equipment off instead of idling, compost on site, and
reuse pots and trays instead of replacing them.

## Integrated pest management

IPM is the trade's answer to "what do we do about pests?", and its steps
are in a deliberate order.

1. **Prevent.** Right plant, right place; healthy soil; clean tools and
   pots; resistant cultivars; crop rotation; do not move firewood.
2. **Identify.** Know exactly what you have. Most insects on a plant are
   not damaging it, and some are eating the ones that are.
3. **Monitor.** Check on a schedule and write down what you find, so you
   see a trend rather than a panic.
4. **Set a threshold.** Decide in advance how much damage actually
   matters. A few chewed leaves on a shade tree is not worth money.
5. **Intervene, least drastic first.** Hand removal, pruning out
   affected material, barriers, traps, encouraging natural enemies.

> [!success]- Why this course does not spray (click to expand)
> Ontario law restricts the cosmetic use of pesticides on lawns and
> gardens, and applying pest control products in a workplace requires
> training and certification a Grade 10 course does not provide. So we
> do the parts of IPM that professionals say matter most and that
> students can legitimately do well: identification, monitoring, record
> keeping and prevention. Anything beyond that is a licensed adult's
> decision, made with the product label in hand.
