This is the real list for our sites — the shop, the school grounds, the
woodlot, the waterfront, and the farm. None of it is exotic, and all of
it has put people in an emergency department, usually people who had done
the job a hundred times before.

| Hazard | Where you meet it | What you do about it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cuts | Everywhere there is an edge | Sharp tools, cut away from yourself, gloves — the blunt tool cuts deeper because you forced it |
| Crush and pinch points | Wheelbarrows, gates, pavers, tailgates, hinges | Know where your hands are before the load moves |
| Eye injury | Trimmers, mowers, grinding, springing branches | Eye protection on before the tool starts; the damage is permanent in a fraction of a second |
| Hearing loss | Mowers, blowers, trimmers, shop machines | Protection on before the noise; it builds painlessly and never comes back |
| Dust and mould | Dry soil, compost, potting mix, old straw | Damp it down, work upwind, wear a dust mask |
| Poison ivy | Woodlot edges, fence lines, shoreline scrub | Three leaflets means walk around it; the oil stays on gloves and tools, and it is never burned |
| Wasps | Ground nests in beds, banks, and long grass | Look before mowing or trimming; withdraw calmly rather than swatting |
| Uneven ground | The woodlot, the shoreline, any working site | Walk it first and look where you place your feet |
| Lifting injuries | Soil bags, pavers, balled trees, tool crates | Two people, load close, legs not back, no twisting |

## Ticks and Lyme disease

Blacklegged ticks live in the woodlot, in leaf litter and long grass, and
can carry the bacterium that causes Lyme disease. Stay on the trail, tuck
long trousers into your socks, wear light colours so you can see one, and
do a tick check the same day — waistband, behind the knees, armpits,
hairline, scalp. If one is attached, tell your teacher: it is removed
promptly with fine-tipped tweezers, gripping as close to the skin as
possible and pulling straight out, and you then tell a parent or guardian
and see a health care provider. Removing it early is what matters, which
is why the check happens the same day and not at the weekend.

## Sun and heat

Heat reaches your judgement before it reaches your body, so the person in
trouble is usually the last to notice. Heavy sweating, headache,
dizziness, cramps, or nausea means stop, get into shade, drink water,
tell someone. Confusion, or hot skin with the sweating stopped, is an
emergency — call for help immediately.

## Water, and vehicles at the farm

Nobody works at the water's edge alone and nobody enters the water. The
bank you cannot see under is the one that gives way, wet rock is
frictionless, and cold water takes the strength out of a strong swimmer
within a minute.

The farm is a workplace with machinery running in it, and an operator in
a cab cannot hear you and may not see you. Never approach a machine that
is running — wait until it stops and the operator waves you in. Never
ride on equipment, never step over a turning shaft, and stay out of the
yard traffic route. Vests go on as you get out of the vehicle.

What to do when any of this happens is
[[Safe Shop and Site Practice]]; the check that catches most of it first
is [[Before You Start Work]].
