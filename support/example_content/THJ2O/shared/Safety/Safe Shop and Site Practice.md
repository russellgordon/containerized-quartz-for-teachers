Safe practice is not a mood. It is a set of specific things people do in
a specific order, every period, whether or not anyone is watching.

## Isolating a machine, and its guards

Lockout means the energy is switched off, physically disconnected, and
cannot be restored while somebody is working on the machine. It exists
because a machine that starts while a hand is inside it does not stop.
Full lockout on fixed equipment is done by trained staff, not students.
Your version is the same principle at a smaller scale: **unplug it and
keep the plug in your own sight**, or pull the spark plug lead, or remove
the battery — before you change a blade, clear a jam, or look closely at
anything. Never trust a switch, and never restore power to a machine you
did not isolate yourself without checking who is near it.

Guards stay on. A guard comes off only for the person authorised to
service that machine, and goes back before it runs. Find one missing,
damaged, or tied back and the machine is out of service: do not run it,
tag it, tell your teacher. That is a report, not a complaint.

## Housekeeping, loads, and water

Most shop and site injuries are the floor's fault. Cords routed around
walkways, not across. Offcuts cleared as they are made. Spills cleaned
when they happen, not at the end. Wheelbarrows parked out of the traffic
line, tools back on the marked wall, materials stacked so nothing slides.
Wash your hands before you eat — soil, compost, treated wood, and fuel
all end up on them.

Plan the route before you lift and check the far end is clear. Wheel it
if you can; a wheelbarrow beats a hero. When you do lift: load close to
your body, feet apart, knees bent, no twisting, and set it down before
you rearrange your grip. Anything awkward, heavy, or above waist height
is a two-person job, and calling for that person is the professional
move.

At the shoreline nobody works alone and nobody enters the water. Stage
tools back from the bank and work facing the water, not backing towards
it — [[Planting a Shoreline]] adds the rest.

## When something goes wrong

Stop, and stop the people around you; power off if you can do it safely.
Do not become a second casualty — get help rather than going in after
someone. Then tell your teacher immediately and report it: injury, near
miss, spill, fire, damage, or a rule you have just realised you broke. A
near miss reported today is the injury that does not happen next month.

Copy this into your work record — the boxes are printed on the page and
nothing you do to them is saved anywhere.

- [ ] Machine isolated before hands go near it
- [ ] Guards present, floors and walkways clear
- [ ] Route planned, help asked for, load kept close
- [ ] Everything reported the same period
