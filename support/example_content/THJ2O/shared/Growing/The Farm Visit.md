Five minutes' drive, and a working vegetable farm — the same work you do
on the bench, at a scale where the decisions cost real money. We go once
in Unit 3 and the visit is assessed through what you record.

## Before we go

Write **three questions** you actually want answered. Specific ones get
remarkable answers; ones you could have looked up waste your only chance
to ask a person who does this for a living.

Good: *How do you decide when to irrigate?* *What is the hardest week of
the year?* *Where does most of this go after it leaves the gate?*

## What to look for

| Look at | The question underneath |
| --- | --- |
| The beds | Spacing, rotation, and why that crop is in that place |
| Water | Where it comes from, how it is applied, what a dry August does |
| Equipment | What is mechanised and what is still done by hand, and why |
| Labour | How many people, when, and where they come from |
| Post-harvest | Washing, cooling, grading, storing — and how fast it happens |
| The outlets | Farm gate, market, wholesaler, restaurant, box scheme |

That last row matters as much as the growing. A crop that grows
beautifully and has nowhere to go is a loss, and a farm's outlets shape
what it plants.

## Recording

Notebook, not memory. Write numbers as they are said and read them back
— "so that is 40 hectares, of which 12 in vegetables?" A number you half
remember is worse than no number.

> [!warning] This is a workplace, and you are guests
> Boots, no earbuds, and touch nothing without asking. Stay clear of
> moving equipment and out of the way of people working. What a grower
> tells you about their business goes in your notebook, not online —
> [[Our Working Agreement]] applies off site as much as on.

Afterwards everyone writes a thank-you. That is why we are still
welcome.
