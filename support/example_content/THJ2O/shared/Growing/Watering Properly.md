More plants are killed by watering than by drought, and almost all of
them are killed with good intentions. Watering is a judgement made plant
by plant, not a chore performed on a schedule, and learning to make that
judgement quickly is one of the most valuable skills in this course.

## Check before you pour

Never water because it is Tuesday. Check first, using whichever method
fits what is in front of you.

- **Push a finger in.** For a bed or a large pot, go down about 5 cm.
  Dry at that depth means water; damp means wait.
- **Lift the pot.** A watered pot and a dry pot of the same size feel
  completely different. Do this a few times deliberately and your hand
  becomes a moisture meter.
- **Look at the surface, then distrust it.** The top centimetre dries
  first and tells you almost nothing.
- **Look early in the day.** Many plants wilt in mid-afternoon heat and
  recover by evening; that is not thirst.

## How to water

Water the **soil**, not the plant. Wet foliage sitting overnight invites
fungal disease, and water landing on leaves mostly evaporates.

For **containers**, apply slowly until water runs freely from the
drainage holes, then stop. A pot half-watered develops a dry core that
later water runs straight past. Do not leave pots standing in saucers.

For **beds and new plantings**, water deeply and less often. A long slow
soak sends roots downward; a daily sprinkle trains them to stay at the
surface, where they die in the first dry week. Aim at the root zone and
mulch afterwards. Morning is best: the plant has water available for the
hot part of the day, and splashed foliage dries quickly.

> [!warning] Overwatering looks exactly like underwatering
> Both give you a wilted, drooping, yellowing plant — because in both
> cases the roots have stopped delivering water. Waterlogged soil drives
> out the oxygen roots need, they suffocate, they rot, and the plant
> wilts while sitting in mud. If a wilted plant's soil is wet, adding
> water will finish it. Check the soil before you decide, every time.

## Doing it safely

Water and electricity share the greenhouse and the shop. Keep cords and
outlets clear of spray, report anything damaged before you use it, and
never handle a plugged-in device with wet hands. Coil hoses off walkways
rather than across them, and mop wet floors — the most common injury
around a watering bench is a slip.

What the water moves through is [[Soils and Growing Media]]; what a
newly planted shrub needs is [[Transplanting and Establishment]].
