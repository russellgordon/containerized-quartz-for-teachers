Seed is the cheapest way to make a great many plants, and the least
forgiving of sloppiness. A tray sown carelessly on Monday looks
identical to one sown carefully — for about ten days. Then one comes up
evenly, the other in patches, and nothing will fix it.

## Before you open the packet

Read the packet: species and cultivar, packed-for date, and usually
depth, spacing and days to maturity. Seed does not last forever, so
expect less of anything two or three years old. Fill your flats with a
**seed-starting mix**, not soil from the beds — field soil compacts and
brings weeds and disease; see [[Soils and Growing Media]].

> [!tip] Write the label before you sow the seed
> Species, cultivar, date, and your initials. Fifteen seconds, and it is
> the difference between a bench of data and a bench of anonymous green
> things. A labelled empty cell is recoverable; a full unlabelled one is
> not.

## Sowing

**Depth** is what people get wrong. Cover a seed to roughly two or three
times its own width. Fine seeds are barely covered, and a few kinds need
light and are not covered at all — the packet will say. Too deep, and
the seedling runs out of stored food before it reaches the light.

**Spacing.** Sowing thickly wastes seed, makes thinning miserable, and
traps humid still air around the stems, which is what disease wants.

**Water** from below where you can, or with a fine rose that will not
wash seed out of place — evenly moist, never soaking, covered until the
first seedlings appear. **Warmth** drives germination, but the moment
seedlings emerge they need bright light, and cooler conditions make
sturdier plants. Pale and stretched means not enough light.

**Damping off** is a soil-borne fungal problem that topples seedlings at
the soil line, usually overnight and in a spreading patch. Prevent
rather than cure: clean flats, fresh mix, thin sowing, air movement
across the bench, and no overwatering — see [[Watering Properly]].

Thin to the strongest, pot on when the first true leaves are developed,
and harden anything going outdoors before
[[Transplanting and Establishment]]. Record every sowing at
[[The Propagation Bench]]: date, mix, days to emergence, percentage up.
