A seed gives you a plant related to its parents. A cutting gives you the
parent again. That is why the nursery trade runs on cuttings: when a
customer buys a named cultivar they are buying a plant propagated
vegetatively from the original, and every one is genetically identical.

## Three kinds, by how woody the shoot is

**Softwood** cuttings, taken in late spring from new growth that snaps
rather than folding, root fastest and wilt fastest, so they need
humidity from the moment they are cut. **Semi-hardwood** cuttings come
later, from shoots beginning to firm up — slower, tougher, more
forgiving. **Hardwood** cuttings are taken in winter from dormant woody
stems, and give us the stakes used in [[Planting a Shoreline]].

## Taking and sticking a cutting

Work in the morning, and choose healthy shoots that are not flowering.

1. Cut a piece roughly 10 to 15 cm long, just below a node — that is
   where roots form most readily.
2. Strip the leaves from the lower third to half; any leaf buried in the
   medium will rot, and cut large remaining leaves back by half.
3. Push it into moist, free-draining medium, deep enough to stand up.
   Keep humidity high and light indirect; bottom warmth speeds rooting.

Some species root faster with a rooting compound. Whether we use one,
and how, is your teacher's decision made with the product label in hand.
Keep everything clean — sharp knife or secateurs, blades wiped between
parent plants, clean flats, fresh medium; see [[Maintaining Your Tools]]
and [[Hand Tools]]. Cut away from your body and your other hand every
time; a propagation knife is sharp enough that a slip is a stitches
injury. After three to six weeks, tug gently: resistance means roots.

> [!success]- Why your cuttings failed (click to expand)
> **Blackened at the base, mushy** — too wet, or leaves were buried.
> **Shrivelled but green** — humidity too low, or leaves left too large.
> **Bleached and crisp** — direct sun on a closed flat cooked them.
> **Still firm** — wrong season, or the wood was too old.
> **Some rooted, some not** — mixed parent material; take from one plant.

Everything stuck at [[The Propagation Bench]] gets a label and a date,
and the strike rate tells you whether your technique is improving.
