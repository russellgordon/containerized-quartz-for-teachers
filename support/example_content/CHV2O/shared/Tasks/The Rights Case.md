**Due mid-way through Unit 2.** Individual. About 1,000 words, plus a
prepared two-minute oral answer to one hostile question.

Take one situation in which a right in Canada was limited, contested, or
used to change something, and argue what should have happened. This is the
task where you take a position and defend it under attack.

## Choosing a case

It has to be real, documented, and about rights rather than about
policy in general. Three kinds work:

- **A court case** — a Charter or section 35 decision, read from the
  reasons themselves. Use [[Court Decisions]].
- **A legislative limit** — a law that limited a right, including one
  passed under section 33.
- **A rights campaign** — people using their freedoms of expression,
  assembly or association to change a rule, and what came of it.

Pick something you have not already made up your mind about. The
strongest submissions every year come from students who changed position
part way through.

## What the argument must contain

**The document.** Name the right by its section, and say which
constitutional document establishes it. Where the case involves a
foundational document — Magna Carta, the *Constitution Act, 1867*, the
*Canadian Bill of Rights* — explain what it contributed and what it could
not do.

**The limit, and its justification.** What did the government say the
limit was for, and what evidence did it bring? Apply the section 1
reasoning from [[Rights, and What Limits Them]] step by step.

**The responsibility side.** Rights carry responsibilities, and this task
asks you to say which ones are engaged: on the rights-holder, on other
citizens, and on the government. This is not decoration — it is a marked
criterion.

**Your judgement**, with the strongest opposing case stated fairly and
answered.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Documents | The right is located precisely in the text that establishes it |
| Analysis | The limit is tested against the actual standard, not against your instinct |
| Responsibilities | Named specifically, and attached to somebody |
| Judgement | A defensible position, with its cost to the losing side acknowledged |
| Defence | You answer the hostile question by engaging it, not by restating your case |

> [!note] Revision is available on this task
> Hand in a draft by the earlier date and you get written feedback and
> one revision. On a rights argument the revision is where most of the
> learning is: first drafts almost always state a position and then
> discover, on being asked one hard question, that the reasoning had a
> gap in it.

[[Whose Rights Win|Whose Rights Win?]] and [[When Should a Right Give Way|When Should a Right Give Way?]] are where
these arguments get tested before they are written.
