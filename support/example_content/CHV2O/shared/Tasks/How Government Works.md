**Due at the end of Unit 1.** Pairs. A written explanation plus a
five-minute walkthrough delivered to the class.

Take one real issue and explain — to someone who has never studied this —
exactly which governments are involved in it, what each of them can and
cannot do about it, and how a party's beliefs shape the approach it
takes. If your explanation would let a stranger work out who to write to,
it has succeeded.

## What you produce

**A written explanation**, about 900 words, covering all three of:

- **The governments involved.** Which orders, which bodies inside them,
  which positions hold the relevant power, and what each is responsible
  for. Almost every real issue involves more than one.
- **Indigenous governments and treaty relationships**, where the issue
  touches them — and say honestly whether it does. If your issue
  concerns land, resources, child welfare, education, policing or
  health, it very likely does. Identify the nation, its government, and
  the treaty or agreement that applies. [[Indigenous Governance]] is the
  ground; do not generalise from one nation to others.
- **The parties, objectively.** Identify the federal parties, place them
  on the political compass with your reasons, and explain how the beliefs
  underpinning two of them lead to different approaches to *this* issue.

**A five-minute walkthrough** with one visual — a diagram of who decides
what — delivered to the class, with questions.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Powers are assigned to the right order and body, and checkable |
| Indigenous governance | Treated as government, named specifically, sourced to that nation where possible |
| Objectivity | Party positions explained from what parties have proposed, without endorsement or mockery |
| Reasoning | You show how a stated belief produces a stated position, rather than asserting both |
| Communication | A stranger could follow it and would know who to contact |

> [!success]- What "objectively" means in the marking of this task
> It means describing a party's position accurately enough that a member
> of that party would agree you had stated it, and explaining the belief
> that produces it. It does not mean pretending every claim is equally
> supported by evidence — where a factual claim is contested, say what
> the evidence shows and cite it. Neutral about parties, honest about
> facts.

Nothing on this page or in your answer should name a current officeholder
without a date attached; look leaders and seat counts up at elections.ca
and parl.ca on the day you write, and say when you looked.

Build on [[How Canada Is Governed]], [[Who Decides What, and Where]] and
[[Who Decided This?]].
