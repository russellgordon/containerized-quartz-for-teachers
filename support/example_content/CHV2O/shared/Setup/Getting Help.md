Ask early. In a course this short, a small question left unasked in week
two is a real problem by week six.

## Ways to reach me

| How | When | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before or after class | Every day | Quick questions, checking your thinking |
| [[Help Sessions]] | The posted times | Working on something with me in the room |
| A booked conversation | Any week | Anything needing more than five minutes |
| Email | Any time; replies on school days | Something you want in writing |

## What to bring

"I don't get it" is a fine opening, and we get much further with one of
these in front of us:

- The document you are stuck on, open, with the confusing part marked.
- Your draft, however rough. A bad paragraph is far more useful to me
  than a description of the paragraph you intend to write.
- The question you cannot answer, written out, and what you have already
  tried.

## When the work is not coming together

Nearly every time a student comes stuck in this course, it is one of
these five:

1. **The issue is too big.** "Homelessness" cannot be investigated in ten
   weeks; "the vote on the shelter zoning amendment" can. Narrow it until
   a decision-maker has a name.
2. **You cannot find who decided it.** Work the trail in
   [[Who Decided This|Who Decided This?]] — order of government, body, instrument, date.
   The minutes are usually the fastest route.
3. **Your sources all agree.** Almost always a sign they came from one
   place. [[Judging Civic Information]] fixes this.
4. **You have a position but no evidence, or evidence but no position.**
   Say what you think in one sentence, then see whether the sources
   support it. If they do not, change the sentence rather than the
   sources.
5. **The action has no target.** If you cannot name the person or body
   who could grant what you are asking for, there is nothing to plan yet.
   [[How Change Actually Happens]] is where to restart.

## If a class gets difficult

Some of what we study is close to home for people in this room — treaty
obligations, immigration, policing, residential schools. If a lesson
stays with you afterwards, or if a discussion went somewhere that was
hard to sit through, tell me. You do not have to explain why, and you
never have to do it in front of the class.
