This site is the course: the schedule, the ideas, the documents, the
assessed work, and the Ministry expectations behind all of it.

| Folder | Holds | You will use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **All Classes** | One page per period — what we did and what to do next | Every day |
| **Concepts** | The ideas of the course, stated cleanly | After the class that uncovered one |
| **Sources** | The documents civics runs on — bills, budgets, decisions — and how each behaves | Whenever a task needs real material |
| **Investigations** | A question, the evidence, and a judgement, worked through | In class, and again before the examination |
| **Discussions** | Seminar questions and how to prepare for them | The night before a seminar |
| **Portfolios** | Your notebook and the final reflection, built across the term | All term |
| **Tasks** | Assessed work, with what earns the marks | Whenever something is due |
| **Tutorials** | This site, and how to write to someone in power | The first time you need one |
| **Setup** | How the class runs, including how we argue about politics | Week one |
| **Style** | How the site itself works | Now |

## Why Sources and Investigations are separate

One is material; the other is a question put to it.
[[Bills and What Happens to Them]] says what that kind of document can
and cannot show; [[Who Decided This?]] takes several kinds and reaches a
judgement about a real decision. Keeping them apart is the difference
between having evidence and using it.

## How the pieces connect

Class pages are the spine. Each one links to the concepts, sources,
investigations and tasks that period actually consists of, so the site
reads forward in time even though the folders are arranged by kind. The
backlinks at the foot of any page show which classes used it — the
fastest way to see where an idea came up, and to notice that one budget
page has served three different arguments.

## The Curriculum connection

Content pages end with a **Curriculum connection**: the Ministry
expectations that page addresses, in the Ministry's own words. Setup and
Style pages carry none, because they are about how the room and the site
work rather than about civics. The site builds a coverage map from those
links, so you can see what the course has actually taught and what it has
only mentioned.

One thing worth knowing about this particular course's expectations: the
2022 Civics and Citizenship policy prints **no examples and no sample
questions anywhere** — every specific expectation is a single sentence
and nothing else. Every example on this site is therefore a teaching
choice, not a Ministry instruction, and a teacher adapting this course
should feel free to replace them with local ones.

## Two conventions

- **Concept pages come after the doing.** They assume you have met the
  idea in a class already, so reading one in advance is less useful than
  it looks.
- **The newest class is on the section landing page.** If you were away,
  start there.
