A square appears, sliced into pieces of different shapes and sizes, some
shaded. The question: *what fraction is shaded — and how did you see
it?* No piece comes labelled. You have to reason from relationships:
this triangle is half of that rectangle, which is a quarter of the
whole. Unit fractions are the atoms everything else is built from.

## How we play

1. The image goes up. Decide the shaded fraction in your head.
2. Answers are collected first — often they disagree, which is gold.
3. Defences: come up and mark the image. Every claim of "this piece is
   $\frac{1}{8}$" has to survive the question *how do you know?*

> [!example]- One square, four ways of seeing (click to expand)
> A square with its left half cut into two stacked rectangles; the top
> rectangle is cut corner to corner, one triangle shaded.
>
> - "The triangle is half of $\frac{1}{4}$, so $\frac{1}{8}$."
> - "Eight such triangles would tile the square — $\frac{1}{8}$."
> - "The left half holds four of them: half of a half of a half."
> - "As a decimal: $0.5 \times 0.25 = 0.125$, which is $\frac{1}{8}$."

## One variation

Reverse it: here is an unmarked square — shade *exactly* $\frac{3}{8}$
of it, no ruler, and prove your shading. More at
[[Fractions, Decimals, and Percents]] and [[Fraction and Percent Practice]].

> [!tip] Name the unit
> Most disagreements dissolve the moment someone asks "a fraction *of
> what*?" Pin down the whole first; the pieces follow.
