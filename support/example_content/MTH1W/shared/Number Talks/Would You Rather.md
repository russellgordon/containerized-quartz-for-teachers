Two offers, one choice: would you rather be paid $15 an hour for a
six-hour shift, or a flat $80 plus $2 for every customer you serve?
There is no answer key — only your reasoning. "I just picked one"
scores zero; a choice counts once mathematics is standing behind it.

## How we play

1. The two offers go up. Choose a side of the room and walk to it.
2. Build a defence with the people who chose as you did.
3. Sides argue. You may cross the floor at any time — changing your
   mind for a good reason is the strongest move in the game.

> [!example]- One duel, four defences (click to expand)
> $15 an hour for six hours, or a flat $80 plus $2 per customer?
>
> - "Hourly is $90 guaranteed. The flat side needs 5 customers to tie."
> - "I set $80 + 2c = 90$ and solved: $c = 5$. Busy shift? Flat wins."
> - "A graph of both offers crosses at five customers — I drew it."
> - "It depends on the shift — my answer is a condition, not a side."

## One variation

Raise the stakes: would you rather have $100 today or $120 in a year?
Now assumptions about saving and borrowing do the arguing — a first
taste of [[Interest and Growth]].

> [!tip] "It depends" is a mathematical answer
> The strongest defences say exactly what it depends *on*, and where
> the tipping point sits. That is money talk you will use for life.
