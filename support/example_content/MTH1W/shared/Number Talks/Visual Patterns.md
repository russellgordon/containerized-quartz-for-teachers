Three figures made of tiles grow across the board: figure 1, figure 2,
figure 3. First question: how many tiles in figure 4? Easy. Real
question: how many in figure 100 — *without* drawing it? The only way
there is to see what stays the same and what grows, which is algebra
before any letters show up.

## How we play

1. Three figures go up. Sketch or imagine figure 4 to get a foothold.
2. Find figure 10, then figure 100 — no tile-by-tile counting allowed.
3. Share rules in words first: "it's three arms that each grow by one,
   plus a centre." The class tests each rule against the figures.

> [!example]- One pattern, four ways of seeing (click to expand)
> Figures with 4, 7, 10 tiles: a centre tile with three growing arms.
>
> - "Three arms of $n$ tiles, plus one centre: $3n + 1$."
> - "Figure 1 had 4, and it grows by 3 each step: $4 + 3(n - 1)$."
> - "I saw an L-shape of $2n + 1$ tiles and one arm of $n$ more."
> - "Multiply the figure number by 3 and add 1 — I checked all three."
>
> All four rules agree on every figure — so all four are the same rule.

## One variation

Run it backwards: start from a rule like $5n - 2$ and design a tile
pattern that makes it visible. That is the whole game of
[[Algebraic Expressions]], played with pictures.

> [!tip] The 100th figure is the honest test
> Anyone can extend a pattern one step by counting. A rule that lands
> figure 100 in one move means you saw the general in the specific.
