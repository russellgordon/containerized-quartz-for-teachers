Everything for this course lives on this site, and it is built to be
used in a particular way. Five minutes here saves you an hour of
wandering later.

## Start where the site starts

The landing page for your section always shows the **most recent
class** — the number talk, the thinking task, what is due. If you were
away, that page is your first stop; if you were here, it is still the
fastest answer to "what was that thing about Thursday?" Older class
pages sit right behind it, one per day, so a week away is five short
pages read in order.

## Three habits that make the site fast

1. **Search first.** Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> (or
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd>) and type any word — a topic, a task
   name, a phrase you half remember. Search is almost always faster
   than browsing folders.
2. **Hover before you click.** Rest your pointer on any link — try
   [[Slope and Rate of Change]] — and the page appears in a small
   preview window. You can check an idea mid-sentence without losing
   your place.
3. **Read the backlinks.** At the bottom of every page is a list of
   every page that links *to* it. On [[Solving Equations]], the
   backlinks show each class and task where the idea earned its keep —
   a ready-made review list nobody had to build.

## Try before you peek

Exercise pages such as [[Equation Practice]] fold their answers away
behind a click. That fold is load-bearing: an honest attempt before
peeking teaches more than ten confirmations after. Predict, commit,
*then* expand — the order is the whole trick.
