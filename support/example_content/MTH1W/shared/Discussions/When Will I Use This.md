This question deserves better than the eye-roll it usually gets, so let
us take it seriously. The honest first answer: more than you think, and
mostly invisibly. Every phone plan, playlist, loan, bus schedule, and
game difficulty curve was designed by someone doing mathematics — and
when you compare plans in [[Which Phone Plan]] or watch a debt compound
in [[Interest and Growth]], you are doing the version that guards your
own wallet. The honest second answer is stranger: even when a topic
never returns, the thinking does. Nobody is asked to factor anything at
a dinner party, but "estimate before you commit" and "what is this per
unit?" run all day, every day, for the rest of your life.

Questions worth arguing about:

1. Name a decision you made this week that had hidden mathematics in
   it. Who actually did that mathematics — you, or someone selling you
   something?
2. A friend says "I will just use an app for everything." Where
   exactly does that plan break down — and where is it honestly fine?
3. Mathematical models set insurance rates, credit limits, and what
   your feed shows next. Is a decision made by a model more fair than
   one made by a person, or less? What would change your answer?
4. Which is worth more to an adult: remembering how to solve
   $2x + 7 = 15$, or the habit of checking whether an answer is
   reasonable? Defend the side you like less.
5. If the second answer is right — the thinking transfers even when
   the topic does not — which other school subject deserves the same
   defence?

Notice what did not happen here: nobody claimed you will use the
quadratic formula at the grocery store, and nobody pretended the
question was silly. That is the deal in this room — ideas get argued
at full strength, the way [[Our Classroom Norms]] promises — and "when
will I use this?" is welcome any day you bring it with a better
argument than last time.
