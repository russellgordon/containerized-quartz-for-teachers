If class ever feels hard, that is not the system failing — that is the
system. The thinking tasks at the whiteboards are chosen so no group
can coast: you meet each problem *before* being shown a method, because
the wrestling is where understanding gets built. Research on learning
backs this up — struggle, revision, and even error are when the
connections your brain is building physically strengthen. Easy
practice feels productive precisely because nothing is changing.

One distinction is worth naming. **Stuck-and-thinking** means you are
still generating moves — a smaller case, a sketch, a re-read.
**Stuck-and-spinning** means rereading the same line while feeling
worse. The first is the point of this course; the second is a signal
to change something, and [[Getting Unstuck]] is a toolkit for making
that switch on purpose.

Questions worth arguing about:

1. Describe the last time you were genuinely stuck and then got it —
   not told it, *got* it. What did the ten minutes before the
   breakthrough honestly feel like?
2. If a teacher could remove all struggle from this course, should
   they? What exactly would you be trading away?
3. Where is the line between productive struggle and just suffering?
   From the inside, how would you know which one you are in?
4. Some people hide being stuck; others announce it instantly and wait
   to be rescued. What is each habit protecting — and what would the
   brave middle look like?
5. Does watching a classmate struggle and recover teach you something
   your own struggle cannot?

This is why our groups are random and our whiteboards are vertical:
stuck is public, shared, and temporary in this room — never private
and permanent. The agreements that make that safe are in
[[Our Classroom Norms]], and the day struggle stops feeling like
evidence against you is the day this course starts working.
