Before you finished breakfast today, you generated data: when your
phone woke, what you tapped, how long you hovered over one video
before scrolling past. None of it was collected by accident. Somewhere
that stream is being stored, graphed, and modelled — the same trend
lines we build in [[Scatter Plots and Trends]], aimed at you instead
of drawn by you. This course teaches you to make
[[A Data Story|data tell a story]]; this discussion asks who is
telling stories with yours, and who profits when they land.

Questions worth arguing about:

1. List what one app on your phone likely knows about you. Then the
   harder question: what could it *infer* — guesses about mood, money,
   or friendships — that you never typed in anywhere?
2. A store offers ten percent off if you let it track every purchase.
   Fair trade? What discount would change your mind — and what does it
   mean that the trade has a price at all?
3. "If you have nothing to hide, you have nothing to fear." Attack or
   defend it with one specific, concrete example.
4. When data about a neighbourhood — collisions, arrests, house
   prices — gets collected and published, who benefits? Who can be
   harmed by the very same dataset?
5. Storage is cheap enough that data now lasts roughly forever. What
   is something true about you at fourteen that ought to be allowed to
   expire?

Later this course you will switch chairs: in the modelling projects
you become the one deciding what to collect, how to store it, and what
the model gets used for. The questions above are the designer's
questions — and once you have asked them about your own project, you
are entitled to ask them of every app, store, and school that collects
you. That is the practice: nobody in this room hands over data, or
gathers it, without asking who it serves.
