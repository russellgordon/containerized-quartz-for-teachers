## What you are making

The Math Fair is the culminating task: one curated exhibit, two lanes.

**The story lane.** Research one concept — a number concept, an
algebraic concept, or a geometric or measurement concept — and tell the
story of its development and use in a specific culture or community,
and its relevance today. Zero alone crossed three cultures on its way
to Europe; every measurement system is a story about what people needed.

**The problem lane.** Re-solve your own best problem from the course,
better — a new method, a clearer representation, an extension — and
show both solutions side by side, with what changed in you between them.

Every exhibit also carries a **growth statement** built from your
[[Math Journal]] — [[Showing Growth]] shows how to mine it, and the
statement you write here becomes the seed of your [[Final Reflection]].

At the fair you present twice over: a visitor-facing display that stands
alone for a stranger, and a defended conversation with visitors and me,
where you explain your choices.

## How to work

1. Choose your lane in the first studio period, and say why aloud to
   one other person — hearing yourself choose is half the choosing.
2. Story lane: go past the first search result — the interesting
   history is rarely on page one. Problem lane: re-solve first, compare
   after; do not let the old solution steer the new one.
3. Write the display for a stranger — [[Writing About Math]] is the
   guide. A visitor should understand the exhibit with you silent.
4. Reread your journal start to finish and pull three dated, specific
   moments of growth — claims need evidence, even claims about yourself.
5. Rehearse the conversation with a peer playing a skeptical visitor.
   Rehearsal time is class time, and it counts — [[How Marks Work]].

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like at your table |
| --- | --- |
| Stands alone | A stranger follows the exhibit without you narrating |
| Sound mathematics | The maths shown is accurate and connected to today |
| Honest sourcing | Research is credited; problem work is genuinely yours |
| Evidenced growth | The statement cites specific, dated journal entries |
| Defended, not recited | Questions get reasons, not a replayed script |
| An open door | The display invites questions rather than ending them |

## Reflect

The growth statement is this task's written reflection; the defended
conversation is its spoken one. Together they answer: what can you see
now that you could not see in September, and how do you know?

> [!success]- If you cannot choose an exhibit (click to expand)
> Flip through your journal for the entry with the most crossings-out —
> that struggle is your problem-lane candidate. For the story lane,
> start with a number or a way of measuring that your own family or
> community uses, and trace it back.
