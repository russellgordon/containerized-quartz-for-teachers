A toy figure, a supply of identical rubber bands, and a final drop
from the top of the stairwell. The contest: give your figure the
greatest thrill — the closest pass to the floor — without touching it.
You get one official drop, and no test drops from the final height.

## The task

Decide how many rubber bands to use for the big drop — before seeing
it tried. You may test as much as you like from *low* heights: chain a
few bands, drop, measure, repeat. The catch is that the winning answer
lives at a height you are not allowed to rehearse, so your small data
has to carry you there. Plan what to measure, measure it carefully,
and commit to a number your group can defend.

> [!question]- Getting started (click to expand)
> - One band, then two, then three… what exactly will you measure
>   each time, and from where to where? Agree before you drop.
> - Messy measurements are normal. Drop three times and take the
>   middle value — decide your rules before the data arrives.
> - What does *one more band* buy you, in centimetres? If you can say
>   that, heights you never tested start feeling predictable.

## What mathematics tends to surface

A table of bands against drop distance becomes a scatter plot, and
the scatter plot wants a line through it — the modelling move at the
centre of [[Scatter Plots and Trends]]. The line's steepness is
centimetres of drop per band; its starting value is the figure's own
height plus the slack of the chain; and reading both out of your data
is [[Slope and Rate of Change]] doing real work. [[Using Desmos]] will
happily fit the line — but *you* say what its numbers mean.

## An extension

Before the official drop, publish a prediction *with a margin*: "we
will pass within 10 cm of the floor." Afterwards, hold the autopsy:
where did the model bend the truth — and would doubling the figure's
mass change the steepness, the starting value, or both?

> [!note] The answer is not on this page
> There is no answer to print — every figure, band batch, and
> stairwell differs. The class builds the modelling arc together.
