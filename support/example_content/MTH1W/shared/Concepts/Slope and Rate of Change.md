You met this idea in class before it had a name — walking a
[[Bungee Drop]] prediction line, asking "how much more cord per storey?".
That "how much more per one more" is **slope**: the rate at which one
quantity changes as another ticks up by one. It is the single most
reused idea in this course.

## The formula, and what it actually says

$$
\text{slope} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}
$$

The triangle symbol $\Delta$ just means "change in". The formula is a
recipe: pick two points, subtract to find each change, divide. But read
it as a sentence and it says something plainer: *for every one step
across, how far up?*

| Situation | Slope means | Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A phone plan graph | Cost per extra gigabyte | $/GB |
| Bungee cord and storeys | Stretch per storey | cm/storey |
| Filling a pool | Litres per minute | L/min |
| A staircase | Steepness | rise per tread |

Same idea every time — only the costumes change. When a
[[Linear Relations|linear relation]] is written as $y = mx + b$, the
slope is the $m$, sitting in plain sight.

## Reading slope from a graph

Steeper means faster change. Downhill (left to right) means the slope is
negative — the quantity is shrinking. Flat means zero: no change at all.
Try covering the numbers on any graph in [[Scatter Plots and Trends]] and
saying only from the shape: what is happening, and how fast?

> [!success]- Quick self-check (click to expand)
> A line passes through $(2, 5)$ and $(6, 17)$. Slope
> $= \frac{17-5}{6-2} = \frac{12}{4} = 3$. Three up for every one
> across — and if that line is a phone plan, every extra unit costs $3.

Slope is also your first meeting with an idea that runs all the way to
calculus: measuring change. [[Slope and Graphing Practice]] builds the
mechanics; [[Which Phone Plan]] is where choosing between slopes starts
mattering in dollars.
