Canadian geographic data is unusually good and almost all of it is free.
The difficulty is not access — it is knowing which agency holds what, and
resisting the first search result, which is often a summary of a summary
of a page you could have read yourself.

## People

Statistics Canada holds the census. Census Profile, 2021 is the entry
point, searchable by place name, postal code or geographic code, with no
account and no fee:
[www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/dp-pd/prof/index.cfm?Lang=E](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/dp-pd/prof/index.cfm?Lang=E)
(accessed August 2026). The technique — and the count-versus-estimate trap
that ruins more Grade 9 work than any other single error — is in
[[Working With Census Data]].

## Weather and climate

Environment and Climate Change Canada runs the station archive. [Canadian
Climate Normals](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/climate_normals/) gives the
long-run averages, currently for the **1991–2020** period, and the
[historical data
search](https://climate.weather.gc.ca/historical_data/search_historic_data_e.html)
gives daily records station by station (accessed August 2026). Toronto
Pearson's 1991–2020 normals, for a worked comparison, are a January daily
average of −5.0 °C, a July daily average of 22.1 °C, and 806.8 mm of
precipitation a year.[^fees]

## Land, relief, and boundaries

Natural Resources Canada publishes the [Atlas of Canada interactive
maps](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/maps-tools-publications/maps/atlas-canada/interactive-maps) —
relief, major drainage areas, land cover, physiographic regions — plus
[Toporama](https://atlas.gc.ca/toporama/en/index.html) for topographic
detail, and [geo.ca](https://geo.ca/home/) as the federal search front end.
Ontario's open data catalogue carries the provincial layers a school
actually wants, such as the [Greenbelt outer
boundary](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/greenbelt-outer-boundary), under
the Open Government Licence – Ontario (all accessed August 2026).

## Classroom material you can hand out

Canadian Geographic Education publishes resources free and charges nothing
for membership. The [Biinaagami Great Lakes activity
package](https://cangeoeducation.ca/en/maps/biinaagami/) downloads without
booking anything or logging in (accessed August 2026). Its giant floor
maps run on a booking portal that reopens **18 August 2026**: eight maps
tour free of charge, and nine former maps now need a shipping quote by
email, so check the current list before promising one to a class.

## Choosing sources that disagree

Data is not the same as evidence. An investigation built entirely on
federal statistics is not balanced; it is one perspective with numbers. On
any contested issue, gather four kinds of source and notice what each
cannot say: the **proponent's** own material, the **regulator's** file,
the **affected communities' own publications** — including, where the
issue touches treaty land or traditional territory, the nation's own
statements — and independent **reporting or research**. Where two of them
disagree about a fact, the disagreement is itself a finding.

Then test each with [[Judging a Source]] before it goes in.

[^fees]: Two live traps. Environment and Climate Change Canada does
publish a price list for *custom* datasets compiled by its staff on
request; those fees do not apply to anything you download yourself. And
the archive lives at `climate.weather.gc.ca` with no `www.` in front — the
`www.` version fails on its security certificate.
