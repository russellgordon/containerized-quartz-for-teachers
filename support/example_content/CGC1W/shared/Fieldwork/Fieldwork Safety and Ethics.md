We go outside in this course — the shoreline, the woodlot, a street, and
a working farm. This page is what makes that possible, and it is not
optional reading.

## Safety

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Stay with the group and in sight of an adult | Shorelines and woodlots have edges, water, and holes |
| Closed shoes always; boots at the farm | Equipment, uneven ground, and things underfoot |
| Tell me about anything medical before we go | Asthma, allergies, mobility, medication — I need to know, and it stays with me |
| No wading, no climbing banks, no crossing fences | The bank you cannot see under is the one that gives way |
| Phones for photographs and maps, not for company | You cannot hear an instruction through earbuds |
| At the farm, touch nothing without asking | It is a workplace with machinery and living things |

If weather makes a day unsafe we cancel it, and you will know by the
morning.

## Ethics — the part people forget

Fieldwork means going to places that belong to somebody and looking at
things that involve other people's lives.

- **Land has an owner and a history.** We are on the traditional
  territory of Indigenous peoples whose relationship with this land did
  not end when a survey line was drawn. That is a fact about where we
  stand, not a preamble — see [[Land, Treaty, and Title]].
- **Ask before you enter.** We have permission for the sites we use.
  Permission is specific: it does not extend to the next field over.
- **Photograph places, not people.** No photographs of anyone who has
  not agreed, ever — and no photographs of the inside of anyone's home
  or vehicle from the street.
- **Leave it as you found it.** Nothing collected without permission, no
  digging without being asked to, gates as you found them.
- **What people tell you is theirs.** If a farmer tells you something
  about their business, it goes in your notebook, not on the internet.

> [!warning] The farm is somebody's living
> Everything you see there is a decision somebody made with real money.
> Ask, and you will get remarkable answers. Assume, and you will write
> something wrong about a person who could read it.
