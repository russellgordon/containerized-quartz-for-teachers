**The culminating task. Four class periods at the end of Unit 4, ending
with a presentation to a real audience.** Groups of three or four.

One geographic question about the place you actually live, investigated
properly and answered in public.

## The four days

| Day | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Scope: the question, the evidence you will need, the audience |
| 2 | Evidence: a site visit — the shoreline, the woodlot, the farm, or a street — plus data |
| 3 | Analysis: the concepts of geographic thinking applied to what you found |
| 4 | The presentation, to your audience, with questions taken |

## The audience is real

A municipal planner, a conservation authority staff member, a councillor,
a farmer, a community association, or the school's own administration.
Your teacher arranges the invitation; you write to them yourself, and you
send them your question in advance.

## What you deliver

- **The inquiry question**, and why it matters here.
- **Evidence you gathered yourself** — field observation, a survey, an
  interview, or your own mapping — alongside published data.
- **An analysis** that uses the four concepts explicitly: what is
  significant about where this is, what patterns and trends you found,
  what is connected to what, and whose perspective changes the answer.
- **A conclusion** with a recommendation, its cost, and what you would
  need to be more certain.
- **A one-page leave-behind** your audience keeps.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| The question | Specific, local, answerable, and worth asking |
| Method | Evidence gathered systematically, with its limits admitted |
| Geographic thinking | The four concepts used to reason, not named as decoration |
| The presentation | Twelve minutes a busy adult would find worth their time |
| Conduct | On time, prepared, and courteous to people who gave you their day |

> [!tip] The best questions come from something you noticed
> A drain that always floods. A field that became houses. A shoreline
> path that ends abruptly. Start from something real you have seen, and
> the research will have somewhere to go.

[[The Concepts of Geographic Thinking]] is the analytical spine, and
[[Fieldwork Safety and Ethics]] governs the site visit — including how
you behave on land that belongs to somebody.
