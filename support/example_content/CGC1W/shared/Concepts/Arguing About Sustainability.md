Two people can look at the same mine proposal, agree on every fact in it, and
still want opposite outcomes. That is not one of them being wrong. It is what a
**perspective** is — a position that decides which facts count most.

## Perspective is not the same as opinion

An opinion is what you happen to think. A perspective comes with a stake:
something the holder stands to gain or lose. You can describe one, predict what
it will say next, and take it seriously without agreeing with it. That is the
skill this unit wants.

| Perspective | The question it puts first | What it counts as evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economic | What does this cost, and what does it earn? | Jobs, royalties, tax revenue, price forecasts |
| Environmental | What does this do to the system over time? | Water chemistry, habitat, carbon, cumulative effects |
| Social | Who lives with the result? | Health, housing, language, who arrives and who leaves |
| Political | Who has the authority to decide? | Jurisdiction, treaties, permits, elections |

Most real arguments are not economy against environment. They are two groups
using different evidence to answer different questions, then being surprised
that they disagree.

## One place, several positions

Northern Ontario's Ring of Fire, in the James Bay Lowlands, holds chromite,
nickel and copper under muskeg with no year-round road to it. Webequie First
Nation and Marten Falls First Nation have each been leading environmental
assessments for road corridors, and have signed agreements with Ontario on that
work. Neskantaga First Nation has opposed the road and asked the federal
government for a full impact review, raising the Attawapiskat River, burial
sites, and its own drinking water — Neskantaga has been under a boil-water
advisory since 1995.

Notice what that means. There is no single "First Nations position", and
writing as though there were is the fastest way to get an analysis wrong.
Communities in the same region, facing the same project, have reached different
conclusions for reasons that make sense from where they stand.

The land is part of the argument too: those peatlands store enormous amounts of
carbon, and a road changes how water drains through them.

When you write about an issue like this, name the perspective before you give
the argument. "Environmental groups say" and "the province says" are not
neutral framing — they are your reader's only clue about the stake behind each
claim.

You will practise this on a resource of your choosing in
[[The Resource Investigation]], and the question of who holds authority over
land is worked separately in [[Land, Treaty, and Title]].
