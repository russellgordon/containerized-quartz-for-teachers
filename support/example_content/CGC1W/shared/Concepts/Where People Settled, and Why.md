The farm we visited is not on that field by accident. Its soil is glacial
till reworked by meltwater, deep enough to plough, and the lake a few
kilometres away holds off the first frost by a couple of weeks each
autumn. Change either of those and it is a different farm, or no farm.

That is the pattern for the whole country. Physical characteristics do
not force people to do anything, but they make some activities cheap and
others expensive, and over a long time cheap wins.

| Region | What the land offers | What people do with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Great Lakes–St. Lawrence Lowlands | Deep soils, long frost-free season, water that moderates temperature | Tender fruit, vegetables, most of Canada's manufacturing and population |
| Interior Plains | Thick dark soils on flat land, dry summers | Grain and oilseed farming, ranching on the drier margins |
| Canadian Shield | Metal-bearing rock, forest, lakes, moving water | Mining, forestry, hydroelectricity, tourism and cottaging |
| Appalachian Region | Shallow offshore banks, mixed forest, sheltered inlets | Fisheries, forestry, harbour communities |
| Western Cordillera | Steep drops, heavy snow, big trees, deep valleys | Hydro power, forestry, mountain recreation |
| Arctic Lands | Sea ice, marine mammals, caribou, tundra | Country food harvesting, mining, marine shipping in the open season |

Sudbury is the clean example. Roughly 1.85 billion years ago something
struck the Shield hard enough to melt a basin of rock, and that melt
sorted itself into one of the richest nickel and copper deposits on
Earth. A city of more than a hundred and fifty thousand people exists
because of a single event nobody witnessed.

## The interaction runs both ways

Physical characteristics shape how communities live, and communities also
carry knowledge of the physical world that took generations to build.
Inuit knowledge of sea-ice conditions — where it is safe, when it moves,
how it behaves in a given wind — is detailed, specific to each stretch of
coast, and is now used alongside satellite monitoring in several northern
ice-information programs. It is not folklore standing in for data. It is
a different and longer dataset.

You saw one of these interactions from the inside on [[The Farm Visit]].
