Announcing a strategy and having a strategy work are different events,
sometimes separated by decades. The useful question is never "is this a good
idea?" but "what changed, by how much, compared to what would have happened
anyway?"

## Two Ontario cases, two verdicts

Sudbury spent a century being stripped by smelter emissions — sulphur dioxide
killed the vegetation and the bare soil washed off the rock. The regreening
program began in 1978 and more than ten million trees have been planted since.
It worked, but it worked because smelter emissions fell by more than 95 per
cent over the same period. Trees planted into acid rain would have died. The
visible strategy succeeded because an invisible one did first.

Ontario's blue box began in Kitchener in 1981 and made recycling normal. What
it did not do was make the companies producing the packaging pay —
municipalities did, out of property taxes, with no say in what got made. On 1
January 2026 a three-year transition finished handing the system to those
producers, who now fund and run it. Whether that changes what reaches landfill
is a question for the tonnage data in a few years, not for today's
announcement.

## Testing any strategy

> [!success]- Four questions to ask before you judge one (click to expand)
> **What exactly was it supposed to change?** A strategy with no stated
> target cannot fail, which is convenient for whoever announced it.
>
> **What is being measured, and by whom?** Tonnes collected is not tonnes
> recycled. Hectares protected is not hectares intact.
>
> **Compared to what?** If waste fell during a recession, the recession
> may be doing the work.
>
> **Who bears the cost, and who gets the benefit?** These are frequently
> different people, and often in different places.

## Strategies do not fail in only one way

A strategy can miss its target, hit its target and not matter, or hit its
target and move the problem somewhere else. Shipping low-grade plastic overseas
counted as recycling for years, here and elsewhere, until the receiving
countries refused it. The local numbers were never false. They were measuring
the wrong end of the pipe.

Judge strategies the way you judge sources in [[Judging a Source]] — by asking
what the evidence can and cannot show.
