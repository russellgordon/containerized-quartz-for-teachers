A population is not a number. It is a shape — how old people are, how fast the
group is growing, where inside the country it sits, and where it came from. Six
measures will get you most of the way to describing any community in Canada,
including this one.

| Measure | What it tells you | Where it misleads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population | Size, nothing more | Depends entirely on the boundary drawn |
| Natural increase | Births minus deaths | Can be negative while population grows |
| Growth rate | Change per year, as a percentage | A big percentage of a small place is a few people |
| Density | People per square kilometre | Averages ice caps and downtowns together |
| Age structure | Who needs schools, who needs care | Hides sharp differences between neighbourhoods |
| Dependency ratio | Non-working ages against working ages | Assumes everyone 15–64 works, and nobody else does |

## What the last census found

The 2021 census counted 36,991,981 people in Canada, at a density of about 4.2
per square kilometre — a number so meaningless on its own that it is a good
lesson in itself, since 73.7 per cent of those people lived in one of the large
urban centres. Immigrants made up 23.0 per cent of the population, the highest
share since Confederation. Indigenous peoples — First Nations, Métis and Inuit
— numbered 1.8 million, 5.0 per cent, and 44.3 per cent of them lived in large
urban centres, up from five years earlier.

By 1 July 2025, Statistics Canada estimated the population at about 41.7
million, but growth that year was the slowest in decades outside 2020.
Population is a moving target; the year attached to a figure is part of the
figure.

> [!tip] Say the boundary out loud
> "Toronto" is 2.8 million people or 6.2 million people depending on
> whether you mean the city or the census metropolitan area. Neither is
> wrong. Quoting one and comparing it to the other is. Whenever you write
> a population number, write what area it covers and what year it is
> from, in the same sentence.

Canada takes a census every five years. The one taken this past May will start
producing numbers next year, and those numbers will move some of the figures
above.

Pull the real data for your own community in [[Working With Census Data]], and
turn the shape into an argument in [[The Population Profile]].
