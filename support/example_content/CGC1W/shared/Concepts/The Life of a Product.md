An aluminum can weighs about thirteen grams and has been most of the way around
the world before you open it. Following one is the cheapest way to see that
every stage of "the economy" happens somewhere specific, to somebody specific.

## Five stages, five geographies

**Extraction.** Aluminum starts as bauxite, mined in the tropics — Guinea,
Australia, Brazil. Canada mines none of it. Open-pit bauxite mining clears
forest and produces a caustic red residue that has to be stored somewhere
permanently.[^stages]

**Production.** Canada smelts a great deal of aluminum anyway, almost all in
Quebec, because smelting is really a way of selling electricity — it takes
enormous constant power, and Quebec's rivers supply it cheaply and cleanly.
Location follows energy, not ore.

**Distribution.** The can is filled near where it will be sold, because
shipping full cans is shipping water. Empty cans travel; drinks mostly do not.

**Consumption.** You drink it in a few minutes.

**Disposal.** Here the geography turns local and political. In most provinces
that can carries a deposit you get back; in Ontario the deposit system covers
alcohol containers, so a pop can carries none and goes to the blue box instead.
Remelting aluminum uses a small fraction of the energy of making it from
bauxite, which makes the recovery rate the main event rather than an
environmental footnote.

## Why the tracing is the point

Nobody involved makes a decision about "sustainability". A mining company
decides where ore is cheapest, a smelter where power is cheapest, a grocery
chain what shelf space earns, a province whether to charge a deposit. The
environmental result is what those separate decisions add up to, in places that
never talk to each other.

That is why the fix is rarely a single fix. A deposit changes disposal but not
extraction. A cleaner smelter changes production but not the mine. Anyone
claiming one change solves the whole life cycle has skipped four of the five
stages.

reason Quebec smelts and Guinea mines is the same reason worked out in

[^stages]: Bauxite is refined into alumina, a white powder, before
    smelting into metal. Those three steps often happen on three
    continents, which is why "where is this from?" has more than one
    answer.
