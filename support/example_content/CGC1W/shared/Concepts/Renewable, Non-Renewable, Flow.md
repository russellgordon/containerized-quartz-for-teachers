These three words sort resources by **rate**, not by virtue. A renewable
resource is not automatically the responsible choice, and a
non-renewable one is not automatically reckless. What the categories tell
you is how the resource behaves if you keep taking it.

| Type | The rule that defines it | Canadian examples | Where it goes wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Renewable | Replaces itself, but only at a certain speed | Forests, fish stocks, soil, fresh water, wildlife | Harvesting faster than it replaces — at which point it behaves exactly like a non-renewable resource |
| Non-renewable | The stock was made over geological time and is not being remade | Nickel, copper, gold, potash, oil, natural gas, uranium, aggregate | Treating the reserve figure as fixed; it moves with price and technology, in both directions |
| Flow | Exists as a process, not a stock; there whether you use it or not | Wind, sunlight, running water, tides | Cannot be stockpiled or shipped — you must use it when and where it occurs, or lose it |

## The category that is easiest to get wrong

Renewable is the dangerous one, because the word sounds like a guarantee.
Northern cod off Newfoundland was one of the largest fish stocks in the
world and had supported communities for centuries. Improved technology
allowed a catch far above the rate at which the fish replaced themselves.
In 1992 Canada closed the fishery, and tens of thousands of people in
Newfoundland and Labrador lost their work in a single announcement. The
stock has not returned to anything like what it was.

Nothing about cod changed. What changed was the ratio between how fast we
took it and how fast it came back. That ratio is the only thing the word
"renewable" is about.

## Flow resources have a geography problem, not a supply problem

There is no shortage of wind or sunlight in Canada. The difficulty is
that a flow resource is produced where the physical conditions are, at
the times they occur, and electricity is wanted somewhere else at other
times. Every serious argument about wind and solar in this country is
really an argument about transmission and storage.

The woodlot is a renewable resource that a class can actually measure —
see [[The Woodlot Study]] for what a sustainable rate of removal looks
like on one hectare. You will pick one Canadian resource and follow it
properly in [[The Resource Investigation]].
