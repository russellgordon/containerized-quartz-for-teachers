Some population problems do not stay inside a border. Water scarcity,
displacement by war or drought, disease that travels at the speed of air
traffic, and the rapid growth of cities that were not built for it are all
issues Canada takes part in — sometimes as a donor, sometimes as a destination,
sometimes as a cause.

## Displacement is the one that reaches here first

The number of people forced from their homes by war and persecution worldwide
has been above 100 million since 2022, a level the UN refugee agency had never
recorded before. UNHCR counted 117.8 million at the end of 2025 — 41.6 million
refugees, 68.7 million displaced inside their own country, and 9 million
asylum-seekers waiting on a decision. That was 5.4 million fewer than a year
earlier, the first fall the agency had recorded in a decade — and it is still
close to three times the population of Canada. Most are not in wealthy
countries: the large majority are hosted by states neighbouring the one they
left, often for years, usually with fewer resources than Canada has.

Canada resettles a small fraction of that total. It is a meaningful fraction by
the standards of resettlement programs and a tiny one by the standards of the
problem, and an honest analysis contains both sentences.

Climate is now part of this. Drought, flooding and failing crops move people
internally long before anyone crosses a border. In law, a refugee is someone
outside their own country with a well-founded fear of persecution; a person
displaced inside their country, or by drought, has no equivalent status. That
gap is a live argument, not a settled definition.

## Where Canada acts, and how you judge it

Individuals give and volunteer. Non-governmental organizations run projects and
lobby governments. The federal government sets immigration and refugee policy,
funds aid, and works through bodies such as the World Health Organization and
the UN.

> [!success]- Judging an international response (click to expand)
> **Is the aid tied?** Aid that must be spent on the donor country's own
> goods and consultants returns much of its value to the donor.
>
> **Is it relief or capacity?** Food shipments save lives now; a
> functioning clinic changes the next decade. Both are needed, and they
> are not interchangeable.
>
> **Who set the priorities?** Programs designed with the receiving
> community tend to survive the funding ending. Programs designed for
> them tend not to.

international sources to the standard you use at home, in [[Judging a Source]].
