A region is an argument. Someone decided that this piece of Canada is
different enough from the next piece to deserve its own name and its own
line on a map. Your job is to say what the evidence for that line is.

Canada's physical regions are drawn from three things: what the rock is,
what shape the surface takes, and where the water goes. Draw the map from
language, or farming, or election results, and the lines land somewhere
else. None of those maps is wrong. They are answers to different
questions.

## The seven, and how you would know

| Region | The rock beneath | What you would see from the ground |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canadian Shield | Ancient igneous and metamorphic rock, some of the oldest on Earth | Bare grey and pink outcrops, thin soil, lakes past counting |
| Hudson Bay Lowlands | Flat sedimentary layers, still rising since the ice left | Peat and muskeg, almost no slope in any direction |
| Great Lakes–St. Lawrence Lowlands | Sedimentary rock buried under deep glacial deposits | Gentle relief, deep soil, the Niagara Escarpment as the one hard edge |
| Appalachian Region | Folded rock worn down for 300 million years | Rounded hills, drowned valleys, a coast made of inlets |
| Interior Plains | Flat-lying sediment from an ancient inland sea | Long horizons, rivers cut deep below the surface, badlands |
| Western Cordillera | Young folded and faulted rock, still rising | Steep ranges, sharp peaks, valleys running north and south |
| Arctic Lands | Mountains in the far northeast, low plains to the west | Permafrost, tundra, no trees |

The boundaries are real but they are not fences. Walk north from the
lowlands and the soil thins, the outcrops appear, and somewhere in that
kilometre you have entered the Shield. Cartographers put the line where
the change is fastest.

## Regions people made, on land the ice made

Some of Canada's most closely described landscapes are cultural as well
as physical. Pimachiowin Aki, on the Shield where Manitoba meets Ontario,
was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2018 — the first Canadian
site recognized for both its natural values and the living relationship
of the Anishinaabe communities who proposed it. The boreal forest, the
rivers, and the people who have kept the land are named together, because
in that nomination they are not separable.

You will draw your own regional line, with evidence, in
[[The Region Study]]. If you want to see a boundary before you name it,
[[Reading a Topographic Map]] shows you how contour spacing gives away a
change in rock long before a textbook does.
