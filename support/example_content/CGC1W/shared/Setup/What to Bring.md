Small list, and the first two matter more than you would think.

| Bring | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A notebook you keep all term | Your field notebook — see [[The Field Notebook]]. Paper works in rain and does not run out of battery |
| A pencil, not only a pen | Pens fail in cold and on wet paper; pencils do not |
| A device you can map and research on | The school has loaners; most mapping work is browser-based |
| Outdoor clothes on field days | The class page names the day a week ahead |
| Closed shoes for the farm and the woodlot | Required, not advisory |

## On field days

Dress for the weather that is actually forecast, not the weather you
would prefer. We go out in most conditions, and the ones we cancel are
announced by the morning of.

For the farm visit: closed shoes, nothing you mind getting muddy, and
leave the earbuds at home — you will be near equipment and around people
working.

> [!tip] Take photographs with a purpose
> A photograph of a slope, a shoreline, or a street is evidence if you
> note where you stood and which way you faced. Without that, it is a
> nice picture. Your notebook is what turns one into the other.

## Software

Everything can be done with a browser, a word processor, and a
spreadsheet — all provided by the school. The mapping tools we use are
free; [[Using a Web GIS]] says which and how to get in.
