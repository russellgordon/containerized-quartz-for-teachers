This course discusses land, money, immigration, climate, and who gets to
decide — subjects people hold strong views about, and some of which are
about students in this room. This is how we work.

## In discussion

- **Argue with the claim, not the person.** "That assumes the data covers
  everyone" is an argument. Dismissing someone is not.
- **Bring evidence.** You are entitled to your own view and not to your
  own facts.
- **Let people finish**, especially when you are sure they are wrong.
- **Change your mind out loud.** It is the most respected thing anyone
  does in a seminar here.

## About people and places

Some of what we study is somebody's home, somebody's job, or somebody's
family history.

- No naming private people or households in your work.
- When you write about a community, write as though someone from it will
  read it — because in this course they sometimes do.
- Nobody has to share a personal experience. "Pass" is a complete answer.

## About Indigenous lands, treaties, and communities

This course deals with treaty relationships, land claims, and resource
decisions on territories that are not the Crown's to define alone.

- We use sources from the nations involved when we describe their
  positions, rather than describing them for them.
- We treat treaties as agreements that are in force now, not as history.
- Disagreement between governments and nations is real, and we represent
  it rather than smoothing it into a settled story.

## Outside

The field rules are in [[Fieldwork Safety and Ethics]] and they are not
negotiable — they are what lets us keep going out.

> [!warning] If something here lands close to home
> A discussion about immigration, housing, land, or climate can be
> personal in ways the rest of the room may not see. Tell me beforehand
> or afterwards, and we will handle it. You do not owe me the reason.
