**The question:** obligations travel badly. How far — across a border,
across a watershed, across sixty years — does one community's
responsibility actually reach?

Four cases, and they do not all pull the same way: a refugee arriving
from a war, aid sent to a country in a drought, emissions released here
that warm somewhere else, and water taken upstream from the people who
live downstream. In each, somebody benefits and somebody else absorbs the
consequence, and the two are rarely in the same place.

## Prepare

Choose one of the four. Bring what Canada — a government, an
organization, or people acting on their own — currently does about it,
and one figure with a source and a year on it.
[[Global Population Questions]] is the reading; [[Finding the Right Data]]
is how you get the number honestly.

## Three positions

**Proximity first.** Obligation weakens with distance. You owe most to
the people you share services, taxes, and a council with, and a community
that cannot house or treat the people already in it has nothing spare to
export. [[Policy Responses]] is where this argument gets tested.

**Contribution.** You owe in proportion to what you caused. Emissions and
upstream water use create obligations regardless of distance, because the
harm crossed the distance first.

**Common humanity.** A person's claim on help does not depend on where
they were born or on who caused their situation. Need is the whole
argument.[^limits]

[^limits]: The honest objection: every community has finite housing,
    clinics, teachers, and money, and pretending otherwise is how
    promises get broken. The serious version of this position sets a
    limit and says out loud where it is.

## In the seminar

State your case, then take the hardest question from a position you did
not choose. Two tests: does your argument survive being applied to *your*
community with the situation reversed, and can you name its cost? A
position with no cost attached is a preference in an argument's clothing.
Carry the strongest version you heard into [[The Policy Brief]].
