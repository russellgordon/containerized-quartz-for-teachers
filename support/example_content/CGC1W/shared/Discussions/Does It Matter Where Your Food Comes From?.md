**The question:** you are standing in front of two bags of carrots. One
grew five minutes from here, one arrived from three time zones away and
costs less. Does the choice matter, and to whom?

"Buy local" is the easiest slogan in geography and one of the hardest
claims to defend with numbers. Canada's growing season is short, our
population is spread thin along a long supply line, and the household
that most needs cheap food usually has the least room to choose.

| Claim | What supports it | What complicates it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local food travels less | Fewer kilometres from field to shelf | How it was grown and stored can outweigh how far it moved |
| Local food keeps money here | The farm, its wages, and its suppliers are in this economy | The distant farm supports a community too |
| Local food is fresher | Hours, not weeks, since harvest | True in September; ask again in February |
| Everyone should buy local | It is a real choice for some households | Price and store location decide it for many others |

## Prepare

You have stood in a working vegetable field. Go back to your notes from
[[The Farm Visit]] and bring three things: what that farm actually grows,
what its season is, and one thing the grower told you about cost or
distance that you did not expect. Then price one item — local and
imported — on the same day, and write both numbers down.

## Two positions

**It matters.** A food system with a nearby farm in it is one that can
absorb a shock — a closed border, a bad harvest elsewhere, a fuel spike.
Protecting the land around a town is protecting a capability, and once
that field is a subdivision the choice is gone permanently.

**It matters less than you think.** Distance is a poor proxy for impact,
"local only" rules out much of the produce aisle for much of a Canadian
year, and telling a household on a tight grocery budget that its carrots
are a moral failure is an argument that deserves to lose.

## In the seminar

Argue with your two prices on the table. Then take the question that
sorts opinion from analysis: what would have to change — in price,
season, or distance — for you to switch? Bring the answer to
[[Feeding a Country]], and the sharper version of it to
[[The Local Inquiry]].
