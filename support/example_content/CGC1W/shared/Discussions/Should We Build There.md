**The question:** a region needs housing and the flat, cheap, available
land is the land nature has a claim on. Do you build?

Every one of the four sites below has been built on somewhere in Canada,
and every one has produced a bill somebody later had to pay. The argument
is not whether the risk exists. It is who carries it, and what the
alternative costs.

| Site | The case for building | What you are trading away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floodplain | Flat, serviced, next to an existing town | A hazard that arrives on its own schedule |
| Wetland | Cheap, and it looks like waste ground | Drainage, filtration, and habitat for everything downstream |
| Prime farmland | Level, drained, ready for a subdivision tomorrow | Soil that took millennia and cannot be relocated |
| Wildland edge | The view people will pay for | A house inside a fire's fuel supply |

## Prepare

Bring one real Canadian example — a subdivision, a flood, a fire, a
rezoning fight — with the year, the location, and one number you can
source. [[Building Where Nature Pushes Back]] and
[[Hazard, Exposure, Risk]] give you the vocabulary. If you did the
[[The Land Use Walk\|land use walk]], bring what you saw.

## Two positions, both serious

**Build.** People need somewhere to live now, and the shortage is
measurable in rents and in who has to leave the community they grew up
in. Refusing every risky site pushes development further out, which is
[[Sprawl and Its Alternatives\|sprawl]] with a conscience — the same
farmland lost, just further from the jobs.

**Don't.** A hazard map is a forecast of a bill. Rebuilding a flooded
street costs more than never zoning it, prime farmland is not replaceable
at any price, and the household that ends up in the floodplain is rarely
the one that made the decision.

## In the seminar

Argue the site, not the principle: take one of the four rows and defend a
specific decision on it. Then answer the question that decides most real
cases — *if not there, then where, and who moves?* If your answer is "we
build differently," say what that costs and who pays the difference.

The version of this you have to commit to is
[[The Land Use Proposal]], where "it depends" is not an available answer.
