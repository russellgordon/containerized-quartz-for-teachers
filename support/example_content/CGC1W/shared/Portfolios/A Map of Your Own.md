One map, of one place you choose, made three times across the term. You
keep all three versions and a short reflection on each, and the
reflections are the point — a map is an argument about what matters, and
you will change your mind twice.

## Choosing the place

Somewhere you can return to and check: the shoreline, the woodlot, the
farm, your street, the route you travel to school. Small enough to gather
your own data about, real enough that something about it is in question.

## The three versions

**One, by hand, in Unit 1.** Paper, pencil, a north arrow, a scale, a
legend, and a title saying what the map is *for*. No software. You will
find out what you actually know about the place, which is usually less
than expected.

**Two, in Unit 2, using data you did not collect.** Rebuild it in a web
GIS with at least one published layer — elevation, land cover, zoning,
watersheds. [[Using a Web GIS]] and [[Reading a Topographic Map]] are the
tools. Cite every layer.

**Three, in Unit 3 or 4, thematic.** One variable, mapped properly:
classes chosen and justified, a legend a stranger can read, the source
and year on the face of the map. [[Making a Thematic Map]] and
[[Working With Census Data]] cover the technique.

## The reflection on each version

About 150 words per version, written when you finish it, answering two
questions: what does this version show that the last one could not, and
what is it still unable to show? Name the choice that hid something — the
scale you picked, the class breaks, the boundary you drew, the thing you
left off because it was inconvenient.

> [!note]- Every map leaves something out (click to expand)
> That is not a flaw to apologize for; it is how a map works. The
> difference between a good map and a misleading one is whether the
> maker knows what was left out and says so. Your reflection is where
> you say so.

Keep the three side by side at the end of term. The distance from one to
three is the clearest evidence in your portfolio that your
[[The Concepts of Geographic Thinking\|spatial reasoning]] changed.
