What you should be able to do by the end of CGC1W, in plain words first
and in the Ministry's words underneath.

## By the end of the course you should be able to

- **Ask a geographic question worth answering** about a real place, and
  say what evidence would answer it.
- **Read a map and a dataset** — a topographic sheet, a census profile,
  a climate record — and say what they show and what they leave out.
- **Explain a place**: why the land is like that, why people are where
  they are, and how those two facts are connected.
- **Weigh a decision** about land, resources, or growth from more than
  one position, including positions you do not hold.
- **Make a map that argues** rather than decorates.
- **Say what you actually observed**, separately from what you concluded.

## The self-check that matters

Stand somewhere in this community and answer three questions: what is
this place made of, who lives here and why, and what is about to change?
If you can answer those with evidence, you can do this course — and
that is exactly what [[The Local Inquiry]] asks.
