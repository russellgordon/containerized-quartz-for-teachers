In the James Bay Lowlands of northern Ontario, centred on McFaulds Lake, a
crescent-shaped mineral belt of roughly 5,000 square kilometres holds
chromite, nickel, copper and platinum group elements — roughly 500 to 550
kilometres north-northeast of Thunder Bay, with **no all-season road**. It
has been argued about since 2007, and **nothing has been mined there.**
Read that again first, because much of what is written about the Ring of
Fire describes a mining region, and there is no operating mine.

The deposits lie under the Hudson Bay Lowland, the **second-largest
peatland complex on Earth**, holding an estimated 30 to 39 billion tonnes
of soil carbon; the Ring of Fire peatlands hold about 550 million tonnes
([Wildlife Conservation Society
Canada](https://wcscanada.org/our-work/wild-places/peatlands/), accessed
August 2026). Communities nearby call it the **Breathing Lands**. Peat
holds carbon by holding water, so anything draining it — a road embankment
included — is part of the carbon question, not separate from it. The
Eagle's Nest nickel-copper deposit belongs to **Wyloo**, an Australian
company; sources naming "Ring of Fire Metals" use a former name for it.

## Where things stand

| Project | Whose project | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Webequie Supply Road, 107 km | Webequie First Nation | Ontario approved the environmental assessment 18 June 2026, the federal decision statement followed 25 June 2026, and **construction began 25 June 2026** |
| Marten Falls Community Access Road, ~184 km | Marten Falls First Nation | Federal review report finalized 6 August 2026; **decision pending** |
| Northern Road Link, 117–164 km | Marten Falls and Webequie jointly | Earliest stage, still in the federal impact statement phase |
| Eagle's Nest mine | Wyloo | **No construction.** In February 2026 the federal agency decided it does not warrant a federal impact assessment; the provincial requirement was removed under Ontario's Bill 5 |

Two things are widely reported wrongly: the Ring of Fire was **never
designated a special economic zone**, and in March 2026 the Premier said
he saw no reason to use that power; and **no Ring of Fire project has been
referred to the federal Major Projects Office**. Meanwhile a federal
**Regional Assessment** co-led by the impact assessment agency and **15
First Nations** issued an interim report in February 2026 and does not
report finally until **20 July 2027** — after road construction has
already started
([tracker](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/evaluations/proj/80468), accessed
August 2026). Ontario projects more than 70,000 jobs and \$22 billion over
30 years; that is the government's estimate, and say so when you quote it.

## Positions that genuinely disagree

**Webequie First Nation** is the proponent of its own supply road and
signed a \$39.5 million partnership with Ontario in October 2025, framing
it as opportunity on the nation's own terms. **Marten Falls First Nation**
is the proponent of its access road, co-proponent of the Northern Road
Link, signed a comparable agreement in December 2025, and describes the
work as economic reconciliation and an end to remoteness.

**Neskantaga First Nation** opposes the current process, citing burial
grounds along the Attawapiskat River, its own unresolved drinking-water
conditions, and the speed of approvals. **Attawapiskat First Nation**
opposes development in the peatlands and is a plaintiff in the
constitutional challenge to Bill 5 and C-5. Since June 2025 the two have
jointly maintained a land-defence encampment at the proposed Northern Road
Link crossing.

**Aroland First Nation** breaks the simple version. It signed an agreement
in January 2025 covering a road segment in its territory and electrical
power — and stated publicly in June 2025 that the agreement does not
indicate consent for mining, that it does not stand with Ontario in
support of Bill 5, and that it stands with the nations opposing it.
Aroland is also a plaintiff. Supporting a road is not consenting to a
mine. **Matawa First Nations**, a tribal council of nine, holds members on
opposite sides, and Attawapiskat is not a Matawa member. Both lawsuits —
the Bill 5 and C-5 challenge filed by nine First Nations in July 2025, and
a Federal Court review of the Webequie approval filed in July 2026 — are
**unresolved**.

## Questions

1. Construction began in June 2026; the Regional Assessment reports in
   July 2027. What does each side gain and lose from that ordering?
2. Aroland signed an agreement and sued to overturn the enabling law. What
   does that show that a "for or against" list cannot?
3. Both assessment requirements for Eagle's Nest were removed. Who is left
   holding the job those assessments were meant to do?
4. Build the strongest climate argument **for** the mine, then **against**.
5. Whose consent would have to be given, and how, before you would call a
   decision here legitimate?
