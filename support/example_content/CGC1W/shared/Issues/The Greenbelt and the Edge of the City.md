A line was drawn around the Greater Golden Horseshoe in 2005 and building
was restricted outside it. That is the whole mechanism, and nearly every
argument about growth in southern Ontario since has been about where the
line sits, who may move it, and how.

## The situation

The **Greenbelt Act, 2005** protects farmland, forests, wetlands and
watersheds across what Ontario describes as over 800,000 hectares,
stretching about 325 kilometres from the eastern end of the Oak Ridges
Moraine near Rice Lake to the Niagara River.[^size] The plan in force is
the Greenbelt Plan (2017), amended most recently in October 2024
([ontario.ca/page/ontarios-greenbelt](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontarios-greenbelt),
accessed August 2026); the boundary downloads as a map layer from
[Ontario's data catalogue](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/greenbelt-outer-boundary).

In **December 2022** Ontario removed or redesignated 15 areas totalling
about 7,400 acres while adding about 9,400 acres elsewhere. In **August
2023** the Auditor General reported that the selection "cannot be
described as a standard or defensible process," that 14 of the 15 sites
reached the project team from the Housing Minister's political staff, that
owners "could see a \$8.28 billion increase in the value of their land,"
and that 76 per cent of the removed acreage was in active agricultural
use. Days later the Integrity Commissioner described a process "marked by
misinterpretation, unnecessary hastiness and deception."

In **December 2023** the removals were restored, and Bill 136 moved the
boundary out of regulation and into the statute itself — the durable
change, since altering it now takes an Act of the legislature rather than
a Cabinet decision. An RCMP investigation remains open with no charges; a
developer's constitutional challenge to the restoration, filed December
2023, is unresolved; and the legally required ten-year review of the Plan,
due to begin in February 2025, had still not formally started as of
October 2025. Separately, **Highway 413** is legislated to proceed on an
accelerated assessment and will cross Greenbelt and immediately adjacent
lands, with first construction contracts announced in August 2025.

## Positions that genuinely disagree

**Protect it.** Class 1 soil took thousands of years to make and sits
exactly where cities want to expand; Ontario's farmland fell from about
12.35 million acres in 2016 to 11.77 million in 2021. A boundary that can
be moved is not a boundary.

**Build on it.** Housing cost is the urgent problem, and restricting
supply at the edge raises land prices inside the line. Protection has a
price, and somebody pays it.

**Build inside it instead.** Intensification uses infrastructure already
paid for and is cheaper per household — and is contested block by block.

**The process argument, which is separate.** Two independent officers
found the 2022 removals improperly made. You can think the Greenbelt
should shrink and still think this was the wrong way to shrink it.

## Questions

1. Moving the boundary now requires legislation. Who does that protect,
   and from what?
2. If a growth boundary raises land prices inside it, is that an argument
   against the boundary or for building more inside it?
3. The 2022 changes removed 7,400 acres and added 9,400. Why is "the
   Greenbelt got bigger" an incomplete answer?
4. Highway 413 will cross protected land under a law passed for that
   purpose. How durable is any land protection, then?
5. Whose interests would have to be represented for you to call the next
   boundary decision legitimate?
