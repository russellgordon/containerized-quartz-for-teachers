In Grade 12 the marks move from getting the number to justifying the
choice. Two answers with the same arithmetic can be worlds apart.

## Say which principle, and why that one

"Momentum is conserved here because the collision is over before friction
can act" is a physics sentence. "I used $p = mv$" is a report of what you
typed into a calculator.

## Diagrams are part of the answer

A vector diagram with components marked, a free-body diagram with the
axes tilted, a field diagram with the direction of the force on a positive
test charge — these are not decoration. In two dimensions they are how the
problem gets solved, and a solution without one is usually a solution with
a sign error in it.

## Every number carries a unit, a direction where it needs one, and an
## uncertainty where you measured it

$1.4 \times 10^3\ \text{N}$ toward the centre of the circle. Not "1400".

## Words we use precisely

| Word | Means here | Does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Centripetal force | The net force, directed to the centre | An extra force you add |
| Centrifugal | An apparent force in a rotating frame | A real force pushing outward |
| Field | What exerts the force at that point | The force itself |
| Relativistic | Depending on the observer's frame | Fast |
| Quantum | Coming in discrete amounts | Small, or mysterious |

The first two rows account for most of the wrong answers in Unit 1, and
the last row for most of the loose talk in Unit 5.

> [!tip] The test for a good sentence
> Could a classmate reproduce your reasoning from what you wrote — not
> your arithmetic, your reasoning? That is the standard here.
