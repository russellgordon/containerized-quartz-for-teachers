> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** half a class. **Groups of three.** No write-up — the
> observations are the point, and they carry the whole unit.

Two dippers in a tray of water, and the interference pattern that every
later page in this unit refers back to.

## Safety

Water and mains electricity share a bench here. The lamp stays clear of
the tray, hands dry before touching the supply, and spills reported
immediately.

## What to do

1. One dipper first. Watch circular wavefronts spread out.
2. Two dippers, same frequency, in phase. Find the **nodal lines** — the
   still water where two waves cancel — and count them.
3. Increase the frequency. What happens to the spacing of the nodal
   lines?
4. Move the dippers further apart. Predict first, then look.
5. Put a barrier with one narrow gap in the tank. Watch the wave spread
   out beyond it — diffraction, with your own eyes.

## What to carry forward

Steps 3 and 4 are the two dependencies you will meet again as equations in
[[Young's Double-Slit Experiment]]. Having watched them in water, the
light version is a change of scale rather than a new idea.
