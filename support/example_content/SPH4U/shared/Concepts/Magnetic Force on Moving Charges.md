$$F_m = qvB\sin\theta$$

A magnetic field exerts a force on a charge only when the charge MOVES,
and only on the component of its motion perpendicular to the field. A
stationary charge in a magnetic field feels nothing.

## The direction is sideways

Perpendicular to both the velocity and the field, which is why right-hand
rules exist and why the resulting motion is circular: a force always
perpendicular to velocity changes direction and never speed.

Setting $qvB = mv^2/r$ gives the radius:

$$r = \frac{mv}{qB}$$

which is how a mass spectrometer sorts isotopes, and how a cyclotron
keeps particles on track.

## The same force on a wire

A current is moving charge, so a current-carrying wire in a field feels
$F = BIL\sin\theta$ — the motor principle from Grade 11, now with the
underlying reason attached.

## Earth's shield

Charged particles from the Sun spiral along Earth's magnetic field lines
instead of striking the atmosphere head-on. Where the lines dive into the
poles, they collide with air and make the aurora.
