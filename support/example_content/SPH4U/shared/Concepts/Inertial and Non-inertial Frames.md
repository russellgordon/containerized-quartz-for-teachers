An **inertial** frame is one where Newton's first law holds: things left
alone keep their velocity. A frame that is accelerating is
**non-inertial**, and inside it objects appear to accelerate with nothing
pushing them.

## The bus

A bus brakes and you lurch forward. From the road (inertial), nothing
pushed you — you kept your velocity while the bus slowed. From inside the
bus (non-inertial), you were shoved by an invisible force.

Both descriptions predict the same bruise. Only one of them can name what
exerted the force.

## Fictitious forces

The centrifugal force, the Coriolis force, and the force that presses you
into your seat as a lift starts are all fictitious in this precise sense:
they appear when you insist on using Newton's laws in a frame where they
do not apply. They are not imaginary — the effects are real — but they
have no third-law partner, which is the giveaway.

## Why it matters here

Because "the centrifugal force flings the object outward" is the single
most common wrong sentence in Unit 1, and this is why it is wrong. See
[[Centripetal Force]].
