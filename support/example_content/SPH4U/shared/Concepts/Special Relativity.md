Two postulates, and everything strange follows from them:

1. The laws of physics are the same in every inertial frame.
2. The speed of light in vacuum is the same for every observer, whatever
   their motion.

The second one is the outrage. It says a beam of light passes you at $c$
whether you chase it or flee it — which cannot be true of anything else,
and is measured to be true of light.

## Time dilation

$$\Delta t = \frac{\Delta t_s}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$$

A moving clock runs slow, as judged from the frame it is moving in.
"Judged from" matters: each observer sees the OTHER's clock running slow,
and both are right, because they disagree about which events are
simultaneous.

## Length contraction

$$L = L_s\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$$

A moving object is shorter along its direction of motion. Not squashed —
measured shorter, because measuring a length means locating both ends at
the same time, and "the same time" is frame-dependent.

## Why nobody notices

At 100 km/h the factor differs from 1 by about $10^{-14}$. At 0.9c it is
2.3. The effects were always there; we lived our whole lives below the
speeds where they show.

## The evidence

Muons created in the upper atmosphere reach the ground in numbers that
only make sense if their clocks run slow — or, from the muon's frame, if
the atmosphere is contracted. Both descriptions agree on what the detector
counts.
