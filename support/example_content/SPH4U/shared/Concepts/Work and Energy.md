$$W = Fd\cos\theta \qquad W_{net} = \Delta E_k$$

The work–energy theorem is the bridge between the force picture of Unit 1
and the energy picture of Unit 2: the net work done on an object equals
the change in its kinetic energy.

## When to reach for energy instead of forces

- The path is complicated but the endpoints are known.
- The force varies (a spring, a bow) and $F = ma$ would need calculus.
- You want a speed and do not care about time.

Energy has no direction, which is exactly why it is easier — and exactly
why it cannot answer questions about direction. For those, use momentum.

## Work done by a varying force

The area under a force–displacement graph. For a spring that area is a
triangle, which is where $\tfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ comes from — see
[[Hooke's Law and Elastic Potential Energy]].

## Conservative and non-conservative

Gravity gives back everything you spend against it; friction never does.
That distinction is why potential energy can be defined for gravity and
not for friction, and it is the reason a real roller coaster does not
return to its starting height.
