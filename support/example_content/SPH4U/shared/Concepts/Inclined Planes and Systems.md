Grade 11 met these; Grade 12 asks for the general case, with friction, at
any angle, and with objects connected in ways that share an acceleration.

## On an incline

With axes along and perpendicular to the slope:

$$F_{g,\parallel} = mg\sin\theta \qquad F_N = mg\cos\theta$$

so an object slides when $mg\sin\theta > \mu_s mg\cos\theta$, which
reduces to $\tan\theta > \mu_s$ — mass cancels, which surprises people
every year.

## Connected objects

Objects joined by a rope share the magnitude of their acceleration. Two
routes:

- **System first:** external forces over total mass gives $a$.
- **Object by object:** one $F_{net} = ma$ per object, solved together —
  slower, but it yields the tension.

## Where the marks are

In stating the direction you called positive for EACH object. A mass
descending on one side while another climbs an incline has two natural
"positives", and the sign convention that connects them is the step most
worth writing down.
