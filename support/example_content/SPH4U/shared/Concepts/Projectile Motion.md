A projectile is anything moving under gravity alone. Its path is a
parabola, and the reason is one sentence: **the horizontal and vertical
motions are independent.**

## The two motions

| Horizontal | Vertical |
| --- | --- |
| No force, so no acceleration | $a = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ |
| $v_x$ constant | $v_y$ changes uniformly |
| $\Delta x = v_x \Delta t$ | The Grade 11 equations, unchanged |

The only quantity the two share is **time**. That is the hinge of every
projectile problem: solve one direction for $\Delta t$, carry it to the
other.

## The famous demonstration

A ball dropped and a ball fired horizontally at the same instant hit the
floor together. The one moving sideways does not "fall slower" — its
vertical motion never knew about the horizontal one.

## Range

For a projectile landing at its launch height, range is greatest at
$45^\circ$, and complementary angles give the same range. Both facts fall
out of the equations; neither is worth memorising without deriving once.
[[Projectile Range]] tests them with a launcher.
