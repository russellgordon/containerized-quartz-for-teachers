Momentum is conserved in every collision. Kinetic energy is not — and that
difference is the whole classification.

| Type | Momentum | Kinetic energy | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elastic | Conserved | Conserved | Billiard balls, gas molecules |
| Inelastic | Conserved | Some lost | Most real collisions |
| Perfectly inelastic | Conserved | Most lost | Objects that stick together |

## Where the energy goes

Into deformation, heat, and sound. A car crash converts an enormous
fraction of the kinetic energy into bending metal — which is the point of
crumple zones, and the reason a "perfectly elastic" car would be lethal.

## Two equations, two unknowns

An elastic collision gives you both conservation laws, which is exactly
enough to solve for two final velocities. An inelastic one gives you
momentum only, so you need another piece of information — usually that the
objects move off together.

## The check worth doing

Compute the kinetic energy before and after. If it went UP, you have made
an arithmetic error or there was a spring or explosive involved. If it
went down, say by how much and where it went.
