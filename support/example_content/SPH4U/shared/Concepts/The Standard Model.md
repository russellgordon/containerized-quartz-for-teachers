Everything ordinary is built from a small number of particles, and the
list is short enough to put on a page.

## Matter particles

| Family | Examples | Feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quarks | up, down (and four heavier) | All four forces |
| Leptons | electron, muon, tau, and three neutrinos | Not the strong force |

Protons and neutrons are not fundamental: each is three quarks. The
electron, so far as anyone can measure, is.

## Force carriers

| Force | Carrier | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electromagnetic | Photon | Infinite |
| Strong | Gluon | $\sim10^{-15}\ \text{m}$ |
| Weak | W and Z bosons | $\sim10^{-18}\ \text{m}$ |
| Gravity | (not in the model) | Infinite |

That last row is the honest embarrassment of modern physics: the most
familiar force is the one the standard model does not contain.

## The Higgs

Confirmed at CERN in 2012, and the reason particles have mass at all. It
was predicted in 1964 — a forty-eight year gap between the idea and the
evidence, which is worth remembering whenever someone says science moves
fast.
