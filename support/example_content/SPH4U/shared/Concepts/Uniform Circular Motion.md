Moving in a circle at constant speed is accelerated motion, because
velocity is a vector and its DIRECTION is changing constantly.

## The acceleration points to the centre

$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$$

Always toward the centre, always perpendicular to the velocity. Which is
why the speed does not change: a force perpendicular to motion changes
direction and nothing else.

## In terms of period

Since one revolution covers $2\pi r$ in a time $T$:

$$v = \frac{2\pi r}{T} \qquad \text{so} \qquad a_c = \frac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2}$$

Which form to use is a question about what you measured. A stopwatch gives
you $T$; a photogate gives you $v$. [[The Flying Pig]] uses both, and
compares.

## Deriving it

The derivation from similar triangles — the velocity triangle is similar
to the position triangle — is worth doing once, in class, because it is
where the $v^2$ comes from and why the result contains no mass.
