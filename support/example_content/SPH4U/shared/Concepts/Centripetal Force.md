$$F_c = \frac{mv^2}{r}$$

Read this carefully, because the sentence most students say about it is
wrong: **centripetal force is not a new force.** It is a job description.
Whatever real force happens to point at the centre IS the centripetal
force.

| Situation | The centripetal force is | Exerted by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ball on a string | Tension | The string |
| Car on a curve | Friction | The road |
| Satellite in orbit | Gravity | The Earth |
| Conical pendulum | The horizontal component of tension | The string |
| Electron in a magnetic field | The magnetic force | The field |

## The force that is not there

Nothing pushes outward. The feeling of being thrown outward in a turning
car is your body continuing straight while the car turns into you — see
[[Inertial and Non-inertial Frames]]. Draw an outward arrow on a free-body
diagram and the physics stops working.

## Vertical circles

At the top of a loop, gravity and the track both point down, and their sum
must equal $mv^2/r$. The minimum speed is where the track contributes
nothing:

$$v_{top}^2 = gr$$

Which is exactly the condition a Grade 11 roller coaster had to satisfy
without being able to prove it — see
[[The Roller Coaster Loop, Properly]].
