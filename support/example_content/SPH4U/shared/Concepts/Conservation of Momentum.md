In an isolated system — no external forces — the total momentum before an
interaction equals the total after:

$$\sum \vec{p}_{before} = \sum \vec{p}_{after}$$

## Why it is true

Newton's third law. The forces the two objects exert on each other are
equal and opposite and act for the same time, so the impulses are equal
and opposite, so the momentum changes cancel exactly.

That is a two-line proof of a law that holds from billiard balls to
galaxies, and it is worth being impressed by.

## "Isolated" is doing work in that sentence

Gravity and friction are external. A collision on a bench is treated as
isolated because the collision lasts milliseconds and friction has no time
to matter — not because friction is absent. Stating that assumption is
part of a good answer.

## Explosions run it backwards

A rifle recoils, a rocket accelerates, a skater pushes off a wall: total
momentum starts at zero and stays there, so the two pieces carry equal and
opposite momentum. That is also how rockets work in vacuum, where there is
nothing to push against.
