$$\vec{p} = m\vec{v} \qquad \vec{J} = \vec{F}\Delta t = \Delta\vec{p}$$

Momentum is mass times velocity — a vector, which is the whole reason it
does work that energy cannot.

## Impulse is force spread over time

The same change in momentum can come from a large force briefly or a small
force for longer. That is not a curiosity; it is the design principle
behind every safety device you have ever used.

| Device | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Airbag | Extends $\Delta t$, so $F$ falls |
| Crumple zone | Same, over the whole front of the car |
| Helmet foam | Same, in 30 mm |
| Catching a ball with soft hands | Same, done by instinct |

## Reading a force–time graph

Impulse is the AREA under it. A real collision's force spikes and falls in
milliseconds; the area under that spike is what the seat belt has to be
designed for.

## Why momentum and not just energy

Because kinetic energy is not conserved in a collision unless it is
elastic, but momentum is conserved in every collision, always, provided no
external force acts. That makes it the more reliable tool — see
[[Conservation of Momentum]].
