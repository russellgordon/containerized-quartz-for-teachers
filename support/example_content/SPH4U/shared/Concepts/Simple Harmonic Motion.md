When the restoring force is proportional to the displacement — Hooke's law
— the motion that results has a particular character: **simple harmonic
motion**.

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$$

## What SHM looks like

Position, velocity, and acceleration are all sinusoidal, and out of step
with one another: velocity is greatest where displacement is zero,
acceleration is greatest where displacement is greatest, and they point in
opposite directions.

## The surprising part

The period does not depend on the amplitude. Pull the mass twice as far
and it still takes the same time to return. Galileo noticed this for
pendulums by timing a swinging lamp against his pulse, and it is why
pendulum clocks were possible at all.

## Energy in SHM

Energy sloshes between elastic and kinetic, twice per cycle, with the
total constant if damping is small:

$$E_{total} = \tfrac{1}{2}kA^2$$

Damping — friction, air resistance — drains it, which is what makes a real
oscillation die out and why car suspensions are built to damp on purpose.
