Two everyday places the same interference physics shows up.

## Gratings

Thousands of slits instead of two. Each contributes, so the bright maxima
become very sharp and very bright, and different wavelengths separate
cleanly:

$$n\lambda = d\sin\theta$$

That sharpness is why a grating, not a prism, is the instrument of choice
for measuring spectra — including the emission spectra that Unit 5 needs.

## Thin films

Light reflects from both the top and the bottom of a thin layer — oil on
water, a soap bubble, the coating on a camera lens. The two reflections
interfere, and which colours cancel depends on the thickness, which is why
the colours swirl as the film thins.

Anti-reflective coatings are this, engineered: a layer a quarter of a
wavelength thick makes the two reflections cancel, so more light gets
through the lens instead of bouncing back.

## The scale that makes it work

Both effects need path differences comparable to a wavelength — hundreds
of nanometres. That is why a soap film shows colour and a window pane does
not.
