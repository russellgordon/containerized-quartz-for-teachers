In one dimension a vector is a number with a sign. In two it is a number
with an angle, and almost every technique in this course exists to avoid
doing trigonometry twice.

## Resolve, then add

Break every vector into components along axes you chose, add the
components separately, then rebuild. The method is on
[[Working with Vectors]]; the reason it works is that perpendicular
directions do not talk to each other — what happens along $x$ is
unaffected by what happens along $y$.

That independence is not obvious and is worth doubting until you see it in
[[Projectile Motion]], where it does the whole job.

## Choosing axes is a strategy, not a formality

Tilt them along an incline and gravity splits instead of the motion.
Point one at the centre of a circle and the net force has only one
component. The problem does not change; the arithmetic collapses.
