$$F_g = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$$

Every mass attracts every other mass. The force is proportional to both
masses and falls off as the square of the distance between their centres.

## The inverse square, geometrically

The influence spreads over a sphere, and a sphere's area grows as $r^2$,
so the intensity falls as $1/r^2$. The same geometry governs light, sound,
and the electric field — which is why they share the form.

## What was universal about it

Before Newton, the fall of an apple and the orbit of the Moon were
different subjects. The claim that ONE equation governs both — that the
Moon is falling, and simply keeps missing — is among the largest
unifications in the history of science.

## Field strength

Dividing by the test mass gives the field:

$$g = \frac{Gm}{r^2}$$

At Earth's surface that evaluates to $9.8\ \text{N/kg}$ — the same
number, and the same thing, as the $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ from Grade 11.
