$$E = mc^2$$

Mass and energy are the same thing in different units, and $c^2$ is the
exchange rate. Because $c^2$ is about $9 \times 10^{16}$, a very small
mass corresponds to an enormous energy.

## Where it is visible

- **Nuclear fission and fusion.** The products weigh less than the
  reactants; the difference is the energy released.
- **Particle accelerators.** Kinetic energy becomes new particles in a
  collision — mass created from motion, routinely.
- **The Sun.** About four million tonnes of mass per second becomes the
  sunlight falling on your face.

## What it does NOT say

It does not say mass can be "converted into energy" as if they were
different substances that trade places. It says a system's mass IS a
measure of its energy content. Heat a brick and it becomes very slightly
more massive.

## Total energy of a moving object

$$E = \frac{mc^2}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$$

which grows without limit as $v \to c$ — the reason nothing with mass can
reach the speed of light. It is not that we lack a big enough engine; the
energy required diverges.
