The Large Hadron Collider cost roughly nine billion dollars and confirmed
the Higgs boson. The proposed successor would cost more.

## The physics you need first

Colliders turn kinetic energy into mass, at the rate $E = mc^2$. Finding
a heavier particle requires more energy, and energy costs money — the
relationship between what you want to see and what it costs is not
negotiable, it is physics.

## The question

Is the next collider worth building? Argue it as a spending decision
against alternatives — climate research, public health, other physics —
rather than as a matter of taste.

## Positions worth taking seriously

- Fundamental research has an unreliable but enormous long-run payoff
  (the web came out of CERN, incidentally rather than by design).
- The same money buys more knowledge elsewhere.
- International megaprojects have value as international projects,
  separate from their physics.

Everyone must state one strong argument for the side they do not hold.
