Power lines, phone masts, wireless routers, MRI scanners. All of them put
fields where people are. Which of those should we worry about, and on what
evidence?

## The physics you need first

Photon energy is $E = hf$. Ionizing radiation — the kind that breaks
chemical bonds — starts in the ultraviolet, around $10\ \text{eV}$. A
phone's photons carry about $10^{-5}\ \text{eV}$: a million times too
little, no matter how many arrive.

That does not make every question closed. Heating is a real effect at
sufficient power, and "no known mechanism" is a weaker claim than "no
effect".

## The question

Where should the burden of proof sit for a technology whose fields are
non-ionizing but ubiquitous? What evidence would change your mind, in
either direction?

## Rules

Any claim about health effects needs a source with a study design behind
it. "I read that…" is where this discussion starts, not where it ends.
