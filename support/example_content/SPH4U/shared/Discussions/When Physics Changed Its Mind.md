Twice in twenty-five years, physics gave up something it was certain of:
absolute time, and the continuity of energy. Both times the evidence came
first and the discomfort lasted a decade.

## The question

What does it take to overturn a well-established theory — and how would
you tell a genuine revolution from a fashionable one at the time it was
happening?

## Cases to bring

- Michelson and Morley found no ether wind. Physics spent eighteen years
  trying to save the ether before accepting the alternative.
- Planck introduced quantisation as a mathematical trick he disliked.
- Continental drift was ridiculed for fifty years, then confirmed.
- Cold fusion was announced, celebrated, and could not be reproduced.

## The uncomfortable part

The last case looks exactly like the others at the moment of announcement.
What distinguishes them is what happened next — which means the honest
answer to "how would you know at the time?" may be that you would not.
