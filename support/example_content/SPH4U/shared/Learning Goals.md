By the end of this course you will be able to take a situation nobody has
worked out for you — a satellite, a collision, a beam of light, a particle
at nearly the speed of light — and decide which physics applies.

In plain words, you will be able to:

- work with forces and motion in two dimensions, including the circular
  motion that keeps satellites up and cars on curves;
- use conservation of energy AND conservation of momentum, and know which
  one a situation calls for;
- describe gravitational, electric, and magnetic fields with the same
  language, and see why that is a discovery rather than a convenience;
- explain the behaviour of light with a wave model, and say what evidence
  forces that model;
- state what special relativity and quantum mechanics claim, and what
  experiment made each unavoidable.
