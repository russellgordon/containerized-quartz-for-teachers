Grade 12 physics is the same subject as Grade 11 with the training wheels
off. The equations you brought with you are still true; they now have to
work in two dimensions, in fields you cannot see, and at speeds where
common sense stops being a guide.

## The shape of a class

1. **A situation, not a question.** Usually one where your Grade 11
   instinct gives the wrong answer.
2. **Work it in groups, at the boards.** Being wrong in public is the
   fastest route in this course, and everyone here is going to be wrong
   about circular motion at least once.
3. **Name it.** The formal treatment arrives after you have argued.
4. **Practise, then extend.** See [[Exercises/index|Exercises]].

## What this course assumes

That you can draw a free-body diagram, use $F_{net} = ma$, and follow
energy through a system. If any of those feel shaky, say so in the first
week — they are the floor everything else stands on, and they are quick to
rebuild.

## The lab

More lab time than Grade 11, and more of it designed by you: several
investigations give you a question and a bench, not a procedure. Read
[[Safety in the Lab]] before the first one.

## Getting unstuck

"I expected X and got Y" still works. In this course you should add "and
here is the diagram I drew". Half of Grade 12 physics errors are visible
in the diagram before they reach the algebra. See [[Getting Help]], and
come to [[Help Sessions]] — the times are on that page.

Every equation the course uses is collected on [[Formula Reference]], which
you may bring to every test.
