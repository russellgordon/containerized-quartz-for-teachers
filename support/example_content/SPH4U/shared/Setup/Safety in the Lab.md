The hazards in Grade 12 physics are quieter than in chemistry and easier
to underestimate: a laser you cannot look at, a spring under tension, a
mass on a string moving at head height.

## Before every investigation

- [ ] Read the whole procedure first, including the analysis.
- [ ] Know where the power cut-off and the first-aid kit are.
- [ ] Bags under the bench; nothing in the aisle.
- [ ] Eye protection wherever anything can launch, snap, or shatter.

## The three that matter most here

| Hazard | Where it shows up | The rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laser light | Double-slit and diffraction work | Never at eye level, never at a person, never at a reflective surface |
| Objects on a string | Circular motion, pendulums | Clear a full circle plus a metre; everyone stands outside it |
| Stored elastic energy | Springs, launchers | Never aim along a bench; release under control |

> [!danger] Lasers
> Even a classroom laser pointer can damage a retina faster than you can
> blink. It stays below shoulder height, it points at a screen or a wall,
> and it is off the moment measurement stops. There is no version of this
> rule that bends.

What to have with you on a lab day is on [[What to Bring]].

## Designing your own procedure

Several investigations here ask you to design the method. A procedure you
wrote is checked by me BEFORE anything is switched on — that is the price
of the freedom, and it is not negotiable.
