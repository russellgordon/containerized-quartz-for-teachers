What I check for at the end of the semester, so you can check for it
first.

## Contents

- [ ] [[Your First Entry]] present, from the first week
- [ ] An entry for each investigation you ran — there are eight of them
- [ ] Every one of those entries contains a prediction recorded
      **before** the measurement, with the model it came from named
- [ ] Raw readings written down, not only the calculated answers
- [ ] The temperature recorded anywhere a constant from the data booklet
      was used
- [ ] At least one entry where a model did not describe what you
      measured, and you worked out which of you was at fault
- [ ] At least one entry where a number came out wrong and you chased it
- [ ] Notes from at least two discussions, saying what moved
- [ ] A [[Showing Growth]] comparison from mid-semester
- [ ] [[Final Reflection]] completed
- [ ] Dates on everything, in order, readable by a stranger

## How it is assessed

| Criterion | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Selection | A range, not only the entries where you looked good |
| Evidence | Numbers with units and the right significant figures — not adjectives |
| Quantitative honesty | Differences compared against your uncertainty as a matter of routine |
| Model awareness | The model named, and its boundary stated in your own words |
| Reasoning | Mechanism attempted, not just the pattern described |
| Limitations | Named by you, sized, and with the direction they pushed |
| Growth | Change shown with two pieces side by side, not asserted |
| Care | Organised well enough that a reader can follow it cold |

## The two rows that separate this course from last year

**Model awareness.** Grade 11 marked you on whether your prediction had
a mechanism attached. Grade 12 marks you on whether you know what the
mechanism assumes. Collision theory assumes the particles are free to
meet; VSEPR assumes electron pairs arrange themselves to be as far apart
as possible and says nothing about why bonds have the lengths they do; a
table of standard potentials assumes 1 mol/L and 25 °C. Writing "I used
Le Châtelier's principle here, and it predicts a direction rather than a
final amount" takes one clause and is the difference between describing
a result and understanding it.

**Quantitative honesty**, which has moved on. Last year the required
sentence was *unchanged to within the balance's resolution of 0.01 g*.
This year the same instinct has to survive contact with logarithms and
powers. A pH quoted as 4.362 from a meter that reads two decimal places
is an overclaim; a $K_c$ built from concentrations known to two
significant figures is not a four-figure number however many digits the
calculator offers; and a difference of 0.3 in pH is a factor of two in
concentration, which is a much larger claim than the small-looking
number suggests. See [[Working with Logarithms in Chemistry]] and
[[Significant Figures and Units]].

> [!tip] The five-minute audit
> Open three entries at random.
>
> If none of them contains a number with a unit, that is your gap. If
> none contains a sentence beginning "I was wrong about", that is your
> second. If none names a model, that is your third — and it is the one
> that most often separates a level 3 journal from a level 4 one here.
>
> Then check the digits. If every number in all three entries is quoted
> to the same number of decimal places regardless of what measured it,
> go and read [[Significant Figures and Units]] before you hand it in.
