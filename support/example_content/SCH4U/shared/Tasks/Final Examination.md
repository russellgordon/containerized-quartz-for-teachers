> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · three hours, in the examination period · written, with a
> data booklet and a calculator · the whole course, weighted toward the
> reasoning rather than the recall

## What it is for

Everything else this semester was done with a partner, a bench, and time
to check. This is the one piece of evidence that is unambiguously yours,
produced under the same conditions as everybody else's. In a Grade 12
course it does one more thing: it is the closest experience you will
have to a first-year university assessment, and that is worth meeting
here, where the stakes are lower and I am the one marking it.

## What is on it

| Part | Roughly | What it asks you to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Structure and organic chemistry | 20% | Electron configurations, shapes, polarity, intermolecular forces; naming and comparing classes of organic compounds |
| B. Energy and rates | 25% | Calorimetry, Hess's law, enthalpy of reaction; rate laws, orders, and collision theory |
| C. Equilibrium and acids | 25% | Equilibrium expressions and calculations, Le Châtelier, pH, buffers, titration curves |
| D. Electrochemistry | 20% | Oxidation numbers, half equations, cell potentials, galvanic against electrolytic |
| E. Reading an investigation | 10% | Data you have not seen: process it, state the claim, state its uncertainty |

Every part asks you to *explain*, not only to calculate. A correct
number with no reasoning earns part marks; correct reasoning with an
arithmetic slip earns most of them.

## What to expect, precisely

- **A data booklet is provided**: periodic table, reduction potentials,
  common constants, and solubility data. What you memorise is how to use
  them — [[Reading a Reduction Potential Table]] and
  [[Reading an Equilibrium Table]] are the two skills most often lost
  under time pressure.
- **Logarithms appear** in the acid and equilibrium questions.
  [[Working with Logarithms in Chemistry]] is the page to revisit, and
  the arithmetic is expected to be done properly rather than estimated.
- **Explanations are marked on mechanism**, not vocabulary. "The
  equilibrium shifts right" earns little; saying which rate changed and
  why earns full marks.
- **Significant figures and units** are marked throughout, per
  [[Significant Figures and Units]].

## How to prepare

1. **Rework problems, do not reread them.** Five from each unit's
   practice set, cold, with the data booklet only.
2. **Rebuild the connections.** Enthalpy, rate, and equilibrium are
   three views of the same reaction; redox and cells are bookkeeping
   plus a table. Draw those relationships from memory — the gaps are
   your study list.
3. **Re-read your own investigations.** Your
   [[Chemistry Journal]] entries about what went wrong at the bench are
   the most efficient revision in the course.
4. **Practise the explanations out loud.** If you cannot say why a
   catalyst changes a rate without using the word "faster", you do not
   have it yet.
5. **Bring questions to the review classes.** This page is what is on
   the examination.

> [!tip] In the three hours
> Budget by the weightings above and hold to them. If a calculation
> stalls, write the route you intended in words and move on — a
> described method earns marks, and a blank space earns none.

## How this is assessed

Against the same expectations as everything else. Per [[How Marks Work]],
this examination is part of the final 30% of the course mark alongside
[[The Chemistry Showcase]], so that neither one afternoon nor one
investigation decides your grade alone.
