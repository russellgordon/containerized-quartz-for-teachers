## Three things worth knowing

1. **Links go both ways.** At the bottom of every page is a
   **Backlinks** list showing which pages link *to* it. Land on
   [[Dynamic Equilibrium]] and you can see every class, investigation,
   and exercise set that used it.
2. **Hover to peek.** Hovering over a link previews the page in a small
   window without leaving the one you are on — useful for checking one
   definition in the middle of a calculation.
3. **Search is fast.** Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> — or
   <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> — and start typing. It searches the text
   of every page, not just the titles, so searching for a formula, a
   constant, or a phrase you half remember will find the page that
   discusses it.

## What changed since Grade 11

The **Reference** folder is not a set of tables any more. This course
runs on a data booklet you are issued, and its numbers cannot usefully
be duplicated on a website — so the Reference pages here teach you how
to *read* those tables instead: what conditions a value assumes, what
its sign convention is, what it is silent about. Start with
[[Reading an Equilibrium Table]] and
[[Reading a Reduction Potential Table]], and expect to open them beside
the booklet rather than instead of it.

**Tutorials** has gained a page that Grade 11 did not need at all.
[[Working with Logarithms in Chemistry]] exists because Unit 4 turns
concentration into pH and back, and a surprising amount of trouble in
that unit is arithmetic wearing a chemistry costume.

## The order pages get written in

Concept pages assume you have already met the idea. That is deliberate:
you do the investigation first, we compare what different groups
actually measured, and only then does the idea get its name and its
clean statement. So a concept page will often open by referring to the
lab you have just finished. If you are reading ahead and it feels like
it starts in the middle, that is why.

> [!tip] The habit that gets you unstuck fastest
> Lost on a class page? Follow its link to the concept. Lost on a
> concept page? Scroll to its backlinks and find the class where we did
> it, then read what we actually did that day. Between them, those two
> directions answer most "when did we cover this" questions in under a
> minute.
