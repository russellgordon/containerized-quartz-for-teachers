One page per class, newest at the top of the list: the agenda we followed
and anything to do before next time. Away? Start here, run the warm-up
yourself, and get to a bench before you read anything about it — the page
holds the plan, but in this shop the hardware comes first and the ideas
earn their names after.

What has changed since Grade 10 is the standard. Last year the question
was whether it worked. This year it is at what current, within what
tolerance, and how you know — so almost every agenda below runs in the
same order: measure, discuss, name it, practise.

Our semester runs in four units:

- **Unit 1 — Circuits You Can Trust**: voltage, current, resistance,
  schematics, soldering, and heat, ending in [[The Working Circuit]].
- **Unit 2 — Digital Logic**: binary and hex, gates at the bench, Boolean
  simplification, and memory, ending in [[The Logic Machine]].
- **Unit 3 — Microcontrollers and Embedded Code**: pins, sensors, motors,
  and code somebody else can read, ending in [[The Embedded Device]].
- **Unit 4 — Networks, Systems, and the Profession**: addressing, cabling,
  standards, and careers, ending in [[The Engineering Project]] and the
  demonstrations at [[The Engineering Showcase]].
