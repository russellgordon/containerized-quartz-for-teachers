This site is the course's memory — every class, concept, lab, practice
set, and datasheet habit lives here. Five habits make it fast to use,
and fast matters when you are looking something up with one hand while
a circuit waits on the bench.

## The five habits

1. **Land on today.** The home page always surfaces the most recent
   class, so "what did we do?" and "what is due?" are one click from
   anywhere. If you were away, start there before asking anyone.
2. **Search first.** Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> on a Mac or
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> on Windows and type any word —
   "burden voltage", "tolerance", "flyback", "nibble". Search reads
   every page at once and is nearly always faster than navigating.
3. **Hover before you click.** Rest your pointer on any
   [[Ohm's Law|link like this one]] and a preview of the target page
   appears. You can confirm a formula or a limit without leaving the
   lab instructions you are halfway through.
4. **Read the backlinks.** At the bottom of every page, *Backlinks*
   lists every page that links **to** this one. On a concept page,
   that is an instant map of every lab and practice set where the idea
   does real work — the site's web, walked backwards.
5. **Predict before you unfold.** Practice pages fold their worked
   answers away behind a callout. The fold is a tool, not a lock:
   commit to your own number first, then open it and compare. Opening
   first feels efficient and teaches nothing, which is the same
   mistake as switching on the supply before writing down a
   prediction.
