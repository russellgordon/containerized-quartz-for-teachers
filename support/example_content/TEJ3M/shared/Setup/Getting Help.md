Stuck is a normal working condition in this trade, and half of this
course is learning to be stuck *well*. The order to work through:

1. **Measure before you guess.** Grade 10 said look, listen, and read.
   Grade 11 adds the meter, and it goes first: is there voltage where
   there should be voltage, and is ground actually connected? Most
   dead circuits are answered by those two measurements before any
   theorising begins. [[Getting Unstuck]] has the full method.
2. **Check the site.** The class page says what we did; the
   [[Concepts/index|concept pages]] state every idea precisely; every
   practice set in [[Exercises/index|Exercises]] has worked answers
   folded below; and every tutorial in [[Tutorials/index|Tutorials]]
   covers a bench skill properly rather than in passing.
3. **Read the datasheet.** If the question is "what is this part
   supposed to do", the manufacturer has already answered it in
   writing. [[Reading a Datasheet]] is how to find the answer in under
   two minutes.
4. **Ask your bench.** Explaining a fault out loud fixes a startling
   proportion of it before anyone replies. Remember the norm: helpers
   ask questions, they do not grab the probes.
5. **Ask me in class.** Bench time is what it is for. Anything
   involving powering something up for the first time, an unfamiliar
   instrument, heat, or a smell — ask **first**, not after.
6. **Come to a help session.** Times are on [[Help Sessions]]. Bring
   the specific broken thing. "My circuit does not work" is hard to
   help with; "this node reads 0.2 V and I expected 3.3 V" is easy.

> [!tip] Name where it broke, with a number
> "No output, supply reads 5.02 V at the rail, pin 7 reads 0 V, pin 14
> reads 4.98 V" is a symptom report a working technician would be
> proud of — and it is already most of the way to the fix. Half the
> value of asking well is that you often answer yourself in the
> middle of the sentence.

One more thing, and it matters more than any of the above. If the
trouble is bigger than the course — time, workload, something at home,
or anything happening online that worries you — tell me straight away,
in whatever way is easiest for you. And any safety incident, however
small it feels, gets reported the moment it happens: see
[[Safety in the Lab]]. Reporting is information, never trouble.
