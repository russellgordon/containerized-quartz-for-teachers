By the end of this course you will be able to look at a circuit and say
what it is doing and why, put a number on it before you measure, and
build something that keeps working after you walk away. Grade 10 asked
whether it worked. This year asks at what current, within what
tolerance, and how you know. In the curriculum's own words, three of
the goals are:

Put plainly, week to week that means:

- **Predict, then measure** — a number on the whiteboard before the
  meter is switched on, every time. [[Ohm's Law]] is where that habit
  starts and [[Predict the Circuit]] is where it gets drilled.
- **Build to a standard** — soldered joints that survive handling,
  wiring somebody else can trace, and documentation that lets a
  stranger rebuild your work; see [[Documenting Your Build]].
- **Make hardware obey code** — a microcontroller reading real sensors
  and driving real loads, safely, in [[The Embedded Device]].
- **Work like a professional** — safety as practice rather than
  compliance ([[Safety in the Lab]]), datasheets as primary sources
  ([[Reading a Datasheet]]), and honest accounting for what hardware
  costs the world ([[Who Pays for the Hardware]]).
