Grade 10 practice asked you to identify things. This year's asks you to
produce a number, carry its units through the working, and say whether
the answer could be real. Every worked answer below shows the units
travelling with the arithmetic, because that is where mistakes announce
themselves — if volts divided by ohms does not come out in amperes, the
working is wrong even when the button-pushing was right.

Each set follows a concept page and a bench activity. Build the idea
there first; these questions are for making it dependable.

A calculator is fine and expected. What is not fine is an answer with no
unit on it, or one you would not defend at the bench — a current of forty
amps through a breadboard is arithmetic that has stopped describing
anything real.

Stuck partway through a set? Good — that is the useful part. Start with
[[Getting Unstuck]], and remember that in this shop being stuck is not an
emergency. It is the job description.
