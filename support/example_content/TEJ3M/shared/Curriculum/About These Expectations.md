The expectations in this folder are reproduced from **The Ontario
Curriculum, Grades 11 and 12: Technological Education** (revised 2009),
the Grade 11 university/college preparation course **TEJ3M — Computer
Engineering Technology**, published by the Ontario Ministry of
Education.

> [!success] These codes are the Ministry's own
> Unlike some older curricula, this document **prints its codes**. The
> strand letters, the overall expectations `A1`, `A2`, …, and the
> specific expectations `A1.1`, `B4.3`, … all appear exactly this way
> in the Ministry's pages. When you cite `B4.3` on an assignment or in
> a course outline, you are using the Ministry's own address for that
> expectation, not a local convention.

Two notes about this course's status, because they are easy to get
wrong:

- **This curriculum is current.** The Ministry replaced the Grades 9
  and 10 technological education curriculum effective 2024–25, which is
  why the Grade 10 course this one follows from has moved on. Grades 11
  and 12 technological education courses, including TEJ3M, continue to
  be based on the 2009 document reproduced here.
- **The 2009 document prints no teacher prompts.** Where a mathematics
  curriculum offers "Sample problem:" text beneath an expectation, this
  one does not; what you see on each page is the whole of what the
  Ministry prints.

Each page keeps its code, and every lab, concept, and task in this
course links back to the codes it addresses. The codes are also tags,
so every page tagged `#B3` can be gathered in one place.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing
this?", the answer is a link. When a department writes a course
outline, or a teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes
are what make one person's planning legible to another.
