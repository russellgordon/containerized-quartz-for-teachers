Electronics looks like the neutral half of technology — no opinions,
just current and copper. But every component on your bench was mined,
refined, manufactured, shipped, priced, certified, and will one day be
thrown away, and every one of those steps was a decision somebody
made and profited from. This year you also start making decisions of
your own that other people have to live with: what current to run a
part at, what to test before you call something finished, what to
write on the label.

Two rules. The first comes straight from [[Our Classroom Norms]]: we
argue with ideas, never at people, and coming in ready to change your
mind is a strength, not a lack of one. The second is new for Grade 11:
a claim about a quantity needs a source. "Batteries are terrible for
the environment" is a mood. "Producing a lithium-ion cell costs this
much energy per kilowatt-hour of capacity, according to this study" is
an argument somebody can actually engage with — or refute, which is
better still.
