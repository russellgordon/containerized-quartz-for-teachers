Five minutes at the start of class, every class. If you were in the
Grade 10 shop, most of these names are familiar — same routines, same
room, same five minutes. What has changed is the standard. Last year a
good answer described what would happen. This year a good answer
carries a number, a unit, and a reason you believe it.

| Warm-up | What it trains |
| --- | --- |
| [[Name That Part]] | Naming parts, and reading what is printed on them |
| [[Predict the Circuit]] | Committing to a value before the meter is on |
| [[Read the Schematic]] | Following a drawing the way current follows wire |
| [[Binary Bites]] | Binary, decimal, and hex, with the method defended |
| [[Spot the Hazard]] | Seeing an unsafe bench before anyone energises it |
| [[Which One Doesn't Belong]] | Component vocabulary, argued out loud |
| [[Tech Headlines]] | Weighing claims made about technology |

None of these needs a powered bench, and none of them is filler. Each
one keeps warm a habit the shop will later demand while you are busy,
tired, and holding a hot iron: precise names, quantitative guesses,
reasoning you can defend, and an eye that catches trouble before it
gets expensive.

> [!tip] Never a race
> The first hand up is not the win condition here and never will be.
> A routine that has become boring has done its job — that is what
> fluency feels like from the inside. When you get there, spend the
> spare attention on the bench beside you.
