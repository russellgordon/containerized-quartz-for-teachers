> [!abstract] At a glance
> Benches of two · six bench periods across Unit 2 · one working
> system, one client handover package · assessed on all four categories

## What you are making

A small business hires your bench. They need a workstation built to a
brief, a peripheral installed, a home-office network that reaches the
internet safely, and documentation good enough that the next
technician — who will not be you — can take over.

This is the task where everything from Unit 2 arrives at once, and it
is deliberately more like a job than like a school assignment: the
client changes their mind once, partway through, and you handle it.

## What you deliver

1. **The machine**, built to the brief in [[Build a Machine to Spec]],
   posting, updated, and benchmarked against each requirement.
2. **The operating system**, chosen with a written reason from
   [[Operating Systems]], installed, patched, and configured with
   separate administrator and daily-use accounts.
3. **A peripheral**, installed, driver-configured, and demonstrated
   working.
4. **The home-office setup**: the workstation, the peripheral, and the
   client's existing laptop sharing what they need to share and nothing
   else.
5. **The network drawing**, to the standard in
   [[Network Types and Topologies]] — every device, address, port, and
   run, drawn as built rather than as planned.
6. **The security section**, per [[Securing a Network]]: wireless
   protection, firewall rules and why, accounts, what is encrypted, and
   where the backups go.
7. **A maintenance plan**: what to run, how often, and how to restore —
   with one restore already tested and timed.
8. **The client paperwork**: a plain-language acceptable-use note for
   the household or business, and a licence record for every piece of
   software installed. [[Licensing, Policy, and Privacy]] is the
   reference.

## The handover

Another bench receives your package and, without speaking to you,
answers three service questions from it: what is the administrator
account, where does the backup go and how is it restored, and which
port on the switch serves the printer. If they cannot, the job is not
finished — however well the system runs.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Parts, settings, and services chosen correctly for the stated requirements |
| Thinking | Faults diagnosed in order and documented; the mid-project change absorbed without a rebuild |
| Application | It works, and each requirement has a measurement rather than an assurance |
| Communication | A handover package a stranger can act on, in the client's language rather than yours |

> [!tip] Where marks are usually lost
> Not on the build. On the documentation written at the end from
> memory, which is always thinner than the notes you would have had if
> you had written as you went. [[Documenting Your Build]] exists for
> exactly this, and the benches that use it finish earlier.
