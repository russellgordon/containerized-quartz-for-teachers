Everything on this site is a page, and every page lives in one of a
handful of folders. Once you know what each folder is *for*, you can
always guess where something lives — and guessing correctly is much
faster than browsing.

| Folder | What lives there | When you go there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All Classes | One page per class: agenda and to-dos | You were away, or you forget what is due |
| Concepts | The ideas, stated cleanly and precisely | After the bench, when you want it exact |
| Labs | The hands-on builds, step by step | Before, during, and after bench time |
| Warm-Ups | Our daily five-minute routines | You want the rules, or extra reps |
| Exercises | Practice sets, worked answers folded below | You want to find out whether it stuck |
| Code | MicroPython for the embedded work | Programming days, and the curious |
| Tasks | The projects, with their success criteria | Before, during, and after any task |
| Discussions | The arguments we keep returning to | Preparing to talk, or thinking afterwards |
| Portfolios | Your Tech Journal and its guides | Log time, and at the end of each unit |
| Tutorials | Bench skills, taught properly once | The moment a how-do-I question hits |
| Setup | Safety, routines, marks, help | Your first week, then whenever |
| Style | Pages about the site itself | You wonder why the pages look this way |

## The site is a web, not a filing cabinet

The folders are only half of the organisation. The other half is
**links**. A concept links to the labs that put it in your hands and
to the practice that tests it; a tutorial links to every lab that
depends on the skill; and the **Backlinks** panel at the bottom of any
page lists every page pointing the other way, gathered automatically
and never maintained by hand.

That means you can start anywhere and follow the thread. Land on
[[Ohm's Law]] and its backlinks show you every place in the course
where that relationship does real work. Land on
[[Using a Multimeter]] and you find every lab that assumes you can
drive one.

> [!tip] Lost? Search.
> Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd>
> and type any word from any page — a unit, a part number, a symptom.
> It is almost always faster than browsing, and it searches the whole
> site rather than the folder you happen to be standing in.

The full tour of what pages can actually *do* — typeset mathematics,
coloured code, diagrams, folded answers — is in
[[What This Site Can Do]].
