One page per text we read together. Each gives you the context you need
before you start, the questions we will argue about after, and what the
text is doing that is worth stealing for your own writing.

## Texts we all read

- [[I Lost My Talk]] — Rita Joe, 1978. A poem in four short stanzas that
  most people have to read three times.
- [[Borders]] — Thomas King, 1993. A mother, a son, and one word repeated
  at a border crossing.
- [[A Mountain Legend]] — Jordan Wheeler. Two boys, one mountain, two
  centuries apart.
- [[Sugar Falls]] — David A. Robertson and Scott B. Henderson, 2011. A
  graphic novel built from Elder Betty Ross's own account.
- [[Poems We Will Argue About]] — the short poetry unit, and why these
  five.
- [[To This Day]] — Shane Koyczan's spoken-word piece, watched and then
  read.

## The literature circles

You choose one of these and read it with a group. All four are in the
room; all four are worth your term.

- [[The Marrow Thieves]] — Cherie Dimaline
- [[Indian Horse]] — Richard Wagamese
- [[Fatty Legs]] — Christy Jordan-Fenton and Margaret Pokiak-Fenton
- [[The Breadwinner]] — Deborah Ellis

## And the one nobody assigns you

The book in your bag, for the silent reading in every second class. It
does not appear on this list because it is yours. It counts for more than
you think: reading volume is what makes hard texts stop being hard.

> [!note] Content, named in advance
> Several of these texts deal with residential schools, racism, war, and
> bullying. Each page says so at the top. The agreements in
> [[Our Agreements]] apply to all of them.
