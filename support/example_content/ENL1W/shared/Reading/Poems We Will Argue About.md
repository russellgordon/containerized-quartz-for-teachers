Five poems, read across two weeks. They are here because they are short
enough to know completely and because they disagree with each other about
what a poem is for.

## The five

| Poem | Poet | Why this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I Lost My Talk" | Rita Joe (Mi'kmaw) | Grief, and a demand, in four stanzas — see [[I Lost My Talk]] |
| "Letter to Sir John A. Macdonald" | Marilyn Dumont (Métis) | A poem that answers a country's founding story directly |
| "Suzanne" | Leonard Cohen | A song that is also a poem, and an argument about whether that matters |
| "The Bull Moose" | Alden Nowlan | A crowd, an animal, and a last line that changes everything before it |
| "To This Day" | Shane Koyczan | Written to be spoken; see [[To This Day]] |

## How we will read them

**Aloud, first, always.** A poem read silently is a poem half-read. Line
breaks are instructions for breathing.

**Then in three passes**, the method from [[Reading Like a Writer]]: once
for what happens, once with a pen, once through your own marks.

**Then out loud again**, by somebody else, so you can hear how much a
reading is an interpretation.

## What to look for in any poem

- **The line breaks.** What word is left hanging at the end of a line?
  That word is being pointed at.
- **The turn.** Most poems change direction somewhere — a *but*, a
  stanza break, a shift in tense. Find it.
- **Sound.** Repeated consonants slow a line; open vowels stretch it.
  Read it aloud and listen for where the poem speeds up.
- **What is not there.** Poems leave things out on purpose. The gaps are
  where you do the work.
- **The last line.** Poems are built backwards from them more often than
  not.

## The argument we will actually have

Is a song lyric a poem? Is a spoken-word piece a poem, or a performance
that happens to use words? Does it change if it was never written down?

Come with a position and an example, and be prepared to have it moved.
The best answer anyone has given in this room started with "it depends
what you think a poem is *for*", which is exactly the right way in.
