> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Richard Wagamese**, Ojibwe writer from Wabaseemoong Independent
> Nations (1955–2017) · *Indian Horse* (Douglas & McIntyre, 2012) ·
> novel · one of the four [[The Book Club|literature circle]] choices

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> Residential school, including abuse; racism in and around hockey;
> addiction. It is unflinching, and it is also one of the most loved
> Canadian novels of the last twenty years.

## The premise

Saul Indian Horse, in treatment as an adult, writes the story of his
life: taken to St. Jerome's residential school as a boy, finding hockey
there, and being extraordinary at it in a country that would not let that
be simple.

## What to notice

- **The frame.** Saul is writing this for a reason, at a moment, to
  somebody. Ask what the frame does to how much you trust him.
- **Hockey prose.** Wagamese writes the game as beauty and as escape.
  Notice how the sentences move when Saul is on the ice, and what happens
  to that rhythm later.
- **The land.** Wagamese writes land as relation, not as scenery — see
  [[Indigenous Storywork]].
- **What Saul does not tell us, and when he finally does.** The novel
  withholds something. Ask why a writer would do that, and what it costs
  the reader when it arrives.
- **Silence.** Who is silent, when, and what silence is being used for.

## Questions we will argue about

1. What is hockey in this novel? Freedom, escape, weapon, trap — argue
   for one and be ready to defend it against the ending.
2. Is the ending hopeful? What in the last twenty pages would you point
   at?
3. Wagamese has said he wanted to write a story about a person, not a
   symbol. Does the novel manage that?

## For your circle

Take the school chapters at whatever pace your group needs, and check in
with each other. That is not a soft instruction — it is how a reading
group handles a book like this properly.
