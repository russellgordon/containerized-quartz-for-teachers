> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Deborah Ellis**, Canadian writer · *The Breadwinner* (Groundwood
> Books, 2000) · novel · one of the four
> [[The Book Club|literature circle]] choices

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> Life under the Taliban in Kabul: a father arrested, a family without
> income, and a child taking on an adult's risk. Ellis wrote it after
> interviewing women and girls in Afghan refugee camps in Pakistan.

## The premise

Parvana is eleven. Her father is arrested, and in a city where women and
girls may not work or move about freely, her family has no way to buy
food. She cuts her hair, wears her dead brother's clothes, and goes out
into the market as a boy.

## What to notice

- **How small the stakes look, and how large they are.** Bread, a tooth,
  a window. Ellis keeps the scale domestic on purpose.
- **What Parvana notices.** Her attention is a character in itself — see
  [[Character]].
- **The other women.** Mrs Weera, Nooria, the woman at the window. Ask
  what each one shows about the same situation.
- **Where the novel refuses to explain.** Ellis rarely stops to give
  political background. Ask why, and whether it works.

## Questions we will argue about

1. Is Parvana brave, or does she simply have no alternative? Does the
   novel let you separate those?
2. Ellis is a Canadian writer telling an Afghan story, from interviews.
   What responsibilities come with that, and how can you tell from the
   book whether she took them seriously? Compare this with the way
   [[Sugar Falls]] handles the same question.
3. The ending is not a resolution. What is it, and what is that choice
   for?

## For your circle

Ellis donated royalties from this book to a fund for education for Afghan
girls. That is a fact about the book's life in the world, not about its
quality — but it is worth knowing when your group argues about question 2.
