> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Christy Jordan-Fenton** and **Margaret Pokiak-Fenton** ·
> *Fatty Legs: A True Story* (Annick Press, 2010) · memoir, with
> photographs · one of the four [[The Book Club|literature circle]]
> choices

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> Residential school, and a child's humiliation by a particular nun.
> It is a true account, told with more humour and more defiance than
> students expect.

## The premise

Olemaun — an eight-year-old Inuvialuit girl — wants to learn to read, and
argues her father into letting her go to the outsiders' school on Banks
Island. What she finds there is not what she went for. The book is her
own account, written down with her daughter-in-law.

## What to notice

- **She chose to go.** The book opens with a child insisting on school.
  Hold that against everything that follows.
- **The photographs.** Real ones, of a real person. Ask what they do to
  how you read the prose beside them.
- **Two names.** Olemaun and Margaret. Track where each is used and by
  whom — the naming is the story.
- **Humour and defiance.** The book is funny. Ask what the humour is
  doing; it is not decoration.
- **The red stockings.** Follow that object through the book. It is a
  [[Literary Devices|symbol]] that a real person actually wore.

## Questions we will argue about

1. Olemaun wins something at the end. What, exactly, and at what price?
2. What does it change that this is memoir rather than fiction? Should it
   change how we argue about it?
3. Two authors, one story. Whose book is this — and how would you know?

## For your circle

This is the shortest of the four, which means your group can read it
twice: once for what happens, once for how it is built. Groups that have
done that have run the best discussions in this room.
