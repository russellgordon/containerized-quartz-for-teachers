> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Jordan Wheeler**, Cree writer (born 1964) · short story · two boys,
> one mountain, separated by generations

## The situation

A boy on a school trip climbs a mountain he was told not to climb. As he
goes, the story of another boy — Muskawashee, climbing the same mountain
long before, toward an eagle's nest and toward manhood — moves alongside
his, and the two stories begin to touch.

## What to notice

- **Two timelines, one place.** Mark where the story switches. The
  switches are not random; they happen at moments that rhyme.
- **The mountain as more than setting.** Ask what it means to each boy,
  and whether the story treats it as a thing or as a presence — see
  [[Setting and Mood]].
- **What counts as proof.** Both boys are trying to demonstrate
  something. To whom?
- **The ending.** Students argue about what literally happens. That
  argument is worth having, and it is not the same as arguing about what
  the story means.

## Questions we will argue about

1. What is the story saying about how a person becomes an adult — and
   does it think the two boys' routes are the same route?
2. Where does the story leave the line between the physical and the
   spiritual, and is it a line at all?
3. If you read the ending literally and I read it symbolically, can we
   both be right? What would settle it, and is "settling it" even the
   goal? Take that to [[Indigenous Storywork]].

## The craft worth stealing

Wheeler cuts between two stories without ever announcing it. No "meanwhile,
long ago" — just a paragraph break and a change of light. Try it in your
own narrative: trust the reader to notice, and see how much faster the
piece moves.
