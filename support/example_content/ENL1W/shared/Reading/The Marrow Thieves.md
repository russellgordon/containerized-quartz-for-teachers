> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Cherie Dimaline**, Métis writer · *The Marrow Thieves* (Dancing Cat
> Books, 2017) · dystopian novel · one of the four
> [[The Book Club|literature circle]] choices

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> A future in which Indigenous people are hunted for their bone marrow —
> written in deliberate parallel to residential schools. Violence, loss
> of family, and grief.

## The premise

The world's climate has broken and most people have lost the ability to
dream. Indigenous people can still dream, and the dreaming is in their
bone marrow — so the state builds new schools to harvest it. Frenchie,
sixteen, travels north with a found family, and the novel is mostly what
happens between them on the way.

## What to notice

- **"Coming-to" stories.** Each character tells how they came to the
  group. Ask why Dimaline structures the book this way, and what happens
  to the pace each time she stops the plot for one.
- **Story as survival.** Miig's teaching sessions are the novel arguing
  for itself. Track what is passed on and how.
- **Language.** Watch for when Cree words appear, and to whom they are
  given.
- **The north.** What it is in the novel, versus what it is on a map.
- **The parallel.** The new schools are not a metaphor invented for
  fiction. Read the novel next to what you learned from [[Sugar Falls]]
  and say what the fictional frame lets Dimaline do that a history cannot.

## Questions we will argue about

1. Is this a book about the future or about the past? What does answering
   "both" cost you as a reader?
2. Which relationship in the group carries the novel's argument about
   family?
3. The ending. Hopeful, unresolved, or earned — and what would you have
   needed to be convinced?

## For your circle

Your group sets its own reading schedule in the first meeting and keeps
it. Bring one passage each session that you marked, with your reason —
[[Reading Like a Writer]] is the method, and "I do not know why this
one stuck" is an acceptable reason to start from.
