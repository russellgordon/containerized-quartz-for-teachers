> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Shane Koyczan**, Canadian spoken-word poet · "To This Day" (2013) ·
> watched as an animated film, then read as a text, then heard again

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> Bullying, its long aftermath, and self-harm. If any of that is close to
> you, [[Our Agreements]] applies: you may step out, and you need not
> explain.

## Why we watch it before we read it

Because it was written for the ear. Reading it cold on a page removes the
thing it was built out of — pace, breath, the pause before a line lands.
We watch, then we read, then we watch again, and the third pass is where
students notice how much of the effect was *timing*.

## What to notice

- **Repetition as structure.** Not decoration — the returning phrases are
  the skeleton of the piece.
- **Metaphor doing the heavy work.** Track the images for pain and for
  survival; they are not the same images.
- **Direct address.** Who is "you" in this piece, and does it change?
- **The turn.** There is a point where the poem stops describing and
  starts insisting. Find the line.
- **The animation.** Dozens of artists made it, in different styles.
  Ask what that choice says about the poem's argument — and whether the
  film adds something the text alone cannot.

## Questions we will argue about

1. Is this a poem, a speech, or a performance? Does the answer change how
   we should judge it? Take it to [[Poems We Will Argue About]].
2. The piece was made with an audience of millions in mind. Does writing
   for a huge audience make a text less honest, more honest, or neither?
3. Which lines survive being read flat on a page, and which need a voice?
   What does that tell you about what to write down and what to perform
   in [[Say It Out Loud]]?

## What to steal for your own performance

Koyczan slows down almost exactly where an amateur would speed up. Watch
the last ninety seconds twice with [[Reading Aloud]] open beside you, and
mark every pause longer than a second. That map is a lesson in delivery
you cannot get from a page of advice.
