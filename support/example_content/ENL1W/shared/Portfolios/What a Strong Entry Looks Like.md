Two entries about the same class, both real in shape, one much more
useful than the other.

## Thin

> Today we read a poem by Rita Joe. It was sad. I liked it because it was
> short. We talked about residential schools.

Nothing here is wrong. It is just not evidence of anything: a person who
read the poem and a person who heard about it would write the same three
sentences.

## Strong

> "I Lost My Talk" — Rita Joe. The word I keep coming back to is
> "snatched". She could have written "took". Snatched is fast, and it is
> rude, and it needs a person doing it, so the poem never lets the school
> be a place where something just happened.
>
> I said in the discussion that the last stanza was forgiveness. Amara
> said it is not forgiveness, it is a demand — "let me find my talk" is
> an instruction, and the person being instructed is the one who took it.
> I think she is right, and I want to check the pronouns again.
>
> Question for the writer: why English?

Three moves make the difference: a **specific word** quoted, a **thought
that changed** and who changed it, and a **question left open**.

## The shape to copy

| Move | Prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Notice | One quoted detail — a word, a line, a panel, a shot |
| Think | What it does, in your words |
| Argue | What somebody else said, and what you now think |
| Ask | A question you cannot yet answer |

## Length

Five to ten lines is a real entry. A page is a good entry. Three pages
once a term and nothing else is not a journal, it is an essay with a
gap after it.
