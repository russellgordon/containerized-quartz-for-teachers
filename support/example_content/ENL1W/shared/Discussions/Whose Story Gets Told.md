## The question

Who has the right to tell a story that is not their own — and what
changes when they do?

## Where this comes from in our reading

- [[Sugar Falls]] was written by David A. Robertson from Elder Betty
  Ross's account, with her permission, and the book says so on its first
  pages.
- [[The Breadwinner]] was written by Deborah Ellis, a Canadian, from
  interviews with Afghan women and girls in refugee camps.
- [[Borders]] is Thomas King writing from inside a story about identity
  and a border.

Three different relationships between a teller and a story. Not obviously
three different ethics.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anyone may write anything | Imagination is the whole art; restricting subject matter kills fiction | The right to write is not the right to be published, believed, or paid, and the harm falls unevenly |
| Only insiders should tell a community's stories | Communities have been misrepresented by outsiders for centuries, with real consequences | Taken strictly, it would make most historical fiction impossible and can lock writers inside one identity |
| It depends on relationship and permission | Robertson had Betty Ross's account and her blessing; that is a different act from writing about strangers | "Permission" from whom? Communities are not single people with one voice |
| It depends on what the telling replaces | A story that displaces the community's own telling does harm a respectful one does not | Hard to know in advance; easy to use as an excuse either way |

## Ground rules for today

- Argue about the practice, not about a person in the room.
- "I would need to know…" is a legitimate contribution. Say what you
  would need.
- You may change your mind out loud. That is the point.

> [!question] The complication worth raising
> Publishers, schools, and prize juries decide what gets printed and
> taught long before readers decide what to like. Does the question
> "who may tell this story?" matter more or less than "who decides which
> tellings we ever see?"

## Afterwards

One paragraph in your [[Reading and Writing Journal]]: the position you
came in with, the strongest thing said against it, and where you stand
now. "Unchanged, but for a better reason" is a real answer.
