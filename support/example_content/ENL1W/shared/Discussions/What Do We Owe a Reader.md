## The question

A writer can make a reader uncomfortable, confused, or upset. When is
that a failure, and when is it the whole point?

## Start with three moments from our own reading

- [[Sugar Falls]] shows what happened to a child, in pictures, without
  softening it.
- [[Borders]] refuses to explain the mother's position — to the officers
  or to us.
- [[To This Day]] describes self-harm to an audience of millions,
  including people it describes.

Each one made a choice about a reader's comfort. None of them made the
same choice.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clarity is a duty | If a reader cannot follow you, you have failed, whatever you intended | Some things are genuinely difficult, and flattening them is its own dishonesty |
| Difficulty is respect | Refusing to explain treats a reader as capable | Also a convenient excuse for writing that has not been finished |
| Do no harm | A text that could hurt a vulnerable reader should warn them | Warnings can also announce the ending, and can be used to avoid the text entirely |
| Tell the truth, then get out of the way | The writer's obligation is accuracy, not comfort | Whose truth, and told to whom? |

## Turn it on yourselves

Every position above becomes practical this week, because you are
writing. Take the piece you are drafting for
[[Who You Are, in Three Hundred Words]] and ask:

- What am I not saying, and is that craft or fear?
- Who could read this, and what do I owe them?
- Where have I made this harder than it needs to be, and why?

## Afterwards

In your journal: one sentence naming something you decided to leave out
of your own writing, and whether you now think that was the right call.
You will not be asked to share it.
