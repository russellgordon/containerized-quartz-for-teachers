> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · four class periods of work time, then handed in ·
> 300 words, hard limit · assessed on Communication, Thinking, and
> Application

## The task

Write a personal narrative about one moment — not a summary of your life,
one moment — that shows a reader something true about who you are.

Three hundred words is roughly one page. The limit is the point: it
forces every sentence to earn its place, and it makes the difference
between drafting and editing impossible to fake.

## What makes it work

1. **One moment.** An afternoon. A conversation. Ten minutes. If your
   piece covers a year, it is a summary, and summaries do not move
   anybody.
2. **In the moment, not above it.** Put us there: what you could see,
   hear, and smell. See [[Setting and Mood]].
3. **Something at stake.** Not necessarily dramatic — a small thing you
   could lose is plenty.
4. **A turn.** Something changes: what you understood, what you decided,
   or what you were willing to admit.
5. **Your voice.** It should sound like you talking at your most
   deliberate. [[Voice]] explains what that is made of.

## How it will be built

| Period | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Three possible moments, six minutes each, in your journal. Choose one |
| 2 | First draft, by hand, no editing allowed until the end |
| 3 | Workshop: read to a partner, who says what they saw and what they wanted more of |
| 4 | Cut to 300 words. This is the hardest and most instructive period of the four |

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Communication | A reader is in the moment with you; the sentences vary and hold together |
| Thinking | The moment is chosen well and the turn is real, not announced |
| Application | The craft from [[The Sentence]] and [[Voice]] shows up in your choices |

> [!tip] The cut is the lesson
> Almost everybody drafts 450 words. Cutting 150 without losing the piece
> teaches more about writing than the drafting did — you will find whole
> sentences that were explaining what the story already showed.

> [!note] What you may keep private
> This is a personal narrative, not a confession. Write about a moment
> you are willing to have read. If you want to write about something
> harder, write it — and tell me at the top that it is not for the
> workshop, and it will not be.
