Everything I mark, with the criteria published before you start. Each one
runs across several classes, and the work time is part of the task: what
you do with it is assessed, as [[How Marks Work]] explains.

- [[Who You Are, in Three Hundred Words]] — Unit 1. A personal narrative,
  drafted, workshopped, and cut to length.
- [[The Book Club]] — Unit 2. Your literature circle: the reading, the
  talking, and one written response that could not have been written by
  somebody who skimmed.
- [[Say It Out Loud]] — Unit 3. Three minutes, spoken, to this room.
- [[The Fact Check]] — Unit 4. Take one claim apart and publish what you
  found.
- [[The Argument]] — Unit 4. A short essay that changes somebody's mind
  about something small and real.
- [[The Portfolio Conversation]] — the final evaluation, in the last
  week.

> [!tip] Read the criteria table first
> Every task page has one, and it says what separates a strong piece from
> an adequate one. Reading it before you start is worth more than an hour
> of polishing at the end.
