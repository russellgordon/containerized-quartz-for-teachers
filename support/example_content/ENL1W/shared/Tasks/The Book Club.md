> [!abstract] At a glance
> Groups of four or five · six weeks, with a circle meeting every third
> class · one reading log and one written response · assessed on
> Knowledge, Thinking, and Communication

## The task

Choose one novel, read it with a group, and end up with something to say
about it that could not have been said by somebody who read a summary.

The four books are on [[Reading/index|the reading page]]:
[[The Marrow Thieves]], [[Indian Horse]], [[Fatty Legs]], and
[[The Breadwinner]]. Read the first page of each before you choose.

## How a circle runs

Your group sets the schedule in the first meeting and holds itself to it.
Each meeting, everybody arrives with:

- **One passage** you marked, and the reason. "It stuck and I do not know
  why" is a legitimate reason to start from.
- **One question** you cannot answer alone.
- **Your reading log** up to date.

Meetings are twenty minutes and they are not a quiz. I sit in on each
group twice and say nothing for the first ten minutes.

## The reading log

One entry per meeting, in your [[Reading and Writing Journal]]: what you
read, one passage with your note on it, one question, and one sentence
about what somebody else in the group said that you had not thought of.

That last line is the one that matters. A circle where nobody records
being changed by anybody is four people reading in the same room.

## The written response

Eight hundred words, at the end, on a question your group could not
settle. Not a book report — an argument, built the way
[[Writing About Texts]] describes: claim, evidence, explanation.

You may write on a question from your book's page, or one your group
invented. Invented ones are usually better and I will help you sharpen it.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | You know the book — the whole book, including the parts that were work |
| Thinking | The response argues something, with evidence, and handles the strongest objection |
| Communication | A reader who has not read the novel can follow you; quotations are integrated, not dropped |

> [!warning] The failure mode of literature circles
> One person reads and four people nod. It is obvious from the meetings
> within ten minutes, it is unfair to the reader, and it wastes six weeks
> for everybody else. Your log is how you show it did not happen here.
