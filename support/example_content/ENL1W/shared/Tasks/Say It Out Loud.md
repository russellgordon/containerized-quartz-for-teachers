## The task

Write something to be *heard* and then perform it. Three minutes.
Anything from:

- a spoken-word piece of your own, in the tradition of [[To This Day]];
- a reading of a poem or passage by somebody else, prepared properly —
  the interpretation is yours;
- a short speech that makes one argument;
- a piece of your own narrative writing, rewritten for the ear.

## Written for the ear is a different craft

| On the page | Out loud |
| --- | --- |
| Long sentences with clauses | Shorter units; the ear cannot reread |
| Vocabulary chosen for precision | Vocabulary chosen for sound as well |
| Paragraphs | Breaths |
| Repetition looks lazy | Repetition is structure |
| A reader can go back | You get one pass, so signpost |

Read your draft aloud on day one. If you cannot get through a sentence in
one breath, it is not written for the ear yet.

## Delivery

[[Reading Aloud]] is the method: breath marks, the stressed word in each
sentence, a planned pace, and eye contact at the ends of sentences. You
hand in your script *with those marks on it*, because the marks are
evidence of preparation and they are assessed.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Communication | The room follows you; pace, volume, and pauses are chosen rather than accidental |
| Thinking | The piece has one clear idea and a shape — it goes somewhere |
| Application | The script shows the choices you made for the ear, and the delivery marks match what you did |

## If speaking in front of people frightens you

Then this task is the reason you take an English course, and we will
build to it. Options, all of which have been used in this room: perform
to me first; perform to four people; record it and introduce the
recording live. Come and talk to me in the first week of the unit. What
does not happen is skipping it.

> [!tip] The single biggest improvement available
> Slow down and pause. Nervous speakers rush and never stop; the effect
> from the outside is that nothing lands. Mark two deliberate silences in
> your script and hold them for longer than feels comfortable. It will
> sound normal to the room and enormous to you.
