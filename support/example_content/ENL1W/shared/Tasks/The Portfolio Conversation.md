> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · the final evaluation, worth 30% of the course · a
> fifteen-minute conversation with me, prepared for over four classes ·
> your portfolio open on the desk between us

## What it is

You bring your work from the whole course and we talk about it. I ask
questions; you answer them with evidence in front of you. It lasts about
fifteen minutes and it is the most useful assessment I know of, because
it is impossible to do well without having done the course.

## What you bring

Everything on the [[Portfolio Checklist]], plus three chosen pieces:

1. **The piece you are proudest of.**
2. **The piece that taught you the most** — usually a different one, and
   often one that did not go well.
3. **Your growth pair**, per [[Showing Growth]]: two pieces side by side
   with three named differences.

## The questions, published in advance

There is no trick here. These are the questions, and you may prepare
every one of them:

- Read me three sentences you are proud of. What are they doing?
- Show me a place where you changed your mind about a text. What changed
  it?
- Where did somebody else's reading beat yours, and what did you do
  about it?
- What can you do now as a writer that you could not do in September?
  Prove it with the pair.
- What still does not work? What is your plan for it?
- Which text from this course will you still be thinking about in a year,
  and why that one?

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | You know your own work and the texts it came from |
| Thinking | You can judge your own writing — including honestly, where it is weak |
| Communication | You speak clearly, listen to the question actually asked, and use evidence |
| Application | You use the language of the course — theme, voice, point of view — because it is useful, not because it is on a list |

## The four classes before it

Two are work periods for assembling the portfolio, one is a rehearsal in
pairs using the published questions, and one is an open clinic. Nobody
walks into this cold.

> [!important] Why not an exam
> Because a three-hour paper measures how fast you write under pressure,
> and this course is about what you can do with a text and a reader. A
> conversation with the evidence on the table measures that directly —
> and it cannot be produced by anybody, or anything, other than you.
