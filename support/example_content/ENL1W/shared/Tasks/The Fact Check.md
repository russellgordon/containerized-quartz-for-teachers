## The task

Find a claim that is circulating, check it properly, and publish what you
found in a form somebody would actually read or watch.

## Choosing a claim

It must be:

- **Circulating now** — you saw it, somebody sent it to you, it is in a
  feed.
- **Checkable.** "Immigration is bad for Canada" is an argument, not a
  claim. "Immigration to Canada fell by half last year" is checkable.
- **Not about anybody at this school.** No exceptions.

Bring three candidates to the first period; we will sharpen one.

## The check

Use the method in [[Finding and Judging Sources]] and record it as you
go:

1. **Find the original.** A screenshot is not a source.
2. **Identify who made it and what they gain.**
3. **Find the underlying evidence** — the study, the dataset, the
   document.
4. **Look for independent confirmation.** Three articles from one press
   release count once.
5. **Reach a verdict you can defend**: true, false, true-but-misleading,
   or unresolved. **Unresolved is a real verdict** and takes more courage
   than the other three.

## What you publish

One of:

- a written check of about 400 words, structured claim → evidence →
  verdict;
- a social card, designed, with the check on the back side;
- a ninety-second video.

Whatever the form, it must name its sources so a reader can check *you*.
[[Reading a Media Text]] is the reference for making the form work.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Thinking | The check is real: you went to the origin, and you say what would change your verdict |
| Application | The published form fits the audience and uses its conventions deliberately |
| Communication | A stranger understands the verdict in ten seconds and the reasoning in ninety |

> [!important] The one thing that fails this task
> Deciding the verdict first. If your check never had a chance of
> surprising you, it was not a check. The strongest submissions this room
> has seen ended with "the thing I wanted to be false turned out to be
> true, and here is the evidence."
