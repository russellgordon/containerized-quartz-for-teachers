The reference is [[Punctuation That Changes Meaning]].

**1.** Add punctuation so that these mean two different things:
*The teacher said the student was wrong*

> [!success]- Answer 1
> *The teacher said the student was wrong.* (The teacher's opinion.)
> *"The teacher," said the student, "was wrong."* (The student's.)
> Punctuation moved the blame.

**2.** Its or it's? *The poem lost ___ last stanza somewhere in the
retyping, and ___ hard to tell what is missing.*

> [!success]- Answer 2
> *its* last stanza (belonging to it); *it's* hard to tell (it is). There
> is no third option and no apostrophe in the possessive.

**3.** Punctuate: *Rita Joe who was born in Whycocomagh wrote the poem
decades after leaving the school*

> [!success]- Answer 3
> *Rita Joe, who was born in Whycocomagh, wrote the poem decades after
> leaving the school.* The commas mark an aside — take them out and the
> clause starts defining *which* Rita Joe, which is not what you mean.

**4.** Semicolon or comma? *The room went quiet ___ nobody wanted to be
first.*

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Semicolon. Both halves are complete sentences, so a comma alone would
> splice them. A comma plus *and* would also work.

**5.** Fix: *We read three poems, "I Lost My Talk", "The Bull Moose" and
Borders which is a short story.*

> [!success]- Answer 5
> *We read three poems — "I Lost My Talk", "The Bull Moose", and "Suzanne"
> — and one short story, "Borders".* The original mixed a poem list with
> a story and lost the reader; the dashes hold the list apart from the
> sentence.

**6.** Colon or dash? *She wanted one thing ___ to be believed.*

> [!success]- Answer 6
> Either. The colon announces formally; the dash lands harder. In a
> personal narrative, take the dash; in an essay, take the colon.

**7.** Quote this line inside your own sentence, punctuated correctly:
Rita Joe writes *You snatched it away*.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> *Rita Joe writes that the school "snatched it away" — the verb does the
> accusing that the rest of the stanza never states.*
> Short quotation inside the sentence, quotation marks, and your own
> sentence carries the punctuation.
