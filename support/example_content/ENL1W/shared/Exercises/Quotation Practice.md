The conventions are on [[Writing About Texts]]. Every answer here is one
of many that would work.

**1.** Fix the dropped quotation: *The mother refuses to answer. "Blackfoot."
This shows she is proud.*

> [!success]- Answer 1
> *The mother answers the officers' question with one word — "Blackfoot"
> — and then simply repeats it, which turns an answer into a position.*
> The quotation is now inside a sentence, and the claim is sharper than
> "proud".

**2.** This quotation is too long for the point being made. Cut it to the
words that matter: *"I lost my talk / The talk you took away. / When I
was a little girl / At Shubenacadie school."* — the point is that the
poem names a person doing the taking.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> *the poem calls it "the talk you took away"* — five words, and they
> carry the whole claim. Quote the phrase you are analysing, not the
> stanza it lives in.

**3.** Write the explanation sentence that is missing: *Wagamese writes
the ice as "the only place the noise stopped".*

> [!success]- Answer 3
> One version: *The noise is everything the school does to him, and the
> sentence measures the rink not by what it offers but by what it
> removes — which is why hockey in this novel is never simply a joy.*

**4.** Correct the tense: *Frenchie decided to stay behind, and Dimaline
showed us his reasoning two chapters later.*

> [!success]- Answer 4
> *Frenchie decides to stay behind, and Dimaline shows us his reasoning
> two chapters later.* Present tense for what happens in a text; past
> tense only for the world outside it.

**5.** Punctuate and format: the poem is called I Lost My Talk and the
novel is called The Marrow Thieves.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> *The poem is called "I Lost My Talk" and the novel is called
> *The Marrow Thieves*.* Short works in quotation marks, long works in
> italics.

**6.** Take a claim from your current draft and find the shortest
quotation that proves it. Then write two sentences of explanation.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> No fixed answer. The test: could a reader who has not read the text
> follow your paragraph? If your explanation only makes sense to somebody
> who already knows the book, it is summary, not analysis.
