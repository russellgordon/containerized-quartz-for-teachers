The reference is [[The Sentence]]. Write your answers before unfolding.

**1.** Join these into one sentence three different ways: *She read the
poem twice. She still could not explain it.*

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Comma + joining word: *She read the poem twice, but she still could not
> explain it.*
> Semicolon: *She read the poem twice; she still could not explain it.*
> Complex: *Although she read the poem twice, she still could not explain
> it.*
> The third is the strongest, because it says which idea depends on
> which.

**2.** Fix the comma splice: *The bell rang, nobody moved.*

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Any of: *The bell rang, but nobody moved.* / *The bell rang; nobody
> moved.* / *The bell rang. Nobody moved.* The semicolon is best if you
> want the two facts to sit together; the full stop is best if you want
> the stillness to land.

**3.** This is a fragment. Make it a sentence, then keep it as a fragment
on purpose in a two-sentence passage: *Because he never came back.*

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Sentence: *He never came back.*
> On purpose: *She waited at the window until the streetlights came on.
> Because he never came back.* The fragment now lands like a verdict —
> which only works because the sentence before it is complete.

**4.** Rewrite with a stronger verb and no adverb: *He walked quickly
across the room and closed the door in an angry way.*

> [!success]- Answer 4
> *He strode across the room and slammed the door.* Two verbs doing the
> work of two verbs and two adverbs.

**5.** Three sentences in a row start with *The*. Rewrite so that each
starts differently, without changing the meaning:
*The classroom was cold. The heater had been broken since October. The
caretaker had stopped promising to fix it.*

> [!success]- Answer 5
> One possibility: *In the classroom, our breath showed. Since October
> the heater had been broken, and the caretaker had stopped promising to
> fix it.* Two sentences now, varied openings, and the first is more
> concrete than "was cold".

**6.** Cut this to under fifteen words without losing the meaning:
*It is important to note that the reason the poem is effective is because
of the fact that it is very short.*

> [!success]- Answer 6
> *The poem works because it is short.* Seven words. Everything cut was
> throat-clearing.

**7.** Write one sentence of at least twenty-five words that a reader can
still follow, and then a three-word sentence after it. Say what the pair
does.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> Example: *When the discussion finally turned to the last stanza, and
> everybody had said what they thought the poem was doing, Amara pointed
> out that nobody had noticed who the poem was actually addressed to. She
> was right.*
> The long sentence builds; the short one lands. That contrast is the
> most reliable rhythm effect in English prose.
