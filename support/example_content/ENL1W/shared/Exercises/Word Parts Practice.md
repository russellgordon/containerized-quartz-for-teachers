The reference is [[Where Words Come From]]. Work them out before you
unfold; guessing well is the skill.

**1.** Break into parts and define without a dictionary: *transcription*,
*indestructible*, *retrospective*.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> *trans* (across) + *script* (write) + *-ion* (act of) — the act of
> writing something across, from speech to page.
> *in-* (not) + *de-* (down) + *struct* (build) + *-ible* (able to be) —
> not able to be pulled down.
> *retro-* (back) + *spect* (look) + *-ive* (adjective) — looking back.

**2.** *Contradict* and *verdict* share a base. What is it, and what does
each word mean because of it?

> [!success]- Answer 2
> *dict* = say. *Contra-* (against) + say = to speak against.
> *Ver-* (true) + say = a true saying, which is what a jury delivers.

**3.** Guess the meaning from the sentence, then check: *The commission's
report was unequivocal about who was responsible.*

> [!success]- Answer 3
> *un-* (not) + *equi* (equal) + *voc* (voice) — not of two voices, so
> admitting no doubt. The context ("about who was responsible") points
> the same way.

**4.** Which word in each pair is the plainer one, and when would you
choose the other? *ask / interrogate*, *end / terminate*, *help / assist*.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> The first of each. Choose the second only when the register calls for
> it: a police procedure interrogates, a contract terminates, a job
> description assists. In a personal narrative, all three would sound
> borrowed.

**5.** Build three real words from *port* (carry) using prefixes you know,
and define each.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> *import* (carry in), *export* (carry out), *deport* (carry away),
> *transport* (carry across), *support* (carry from underneath).
> The last one is worth noticing: support is not an abstract kindness,
> it is holding something up.

**6.** Find two words in the last text we read that you cannot define,
break them into parts, guess, then look them up. Write which guess was
wrong and why.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> No fixed answer — but the wrong guesses are the valuable half of this
> exercise. Keep them on your vocabulary page with the correction.
