This is an English class, which means most of the period you will be
reading, writing, or talking about what you read and wrote. Very little
of it is me at the front.

## The shape of a period

| Part | Roughly | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | 10 minutes | A short text, a question, or a piece of writing on the board. Everyone writes or talks; nobody watches |
| The work | 45 minutes | Reading, drafting, conferring, workshopping — whatever the class page says |
| Landing | 10 minutes | What we found out, said out loud, and written into your journal |
| Before you go | 5 minutes | The one thing to do before next class |

The class page for each day says which of those the period is. Read it
before you arrive and you will never be lost.

## Independent reading is not optional

Fifteen minutes of every second class is silent reading, with a book you
chose. Not the class text — yours. If you do not have one, that is my
job to fix and I will fix it in the first week.

This is not a filler activity. Reading volume is the single strongest
predictor of vocabulary, and vocabulary is what makes hard texts stop
being hard.

## Talking

You will talk in every class: in pairs, in fours, and sometimes to the
whole room. If that is difficult for you, tell me privately in the first
week and we will build up to it — but we will build up to it, because
[[B1. Oral and Non-Verbal Communication|the course asks for it]] and
because the world you are heading into asks for it constantly.

> [!tip] The rule that makes discussions work
> You may say "I disagree" to any idea in this room. You may not say it
> about a person. The distinction is the whole of [[Our Agreements]], and
> it is easier to keep than it sounds.

## Phones

In the tray at the door, unless the class page says otherwise. There are
days when the phone IS the tool — filming, recording, researching — and
on those days the page will say so.

The reason is not discipline. It is that reading a difficult page and
holding a phone are not compatible activities, and every study on this
agrees more strongly than students expect.
