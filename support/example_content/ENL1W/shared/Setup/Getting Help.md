In order of how fast they work.

1. **Ask the person beside you.** In this course, that is not cheating;
   it is what a writing room is. It is only cheating if their words end
   up in your piece.
2. **Read the page.** Almost every "how do I…" in this course has a
   page: [[Reading Like a Writer]], [[The Sentence]],
   [[Citing What You Use]], [[Getting Unstuck]].
3. **Ask me during the work part of the period.** I am walking the room
   for exactly this reason. You do not need to have a polished question.
4. **Come to [[Help Sessions]].** Tuesdays and Thursdays at lunch.
5. **Email me.** Slowest, but it works, and a question sent at 9 p.m.
   will be answered before class.

## What to say when you do not know what to say

These four sentences get you further than any of the essay-writing advice
on the internet, and I would rather hear them than watch you stall:

- "I do not understand what the task is asking."
- "I understand the task and I cannot start."
- "I have a draft and I think it is bad, but I cannot say why."
- "I read it twice and I still do not know what it means."

The last one is the most common, and the answer is almost always the same:
we read a paragraph of it together, out loud, slowly. It takes four
minutes.

> [!tip] The thing students wait too long to say
> "I am behind." Say it early and it is a scheduling problem. Say it in
> the last week and it is a crisis. Nobody in this room has ever been
> told off for saying it early.
