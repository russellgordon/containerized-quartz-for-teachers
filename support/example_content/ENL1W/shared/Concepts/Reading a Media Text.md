A media text is anything made to communicate to an audience: an
advertisement, a film trailer, a news front page, a short video, a
podcast, a poster, a profile. They can be read as closely as a poem, and
they are built from the same [[Literary Devices|devices]] plus some of
their own.

## The five questions

1. **Who made this, and why?** Somebody paid for it or spent time on it.
   What did they want to happen?
2. **Who is it for?** Age, interests, income, language — audiences are
   chosen before anything is made.
3. **What is the message?** Both the stated one and the one carried
   underneath.
4. **What techniques carry it?** Framing, music, colour, editing pace,
   type, what is centred, who speaks.
5. **What is left out?** The most useful question of the five.

## Techniques worth naming

| Technique | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Framing** | Low angle makes a subject powerful; high angle makes them small |
| **Colour** | Warm for safety and nostalgia, cool for distance and control |
| **Editing pace** | Fast cuts create excitement and prevent thought |
| **Sound** | Music tells you how to feel before the image does |
| **Type** | A serif says tradition; a heavy sans says urgency |
| **Placement** | What is centred is the subject; what is at the edge is a claim |
| **Absence** | Who is not shown, and what is never mentioned |

## The same event, two front pages

Two newspapers report one protest. One leads with a photograph of a
broken window; the other leads with the crowd, wide, in daylight. Neither
is lying. Both have made an argument out of a lens choice, and a reader
who cannot see that is being argued at rather than with.

## Making, not just reading

You will make media texts in this course too — a social card, a short
video, a poster — and the fastest way to understand a technique is to use
it deliberately. [[The Fact Check]] and [[Say It Out Loud]] both ask you
to make something an audience will actually meet.

> [!question] Worth arguing about
> An advertisement that tells the truth, beautifully, in order to sell
> something you do not need: is that manipulation? Bring your answer to
> [[Is It True, or Is It Just Fast]].
