Devices are not decoration and naming them is not analysis. A device is a
tool a writer used to make you feel or notice something, and the only
question worth asking is: **what does it do here?**

## The ones this course uses

| Device | What it is | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simile** | Comparison using *like* or *as* | Imports a whole feeling in five words |
| **Metaphor** | Says one thing *is* another | Stronger than simile; no distance |
| **Personification** | Gives human qualities to something not human | Makes a place or object feel intentional |
| **Imagery** | Language aimed at the senses | Puts the reader in the body of the scene |
| **Symbol** | An object that carries meaning beyond itself | Lets a whole idea be present without being stated |
| **Allusion** | A reference to another text or event | Borrows everything the reader already feels about it |
| **Juxtaposition** | Two things placed side by side | The contrast makes the argument |
| **Foreshadowing** | An early hint of a later event | Makes an ending feel inevitable rather than convenient |
| **Flashback** | A jump backwards in time | Explains the present without stopping to explain |
| **Irony** | The gap between what is said or expected and what is | Lets a text criticise without lecturing |
| **Repetition** | Saying it again on purpose | Insists; also builds rhythm |
| **Analogy** | An extended comparison used to explain | Makes an unfamiliar idea land |

## Naming is step one of three

Weak: *The writer uses a simile.*

Middling: *The writer uses a simile, comparing the hallway to something
trapped.*

Analysis: *Comparing the buzzing lights to "something trapped" puts the
reader inside the narrator's panic before the narrator admits to it — the
building is described the way she feels.*

Name it, quote it, say what it does. The third move is the one that earns
the mark, every time.

## Devices in things that are not literature

The same tools run advertising, speeches, and social video: a symbol on a
product, an allusion in a caption, juxtaposition in a two-shot edit. That
is why [[Reading a Media Text]] uses this page rather than starting over.

> [!warning] The device hunt
> A paragraph that lists five devices and explains none of them is worth
> less than a paragraph that explains one. Do not go looking for a
> checklist. Read for what struck you, then find out what the writer did
> to strike you.
