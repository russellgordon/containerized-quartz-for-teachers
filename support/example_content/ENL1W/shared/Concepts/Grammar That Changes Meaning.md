Grammar is not manners. Most of it is meaning: change a tense and you
change when something happened; move a modifier and you change who did
what to whom. This page covers the parts that actually alter what a
sentence says.

## Parts of speech, by what they do

| Part | Does | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noun | Names | Concrete beats abstract: *stairwell*, not *area* |
| Verb | Acts | The engine of the sentence; weak verbs sink prose |
| Adjective | Describes a noun | Two good ones beat five |
| Adverb | Describes a verb | Often a sign the verb is not strong enough |
| Pronoun | Stands in for a noun | Must have one obvious owner |
| Preposition | Places in space or time | Idiomatic; when in doubt, read aloud |
| Conjunction | Joins | Which one you choose states the relationship |
| Determiner | Points | *a* introduces; *the* assumes we know it already |

## Four errors that change the meaning

**Dangling modifier.** *Running for the bus, my bag split.* The bag was
not running. Put the doer straight after the comma: *Running for the bus,
I split my bag.*

**Pronoun with no clear owner.** *When Maya told Priya about the letter,
she cried.* Who cried? Name her.

**Tense drift.** Analysis stays in the present: *Frenchie decides*, not
*decided*, unless you are talking about the real world outside the book.

**Subject–verb disagreement across a long subject.** *The list of names
on the two posters were wrong.* The list *was* wrong. Find the real
subject by deleting the middle.

## Sentence variety is a grammar skill

Beginning with a dependent clause (*Although he had read it twice, …*),
with a participle (*Standing at the border, …*), or with the subject
plainly — these are choices, and rotating them is what stops writing
sounding mechanical.

> [!note] About "rules" you may have been given
> Never start with *And*. Never end with a preposition. Never use *I*.
> These are conventions of certain registers, not laws of English, and
> published writers break all three daily. What matters is knowing which
> register you are in — which is the actual skill, and the one this
> course marks.
