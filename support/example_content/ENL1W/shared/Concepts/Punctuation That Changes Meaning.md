Punctuation is not decoration and it is not a guess about where you would
pause. Each mark does a job, and several of them change what a sentence
says.

## The marks, and what each does

| Mark | Job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full stop** | Ends a complete thought | She left. |
| **Comma** | Separates parts inside a sentence | After the bell, she left. |
| **Semicolon** | Joins two related complete thoughts | She left; nobody followed. |
| **Colon** | Announces what follows | She left for one reason: the noise. |
| **Dash** | Interrupts, for emphasis | She left — quickly — before anyone asked. |
| **Apostrophe** | Possession or omission | the school's roof; don't |
| **Quotation marks** | Somebody else's exact words | "I lost my talk," she writes. |
| **Brackets** | An aside the sentence could live without | The poem (published in 1978) still lands. |

## Where a comma changes the meaning

> *Let's eat, Grandma.* / *Let's eat Grandma.*

Old joke, real principle. Three more that matter in your writing:

- **Before a joining word between two full sentences**: *She read it, and
  she read it again.* Without the comma, that is a different, choppier
  sentence.
- **Around an aside**: *Rita Joe, who was ten, wrote it decades later.*
  Take the commas out and you have changed which Rita Joe you mean.
- **After an introductory phrase**: *After the discussion about
  residential schools, the room was quiet.* The comma tells a reader
  where the sentence's actual subject begins.

## Apostrophes, settled in three lines

- *its* = belonging to it. *it's* = it is. There is no third option.
- Singular possession: *the writer's voice.* Plural: *the writers'
  voices.*
- Never for a plain plural: *the 1990s*, *three books*.

## Quoting punctuation

Canadian convention keeps punctuation inside the quotation marks when it
belongs to the quotation and outside when it belongs to your sentence:

> She calls it "theft".
> She writes, "You snatched it away."

Consistency matters more than the tradition you follow — pick one and
keep it through the piece.
