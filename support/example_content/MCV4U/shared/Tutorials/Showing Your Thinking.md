An answer with no visible reasoning is a rumour: it might be true, but
nobody — including you, three weeks later — can tell why. In this
course the reasoning *is* the work. Derivations, optimizations, and
models built from data all live or die on whether a reader can follow
the chain, and showing it is a concrete skill with named parts.

## The four-part habit on paper

1. **Restate** — one line saying what the problem actually asks, in
   your own words. "Find when the velocity is zero" is a different
   job from "find when the object returns to its start" — half of
   all wrong answers are right answers to a misread question.
2. **Reason** — the steps in order, with a word about *why* each move
   is legal. "The quotient rule applies only where the denominator is
   nonzero, so we note $x \neq 2$ and treat it separately" beats a
   bare arrow — and in an optimization, the why *is* the product:
   the derivative set to zero finds *candidates*, and the sentence
   that checks them against the endpoints is the step that makes the
   answer true.
3. **Units** — carried through every line, not sprinkled on at the
   end. A [[The Speed Camera]] answer should arrive in kilometres
   per hour; a [[Smooth Landing]] descent rate in metres per second,
   and its comfort in metres per second *squared*; a vector's
   magnitude should say what it measures. Units that arrive wrong
   have found your error for you.
4. **Answer sentence** — a full sentence a stranger could read on its
   own: "The car was travelling 63 km/h as it passed the camera, so
   the ticket stands." A boxed $v = 63$ answers nothing by itself.

## At the whiteboards

One rule does most of the work: **the person holding the marker writes
only other people's ideas.** It forces reasoning out loud — you cannot
write what nobody has said — so every line on the board has already
survived one listener. And cross out rather than erase: a corrected
line is a record of thinking, which is what the board is *for*, as
[[Mistakes Are Data]] argues at length.

## The checklist

- [ ] Could someone follow this without me standing beside it?
- [ ] Is there a why beside every non-obvious step?
- [ ] Do the units survive from the first line to the last?
- [ ] Does it end in a sentence answering the actual question?
- [ ] Are my crossed-out attempts still visible?
