Being stuck is not an emergency — in this course it is the expected
state at the start of every thinking task. But there are two kinds of
stuck. **Stuck-and-thinking** is still generating moves; it feels
uncomfortable and is quietly productive. **Stuck-and-spinning** is
rereading the same line while feeling worse; nothing is moving except
the feeling. [[Why Struggle Is the Point]] is the why; this page is
the how — five moves for turning spinning back into thinking.

## Five moves, in rough order

1. **Re-read the problem aloud.** Actually aloud, or at least moving
   your lips. A shocking share of stuckness is a misread question —
   "when is the *velocity* zero" is not "when is the *position*
   zero", and "maximize the volume" is not "maximize the surface
   area" — and the ear catches what the eye skims.
2. **Try a smaller case.** Optimization too big to set up? Build one
   specific box first — a base of 10 cm, say — and compute its
   volume; the general setup is that arithmetic with a letter in it.
   Three dimensions too many? Solve the flat cousin first.
   [[Visual Patterns]] surrenders to whoever shrinks it, and so does
   nearly everything else here.
3. **Sketch it.** The sign of the derivative on a number line before
   any values, the two vectors drawn head-to-tail before any
   components, the situation of an optimization drawn with its
   variable *labelled*. Paper holds the picture so your head can
   hold the thinking — no problem in [[The Box Problem]] ever
   started without a diagram.
4. **Estimate the size of the answer.** Before solving, ask: should
   this tangent slope be nearer 5 or 50? Must this angle be acute,
   because the dot product came out positive? An expected size gives
   you a wall to bounce ideas off — and flags nonsense the moment it
   appears.
5. **Explain the stuck-point to someone.** Not "I don't get it" but
   "my optimal width came out negative, which cannot be right
   because a box cannot have a negative side, so my constraint must
   be…". Half the time you hear the way forward as it leaves your
   mouth.

## When to step away

If all five moves are spent and you are spinning, stepping away is a
strategy, not a surrender — brains keep working on problems in the
background, which is why solutions famously arrive in showers. Leave
yourself a note about exactly where it broke, come back after a walk
or a sleep, and if the wall is still solid, that is what
[[Getting Help]] is for. Arriving with a well-described stuck-point is
not weakness; it is the most efficient question a student can ask.
