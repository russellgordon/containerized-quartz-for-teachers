Two options on the board — "as a bicycle courier, would you rather
be paid 60 cents per kilometre ridden, or a flat 18 dollars per
hour?" — and one rule: your preference is worthless until
mathematics backs it. Most good prompts hide a third answer behind
the two printed ones: *it depends* — and the game is finding on
what.

## How we play

1. Choose a side in silence. Commit before calculating feels safe.
2. Defend with numbers, a table, or a sketch — not with vibes.
3. Hunt the tipping point: where, exactly, does the better choice switch?

> [!example]- The courier duel, argued
> - "The flat rate, obviously — you get paid even at red lights."
> - "Convert them to the same units first. At a riding speed of $v$
>   kilometres per hour, the per-kilometre deal pays $0.60v$ dollars
>   per hour. Now the two offers can actually fight."
> - "Tipping point: $0.60v = 18$ gives $v = 30$. Below 30 km/h,
>   take the flat rate; above it, take the distance rate."
> - "So it depends on your *average* speed across a shift — gridlock
>   afternoons and open-road mornings crown different winners. And
>   notice what the comparison secretly required: turning a rate per
>   kilometre into a rate per hour meant multiplying by a rate of
>   kilometres per hour. Chaining rates together like that is not a
>   trick, it is a law — the same one [[The Chain Rule]] states for
>   functions."

## One variation

"Would you rather land in a plane with a *small velocity* at
touchdown, or a *small acceleration*?" Velocity is how hard the
wheels hit; acceleration is what your stomach feels on the way down
— the rate of the rate. A descent can be slow and still lurching, or
brisk and glass-smooth. Deciding which quantity comfort actually
lives in is a warm-up for [[Smooth Landing]], where your group must
design a descent whose height, velocity, and acceleration *all*
behave — and [[Motion on a Line]] gives the three quantities their
exact relationship.

> [!tip] "It depends" is a mathematical answer
> The strongest defence names the variable it depends on and the
> exact value where the choice flips. For the courier duel that
> variable is average speed and the flip is 30 km/h — and the
> difference between an average rate and the rate *right now* is
> where [[The Speed Camera]] raises the stakes to a ticket in
> court.
