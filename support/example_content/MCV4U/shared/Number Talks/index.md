Most days, the first ten minutes of class are a number talk: one
prompt on the board, no pencils, no calculators. You think, then you
defend your reasoning aloud while we listen for paths unlike our own.
The prompts have grown up one last time — slopes, limits, motion, and
vectors now sit where last year's logarithms did — but the rhythm has
not: there is no single right method, and reasoning is defended in
public. This is the calculus room, the course the other courses were
walking toward, and these ten minutes are still where the sharpest
thinking of the day tends to happen.
