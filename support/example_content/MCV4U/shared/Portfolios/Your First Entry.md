The first entry in your [[Math Journal]] is the hardest, because the
page is blank and you do not yet trust what belongs on it. So for
week one only, the entry is guided: answer the three prompts below, a
few sentences each, written the same day as class. Ten minutes, done.

## The three prompts

1. **What we did** — the facts, briefly. The warm-up, the thinking
   task, where your group got to before time ran out.
2. **What surprised me** — one specific moment you did not expect. In
   the mathematics, in a classmate's method, in yourself.
3. **Where I stand with math right now** — honestly. Everyone
   arrives at calculus with a history — three whole courses of
   functions by now, and for some of you a long wait for this exact
   course — and naming yours is the first piece of data in the
   record.

## What honest looks like

These fragments are the register to aim for — plain, specific, true:

> "We took average speeds over smaller and smaller stretches of the
> trip and the numbers kept heading somewhere — 6.1, 6.01, 6.001.
> Everyone could see the address. Apparently naming what lives there
> is the whole first unit, and I want to know why that needs a whole
> unit."

> "I said the car was 'obviously' speeding at the third reading and
> my group made me say which numbers I was comparing before they
> would write it down. It turned out I was comparing an average to a
> limit without knowing either word. Obvious is apparently still not
> a reason."

> "Advanced functions went fine as long as I could reach for a
> recipe. Today there was no recipe — just position data and a
> question about one instant. I hated the first ten minutes and
> then, weirdly, did not."

None of these is polished, and none performs for a reader. Each names
a real moment and stays with it — which already beats a page of "math
was fine today, I learned a lot."

> [!important] Nobody reads this but me
> As [[Math Journal]] promises: entries are private. I read them;
> other students never do, and nothing you admit here is quoted or
> used as an example without your explicit okay. The honesty you risk
> in week one becomes your best evidence of growth by the final unit
> — but only you decide who ever hears about it.
