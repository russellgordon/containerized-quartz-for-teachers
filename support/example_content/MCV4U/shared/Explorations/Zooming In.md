A claim is on the table: *every graph, zoomed far enough in at a
point, becomes a straight line.* Your group's job is to make the
claim rich — and then to break it.

## The task

In [[Using Desmos]], graph $y = x^2$ and zoom in at $(1, 1)$ until
the curve looks straight. Measure the slope of what you see — pick
two on-screen points and compute rise over run. Repeat at $(2, 4)$
and at $(3, 9)$; record all three slopes and conjecture the pattern.
Now the hunt: find a graph and a point where zooming *never*
straightens anything. Try $y = |x|$. Try $y = \sin x$. Try one of
your own invention. At which points do these graphs straighten, and
at which do they refuse? Finish with a rule the class can vote on:
which functions earn a slope at a point, and what, exactly,
disqualifies the others?

> [!tip]- Facilitation notes — for the teacher
> The slope measured at $x = 3$ should collide with the number the
> secant tables chased on Day 2 — same value, different road; wait
> for someone to notice rather than pointing it out. At the origin,
> $y = |x|$ keeps its corner at every magnification — "it is Vs all
> the way down" is the sentence you are listening for, and it is the
> whole idea of non-differentiability arriving early. Fast groups:
> $y = x^{1/3}$ at the origin straightens into a *vertical* line — a
> tangent whose slope no number can state — and piecing together
> where $y = |x|$ *does* have slopes builds the derivative function
> of a non-smooth function, a Unit 3 idea a fortnight early.

## What mathematics tends to surface

Local linearity: smooth curves are secretly straight up close, and
the slope of that hidden line is the instantaneous rate the secants
were chasing. Corners are the price of admission — where a graph has
one, no single line fits, and no slope exists there at all. The
measured slopes at $1$, $2$, and $3$ line up in a pattern begging to
be named — which is exactly what [[The Derivative]] does next class.

## Where it leads

The tangent's slope becomes the derivative's definition next class,
and the zoomed-in view returns all semester: [[Curve Sketching]]
reads whole graphs through these local slopes, and every "is it
smooth here?" question in the course is settled by today's zoom.

> boards — bring your best broken graph.
