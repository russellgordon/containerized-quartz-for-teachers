A function has gone missing. All the department recovered is the
graph of its derivative — $f'$, the witness. Your group are the
detectives, and the witness saw everything except one thing.

## The task

Your teacher hands each group a different witness: the graph of some
$f'$, and nothing else. Interrogate it. Where was the missing $f$
increasing? Decreasing? Where did it turn, and which way? Where was
it climbing steepest? Where was it curving upward, and where
downward? Sketch a candidate $f$ consistent with every observation —
then look at the board beside yours. They drew a *different* $f$,
and theirs fits the evidence too. How many suspects fit, what do
they all have in common, and what single extra fact would identify
the culprit exactly? Then swap witnesses with another group and run
the interrogation again, faster.

> [!tip]- Facilitation notes — for the teacher
> The sign chart invents itself at the boards — groups shade "up"
> and "down" stretches without being told to, which is the point.
> The productive fight: is a zero of $f'$ automatically a turn?
> Slip a witness whose graph *touches* zero without crossing to the
> fastest group and watch the plateau surprise them. The
> vertical-shift ambiguity is the payoff — infinitely many suspects,
> one shared shape, and a single value of $f$ pins the culprit: the
> $+C$ of next year's calculus, discovered under interrogation. A
> witness like the upward parabola with zeros at $1$ and $3$, plus
> the tip $f(0) = 0$, makes a satisfying closing case.

## What mathematics tends to surface

Direction from the sign of $f'$, turning points where that sign
changes, and — from the slope *of the witness itself* — the missing
function's concavity, including the inflection point where its
bending switches. And the one thing the witness never saw: how high
the whole story sat. Every vertical shift of a working suspect works
too. [[Curve Sketching]] turns today's interrogation into a
checklist.
