**The question:** if you want better work, is more money the answer?

## Where the argument starts

Herzberg's claim is that pay is a hygiene factor: too little makes people
dissatisfied, but more of it does not motivate. It is one of the most
quoted findings in management and one of the most argued with, because
almost everyone reports caring quite a lot about their pay.

Both things can be true. Watch for the distinction between pay as
**security**, pay as **fairness**, and pay as **reward for effort** —
three different mechanisms that get flattened into one word.

## The discussion

1. Start concrete: describe a job you or someone you know has done. Would
   more money have produced better work? What would have?
2. Test Herzberg against [[Theories of Motivation]]. Where does
   goal-setting theory predict something different?
3. Fairness: does a raise motivate the person who received it, or
   demotivate the four who did not? What does that imply about how raises
   are communicated?
4. The case in [[The Motivation Problem]] is on the table. Was that a
   money problem?
5. Land it: you manage a team and have no control over pay. What is left
   to you — and is that a real answer or a consolation?

> [!warning] "Money doesn't motivate" is easy to say from a salary
> Any argument in this seminar has to survive being said out loud to
> someone whose rent is due. Test yours against that.
