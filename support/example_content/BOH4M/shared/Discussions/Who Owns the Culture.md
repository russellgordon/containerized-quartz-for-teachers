**The question:** if culture is what people do by default, who is
responsible when the default is bad?

## Positions

- **The leadership owns it.** Culture follows what leaders reward,
  tolerate, and model. Everything else is decoration.
- **Everyone owns it.** Culture is the sum of what colleagues let each
  other get away with; the strongest signal comes from peers, not the
  top.
- **The structure owns it.** Neither — see [[Design and Culture]]. What
  the organization makes easy is what people will do, regardless of who
  wants what.

## The discussion

1. Describe a culture you have been inside — a team, a workplace, a
   school department. What was normal there that would be strange
   elsewhere?
2. Trace it. Did it come from a leader, from peers, or from the way the
   place was arranged?
3. Take a real problem — say, a workplace where nobody raises problems
   until they are expensive. Which of the three positions gives you
   something to actually do on Monday?
4. Then the uncomfortable one: can a new manager change a culture they
   inherited? What would the first ninety days look like, and what would
   be beyond their reach?

> [!tip] Observable and core
> Most attempts to change culture change the observable level — new
> values, new posters, a renamed meeting. Use
> [[Corporate Culture]] to say what would have to change underneath for
> any of it to hold.
