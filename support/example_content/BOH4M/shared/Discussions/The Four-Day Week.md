**The question:** should this organization move to a four-day week with
no loss of pay?

## Prepare with evidence

Bring one real trial — an organization that tried it, ideally Canadian —
and what happened: hours, output, turnover, customer service, and what
the organization changed to make it work. Trials that failed are as
useful as trials that succeeded, and rarer to find written up.

## The seminar

1. Present your trial in one minute: what changed, what was measured,
   what happened.
2. As a class, separate the arrangements: compressed hours (same hours,
   four days) is not the same as reduced hours at the same pay. They have
   different evidence and different costs.
3. Apply [[Where and How We Work]]: who gains, who loses, and which
   employees does this arrangement quietly not suit?
4. The organizational question: which kinds of work can absorb this and
   which cannot? A software team and a long-term care home are not the
   same problem.
5. Finish as a manager, not a commentator: you have been asked to pilot
   it in one department. Which department, what would you measure, and
   what result would make you stop?

> [!warning] Two things get conflated in this argument
> Whether people can do the same work in fewer hours, and whether they
> should be paid the same for it. The first is an empirical question; the
> second is a question about who gets the gains from productivity. Say
> which one you are arguing.
