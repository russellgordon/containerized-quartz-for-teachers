**The question:** when work is making people unwell, who is responsible
for fixing it — the individual, the manager, or the organization?

## The tension

Organizations increasingly offer wellness programs, meditation apps,
resilience training, and employee assistance lines. All of these are real
benefits, and all of them are aimed at helping an individual cope.

Almost none of them change the workload, the staffing, the schedule, or
the manager.

## Positions to argue

- **Individual responsibility:** adults manage their own health;
  employers who go further are being generous, not obligated.
- **Organizational responsibility:** if the job causes the harm, the job
  is where the fix belongs; a coping program is treating the symptom.
- **Shared, with a line:** support is shared, but there is a level of
  workload no amount of resilience training makes acceptable — and the
  argument is about where that line sits.

## The discussion

1. Take a position and give a concrete case.
2. Apply the test from [[Stress at Work]]: what does each intervention
   change about the *source*?
3. Ontario law requires employers to protect health and safety, including
   psychological safety in specific respects. Does the legal minimum
   settle the ethical question? See [[The Law at Work]].
4. Finish with the manager's question: you cannot hire more staff and
   cannot change the deadline. What can you actually do this week?
