**The question:** how much of a person's attitude at work is about the
work, and how much is about the manager?

Everyone in this room has had a supervisor, a coach, a shift lead, or a
teacher who changed how they felt about showing up. That experience is
data, and today we use it carefully.

## Ground rules

- No names, no identifying details. Describe behaviour, not people.
- Talk about what was *done*, and what it caused. "He was awful" is not
  usable; "he changed the schedule on Fridays without telling anyone" is.
- Nobody is obliged to share anything.

## The discussion

1. Describe one thing a manager did that changed your attitude to a job —
   in either direction.
2. As a class, sort the examples: which changed the *work itself*, which
   changed *recognition*, which changed *fairness*, which changed
   *relationships*?
3. Where does the class evidence agree with
   [[Attitudes and Satisfaction]], and where does it not?
4. The hard one: how much of what we have described could the manager
   actually control, and how much was the organization above them?

> [!warning] The pattern to notice, not the anecdote
> One bad manager is a story. Fifteen stories that all cluster in the
> same column are a finding — and that is the difference between a
> complaint and an analysis.

Keep your example. It is useful evidence for
[[Your Leadership Statement]], where you say what kind of manager you
intend to be.
