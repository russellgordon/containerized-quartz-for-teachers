**The question:** beyond obeying the law and making money, what does a
company owe to anyone?

## Two positions worth taking seriously

**The shareholder position.** A company's managers are trustees of other
people's money. Spending it on causes the owners did not choose is
generous with someone else's property; the right way to do good is to
compete well, employ people, pay taxes, and let owners give away their
own money.

**The stakeholder position.** A company exists by public permission —
limited liability, roads, courts, an educated workforce — and its
decisions land on people who never agreed to them. Owing something back
is not charity; it is the price of the licence.

## Prepare one of these

Bring evidence, not adjectives. One Canadian organization, one decision,
what it cost, and who paid.

## In the seminar

1. Open with your organization and its decision, in ninety seconds.
2. Then answer the hardest question the other side asks you.
3. Before we finish: name one thing the other position gets right.

> [!tip] The strongest argument in the room
> It is usually the one that admits a cost. "This was worth doing and it
> made the product more expensive" beats "this was free and good for
> everyone", because the second one is rarely true and everyone knows it.

Bring what you find here into [[The Ethics Brief]] — a brief that only
argues one side will not do well.
