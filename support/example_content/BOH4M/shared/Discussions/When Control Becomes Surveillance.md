**The question:** where is the line between managing performance and
watching people?

## The situations

An employer can, with varying legal footing, log keystrokes, track
vehicles, record calls, monitor building access, count messages, and
photograph a workstation. Each has a defensible management reason and an
obvious cost.

| The measure | The stated reason | What it also does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GPS on delivery vehicles | Routing, safety, customer estimates | Records every stop, including the ones that are none of the employer's business |
| Call recording | Training and dispute resolution | Changes how people speak to customers, and to each other |
| Productivity software on laptops | Fairness for remote workers | Measures presence, which is not work |
| Camera at a till | Theft, which is real | Watches a person for eight hours |

## The discussion

1. Rank the four by how comfortable you would be working under them, and
   compare rankings. Where does the class disagree most?
2. Apply [[Discipline and Control Measures]]: which of these are external
   controls, and what happens to internal control — self-discipline,
   professional standards — when external control increases?
3. Ontario privacy and employment law gives employees some protections
   here and fewer than most people assume. Does the legal answer settle
   the management question?
4. The test that matters: what does each measure *tell you that you could
   not learn another way*? A control that produces data nobody acts on is
   surveillance with a business case attached.
