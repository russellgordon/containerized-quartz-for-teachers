From blank page to sent, using a memo you will actually write in
[[The Ethics Brief]].

## Start at the end

Before writing anything, finish this sentence: **"I want you to
_______."** If you cannot, you are not ready to write — you are still
deciding.

## The structure

```
To:      Priya Raman, VP Operations
From:    your name
Date:    the date
Subject: Suspend purchasing from Nord Berry pending an audit

Recommendation
One or two sentences. The thing you want done.

Why
Two or three reasons, strongest first, each with its evidence.

What it costs
Money, time, risk, and what we give up by doing this.

Next steps
Who does what, by when.
```

## Working through it

**Subject line.** Write it last, and make it the decision. *Nord Berry
supplier issue* is a topic. *Suspend purchasing from Nord Berry pending
an audit* is a decision, and it is the version that gets read today.

**The recommendation.** Say it plainly. "It is recommended that
consideration be given to a possible suspension" is four hedges wrapped
around a maybe.

**The reasons.** Two or three, not seven. If you have seven, three of
them are the same reason.

**The cost.** Include it even when it is uncomfortable, because your
reader will find it anyway and would rather find it here.

## Before you send

1. Read it aloud. Fix whatever you stumble over.
2. Check every number and every name.
3. Ask: could someone act on this without asking me a question? If not,
   what is missing is usually the "who does what by when".

> [!tip] One page
> A memo longer than a page is a report with a memo on top. If yours is
> running long, put the detail in an appendix and keep the decision
> where the reader will find it.

The conventions themselves are on [[Writing for Business]].
