In this course a claim without a source is an opinion. Sourcing is quick
once you have the habit, and it is the difference between a report a
manager can act on and one they have to verify.

## What to record, as you go

For everything you use: **who said it, where, when, and how you found
it.** Record it the moment you read the source — reconstructing citations
the night before is how facts get attached to the wrong company.

## The forms we use

| Source | What the citation looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Annual report | Corbeau Foods Inc., *2024 Annual Report*, p. 14. |
| News article | Author, "Headline", *Publication*, 12 March 2025. |
| Company website | Corbeau Foods, "Our suppliers", corbeaufoods.example, accessed 4 October 2025. |
| Statistics Canada | Statistics Canada, Table 14-10-0023-01, accessed 4 October 2025. |
| Interview | Interview with A. Nakamura, Operations Manager, 8 October 2025. |
| Job posting | "Aquatics Supervisor", City of Kingston, posted 2 September 2025. |

## Judging a source before you use it

- **Who benefits from this being believed?** A company's own release is
  evidence of what it wants said.
- **When was it written?** A five-year-old market claim is a historical
  fact, not a current one.
- **Is the number sourced?** Trade press often repeats a company's figure
  without checking it; follow it to the original.
- **Is this the whole document?** Executive summaries are written to be
  quoted.

## Interviews

If you interview someone for [[The Organization Study]] or
[[The Management Review]]: say who you are and what the work is for, ask
whether you may quote them by name, and honour whatever they say. If
they ask to be anonymous, cite the role — "a shift supervisor,
interviewed 8 October" — and keep your notes private.

> [!tip] Two sources for anything that matters
> Any claim your recommendation depends on should appear in two
> independent places, and if it does not, say so. Naming the limits of
> your evidence is a strength in a management report, not a weakness.
