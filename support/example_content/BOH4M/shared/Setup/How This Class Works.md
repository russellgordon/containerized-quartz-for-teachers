BOH4M is about how organizations are run, and it is taught the way
management is actually learned: by working real situations, arguing about
them, and then naming what you did.

## A normal period

Most classes follow the same shape, and the class page tells you which
part is which:

1. **Something to work on**, before anything is explained — a case, a
   decision, a headline, a role-play.
2. **The discussion**, where your reasoning gets compared with everyone
   else's.
3. **The name for it**, once you have already met the idea. That is when
   the concept page becomes useful.
4. **Where it goes next** — the task, the portfolio entry, or the
   simulation it feeds.

If you ever wonder why we did something in that order, that is the
reason: an idea you have already wrestled with sticks; an idea you were
handed does not.

## What the course asks of you

- **Come with the reading done.** The seminars do not work otherwise, and
  everyone can tell.
- **Argue with evidence.** Strong opinions are welcome; unsupported ones
  get asked "how do you know?" — by me and, before long, by each other.
- **Work with people who are not your friends.** Teams are assigned for
  most of the term. Managing people you did not choose is the job.
- **Be discreet.** Some of this course involves real organizations and
  real people. What you learn in an interview belongs to them.

## The shape of the term

Four units — what managers do, leading people, planning and change,
organizing — and then eight days of consulting for a real client in
[[The Management Review]]. The course ends with a three-hour case
examination, which is not a memory test: you will have the case, and you
will need everything you have practised.
