You are covering as operations manager for a distribution depot for one
day. It is 8:00 a.m. You have fourteen items waiting and until 11:00
before your first meeting.

## What is in the basket

A driver called in sick and a route is uncovered. A customer's escalation
email from Friday, unanswered. A supplier invoice queried by finance. A
health and safety near-miss report filed last Wednesday, unread. Two
holiday requests for the same week. A recruiter's call about a vacancy
posted six weeks ago. A request from your own manager for figures by
Thursday. An anonymous note about a colleague's conduct. Three routine
approvals. A meeting invitation that clashes with the depot walk-through
you promised the new supervisor.

## The work

In forty minutes, alone: decide for each item whether you do it now,
schedule it, delegate it — with the decision attached — or decline it.
Write the reason in five words. Then answer the two hardest questions:
what will you not get to today, and what have you decided to accept as a
consequence?

## The debrief

1. Sort your decisions into the urgent/important grid from
   [[Managing Your Time]]. Where did your morning actually go?
2. The near-miss report has been unread for five days. Which quadrant is
   it in, and what does its position in your basket say about how the
   depot handles safety — see [[The Law at Work]]?
3. Nobody finishes fourteen items. Compare the things people chose to
   drop, and what that cost.
4. Take the one item you handled worst and re-do it with a strategy from
   [[Solving Management Problems]].

> [!warning] The trap is the easy ones
> Most people clear the three routine approvals first, because finishing
> something feels good. That is twenty minutes spent in the bottom-left
> box while the near-miss sits unread.
