One period, one organization, and a sequence of events delivered every
seven minutes. Teams run the control loop live and find out which part of
it they had not built.

## The setup

Your team manages a mid-sized food producer. You start with a plan, a
budget, staffing levels, and three published quality standards — all of
which you set in the first ten minutes, before you know what is coming.

Then the events arrive: a supplier delivery fails inspection; a machine
goes down; a customer complaint reaches social media; two staff call in
sick; a regulator asks for records.

## The rules

- Every decision is written on the team's sheet with the time it was
  made.
- Any decision that costs money comes out of the budget you set.
- When an event arrives, you have three minutes.

## The debrief

1. For each event, name which kind of control system would have caught it
   — feedforward, concurrent, or feedback. Use
   [[Kinds of Control System]]. Most teams discover they built only the
   third.
2. Look at your three standards. Did you compare anything against them
   during the hour, or did you abandon them at the first event? That is
   step three of [[The Control Process]], and it is the step that is
   usually skipped.
3. Which decisions did you make with no information, and what would you
   have needed?
4. Rebuild your first ten minutes. Given what you now know, what would
   you set up before the events start?

> [!tip] The scenario is a simulation, and that is the point
> Running a plan against invented conditions is a planning tool in its
> own right — see [[Planning Tools]]. It costs an hour here and nothing
> at all compared with finding out live.
