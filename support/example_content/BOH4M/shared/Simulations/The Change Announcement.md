You have five minutes in front of a room that is allowed to push back —
because in a real workplace it will.

## The situation

A 60-person manufacturer is moving from two fixed shifts to a rotating
three-shift pattern in eight weeks. The reason is real: a major customer
has moved to seven-day delivery, and without the change the contract goes
elsewhere and the plant loses a third of its volume.

Roughly a third of the workforce will be worse off — childcare, a second
job, a commute that only works at certain hours. Some will be better off.
Nobody is being laid off, and there is a small budget for accommodation.

## Your five minutes

You are the plant manager. Deliver the announcement, then take questions
for five minutes. Half the class plays the workforce, with briefs — some
supportive, some not, one with a genuine accommodation need, one who
heard a rumour last week that this means redundancies.

Prepare using the five points on [[Leading Change]].

## The debrief

1. In the first thirty seconds, did the room learn what is changing? Time
   it — most people take ninety.
2. Which acceptance strategies did the speaker use, and which did the
   situation actually call for?
3. What happened to the rumour? Was it answered, or ignored into
   growing?
4. Using [[Why Organizations Change]], sort the objections raised: which
   were fear of loss, which were disbelief, and which were "nobody asked
   me"? Each needs a different answer.
5. What did the speaker promise about next steps, and is it a promise
   they can keep?
