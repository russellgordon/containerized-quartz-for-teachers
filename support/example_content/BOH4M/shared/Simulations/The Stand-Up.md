A stand-up is a short, same-time-every-day meeting where a team says what
it is doing and what is in the way. It is the most common meeting format
in working life and the easiest one to run badly.

## The format

Standing, fifteen minutes maximum, each person answering three questions:

1. What did I finish since we last met?
2. What am I working on next?
3. What is blocking me, or about to?

Nothing else happens in the meeting. Anything that needs a discussion
gets named, and the people who need to be in it agree a time — before
everyone leaves the room.

## Running it in class

Your project team runs a real stand-up at the start of each working
period from now until the project is due. Rotate who facilitates. The
facilitator's job is to keep it to fifteen minutes and to make sure the
"who does what by when" is said out loud at the end.

> [!warning] The failure mode is a status report to the teacher
> If every answer is addressed to the person marking the work, the
> meeting has become a performance and stopped being useful. Answer each
> other. I will be listening, not receiving.

## What to notice

Blockers get reported late. Almost everyone waits a day longer than they
should, because saying you are stuck feels like admitting you are slow.
The team that fixes this — usually by having the facilitator ask "what
might block you tomorrow?" — finishes ahead of the one that does not.

That is [[Improving Communication]] in the only place it matters, which
is a real meeting with real work in it.
