You sit on a hiring panel, and you find out how hard a fair interview
actually is.

## The setup

The role: shift supervisor at a garden centre, 30 staff, seasonal peaks.
Three candidates, played by classmates from briefs. Panels of three:
each panel writes its questions and scoring criteria **before** meeting
any candidate.

## Before the interviews

Your panel must produce, on one page:

- The four or five things the job genuinely requires — not a wish list.
- One question per requirement, asked of every candidate identically.
- What a strong, adequate, and weak answer sounds like for each.

That page is the difference between a hiring decision and an impression.

## During

Twelve minutes per candidate. Every panel member scores independently and
does not speak about it until all three are finished.

## The debrief

1. Compare your independent scores. Where did panel members disagree
   most, and was it about the evidence or about the person?
2. Which questions produced answers you could actually score? Which
   produced pleasant conversation?
3. Did anyone ask something that touches a protected ground under
   [[The Law at Work]], even accidentally? What information were they
   actually after, and how should it have been asked?
4. Using [[The Human-Resource Process]], say what happens on day one for
   whoever you hired — and whether your organization is ready for them.

> [!warning] Deciding first and justifying afterwards
> Most panels reach a view in the first ninety seconds and spend the
> remaining ten minutes confirming it. Writing the criteria first is not
> bureaucracy; it is the only defence anyone has found against it.
