This site is the course: the schedule, the ideas, the work, and the
expectations behind all of it.

## The folders, and what each is for

| Folder | Holds | You will use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **All Classes** | One page per period — the agenda and what to do next | Every day |
| **Concepts** | The ideas, stated cleanly | After the class that met the idea |
| **Cases** | Written scenarios to analyse | In class, and again before the exam |
| **Simulations** | Role-plays: setup, rules, debrief | On the day, and to prepare |
| **Discussions** | Seminar questions and how to prepare | The night before a seminar |
| **Portfolios** | Your four portfolio entries | Four times, spread across the term |
| **Tasks** | Assessed work, with what earns the marks | Whenever something is due |
| **Tutorials** | How to do the things this course asks for | The first time you have to do one |
| **Setup** | How the class runs | In week one, and when you forget |
| **Style** | How the site works | Now |

## How the pieces connect

Class pages are the spine. Each one links to whatever that period
actually consists of, so if you want to know when something was used,
look at the backlinks at the bottom of the page.

Underneath the content pages you will find a **Curriculum connection** —
the Ministry expectations that page addresses, in the Ministry's own
words. Those are also gathered in the curriculum folder, and the built
site draws a coverage map from them, which is how you can see what the
course has actually taught.

## Two conventions worth knowing

- **Nothing here is a textbook chapter.** Concept pages assume you have
  already met the idea in class.
- **The newest class is always on the section landing page.** Start
  there.
