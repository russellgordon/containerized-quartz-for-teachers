A six-person product team at a Waterloo software company has stopped
raising problems in its Monday meeting. The meetings still happen; they
are short, agreeable, and finish early.

What has changed over four months:

- Two members now work from home three days a week; four are in the
  office.
- The team lead, Marcus, began replying to concerns with "good point,
  let's take that offline" — usually because the meeting was running
  long.
- A release slipped by two weeks. Three people knew about the underlying
  problem for at least a fortnight before it was raised.
- One member, Ana, raised the same risk twice in March and was told both
  times it was already handled. It was not.

Marcus's view: "They're a senior team. If something's wrong, they'll tell
me."

## Questions

1. Walk the message through [[The Communication Process]]. Where exactly
   did it break for Ana's risk — encoding, channel, decoding, or
   feedback?
2. Which barriers from that page are present here? Give the evidence for
   each.
3. "They'll tell me" is an assumption about silence. What does
   [[Improving Communication]] say about it, and what did the two weeks
   of unspoken knowledge cost?
4. Design the Monday meeting again. What changes about who speaks, in
   what order, and what happens to "let's take that offline"?
5. The remote/office split is a structural fact, not a personality one.
   What would you change about the arrangement, and what would it cost
   the people who work at home?
