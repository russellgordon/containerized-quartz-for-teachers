Northline Outfitters runs four outdoor-equipment stores in northern
Ontario and a small online shop. The founder's plan for the next three
years: eight new stores in southern Ontario, a distribution centre in
Barrie, and a doubling of the online range — funded by a bank loan
secured against the existing stores.

What is in the plan: revenue projections at 30 per cent annual growth;
store locations; a hiring schedule.

What is not: what happens if growth is 10 per cent; who will manage the
new stores; what the southern market's competitors charge; whether the
brand's northern reputation means anything in Mississauga; and the
current stores' margin, which has fallen for two years.

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

1. Classify the strategy using [[Types of Strategic Plan]]. What kind is
   it, and what is it giving up?
2. Which level of strategy is this — corporate, business, or functional?
   What decisions at the other levels does it silently assume?
3. Apply a SWOT honestly. Then say which single entry you would want
   evidence for before signing the loan — see
   [[Analysing a Strategy]].
4. Use [[Why Organizations Change]] to identify what is driving this
   change, and predict where resistance will come from inside the
   existing four stores.
5. You are the bank's analyst. Write the three questions you would ask
   the founder, and say what answer would make you say no.
