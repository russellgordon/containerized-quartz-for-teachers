Fieldnote, a 40-person Toronto design agency, removed all job titles
three years ago. Teams form around client projects, anyone can start one,
and there are no managers — the two founders describe themselves as
"available".

It worked well at 15 people. At 40:

- Two designers have left, both saying they could not tell how to get
  ahead or who decided.
- Client projects are staffed by whoever is asked first, and the same six
  people are asked constantly.
- A junior developer waited five weeks for a decision on a technical
  approach because there was no one whose decision it was.
- New hires take about four months to become productive, against six
  weeks at the agency's old size.
- Both founders are in every significant conversation, and the calendar
  shows it.

The founders' position: "Adding hierarchy would destroy what makes this
place work."

> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see

## Questions

1. Using [[Traditional Structures]] and [[Current Structures]], describe
   what Fieldnote actually has now — not what it says it has. Where does
   authority sit?
2. Explain the four-month onboarding using structure rather than the
   quality of the new hires.
3. The same six people are always asked. What does that tell you about
   how work is allocated, and who is disadvantaged by it?
4. Propose the smallest structural change that addresses the five-week
   decision and the departures, and say honestly what it costs.
5. Answer the founders' objection directly. Is it true?
