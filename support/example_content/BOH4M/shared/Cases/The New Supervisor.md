Priya has been a dispatcher at a regional courier company for four years
and was the fastest one on the floor. In September she was promoted to
supervise the eight dispatchers she used to sit with.

By November her manager notices three things. Dispatch errors are down —
Priya catches them herself, often by redoing the work at the end of a
shift. Two experienced dispatchers have stopped bringing her problems.
And Priya has missed both of the last two planning meetings with the
depot managers, because the floor was busy.

Priya's own account: "I know the work better than anyone here. If I let
it go wrong I'm the one who answers for it."

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

1. Which level of management is Priya working at, and which level is she
   being paid for? Use [[Levels of Management]] and be specific about the
   time horizon of the work she is choosing.
2. Priya's technical skill is the strongest on the team. Explain, using
   the levels, why that is now part of the problem rather than the
   solution.
3. What has her behaviour taught the two dispatchers who stopped raising
   problems?
4. Her manager plans to send her on a two-day time-management course.
   Using the schools in [[Schools of Management Thought]], say what
   assumption that solution makes and whether you agree with it.
5. Write the two sentences you would say to Priya first, if you were her
   manager.
