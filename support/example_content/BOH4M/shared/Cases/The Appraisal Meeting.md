Jordan manages a nine-person accounts team. It is appraisal week, and the
form is a five-point graphic rating scale on six traits: initiative,
accuracy, teamwork, communication, reliability, judgement.

Jordan's draft for one employee, Tomas: 3 out of 5 on everything except
accuracy, where he scored 4.

What Jordan knows, and did not write down at the time: Tomas rebuilt the
month-end reconciliation in October, cutting two days off the close; he
missed two deadlines in March while covering a colleague's leave; and
last week he was short with a junior in front of the team. Jordan wrote
the appraisal on Sunday night from memory.

The company attaches the appraisal to a merit increase. Tomas has told
two colleagues he expects a strong review.

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

1. Identify the rater errors visible in Jordan's draft, using
   [[Performance Appraisal]]. Which ones does "wrote it Sunday from
   memory" produce?
2. What does a 3 out of 5 for *judgement* tell Tomas about what to do
   differently? Answer honestly — that is the case against this
   instrument.
3. Compare this scale with a behaviourally anchored rating scale and with
   360° feedback for this situation. Use [[Appraisal Methods]] and
   recommend one, with reasons.
4. The appraisal is attached to pay. What does that do to the
   conversation Jordan is about to have, and what would you change?
5. Write the first three sentences of Jordan's meeting with Tomas, given
   what actually happened this year.
