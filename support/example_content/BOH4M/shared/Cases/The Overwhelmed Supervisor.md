Dev supervises the evening shift at a long-term care home. Since the
spring he has covered two vacant positions himself while the postings sit
unfilled, run the schedule, and taken the on-call phone every second
weekend.

The record over four months: seven shifts where the home ran one staff
member short; two medication administration errors, both caught; Dev's
own sick days up from one to six; and an incident in which Dev spoke
sharply to a personal support worker in front of a resident's family —
something his file shows no history of.

The home's response so far: a lunch-and-learn on resilience, a poster for
the employee assistance program, and an offer to enrol Dev in a
time-management workshop.

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

1. Sort the sources of stress here using [[Stress at Work]]. Which are
   about the job, the conditions, the people, and the organization?
2. Dev's error rate and the incident with the family are performance
   effects. Explain the shape of the relationship between pressure and
   performance, and say where Dev is on it.
3. Assess the home's three responses. For each, say what it changes about
   the source of the stress. Then say which one you would keep.
4. The vacancies have been posted for four months. Whose problem is that,
   and which management function — planning, organizing, leading,
   controlling — is failing?
5. You have one conversation and a small budget. What do you do first,
   and how do you know in six weeks whether it worked?
