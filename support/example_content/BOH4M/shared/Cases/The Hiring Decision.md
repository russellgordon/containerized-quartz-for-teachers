A municipal recreation department is hiring an aquatics supervisor. Three
candidates reach the final interview.

**Candidate A** — eight years' lifeguarding, two as head guard, no formal
management training. Asked during the interview whether she has young
children, because the panel wanted to know about weekend availability.

**Candidate B** — a recreation management diploma, one year as an
assistant supervisor at a private club. Strongest on paper; the panel
chair golfs with his current manager, who recommended him.

**Candidate C** — six years' experience, currently on a modified schedule
following a back injury, with a request for an accommodation reviewed
annually. Best interview of the three by the panel's own scoring sheet.

The panel's discussion, recorded in the notes: "A might not be able to do
weekends. C's situation could be complicated. B is a safe pair of hands."

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

1. Identify every legal problem in this process, using
   [[The Law at Work]]. Be specific about which one applies to which
   moment.
2. What should the panel have asked Candidate A instead, to get the
   information it actually needed?
3. "C's situation could be complicated" — explain the employer's duty
   here, and what the panel is required to consider before it can be a
   factor at all.
4. The panel chair knows B's manager. Name the perception effects at work
   using [[Perception and Decisions]], and say what a fair process would
   do about it.
5. Redesign the selection process so that the same panel, with the same
   people, would reach a defensible decision. Use
   [[The Human-Resource Process]].
