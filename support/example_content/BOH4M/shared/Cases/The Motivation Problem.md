A Hamilton call centre introduced a quarterly bonus eighteen months ago,
paid on calls handled per hour. Calls per hour rose 14 per cent in the
first quarter and have stayed there.

Also since the bonus: repeat calls about the same issue are up 22 per
cent. Customer satisfaction is down four points. Two of the three most
experienced agents have left, both citing "the numbers". The team's
informal practice of a senior agent sitting with a struggling new one has
stopped entirely.

The operations manager's proposal is a second bonus, for customer
satisfaction, on top of the first.

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

1. The bonus worked. Say precisely what it worked *on*, and use
   [[Motivation in Practice]] to explain the repeat-call number.
2. Why did senior agents stop helping new ones? Answer with the reward
   system, not with character.
3. What would Herzberg predict about the bonus? What would goal-setting
   theory predict? Where do they disagree — see
   [[Theories of Motivation]].
4. Assess the manager's proposal to add a second bonus. What behaviour
   would you expect from an agent optimising for both at once?
5. Recommend an alternative, and state how you would know within a
   quarter whether it was working — including the measure you would
   deliberately *not* use.
