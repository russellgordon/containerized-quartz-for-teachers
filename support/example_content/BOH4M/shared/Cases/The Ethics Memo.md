Corbeau Foods buys wild blueberries from a network of small harvesters in
northern Ontario. A supplier your company has used for eleven years has
been named in a complaint: harvesting crews were housed in accommodation
that failed inspection, and two crew members were paid below the minimum
wage through a subcontractor.

Corbeau's own audit last spring rated the supplier "compliant" — the
auditor visited in March, before the harvest crews arrived.

The choices in front of the management team:

- Terminate the contract immediately, and tell customers why.
- Suspend purchases pending an independent audit paid for by Corbeau.
- Continue buying, with a corrective action plan and quarterly checks.
- Say nothing publicly, fix it quietly, and re-audit next season.

Three facts complicate it. Corbeau is the supplier's largest customer,
and terminating would likely end the business and the seasonal jobs in a
community with few. A competitor has already been asked about this by a
journalist. And the harvest window is six weeks away.

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

1. Do a stakeholder analysis: who is affected, and what does each one
   want? Include people with no voice in the room.
2. Apply the three questions from [[Ethics at Work]] to each option.
3. Which option would you recommend, and what cost are you accepting by
   choosing it? Name the cost explicitly.
4. What does the March audit tell you about Corbeau's control system?
   Use [[Kinds of Control System]] — which kind failed, and which kind
   would have caught this?
5. Draft the four-line recommendation section of a memo to the VP of
   Operations, in the form set out in [[Writing for Business]].
