A mid-sized insurance firm merged two claims departments in January. The
new structure removed a supervisory layer: eleven adjusters now report to
one manager instead of two teams of five reporting to two supervisors.

By June: the manager's one-to-ones have moved from fortnightly to
"whenever there's a problem"; two adjusters have had files reassigned
after errors, one of whom had raised her training gap in February; and a
grievance has been filed alleging that discipline for identical late
filings was applied to one adjuster and not another.

The firm is also three months behind on the accessibility improvements it
committed to when the floors were combined, and one adjuster who uses a
screen reader has been working with a temporary arrangement since March.

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

1. Using [[Trends in Organizational Design]], name what the firm bought
   with this structure and what it paid.
2. The February training gap is now an error and a reassignment. Which
   part of the human-resource process failed, and which control system —
   see [[Kinds of Control System]] — should have caught it?
3. The grievance is about consistency. Explain, using
   [[Discipline and Control Measures]], why inconsistent discipline
   damages every other control in the organization.
4. The accessibility commitment is three months late. Read
   [[The Ground Rules of Change]] and say what the firm's obligation
   actually is, and who is accountable for it.
5. What would you change first, and what would you tell the eleven
   adjusters about it?
