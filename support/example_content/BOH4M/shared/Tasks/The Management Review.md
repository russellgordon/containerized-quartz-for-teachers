**The culminating task. Eight class periods in Unit 4, ending with the
hand-over.** Teams of three or four.

A real organization. A real management question. A review, delivered to
the people who could act on it.

## The engagement

Your team works with a genuine client — a school department, a student
club with a budget, a small business, a community organization, a
municipal service. The client agrees to two conversations and to receive
your recommendations. Your teacher will help you find one; some clients
are arranged before the unit starts.

The question is scoped with the client on day one and must be a
*management* question: how the work is organized, how people are
recruited or kept, how a job is designed, how performance is reviewed,
how a decision gets made.

## The eight days

| Day | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Scope: meet the client, agree the question |
| 2 | The brief: written scope, signed off by the client |
| 3 | Evidence: interviews, observation, documents |
| 4 | Analysis: the course's own tools, applied |
| 5 | Checkpoint conference with your teacher — bring findings, not slides |
| 6 | Recommendations drafted, with costs |
| 7 | Rehearsal, and a hostile question from another team |
| 8 | The hand-over: twelve minutes to the client, with questions |

## What you deliver

- **A written review** (about 1,500 words): question, method, findings,
  recommendations, and what each would cost.
- **The hand-over presentation**, twelve minutes, to the client.
- **A one-page implementation note** the client keeps.

## What the review must engage with

Your recommendations must address at least three of: the structure and
what it makes easy ([[Current Structures]],
[[Trends in Organizational Design]]), the design of a job
([[Designing a Job]]), how people are recruited or kept
([[The Human-Resource Process]]), how performance is reviewed
([[Performance Appraisal]]), and how the work is arranged
([[Where and How We Work]]).

Every recommendation is checked against [[The Law at Work]] and
[[The Ground Rules of Change]] before it goes to the client. A
recommendation that is illegal is not a recommendation.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Method | Evidence gathered systematically, and its limits admitted |
| Analysis | Course tools used where they fit, not sprinkled |
| Recommendations | Specific, costed, legal, and something this client could actually start |
| Communication | Twelve minutes that a busy person would find worth their time |
| Professionalism | The client is treated as a client — on time, prepared, discreet |

> [!warning] You are guests in someone's organization
> Anything you are told in confidence stays out of the report unless you
> have asked. If a recommendation would embarrass a named person, find
> the version that fixes the system instead. That judgement is part of
> the mark.
