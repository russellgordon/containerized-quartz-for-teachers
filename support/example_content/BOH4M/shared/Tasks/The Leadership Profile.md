**Due: the last day of Unit 2.** Individual. A written profile of about
1,200 words.

Choose a leader, argue what makes them effective or ineffective, and
defend the theory that best explains them.

## Choosing well

Choose someone whose decisions you can find: a Canadian business leader,
a not-for-profit director, a union leader, a municipal official, an
Indigenous business or governance leader, a sports coach with a
documented record. Living or dead, celebrated or not.

Avoid the leader everyone has already been told is great. A profile that
argues against the received view, with evidence, is the most interesting
thing you can hand in.

## What it must contain

1. **Three decisions**, described factually, with dates and sources.
2. **The characteristics** those decisions demonstrate — using
   [[Leadership Characteristics]], not adjectives.
3. **The style**, named and evidenced from
   [[Leadership Styles]], including what happened under pressure.
4. **The theory that explains them best**, argued against at least one
   alternative from [[Leadership Theories]]. This is the analytical
   centre of the task.
5. **Your assessment**: effective, and at what cost, and for whom.

## How it is marked

- **Argument** — a defended claim, not a biography.
- **Use of theory** — theories compared, not listed.
- **Evidence** — three real decisions, sourced.
- **Fairness** — you deal with the strongest evidence against your view.

> [!warning] A profile is not a tribute
> The word "inspiring" earns nothing here. What did they decide, who was
> affected, and would the same approach have worked in a different
> situation? That last question is contingency theory doing its job.
