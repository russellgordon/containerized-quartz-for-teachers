**Launched near the end of Unit 2, due early in Unit 3.** Individual.
About 900 words.

A diagnosis. You are handed a workplace where something is wrong with the
people, and asked what is actually causing it.

## The situation

Your teacher will give you one of three scenarios — a team with rising
turnover, a department where two groups have stopped cooperating, or a
high-performing employee whose work has fallen off. Each comes with data:
attendance, output, a survey extract, and two short interviews.

## What to produce

1. **The symptoms**, separated from the causes. List what you can
   actually observe.
2. **Three candidate explanations**, drawn from different parts of the
   course — motivation, conflict, job satisfaction, communication,
   workload.
3. **The evidence for and against each.** This is the marked centre of
   the task: a diagnosis that only argues for its favourite is not a
   diagnosis.
4. **Your conclusion**, with the one piece of evidence you would gather
   next if you could.
5. **What you would do on Monday**, and what you would measure in six
   weeks.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Diagnosis | Symptoms and causes kept apart |
| Use of theory | The right tools chosen from the whole unit, not the most recent one |
| Evidence | Claims tied to the data you were given |
| Judgement | A realistic first action, with a way of knowing whether it worked |

> [!warning] Do not reach for the incentive
> The most common answer to any people problem is "offer a bonus". Before
> you write that, apply the can't/won't test from
> [[Motivation in Practice]] and the conflict styles in
> [[Conflict Styles]] — most of these scenarios are not motivation
> problems at all.
