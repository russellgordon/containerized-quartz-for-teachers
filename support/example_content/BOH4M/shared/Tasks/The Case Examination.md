**The final evaluation. Three hours, in the examination period.**
Individual.

One unseen case, and the whole course.

## What it looks like

You are given a case of about 1,200 words describing an organization with
several problems — the kind you have been working on all term, with the
detail that makes it genuinely arguable. It will not tell you which unit
it belongs to.

You answer in three parts:

1. **Diagnosis** (about 45 minutes). What is actually wrong, separated
   from the symptoms, with the evidence from the case.
2. **Analysis** (about 75 minutes). Apply the tools the case calls for.
   Choosing well is the skill being examined; a paper that applies every
   framework in the course is a paper that could not decide.
3. **Recommendation** (about 45 minutes). What management should do,
   what it costs, what could go wrong, and how they would know within
   three months whether it is working.

The remaining time is for reading and planning, and you should use it —
the strongest papers spend twenty minutes before writing anything.

## What is being examined

| Criterion | What it means here |
| --- | --- |
| Diagnosis | You find the problem under the symptom, and say how you know |
| Selection | You choose the right tools for this case and justify the choice |
| Integration | You use more than one strand, because real organizations do |
| Judgement | The recommendation is specific, costed, and could be started on Monday |
| Communication | Structured, plain, and readable under time pressure |

## Preparing for it

The three review periods before the examination are built for this: we
work old cases against the clock and compare diagnoses. Bring your
worked cases from the term — [[The Flat Structure]] and
[[The Restructure]] are the closest in shape to what you will meet.

> [!tip] Practise the first twenty minutes
> Everyone can write for three hours. The difference between papers is
> almost always made in the first twenty minutes, deciding what the case
> is really about — which is exactly the skill in
> [[Solving Management Problems]].
