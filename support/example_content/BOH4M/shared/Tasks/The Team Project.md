**Launched in the second week of Unit 2; delivered on the last day of the
unit.** Teams of four.

Your team runs a small, real project for a real audience: an event, a
campaign, a service improvement, or a piece of work for a club or
community group in your school or town. It must involve other people who
are not in your team.

## The deliverables

| Deliverable | When | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Team charter | Two days after launch | How you decide, what "done" means, what happens when someone misses a commitment |
| Stand-up record | Every working period | Three lines per person; the facilitator rotates |
| The project itself | Last week of the unit | Delivered to its actual audience |
| Team review | With the project | Each member's account of what the team did well and badly |

## How it is marked

- **The charter, and whether you used it.** A charter written and ignored
  is worth less than a short one you actually invoked.
- **Process evidence** — the stand-up record, and what changed because of
  it.
- **The project's result**, judged by whether the audience got what was
  promised.
- **Communication within the team**, including how a disagreement was
  handled.

Individual marks can differ within a team. The stand-up record, the
charter, and the review are how that is decided — which is the answer to
social loafing described in [[What Makes a Team Work]].

> [!tip] Expect the storming stage
> Your team will have a bad week. Teams that named it in the charter
> handle it in a meeting; teams that did not handle it in the last
> forty-eight hours before the deadline. See [[How Groups Form]].
