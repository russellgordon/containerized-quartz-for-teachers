**Due: the presentation day in the second week of Unit 1.** Pairs.

You will study one real organization closely enough to say how it is put
together and how it is managed — and then say it out loud in eight
minutes.

## Choose an organization you can actually reach

A local business, a not-for-profit, a hospital department, a municipal
service, a farm co-operative, a school board department. It must be one
where you can get past the marketing: an interview, a site visit, an
annual report, or someone who works there and will talk to you.

Global brands are the tempting choice and usually the worse one. You will
find plenty written and nothing you can verify.

## What to produce

| Part | What it must contain |
| --- | --- |
| The structure | Purpose, division of labour, hierarchy of authority, with evidence |
| The levels | Who works at each level, and what their time horizon looks like |
| The theory | Which schools of management thought this organization's practices actually reflect |
| The evidence | Where each claim came from, named on the slide |
| The presentation | Eight minutes, both partners speaking, one clear recommendation at the end |

## How it is marked

- **Accuracy** — the structure you describe is the one that exists, not
  the one on the website.
- **Analysis** — you explain *why* it is arranged this way, and what it
  costs.
- **Evidence** — every claim traceable; an interview quoted properly.
- **Communication** — the eight minutes work as a presentation, using
  what is on [[Presenting to a Room]].

> [!warning] The org chart is not the organization
> Ask who actually decides. In most organizations at least one important
> decision is made by someone the chart does not put in charge of it, and
> finding that is worth more than a tidy diagram.

Build the deck with [[Building a Deck That Works]], and use
[[The Tools of the Trade]] to keep your evidence somewhere you can find
it again.
