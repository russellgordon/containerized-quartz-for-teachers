Management theory is only interesting when it is doing something. That is
what the business press is for in this course: a supply of live cases,
running while you study them.

## The habit

Once a week, find one story about an organization making a decision.
Not a story about a share price — a story about a *choice*. Then answer
three questions in your own notes:

1. What decision was made, and at what level?
2. Which idea from this course explains it best?
3. What would have to be true for the decision to be wrong?

The third question is the one that turns reading into thinking.

## Where to look, and what each source is good for

| Source | Good for | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A national business section | Canadian organizations you can actually visit | Short pieces that only report the number |
| A trade publication | The detail insiders argue about | Assumes you know the industry |
| A documentary | The long arc of a decision | Made with hindsight, which no manager had |
| A company's own release | What they want said | It is an argument, not a report |
| A regulator's filing | What they were required to say | Dense, and worth the trouble |

> [!warning] A press release is evidence of a claim, not of a fact
> "We are committed to sustainability" is evidence that a company wants
> to be seen that way. What they spent, hired, or stopped doing is
> evidence of the commitment. Keep the two apart, especially in
> [[The Responsible Company|the ethics work]].

Bring your story to class on the days the agenda says "headline" — we
open with them, and the best ones become
[[Cases/index|cases]] the whole class argues about.
