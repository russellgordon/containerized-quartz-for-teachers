Stress is not a character flaw and it is not always harmful. A deadline
that sharpens attention is stress doing its job. The kind that concerns a
manager is the sustained kind, which degrades judgement, health,
attendance, and eventually the work.

## Where it comes from

| Source | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| The job | Volume, pace, unclear expectations, responsibility without authority |
| The conditions | Noise, shifts, unsafe or inaccessible workplaces, commuting |
| The people | A difficult manager, unresolved conflict with co-workers, isolation |
| The organization | Restructuring, uncertainty about jobs, a promise that keeps moving |
| Outside work | Illness, care responsibilities, money — which do not stop at the door |

That last row matters because personal and work-related stress do not sit
in separate compartments. They add up in the same person, and the
performance effect is one effect, not two.

## What it does to performance

Performance rises with pressure and then falls — and the fall is not
gentle. The first things to go are the ones a manager most needs: the
willingness to raise a problem, attention to detail, and patience with
other people. Absence and turnover follow later, which is why they are a
lagging indicator and a poor early warning.

## What organizations actually do

| Response | Honest assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Wellness programs | Genuinely useful; not a substitute for fixing the workload |
| Time-management training | Helps when the problem is method, not volume |
| Flex-time and flexible schedules | One of the few that changes the source rather than the symptom |
| Mindfulness and meditation | Real benefit for some; resented when it arrives instead of staffing |
| Employee assistance programs | Important, confidential, and consistently under-used |

> [!warning] The test of a stress program
> Ask what it changes about the work. A program that teaches people to
> cope with an unreasonable workload, while leaving the workload
> unreasonable, has moved the problem onto the individual — which is
> exactly the argument in [[Whose Job Is Wellness?]].

The case [[The Overwhelmed Supervisor]] gives you a real one to diagnose.
