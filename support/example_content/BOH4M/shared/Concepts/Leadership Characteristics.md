Ask a room to describe a good leader and you get adjectives. Ask them to
describe a leader they actually followed, and you get behaviour — which
is the more useful list.

## What keeps appearing

| Characteristic | What it looks like on a Tuesday |
| --- | --- |
| Integrity | The version they tell you matches the version they tell everyone else |
| Drive | They keep going after the interesting part is over |
| Vision | They can say where this is going in a sentence you can repeat |
| Judgement | They are right often enough, and say so when they are not |
| Commitment to equity and diversity | Who gets heard, hired, and promoted changes because they are there |

That last row is not decoration. A leader's commitment to equity shows
up in decisions with names attached: who was invited, who was funded, who
was believed.

## Two traps

**The trait trap.** Lists of characteristics quietly suggest leaders are
born with them. Most of the behaviours above are practised, and the
research that started from traits moved on to behaviour and situation for
exactly that reason — you will meet that story in
[[Leadership Theories]].

**The charisma trap.** The most memorable leader in a room is not
reliably the most effective one. Some of the steadiest leaders you will
study are dull to watch and trusted absolutely.

> [!tip] Test any claim about leadership against a leader you know
> Not a famous one — a coach, a shift supervisor, a club president. Did
> the trait travel? Would it have worked in a different situation? That
> test is the whole seminar in
> [[What Makes a Leader Worth Following]].
