Business writing has one job: let a busy reader decide something. Every
convention below follows from that.

## The shape

**Recommendation first.** An essay builds to its conclusion; a memo opens
with it. The reader may stop after the first paragraph, and they should
still know what you want.

```
To:      the person who can decide
From:    you
Date:    when
Subject: the decision, not the topic

Recommendation — one or two sentences.
Why — the two or three reasons, strongest first.
What it costs — money, time, risk, and what you are giving up.
What happens next — who does what, by when.
```

"Subject: the decision, not the topic" is worth reading twice.
*Warehouse* is a topic. *Close the Dundas warehouse by March* is a
decision, and it is the only one of the two that gets read today.

## The vocabulary

Use the words the field uses — *stakeholder*, *turnover*, *span of
control*, *retrenchment* — and use them exactly. Precision is the point;
sounding impressive is not. If a plain word does the job, that is the
professional choice: *we will spend less on advertising* beats *we will
optimize marketing expenditure*, every time.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| It was decided that the policy would be revised | We revised the policy |
| Going forward | From March |
| Reach out to | Ask, call, email |
| Leverage our synergies | Say what you actually mean |

> [!tip] Read it aloud before you send it
> Anything you stumble over, your reader will stumble over too. This is
> the cheapest editing technique there is and almost nobody uses it.
