Culture is what happens when nobody is enforcing anything: what people do
by default, what they think is normal, what gets someone a reputation.

## Two levels

| Level | What it is | How to see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observable | Dress, language, meetings, stories told about the place, how offices are arranged | Watch for a day |
| Core | The beliefs and values underneath — what is really rewarded, what is unforgivable | Watch what happens when the two conflict |

The observable level is easy to change and easy to fake. The core level
changes slowly, mostly through what leaders actually reward and tolerate,
and it will beat any poster on the wall.

## Culture and strategy

A strategy that requires people to behave in ways the culture punishes
will not happen, and nobody will say so out loud. A firm whose culture
rewards individual heroics cannot execute a strategy that needs
cross-team cooperation — not because people are disloyal, but because
the reward system says otherwise.

That is why culture belongs in a strategy unit rather than a chapter of
its own. When a plan fails for no visible reason, culture is where to
look, and the useful question is not "what are our values?" but **"what
does this place actually reward?"**

> [!tip] Ask a newcomer, in their second week
> They can still see the culture; by month three they will have stopped
> noticing. "What surprised you here?" is the single most informative
> question a manager can ask, and it has a short shelf life.

Structure and culture push on each other constantly — the point of
[[Design and Culture]] — and we argue about who is responsible for
culture in [[Who Owns the Culture?]].
