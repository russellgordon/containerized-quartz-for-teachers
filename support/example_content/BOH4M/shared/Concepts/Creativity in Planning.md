Every plan is chosen from a set of options, and most plans are mediocre
because the set was. Creativity in management is not artistic flair — it
is the discipline of generating more options before choosing one.

## Why groups produce fewer ideas than they should

A meeting called to "brainstorm" typically produces the ideas of the two
most confident people, in the first four minutes, filtered by what they
think the manager wants. The fix is structural, not motivational:

1. **Work alone first.** Everyone writes three options privately before
   anyone speaks. This alone roughly doubles the range.
2. **Collect before you judge.** Every option goes up; criticism waits.
3. **Force variety.** Ask for the cheapest option, the fastest, the one
   that needs no permission, and the one you would choose with unlimited
   money.
4. **Then judge hard**, against criteria set in advance.

Individual creativity supplies range; group work supplies the combination
of ideas that nobody would have reached alone. You need both, and they
belong in that order.

> [!success]- Try it on something real (click to expand)
> Take a genuine problem in your school — the lunch queue, the lost-and-
> found, the club sign-up. Alone, three options in four minutes. In a
> group, combine them into a fourth nobody wrote. Then ask which of the
> four you would actually defend to the person who has to fund it.

> [!tip] Protect the strange idea for five extra minutes
> The idea that gets killed fastest is the unfamiliar one, and it is the
> only kind that can produce a plan your competitors do not already have.

Creativity has to survive contact with constraint, which is why this
comes right before [[Planning Tools]] rather than instead of it.
