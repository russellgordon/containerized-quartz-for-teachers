Human-resource decisions are made inside law. In Ontario a manager does
not need to be a lawyer, and does need to know when to stop and ask one.

## What governs the employment relationship

| Area | The rule, in outline | Where it bites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wages and hours | The *Employment Standards Act* sets minimums: wage, overtime, breaks, vacation, public holidays, notice of termination | Scheduling, unpaid overtime, "salaried so it doesn't count" |
| Employment equity and human rights | The *Human Rights Code* prohibits discrimination on protected grounds and requires accommodation to the point of undue hardship | Interview questions, promotion, scheduling around religious observance or disability |
| Health and safety | The *Occupational Health and Safety Act*: the right to know, to participate, and to refuse unsafe work | Training records, incident reporting, a changed workplace |
| Employee rights | Privacy, protection from reprisal, the right to organize | Monitoring, and what happens after a complaint |
| Bargaining agreements | Where a union is certified, the collective agreement governs and the *Labour Relations Act* applies | Scheduling, discipline, promotion by seniority |
| Accessibility | The *AODA* sets standards for workplaces, information, and hiring | Job postings, interviews, the physical and digital workplace |

## Why this is a management topic and not a legal one

Because the moment where it matters is a Tuesday conversation, not a
courtroom. An interviewer asks a friendly question about someone's
family. A schedule is built that quietly excludes anyone who observes a
sabbath. A worker mentions a hazard and is moved to a worse shift a week
later. None of those started as legal decisions, and all of them end as
legal problems.

> [!warning] The duty is on the employer, and it does not need intent
> Discrimination is judged by effect, not by whether anyone meant it. A
> requirement that is neutral on its face and excludes a protected group
> in practice still has to be justified.

This is why the human-resource process has so much structure in it —
which is the next page — and why every recommendation in
[[The Management Review]] is checked against this one.
