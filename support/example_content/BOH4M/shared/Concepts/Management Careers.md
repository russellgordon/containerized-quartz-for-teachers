Management is a career people arrive at, usually from somewhere else.
Knowing what the routes look like is worth an hour of your Grade 12 year.

## Where the openings are

| Field | Entry-level management role | What it usually asks for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retail and hospitality | Assistant or store manager | Experience, demonstrated reliability; a diploma or degree helps |
| Operations and logistics | Team lead, shift supervisor | Technical knowledge of the work, plus supply chain training |
| Health services | Clinical or program supervisor | The professional qualification first, management second |
| Not-for-profit | Program coordinator | Grant writing, volunteer coordination, a relevant degree |
| Business services | Analyst, then team lead | A business degree, or a college diploma plus results |
| Skilled trades | Foreperson, project manager | The certification, years on the tools, then project training |

## What the routes have in common

Almost nobody starts as a manager. The usual sequence is: do the work
well, take responsibility for a piece of it, then be trusted with people.
Which is why the most useful preparation in Grade 12 is not a management
course alone — it is being genuinely good at something, plus the
communication and analysis this course practises.

## What to check for yourself

- The **actual** requirement in current job postings, not the general
  advice: college diploma, degree, or specific certification.
- Whether the field expects further credentials later (a CPA, a PMP, a
  supervisory certificate) and who pays for them.
- What the first three years look like, from someone doing it — a real
  conversation beats any career site.

> [!tip] Research it the way you research a company
> Use current postings, professional association pages, and Ontario
> labour-market information, and note where each claim came from. A
> career plan built on a page with no date is a plan built on someone
> else's decade.

This is one of the deliverables of [[Your Leadership Statement]], where
you write the next three years down.
