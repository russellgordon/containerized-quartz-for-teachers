Strategies come in recognisable kinds. Naming the kind tells you what to
look for and what usually goes wrong.

| Type | The move | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growth | Get bigger — more locations, products, or markets | Growth funded by debt on optimistic forecasts |
| Retrenchment | Get smaller deliberately — close, sell, or exit | Cuts that remove the capability you will need to recover |
| E-business | Move the transaction, the channel, or the whole model online | Assuming the online version has the same costs and customers |
| Stability | Hold the position on purpose | Mistaking drift for a decision |
| Combination | Grow here, retrench there, at once | Two messages inside one organization |

## Reading a real one

Organizations rarely announce "this is retrenchment". They announce a
*refocus on core strengths*, and it is your job to notice that the core
just got smaller. Read what is being closed, sold, or quietly not
replaced.

E-business strategies deserve particular care in Canada, where
geography changes the arithmetic: shipping to a customer in Thunder Bay
is not the same business as shipping to one in Mississauga, and a plan
that ignores that is a plan for one of them.

> [!tip] Ask what this strategy gives up
> Every real strategy has a cost — a market not entered, a customer not
> served, a margin surrendered for volume. A strategy with no sacrifice
> in it has not been decided yet.

The case [[The Growth Plan]] gives you one to classify and challenge, and
you will name the type in your own [[The Strategic Review]].
