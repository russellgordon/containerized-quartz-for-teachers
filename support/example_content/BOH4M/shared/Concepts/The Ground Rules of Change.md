Change happens inside constraints that are not negotiable. A plan that
ignores them is not bold; it is incomplete.

## The challenges that arrive uninvited

| Challenge | What it does to a plan |
| --- | --- |
| New technology | Changes which skills are needed, and the timeline for retraining |
| The Internet's reach | A local decision becomes public, immediately, and permanently |
| Globalization | Competitors and suppliers are anywhere; so are customers |
| Shifting expectations | What employees will accept about hours and location has changed |

## The legal considerations

In Ontario these are duties, not preferences, and a manager who does not
know them is a liability to their employer:

- **Occupational health and safety.** Workplace safety standards, the
  right to refuse unsafe work, and the duty to keep a changed workplace
  safe — including one that changed last week.
- **Accessibility.** The *Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities
  Act* sets requirements for how workplaces, information, and hiring
  work; a redesign that quietly removes accessible arrangements is not
  a neutral change.
- **Pay equity.** Equal pay for work of equal value is law, and a
  restructure that recreates the same old pattern of who is paid what
  will be examined on exactly that basis.
- **Employment standards and human rights.** Notice, hours, leave, and
  the duty to accommodate, all of which constrain how fast a change can
  legally happen.

> [!warning] "We didn't think about it" is not a defence
> These obligations apply whether or not the plan mentioned them. Build
> them into the plan at the start, where they are cheap, rather than at
> the tribunal, where they are not.

Every recommendation you make in [[The Management Review]] must survive
this page — and we say so on the task, because a recommendation that is
illegal is not a recommendation.
