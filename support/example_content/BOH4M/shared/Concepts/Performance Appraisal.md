An appraisal is the organization's formal answer to a question the
employee has been asking all year: *how am I doing?* Its importance is
out of proportion to the hour it takes, because pay, promotion,
development, and sometimes discipline all attach to it.

## What it is for

| Purpose | The decision it feeds |
| --- | --- |
| Development | What training, coaching, or new responsibility comes next |
| Reward | Raises, bonuses, promotion |
| Documentation | A record when performance has to be managed formally |
| Alignment | Whether this person's goals still match where the organization is going |

Those purposes pull against each other. A conversation meant to develop
someone is a different conversation from one that decides their raise,
and holding both at once teaches people to defend rather than reflect —
which is why many organizations separate them by months.

## What makes one worth having

- **No surprises.** Anything in the annual review should have been said
  when it happened. An appraisal is a summary, not a reveal.
- **Evidence, not impressions.** Specific work, specific dates. Otherwise
  you are appraising your memory, which is where every bias in
  [[Perception and Decisions]] lives.
- **The employee speaks first.** Ask for their assessment before giving
  yours; you will learn what they already know and where the real gap is.
- **It ends with something to do.** A goal, a resource, a date.

> [!warning] Recency, halo, and leniency
> Managers over-weight the last six weeks, let one strong trait colour
> everything, and drift towards rating everyone "meets expectations" to
> avoid conflict. All three make an appraisal useless, and all three are
> reduced by notes taken through the year rather than the night before.

[[Appraisal Methods]] covers the instruments; you will run the
conversation itself in [[The Performance Conversation]].
