A consumer DNA test tells you about your ancestry, your risk of some
conditions, and — unavoidably — about relatives who never took a test.

## The biology you need first

You share about 50% of your variable DNA with a parent or sibling, 25%
with a grandparent, and about 12.5% with a first cousin. That is why a
database containing 2% of a population can identify most of it.

## The cases

- Police have solved decades-old crimes using genealogy databases and a
  distant cousin's upload.
- Insurers in some jurisdictions may use genetic information; in Canada,
  the Genetic Non-Discrimination Act restricts this.
- Ancestry results have revealed non-paternity, adoptions, and siblings —
  routinely, and without warning.

## The question

When you upload your genome, whose information are you giving away? And
does consent from one person cover a family?
