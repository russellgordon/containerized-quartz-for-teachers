Conservation costs money and land. Deciding what to protect means
deciding what matters, and biology alone cannot do that.

## The biology you need first

Extinction rates are currently estimated at 100 to 1000 times the
background rate from the fossil record. Ecosystem function depends on
interactions, not on species counted individually — see
[[Biodiversity and Why It Matters]].

## The question

A wetland can be protected or drained for housing. Both are real needs.
What would you need to know to decide, and which of those things is
biology able to tell you?

## Positions worth taking seriously

- Species have value in themselves, independent of use.
- We protect ecosystems because we depend on them, and the argument
  should be honest about that.
- Triage is unavoidable: with limited money, some species will be let go.

The third one is uncomfortable and is what conservation agencies actually
do. Argue with it rather than around it.
