Work from the pedigrees handed out in class; the reasoning is here.

**1.** Two unaffected parents have an affected child. What does that rule
out?

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Dominant inheritance. An affected child from unaffected parents means
> the allele was carried silently — so it is recessive.

**2.** An affected father has all affected daughters and no affected sons.
Which pattern?

> [!success]- Answer 2
> X-linked dominant. He gives his X to every daughter and his Y to every
> son.

**3.** A trait appears in every generation and affects both sexes about
equally. Which pattern is most likely?

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Autosomal dominant. Appearing in every generation argues against
> recessive; equal sexes argues against sex linkage.

**4.** Why can a pedigree never prove an inheritance pattern outright?

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Because families are small. A pattern consistent with two modes stays
> consistent with both until more data — or a genetic test — settles it.
