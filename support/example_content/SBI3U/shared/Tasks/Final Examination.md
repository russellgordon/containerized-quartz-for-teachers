> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · three hours, in the examination period · written, with a
> calculator · all five strands, weighted as the course spent its time

## What it is for

Everything else this semester was done with a partner, a bench, and time
to look things up. This is the one piece of evidence that is
unambiguously yours — and the closest thing in this course to how SBI4U
and first-year biology will assess you.

## What is on it

| Part | Roughly | What it asks you to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Diversity | 15% | Taxonomic rank and phylogeny; compare prokaryotes, eukaryotes, and viruses; use and criticise a key |
| B. Evolution | 20% | The lines of evidence; selection acting on variation; adaptation and speciation, explained mechanically |
| C. Genetic processes | 25% | Mitosis and meiosis; monohybrid and dihybrid crosses; pedigrees; non-Mendelian inheritance; what a mutation does to a protein |
| D. Animals | 20% | Structure and function across systems; homeostasis and feedback; tracing one failure through two systems |
| E. Plants | 20% | Structure, photosynthesis, transport, reproduction; succession and biodiversity |

Woven through every part: **the skills strand**. One question gives you
data you have not seen and asks you to process it, state a conclusion,
and say what its uncertainty allows you to claim. Another asks for a
labelled biological drawing. A third asks about a career or a
researcher's contribution.

## What to expect, precisely

- **Explain, do not only name.** "Natural selection happened" earns
  little; naming the variation, the pressure, and the differential
  survival earns full marks.
- **Diagrams are marked** on the conventions in [[Biological Drawing]]:
  ruled label lines that do not cross, a title, a magnification where
  it applies.
- **Genetics questions want the working.** The Punnett square, the
  gametes, and the ratio — an answer with no grid earns almost nothing.
- **The data question is not a memory question.** It rewards reading
  carefully, and the marks are in the reasoning rather than the
  arithmetic.
- **Terminology is expected to be exact**: systolic and diastolic,
  homologous and analogous, genotype and phenotype, xylem and phloem.

## How to prepare

1. **Redo questions, do not reread them.** Five from each set, cold:
   [[Classification Practice]], [[Evolution Practice]],
   [[Genetics Practice]], [[Pedigree Practice]],
   [[Animal Systems Practice]], [[Plants Practice]].
2. **Redraw the diagrams from memory** — a cell, a heart, a leaf
   cross-section, a pedigree, a feedback loop. If you cannot draw it,
   you cannot yet explain it.
3. **Re-read your own investigations.** Your [[Biology Journal]] entries
   about what went wrong at the bench are the most efficient revision
   there is, because you wrote them at the moment of understanding.
4. **Say the explanations out loud.** Every long-answer mark on this
   paper is a sentence you could have said to somebody.
5. **Bring questions to the review classes.** This page is what is on
   the examination.

> [!tip] In the three hours
> Budget by the weightings above. Genetics is the largest part and the
> most calculation-heavy — do it while you are fresh. If an explanation
> stalls, write what you DO know about the mechanism; partial
> explanations earn partial marks and a blank space earns none.

## How this is assessed

Against the same expectations as everything else. Per [[How Marks Work]],
this examination is part of the final 30% of the course mark alongside
[[Plant Growth Investigation]], so that neither one afternoon nor one
investigation decides your grade alone.
