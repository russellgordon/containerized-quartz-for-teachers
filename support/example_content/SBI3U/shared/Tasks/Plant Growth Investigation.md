> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Format:** a designed investigation run over three weeks, plus a full
> report. **Groups of three.** **This is the culminating task.**
> **Assessed on:** all four categories.

Design and run an investigation into something that affects plant growth.
You choose the question; I approve the procedure before anything is
planted.

## The rules

- **One manipulated variable.** Everything else controlled, and the
  controls listed.
- **At least three levels** of that variable, plus a control group.
- **At least five plants per group** — biology varies, and three is not
  enough to see past it.
- **A measurable response**: height, mass, leaf area, germination rate.
  Say how you will measure it before you start.

## Questions that have worked

Light colour and stem elongation · salt concentration and germination ·
crowding and final height · soil pH and leaf area · day length and
flowering.

## The report

Purpose, hypothesis with reasoning, method, observations (a table and a
graph), analysis, conclusion, and a section on what you would change —
see [[Writing a Lab Report]].

## Why this plant matters

One page, researched and sourced, alongside the experiment:

- **In Canada.** What role does this plant — or its crop family — play in
  Canadian agriculture, food supply, or economy? Real figures, from
  Statistics Canada, Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, or a producers'
  association.
- **In a culture.** How has a society or culture used this plant, or one
  like it — as food, medicine, material, or in ceremony? If you write
  about an Indigenous use, use sources from that nation or community
  wherever you can, name them, and present the knowledge as theirs.
- **What your experiment implies.** If your variable matters to growth,
  what would that mean for somebody growing it at scale?

[[Plants and Agriculture]] is the starting point and not the finish;
[[Finding and Judging Sources]] is how you judge what you find.

## Why three weeks

Because that is how long the biology takes. Starting on time is part of
the assessment, and the plants do not care about your other deadlines.
