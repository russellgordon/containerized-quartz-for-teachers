A preserved sheep or pig heart, which is close enough to a human one that
the differences are worth discussing.

## Safety

> [!danger] Preserved specimens
> Gloves and apron, ventilation on, scalpel cutting away from you and from
> your partners. Hands washed afterwards even though you wore gloves.
> Specimens go in the designated container, never the bin.

## Method

1. Examine the outside: identify the atria, ventricles, coronary vessels,
   and the great vessels.
2. Feel the difference in wall thickness between left and right ventricle
   from the outside before cutting.
3. Open the heart along the frontal plane. Identify the four chambers, the
   atrioventricular valves, and the semilunar valves.
4. Trace the path of blood with a probe, saying it aloud as you go.

## Analysis

1. Measure both ventricle walls. Express the difference as a ratio and
   explain it with the two circuits from [[The Circulatory System]].
2. Valves are one-way. Describe what you observed that shows this.
3. The coronary arteries supply the heart itself. Why can a muscle
   surrounded by blood not simply absorb what it needs?
