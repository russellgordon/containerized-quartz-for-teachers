> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** half a class. **Individually.** Handled with care — these are
> real conditions affecting real people.

Cut-and-arrange karyotypes from anonymised images: three typical, three
with an abnormality.

## Method

1. Sort the chromosomes by size and banding pattern into 22 numbered pairs
   plus the sex chromosomes.
2. Record the total count and the sex chromosome pair.
3. Identify any deviation from 46, XX or 46, XY.

## Analysis

1. Which of your karyotypes shows non-disjunction? Explain, using
   [[Meiosis]], how it arose.
2. Non-disjunction of chromosome 21 is survivable; of chromosome 1 is not.
   Suggest why, given that chromosome 1 carries far more genes.
3. A karyotype shows chromosome number and gross structure and nothing
   else. Name two genetic conditions it could NOT detect.

> [!note] On language
> These are conditions people live with. We use the medical name, we do
> not use "abnormal" about a person, and jokes are not part of this
> class.
