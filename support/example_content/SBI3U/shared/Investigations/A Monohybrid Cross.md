> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class of counting, after a fortnight of growing.
> **Groups of three.** Full analysis with a chi-square test.

Genetic corn cobs, or fast plants, or a simulated fruit fly cross — the
principle is the same: predict a ratio, then count enough offspring to
test it honestly.

## Method

1. State the expected ratio from the cross, with your reasoning.
2. Count every offspring. Every one — selective counting is how ratios get
   confirmed that were never true.
3. Record observed numbers against expected numbers.

## Analysis

1. Compute the observed ratio. How close is it to 3:1?
2. Apply a chi-square test and state whether the deviation is within what
   chance would produce.
3. Explain why counting 40 offspring and counting 400 give different
   confidence in the same ratio.
4. Design the cross you would run next to determine whether an individual
   with the dominant phenotype is homozygous. (This is a test cross, and
   you should be able to invent it.)
