> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Pairs.** Rates compared across the class.

A potometer measures how fast a shoot takes up water, which is very nearly
how fast it loses it.

## Safety

Cut stems under water, with the scalpel away from you. Glassware is
fragile and the assembly is awkward — assemble it on the bench, not in
mid-air.

## Method

1. Assemble the potometer with a leafy shoot, all joints sealed.
2. Record the bubble's movement over five minutes in still air. Repeat
   three times.
3. Change ONE condition and repeat: a fan (wind), a lamp (light), a bag
   over the leaves (humidity), or leaves partly removed (surface area).
4. Pool the class's results so every condition is covered.

## Analysis

1. Rank the conditions by their effect on transpiration rate.
2. Explain each using stomata and the water-loss trade-off from
   [[Transport in Plants]].
3. Your three repeats will not agree exactly. Say by how much, and why —
   see [[Uncertainty and Error]].
4. A potometer measures UPTAKE, not loss. Under what circumstances would
   the two differ, and does that undermine your conclusion?
