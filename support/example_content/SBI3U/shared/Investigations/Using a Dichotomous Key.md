> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Pairs.** Nothing to hand in; the key you build is
> used again in Unit 5.

A key is a series of either/or choices that ends at a name. Using one is
straightforward; building one teaches you what classification actually
requires.

## Part 1 — Use one

Identify eight leaf specimens with the key provided. Record the path of
choices you took for each, not just the answer.

## Part 2 — Build one

Take ten objects (I will supply a box of assorted seeds, shells, or
fasteners) and write a key that separates all ten. Then swap with another
pair and use theirs.

## What you will discover

- Characteristics must be **observable without special equipment** and
  **not continuous**. "Large" fails; "longer than wide" works.
- The first choice matters most: a bad first split makes every later step
  harder.
- Your key works perfectly for the ten objects you had, and breaks the
  moment an eleventh arrives — which is exactly the problem taxonomists
  have.
