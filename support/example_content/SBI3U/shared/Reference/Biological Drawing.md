A biological drawing is a record of what you observed, not an artwork and
not a copy of a textbook figure.

## The rules

1. **Pencil**, sharp, single clear lines. No shading, no sketchy strokes.
2. **Large enough** to show detail — half a page per drawing.
3. **Title**: what it is, and the magnification or scale.
4. **A scale bar** wherever you can estimate one.
5. **Labels** with straight horizontal lines that do not cross, ending on
   the structure, not in it.

## What is being assessed

Accuracy of proportion and detail, not artistic quality. A wobbly drawing
of what was actually in the field of view scores well; a beautiful drawing
copied from the textbook scores nothing.

## The most common error

Drawing what you know is there rather than what you can see. If the cell
walls are clear and the nuclei are not, draw the walls and note that the
nuclei were not visible at that magnification.
