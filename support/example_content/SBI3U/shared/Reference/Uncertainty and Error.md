Every measurement is a range, not a number. Saying so is not hedging — it
is the difference between a result and a guess.

## Where uncertainty comes from

- **The instrument.** A metre stick marked in millimetres gives you about
  $\pm 0.5\ \text{mm}$ at best.
- **You.** Reaction time on a stopwatch is roughly
  $\pm 0.2\ \text{s}$, and it does not get better with practice as much
  as people think.
- **The organism.** Biological variation is real variation, not error —
  which is why five plants per group beats one, and why
  [[Plant Growth Investigation]] insists on it.

## Reducing it without buying anything

Time ten swings and divide by ten. Your $\pm 0.2\ \text{s}$ becomes
$\pm 0.02\ \text{s}$ per swing, for free, because the reaction error is
spread across ten periods rather than one. This trick runs through half
the investigations in this course.

## Error is not the same as mistake

A mistake is reading the wrong scale. Error is the unavoidable spread in a
careful measurement. A conclusion that blames "human error" for everything
is not analysing anything — say WHICH quantity was uncertain, by how much,
and which way it would push the result.

> [!tip] The sentence a good conclusion contains
> "Our value was $9.4\ \text{m/s}^2$ against the accepted
> $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, a difference of 4%, which is about what the timing
> uncertainty alone would produce."
