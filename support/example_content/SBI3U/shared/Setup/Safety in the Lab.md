Biology labs involve sharp things, hot things, stains that do not wash
out, and material that was recently alive.

## Before every investigation

- [ ] Read the whole procedure, including the disposal instructions.
- [ ] Know where the eyewash, the sharps container, and the biohazard bin
      are.
- [ ] Lab coat or apron for anything involving stains or preserved
      specimens.
- [ ] Gloves for preserved material; goggles whenever a stain or a
      chemical is open.

## The four that matter most here

| Hazard | Where it shows up | What we do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scalpels and slides | Dissection, microscopy | Cut away from yourself; broken glass goes in the sharps bin, never the bin |
| Stains | Microscopy | Methylene blue and iodine stain skin and clothes permanently; gloves and apron |
| Preserved specimens | Dissection | Gloves and ventilation; wash hands after, even through gloves |
| Microorganisms | Culture work | Nothing is opened once sealed; everything is autoclaved before disposal |

## Living things

Anything alive in this room is treated with more care than the
equipment — see [[Working with Living Things]]. That is an ethical rule
before it is a safety one.

> [!warning] The rule with no exceptions
> Nothing goes in a mouth, nothing goes home, and no culture plate is
> opened once it has been sealed. Not for a photograph, not to smell it,
> not because it looks interesting.
