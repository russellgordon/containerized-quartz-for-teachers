Biology is the science of things that are complicated on purpose. Nothing
in this course reduces to one equation, and that is not a flaw in the
subject — it is the subject.

## The shape of a class

1. **Something living, or a picture of it.** A specimen, a slide, a data
   set from a real study.
2. **Observe before naming.** You describe what you see; the terminology
   arrives afterwards, attached to something you have already noticed.
3. **Name it, and connect it.** Every idea here connects to at least two
   others, and the connections are what the exams actually test.
4. **Practise.** See [[Exercises/index|Exercises]].

## The vocabulary problem, honestly

This course has roughly six hundred terms. You will not memorise them by
staring; you will learn them by using them. That is why the
[[Biology Journal]] asks you to write in your own words every week.

## The lab

We work with living and preserved material, and with microscopes. Read
[[Safety in the Lab]] before the first investigation, and read
[[Working with Living Things]] before we handle anything that is alive.

## Getting unstuck

"I expected X and got Y" works here too — with the addition of "and here
is the diagram". See [[Getting Help]], and come to [[Help Sessions]].
