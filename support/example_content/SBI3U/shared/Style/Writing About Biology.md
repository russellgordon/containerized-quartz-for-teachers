Biology writing lives or dies on precision of vocabulary, because the
words carry the meaning that equations carry in physics.

## Use the term, then use it consistently

If you call it a gamete, keep calling it a gamete. Alternating between
"sex cell", "gamete", and "sperm or egg" reads as uncertainty even when it
is not.

## Structure and function, in that order

The strongest sentences in biology connect the two: "The alveolus is one
cell thick, which makes the diffusion distance short enough for gas
exchange to keep up with demand." Structure, then consequence.

## Avoid teleology

Plants do not "want" light and bacteria do not "try" to resist
antibiotics. Write "individuals with the allele survived more often",
which is both correct and no longer.

## Words we use precisely

| Word | Means here | Does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theory | A well-supported explanatory framework | A guess |
| Adaptation | A heritable trait shaped by selection | Adjusting during your life |
| Fitness | Reproductive success | Health or strength |
| Gene | A sequence coding for a product | A trait |
| Random | Not directed toward need | Uncaused, or equally likely |

> [!tip] The test for a good sentence
> Could a classmate draw a diagram from your sentence? If not, it is
> probably naming things rather than explaining them.
