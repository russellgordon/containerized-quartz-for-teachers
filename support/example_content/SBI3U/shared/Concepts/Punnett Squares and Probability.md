A Punnett square is a bookkeeping device for the probabilities that
meiosis produces. It is not magic and it is not a prediction about any
individual child.

## Monohybrid

Cross two heterozygotes ($Aa \times Aa$) and the offspring are expected
in a 3:1 phenotype ratio, 1:2:1 by genotype.

## Dihybrid

Two genes, independently assorting, gives the 9:3:3:1 ratio — but only
when the genes are on different chromosomes, which is exactly the
condition Mendel got lucky with.

## The part students get wrong

"A 1 in 4 chance" does not mean one child in every four. Each conception
is an independent event, and four children can all be affected. The ratio
is what you approach with large numbers, which is why fruit fly crosses
count hundreds of offspring — see [[A Monohybrid Cross]].

## Test cross

To find whether a dominant-phenotype individual is homozygous or
heterozygous, cross it with a homozygous recessive. Any recessive
offspring settles it. Simple, cheap, and still used in plant breeding
today.
