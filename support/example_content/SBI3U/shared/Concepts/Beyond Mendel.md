Most traits do not follow the neat dominant-recessive pattern, and the
exceptions are where the interesting biology lives.

## Incomplete dominance

The heterozygote is intermediate — snapdragons with red and white parents
give pink. The alleles are not blending; both are present and one produces
less pigment.

## Codominance

Both alleles are fully expressed. Human blood type AB is the standard
example: not a blend, both antigens present.

## Multiple alleles

A gene can have more than two versions in a population, even though each
individual carries only two. ABO blood type again: three alleles, six
genotypes, four phenotypes.

## Sex linkage

Genes on the X chromosome show a distinctive inheritance pattern: males,
with one X, express whatever allele they have. Red-green colour vision
deficiency and haemophilia both follow it, which is why both are far more
common in males.

## Polygenic traits

Height, skin colour, and most things that matter are influenced by many
genes plus the environment. They give continuous variation rather than
categories — and they are why "the gene for" is almost always a
misleading phrase.
