One cell becomes two genetically identical cells. It is how you grew, how
you heal, and — when its controls fail — how a tumour forms.

## The phases

| Phase | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Prophase | Chromosomes condense; nuclear membrane breaks down |
| Metaphase | Chromosomes line up on the equator |
| Anaphase | Sister chromatids are pulled apart |
| Telophase | Nuclear membranes reform; chromosomes decondense |

Cytokinesis then divides the cytoplasm — by a pinching furrow in animals,
by a cell plate in plants.

## The point to hold onto

Every daughter cell gets a complete, identical set. Compare that with
[[Meiosis]], which does the opposite on purpose.

## When control fails

The cell cycle has checkpoints. Cancer is what happens when the genes
governing those checkpoints are damaged — which is why the same word
covers hundreds of different diseases with a shared mechanism.
