DNA is a double helix of two antiparallel strands. Each strand is a
backbone of sugar and phosphate with bases hanging inward: adenine pairs
with thymine, guanine with cytosine.

## Why the pairing rule matters so much

Because it makes the molecule self-copying. Separate the strands and each
one specifies its partner exactly. Watson and Crick's paper noted this in
a single famously understated sentence, and it is the whole basis of
inheritance.

## Replication, briefly

Helicase unwinds; DNA polymerase builds new strands using each old one as
a template; the result is two molecules, each with one old strand and one
new — semi-conservative replication, confirmed by the Meselson–Stahl
experiment.

## Where errors come in

Polymerase proofreads, but not perfectly. The mistakes that survive are
mutations: most are neutral, some are harmful, a few are the raw material
of [[Natural Selection]]. Without replication errors there would be no
variation and no evolution — the imperfection is load-bearing.
