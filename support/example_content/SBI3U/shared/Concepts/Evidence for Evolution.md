Evolution is not supported by one line of evidence but by several
independent ones that agree — which is a much stronger position than any
single proof would be.

## The lines

| Evidence | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Fossils | Change over time, in a consistent order, dated independently |
| Comparative anatomy | Homologous structures: the same bones, different jobs |
| Embryology | Shared developmental patterns among related groups |
| Biogeography | Species distributions match continental history |
| Molecular biology | DNA and protein similarity tracks anatomy |
| Observed change | Antibiotic resistance, pesticide resistance, within human lifetimes |

## Homologous versus analogous

A bat's wing and a whale's flipper have the SAME bones in the same
arrangement — homologous, evidence of shared ancestry. A bat's wing and an
insect's wing share a job and nothing structural — analogous, evidence of
convergent evolution. Telling them apart is the skill of
[[Comparative Anatomy]].

## The strongest argument

Independent methods agreeing. The molecular tree of relatedness was built
without reference to the fossil record, and it matches it. Either that is
a very large coincidence, or the two are recording the same history.
