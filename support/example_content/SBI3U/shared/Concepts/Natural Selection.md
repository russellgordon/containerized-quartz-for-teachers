Darwin's mechanism is four observations and one conclusion, and it is
worth stating in that order.

1. Organisms produce more offspring than can survive.
2. Individuals in a population vary.
3. Some of that variation is heritable.
4. Some variants survive and reproduce more than others.

**Therefore** the heritable variants that helped will be more common in
the next generation.

## What it is not

- It is not "survival of the fittest" if "fittest" means strongest.
  Fitness means reproductive success, and it is often about avoiding
  notice, or being tolerable to a pollinator.
- It is not directed. Nothing tries to evolve. Mutation is random with
  respect to need; selection is not random at all, which is the part
  people miss.
- It does not act on individuals. Individuals do not evolve; populations
  do.

## The classic case, carefully

Peppered moths in industrial Britain: the dark form became common where
soot darkened the trees, and rare again after clean air legislation. The
story has been challenged and re-tested repeatedly, and it survived —
which makes it a better example than the tidy version usually told.
