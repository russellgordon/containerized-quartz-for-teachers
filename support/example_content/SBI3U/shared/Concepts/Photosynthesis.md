$$\ce{6CO2 + 6H2O ->[light] C6H12O6 + 6O2}$$

Light energy becomes chemical energy stored in sugar. Nearly every food
chain on the planet starts here, and so did the oxygen in the atmosphere.

## Two stages

**Light-dependent reactions**, in the thylakoid membranes: light splits
water, releasing oxygen as a by-product and producing ATP and NADPH.

**The Calvin cycle**, in the stroma: those products are spent fixing
carbon dioxide into sugar. It needs no light directly, which is why
calling it the "dark reaction" is misleading — it runs in daylight, using
what the light reactions just made.

## What limits it

Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature. Whichever
is scarcest sets the rate — the limiting factor idea, which
[[Measuring Photosynthesis]] tests by changing one at a time.

## The oxygen is a by-product

It comes from splitting water, not from the carbon dioxide. That was
settled with isotope labelling, and it is the kind of question that only
an experiment can answer.
