A tube from mouth to anus, plus glands that empty into it. Food inside
that tube is technically still outside the body — it is not in you until
it crosses a membrane.

## Mechanical and chemical

Chewing and churning increase surface area; enzymes do the chemistry. Both
are needed, and the mechanical part exists to make the chemical part fast
enough.

| Region | Enzyme or secretion | Acts on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mouth | Salivary amylase | Starch |
| Stomach | Pepsin, HCl | Protein |
| Small intestine | Pancreatic enzymes, bile | All three, plus fat emulsification |

## Absorption

The small intestine is folded, villus-covered, and microvillus-covered on
top of that — roughly 250 m² of surface in a few metres of tube. The
surface-area principle from [[Animal Systems Overview]], executed three
times over.

## The large intestine

Water reabsorption, and a microbial community that synthesises vitamin K
and some B vitamins. That community is increasingly understood as an organ
in its own right.
