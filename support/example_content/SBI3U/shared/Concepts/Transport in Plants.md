A tall tree lifts water a hundred metres with no pump. How is a better
question than it first appears.

## Water goes up the xylem

**Transpiration pull:** water evaporates from the leaves, and because
water molecules are cohesive — hydrogen-bonded to each other — the column
is pulled up behind it, unbroken, all the way from the roots. Adhesion to
the xylem walls helps.

The whole system is driven by evaporation and costs the plant no energy
directly. It costs it water, which is the trade the stomata manage.

## Sugars move through the phloem

From source (a photosynthesising leaf) to sink (a root, a fruit, a growing
tip), by pressure flow. Unlike xylem, phloem transport is active and can
run in either direction depending on the season.

## Stomata

Guard cells open and close the pores, trading carbon dioxide entry against
water loss. They close in drought, at night, and in high heat — which is
why a plant in strong sun can stop photosynthesising for reasons that have
nothing to do with light. [[Transpiration]] measures the water side of the
trade directly.
