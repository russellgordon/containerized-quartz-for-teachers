A virus is genetic material in a protein coat. It does not metabolise,
does not grow, and cannot reproduce without a host cell — which is why
"is it alive?" is a genuinely open question rather than a trick one.

## Structure

Nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) inside a capsid, sometimes wrapped in a lipid
envelope stolen from a previous host cell. That envelope is why soap
works against enveloped viruses: it dissolves the lipid.

## Two life cycles

- **Lytic:** the virus takes over the cell, makes copies, and bursts it.
- **Lysogenic:** the viral genome integrates into the host's and is
  copied every time the cell divides, sometimes for years, before
  switching to lytic.

## Where they sit in classification

Nowhere. Viruses are not in any kingdom, because they are probably not a
single group — different viruses appear to have arisen from different
sources, some possibly as escaped fragments of host genomes.

> [!note] Why this page is in the diversity unit
> Because deciding what counts as a living thing is a taxonomy question,
> and viruses are the case that forces the definition to be explicit.
