Plants reproduce sexually, asexually, or both — and many of the ones we
eat have been pushed hard in one direction by us.

## Sexual: the flower

Stamen (anther and filament) makes pollen; carpel (stigma, style, ovary)
receives it. Pollination moves pollen; fertilisation follows; the ovule
becomes a seed and the ovary becomes the fruit.

An apple is a swollen receptacle and a strawberry's "seeds" are the actual
fruits — botany's names and the grocer's do not agree, and the botanical
ones are the ones that predict behaviour.

## Asexual

Runners, tubers, rhizomes, cuttings. Fast, and genetically identical —
which is efficient until a disease arrives that the clone cannot resist.
Every Cavendish banana is effectively the same plant, which is exactly
why a single fungal strain threatens the whole crop.

## The trade-off

Sexual reproduction makes variation and costs energy, pollinators, and
time. Asexual reproduction is cheap and fast and bets everything on the
environment not changing. Most plants hedge by doing both.
