Every question in this unit turns into an agricultural one within a step,
and Ontario is a good place to notice it.

## What the science says about yield

Yield is limited by whichever factor is scarcest: light, water, carbon
dioxide, nitrogen, phosphorus, or temperature. Fertiliser addresses two of
those; irrigation another; greenhouse carbon dioxide enrichment another.
None of them addresses all.

## Costs that arrive later

- **Nutrient runoff** into waterways causes algal blooms, then oxygen
  depletion — Lake Erie has this every summer.
- **Monoculture** maximises efficiency and minimises resilience.
- **Soil structure** degrades under continuous tillage, and takes decades
  to rebuild.

## Practices that trade differently

No-till planting, cover crops, crop rotation, integrated pest management.
Each gives up some short-term yield for soil, water, or resistance
management — which makes them decisions about time horizons rather than
about facts.
