Two divisions, four daughter cells, half the chromosome number, and no two
of them alike. Meiosis exists to make variation, and every step is
arranged to that end.

## Two divisions

**Meiosis I** separates homologous pairs — this is the reduction step,
diploid to haploid. **Meiosis II** separates sister chromatids, like
mitosis.

## Three sources of variation

1. **Crossing over** in prophase I: homologues exchange segments, making
   chromosomes that never existed before.
2. **Independent assortment** in metaphase I: which member of each pair
   goes which way is random, giving $2^{23}$ combinations in humans.
3. **Random fertilisation**: any sperm with any egg.

Multiply those and the number of genetically distinct children two people
could have exceeds the number of people who have ever lived.

## Non-disjunction

When a pair fails to separate, a gamete ends up with an extra chromosome
or one too few. Trisomy 21 is the most familiar outcome, and a karyotype
shows it directly — see [[Reading a Karyotype]].
