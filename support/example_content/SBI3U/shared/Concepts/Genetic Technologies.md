The tools that let us read and alter genomes, and the questions that come
with them.

## Reading

**PCR** copies a target sequence millions of times from a trace amount —
which is what makes forensic and diagnostic testing possible. **Gel
electrophoresis** sorts DNA fragments by size. **Sequencing** reads the
bases directly, and its cost has fallen by a factor of about a million
since 2001.

## Altering

**Restriction enzymes** cut DNA at specific sequences; **ligase** joins
fragments. **CRISPR** targets a chosen sequence with far more precision
than earlier methods, which is why the ethical conversation became urgent
rather than theoretical.

## Applications, with their arguments attached

| Use | The case for | The case against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insulin from bacteria | Cheap, safe, no animal source | Almost none — this one is settled |
| Herbicide-tolerant crops | Yield, less tillage | Resistance, seed ownership |
| Gene therapy | Cures single-gene disorders | Cost, access, uncertain long-term effects |
| Germline editing | Prevents heritable disease | Changes descendants who cannot consent |

That last row is the one [[Editing the Germline]] is for.
