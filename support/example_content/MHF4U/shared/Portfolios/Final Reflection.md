The final reflection is the last piece of writing in this course: one
sustained look **across** your whole [[Math Journal]], written in the
final week. Every other entry looked at a single day. This one reads
the entire record and answers the only question a course can really be
measured by: *what do you leave with that you did not arrive with?*

## Before you write

Reread your journal front to back in one sitting — expect the earliest
entries to feel written by someone else. As you read, flag:

- one entry that embarrasses you a little — that is usually where the
  growth is buried
- one "what I would try" that you actually tried, and what happened
- one conjecture you made, and what became of it

## The shape

Three sections, roughly a page, built the way [[Showing Growth]]
taught — date it, quote your own words, then show the now:

1. **How my mathematics changed.** The biggest difference between
   what week-one-you and now-you can *do*, proven with two cited
   entries — a factored form that became readable at a glance, an
   identity you proved alone with a why beside every line, a
   logarithmic equation you can now take apart, test, and trust.
2. **How my relationship with mathematics changed.** The other axis,
   just as real: what being stuck feels like now, what you do when a
   sketch disagrees with its own factored form, whether "I am not a
   math person" survived the semester. Your first entry is the
   baseline; quote it.
3. **Where the thinking goes next.** This course ends at an edge on
   purpose: the question "how fast, right now?" that
   [[Rates of Change]] walks up to is the question calculus exists
   to answer, and you will meet it again within a year. Logarithmic
   reasoning waits in every lab and every headline that reports a
   magnitude. And the habit of proving a claim line by line — built
   at the boards on identities — is the template for every
   "convince me" that university mathematics will ever hand you.
   Name where each one will find you.

> [!tip] Do not write it as a thank-you card
> The temptation is to perform gratitude and round everything up.
> Resist it. The reflections worth reading include the unit that
> never clicked, the conjecture that died, the fog still standing.
> You have a semester of evidence that honesty is what makes this
> writing good. Trust it one last time.
