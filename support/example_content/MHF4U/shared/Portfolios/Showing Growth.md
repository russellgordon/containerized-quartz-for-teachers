By mid-course, your [[Math Journal]] stops being only a diary and
becomes something rarer: **evidence**. The person who wrote your
week-one entries no longer quite exists — and because that person
wrote honestly, you can prove it. Showing growth means putting your
week-one self and your now-self side by side and letting the
difference speak.

## Growth is a claim plus evidence

"I got better at math" is a claim, worth what any claim is worth:
nothing yet. Growth writing pairs every claim with evidence from your
own record — and the strongest evidence is the gap between two
entries:

> In the first week of the trig unit I wrote that radians were a
> foreign currency — I converted every angle back to degrees before
> I would trust it. Last week in [[The Sound of Two Waves]] I caught
> myself placing $\sin 2$ by picturing where 2 lands on the circle —
> past $\frac{\pi}{2}$, shy of $\pi$, so positive and falling — and
> degrees never entered the room. The unit stopped being a
> translation and became a place I think in.

The claim is *radians became native*. The evidence is two moments,
months apart, both on the record — and nobody can argue with that,
including the part of you that suspects you have not changed. It is
the standard [[How Marks Work]] applies everywhere: what can you
point to?

## How to cite yourself

Treat your earlier entries the way an essay treats sources:

- **Date it** — "In my second-week entry…" — the distance matters.
- **Quote a phrase, not a page.** Your words from the time carry more
  truth than your summary of them now. "Obvious is apparently still
  not a reason", from [[Your First Entry]], says more than "I used
  to skip justification."
- **Then show the now.** A recent moment, just as specific, that the
  earlier self could not have managed — or would not have noticed.

> [!tip] Growth is not always getting more answers right
> Some growth is speed traded for depth. Some is a new noticing: a
> sketch that bounces where the factored form says it should cross,
> a set of constant third differences in [[Visual Patterns]] that
> was invisible in the first week. New noticing is growth, and your
> early entries prove it was not always there.
