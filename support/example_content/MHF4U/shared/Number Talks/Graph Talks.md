A graph appears on the board with no numbers, no labels, no title —
just axes and a shape. Three questions, always in this order: what do
you notice? what do you wonder? what story could this graph be
telling? With no scale to lean on, shape is where the mathematics
lives — and this course is a museum of shapes: arms that rise or
fall, bounces, walls the curve cannot cross.

## How we play

1. Thirty seconds of silent looking. No hands yet.
2. Notice only what is *there*: an arm climbing, a bounce off the
   axis, a wall the curve approaches but never touches.
3. Pitch a story — then point to the feature of the graph that
   each part of your story explains.

> [!example]- Three stories for one curve that rises, dips, and rises
> - "The height of a rollercoaster along its track. The dip is the
>   valley between two hills, and the ends climbing away are the
>   lift hills at either side."
> - "The temperature across a March week — a warm spell, a cold
>   snap, and then spring wins."
> - "It is $y = x^3 - 3x$." True to the shape, but a story with no
>   characters. We sent it back for some.

## One variation

A curve that climbs a wall it never touches. The cost per person as
the group splitting the bill shrinks? The crowding near the front of
a concert? The same picture your group will build in
[[The Reciprocal]] — and the reason the curve *cannot* touch the
wall is a feature worth saying out loud before anyone names it.

> [!tip] Features have names
> End behaviour, turning points, zeros and their multiplicity,
> asymptotes, intercepts — using the right name for the right
> feature is half of every story; that vocabulary is the backbone of
> [[Polynomial Functions]] and [[Asymptotes]] alike.
