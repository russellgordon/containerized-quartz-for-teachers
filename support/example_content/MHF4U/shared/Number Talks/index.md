Most days, the first ten minutes of class are a number talk: one
prompt on the board, no pencils, no calculators. You think, then you
defend your reasoning aloud while we listen for paths unlike our own.
The prompts have grown up again — end behaviour, logarithms, and
radians now sit where last year's growth curves did — but the rhythm
has not: there is no single right method, and reasoning is defended
in public. You are one course from calculus, and these ten minutes
are still where the sharpest thinking of the day tends to happen.
