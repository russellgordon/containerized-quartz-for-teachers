Your group gets the graph of a function $f$ — a plain line first,
then a parabola — and one instruction: draw the graph of
$\frac{1}{f}$, the function whose output at every $x$ is the
reciprocal of $f$'s.

## The task

Work pointwise to start: where $f$ is $2$, the new graph is
$\frac{1}{2}$; where $f$ is $\frac{1}{4}$, the new graph is $4$. The
interesting places are the ones your table of values avoids — what
happens to $\frac{1}{f}$ where $f$ crosses zero? Where $f$ is
enormous? Where $f$ is exactly $1$, or $-1$? Draw the whole graph,
then push: which features of $f$ survive the trip — sign, symmetry,
where it increases or decreases? Finally, run it backwards: could
two different functions produce the same reciprocal graph?

> [!tip]- Facilitation notes — for the teacher
> Give the line first ($f(x) = x - 2$ works), the parabola second
> ($f(x) = x^2 - 4$ rewards the brave). The productive crisis is at
> the zero of $f$: some groups leave a gap, some draw a point "at
> infinity", some give both branches the same sign — let the wrong
> versions stand until pointwise evidence kills them. "What happens
> *just* to the left of $x = 2$? Just to the right?" is the only
> hint most groups need. Fast groups: sketch the reciprocal of
> $f(x) = x^2 + 1$, which has no zeros at all, and say what that
> buys you.

## What mathematics tends to surface

The vertical asymptote is born at the boards: as $f$ shrinks toward
zero, $\frac{1}{f}$ grows without bound, and groups draw a graph
climbing a wall they have not yet named. The horizontal asymptote
appears in mirror image — $f$ running away forces $\frac{1}{f}$ to
hug zero. Fixed points at $1$ and $-1$, sign agreeing everywhere,
and increasing trading places with decreasing all surface as
conjectures. [[Rational Functions]] gives the family its name, and
[[Asymptotes]] gives the walls theirs.

## Where it leads

Every rational function this unit is a quotient wearing today's
ideas: zeros of a denominator become vertical asymptotes, long-run
behaviour becomes horizontal ones, and the sign reasoning your group
ran pointwise becomes the sign chart that solves inequalities. The
unit's task, [[Safe Concentration]], hangs a dosing decision on
exactly these walls.
