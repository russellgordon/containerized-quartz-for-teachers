## What you are making

Every unit this semester ended with someone else's phenomenon —
coasters, doses, tones. This time the phenomenon is yours: anything
you genuinely care about that changes. A sport statistic, a growing
channel, a cooling coffee, a tide, a market, a migration. You finish
with the **function that models it**, built from real numbers you
sourced and cited; a **defence of the family** you chose —
polynomial, rational, sinusoidal, exponential, logarithmic, or a
combination — against at least one rival family; its **rates of
change** — one average rate over an interval that matters and one
instantaneous rate at a moment that matters, both interpreted in the
phenomenon's own units; and an honest statement of **where the model
stops being true**.

## Milestones

- [ ] Phenomenon chosen; data sourced, cited, and plotted
- [ ] Family chosen and defended against a rival, per
      [[What Makes a Model Good]]
- [ ] Model fitted in [[Using Desmos]]; the misfit measured and
      stated in the phenomenon's units
- [ ] Both rates of change computed and interpreted, per
      [[Rates of Change]]
- [ ] The model's domain of honesty stated — where it breaks, and why
- [ ] Defence rehearsed for [[The Functions Symposium]]

## How it is assessed

This is the culminating task, and per [[How Marks Work]] it is
assessed on reasoning, growth, and communication: the defence at the
symposium is where the evidence lives, not in the polish of the
poster. Your [[Math Journal]] across the whole semester is the
growth record — the entry on why you chose this phenomenon, written
at launch, gets its answer in your [[Final Reflection]].

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your work |
| --- | --- |
| A real stake | A phenomenon you can explain caring about |
| An argued family | The rival model taken seriously, then beaten |
| A measured misfit | Model-versus-data disagreement stated in units |
| Rates that mean | Both rates interpreted in the phenomenon's units |
| Honest edges | The model's breaking point named, not hidden |

> [!success]- If no phenomenon feels right
> Pick the one you would talk about anyway. The mathematics can be
> made interesting; your interest cannot be faked, and the symposium
> guests can tell the difference.
