## What you are making

The symposium is the finale: everyone presents the function behind
[[The Signature Function]] to invited guests — other teachers,
administrators, family — who circulate the room the way visitors
work a science fair. Your station holds the graph, the data, the
fitted equation, and you. Every group answers the same two
questions, from every guest who asks: *why is this the right
model?* and *where does it stop being one?* You finish with a
five-minute defence you can give repeatedly, a one-page handout for
your station, and answers to whatever the room throws at you.

## Milestones

- [ ] Station materials made: graph, data, equation, one-page handout
- [ ] The five-minute defence rehearsed until it survives
      interruptions
- [ ] The two symposium questions answered in writing first, then out
      loud, per [[Showing Your Thinking]]
- [ ] [[Final Reflection]] begun while the day is still fresh

## How it is assessed

Per [[How Marks Work]], communication is the evidence today: precise
vocabulary, representations chosen for the audience in front of you,
and honesty about the model's limits. Guests leave written feedback;
your [[Math Journal]] closes with [[Final Reflection]], and the
growth it shows across four volumes is the last word.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your work |
| --- | --- |
| A clear story | A stranger follows the model in five minutes |
| Precision | Vocabulary and notation used correctly, aloud |
| Grace under fire | Questions answered, not deflected |
| Honest limits | The model's edges volunteered, not extracted |

> [!success]- If nerves are the obstacle
> The defence is a conversation, not a recital. You have answered
> these two questions all semester; the guests are simply new people
> asking them. Rehearse once with someone patient, then trust the
> preparation.
