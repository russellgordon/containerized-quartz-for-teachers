> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · three hours, in the examination period · written, with a
> calculator and one formula sheet supplied · all four strands, weighted
> as the course spent its time

## What it is for

Advanced Functions is the prerequisite course. What you can do
unassisted, under time, is exactly what MCV4U and first-year
mathematics will assume on day one — so this paper is less a
gatekeeping exercise than an honest measurement of what you are taking
with you.

## What is on it

| Part | Roughly | What it asks you to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Exponential and logarithmic | 25% | Convert between forms; evaluate logarithms without technology; apply the laws; transform and graph; solve exponential and logarithmic equations, including problems |
| B. Trigonometric | 25% | Radian measure and the unit circle; graph and transform sinusoids in radians; the tangent function; prove identities; solve trigonometric equations |
| C. Polynomial and rational | 25% | Factor theorem and zeros with multiplicity; sketch from an equation; asymptotes and end behaviour; solve inequalities with a sign chart |
| D. Characteristics and rates of change | 25% | Even and odd; combining and composing functions; average and instantaneous rates; sketch a graph from a described rate; solve graphically where algebra will not |

## What to expect, precisely

- **Evaluate logarithms without a calculator.** $\log_8 2$ and
  $\log_{10} 0.001$ are testing the definition, not your device —
  [[Logarithms as Undoing]] is the page, and the method is "what power
  of the base is this?".
- **Radians throughout the trigonometry.** Degrees appear nowhere, and
  the period of $y = a\sin(k(x-d)) + c$ is $\tfrac{2\pi}{|k|}$.
- **One tangent-function question**, where there is no amplitude and
  the period is $\pi$. It is testing whether you noticed.
- **Identity proofs** are marked on layout: one side only, each line
  following from the one above.
- **One question is deliberately not solvable by algebra**, and asks for
  a graphical or numerical solution with the method stated. Presenting a
  numerical root as though it were exact loses the mark that question
  exists to award.
- **One question describes a situation in words** and asks for a sketch
  showing the rate of change, then a verification at three points.
  [[Rates of Change in the World]] has the technique.

## How to prepare

1. **Redo problems, do not reread them.** Five from each set, cold, with
   only the formula sheet: [[Logarithm Practice]],
   [[Radian Measure Practice]],
   [[Identities and Equations Practice]],
   [[Polynomial Graphing Practice]], [[Rational Functions Practice]],
   [[Rates of Change Practice]].
2. **Rebuild the connections.** The four parameters are one idea across
   four families of function; a logarithm is an exponential seen from
   the side; a transformation is a composition. Draw that map from
   memory — the gaps are your study list.
3. **Sketch from memory.** Each parent function, then one transformation
   of each. If you cannot draw it, you cannot analyse it under time.
4. **Say the proofs out loud.** If you cannot state the first move
   before writing, you are hoping rather than working.
5. **Bring questions to the review classes.** This page is what is on
   the examination.

> [!tip] In the three hours
> Budget by the weightings — they are even, and so should your time be.
> If a question stalls, write the method you intended in words and move
> on: a described method earns marks, and a blank space earns none.

## How this is assessed

Against the same expectations as everything else. Per [[How Marks Work]],
this examination is part of the final 30% of the course mark alongside
[[The Functions Symposium]], so that neither one afternoon nor one
project decides your grade alone.
