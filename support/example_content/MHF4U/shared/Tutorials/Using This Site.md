Everything for this course lives on this site, and it rewards being
used in a particular way. Five minutes here saves you an hour of
wandering mid-semester.

## Start where the site starts

The landing page for your section always shows the **most recent
class** — the warm-up, the thinking task, what is due. If you were
away, that page is your first stop; if you were here, it is still the
fastest answer to "which factor gave the vertical asymptote again?"
Older class pages sit right behind it, one per day, so a week away is
five short pages read in order.

## Three habits that make the site fast

1. **Search first.** Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> (or
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd>) and type any word —
   "multiplicity", "radian", a task name, a phrase you half
   remember. Search is almost always faster than browsing folders.
2. **Hover before you click.** Rest your pointer on any link — try
   [[Compound Angles]] — and the page appears in a small preview
   window. You can check a formula mid-proof without losing your
   place.
3. **Read the backlinks.** At the bottom of every page is a list of
   every page that links *to* it. On [[The Factor Theorem]], the
   backlinks show each class and task where that theorem earned its
   keep — a ready-made review list nobody had to build.

## Try before you peek

Exercise pages such as [[Logarithm Practice]] fold their answers
away behind a click. That fold is load-bearing: an honest attempt
before peeking teaches more than ten confirmations after. Commit to
an answer, defend it to yourself, *then* check — the order is the
whole trick.
