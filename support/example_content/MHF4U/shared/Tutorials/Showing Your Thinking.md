An answer with no visible reasoning is a rumour: it might be true, but
nobody — including you, three weeks later — can tell why. In this
course the reasoning *is* the work. Identity proofs, models built
from data, and multi-step equations all live or die on whether a
reader can follow the chain, and showing it is a concrete skill with
named parts.

## The four-part habit on paper

1. **Restate** — one line saying what the problem actually asks, in
   your own words. "Find when the concentration first drops below
   20 mg/L" is a different job from "find the concentration at hour
   six" — half of all wrong answers are right answers to a misread
   question.
2. **Reason** — the steps in order, with a word about *why* each move
   is legal. "Dividing both sides by $\cos x$ is only allowed where
   $\cos x \neq 0$, so we check that case separately" beats a bare
   arrow — and in an identity proof, the why *is* the product: every
   line is a claim of equality with the line before, and each one
   answers the sceptic before the sceptic can ask.
3. **Units** — carried through every line, not sprinkled on at the
   end. A [[Safe Concentration]] answer should arrive in milligrams
   per litre and hours; a [[The Rollercoaster]] height in metres;
   an angle should say out loud whether it is radians or degrees.
   Units that arrive wrong have found your error for you.
4. **Answer sentence** — a full sentence a stranger could read on its
   own: "The level falls below the safe threshold during the ninth
   hour, so the next dose can be taken then." A boxed $t = 8.6$
   answers nothing by itself.

## At the whiteboards

One rule does most of the work: **the person holding the marker writes
only other people's ideas.** It forces reasoning out loud — you cannot
write what nobody has said — so every line on the board has already
survived one listener. And cross out rather than erase: a corrected
line is a record of thinking, which is what the board is *for*, as
[[Mistakes Are Data]] argues at length.

## The checklist

- [ ] Could someone follow this without me standing beside it?
- [ ] Is there a why beside every non-obvious step?
- [ ] Do the units survive from the first line to the last?
- [ ] Does it end in a sentence answering the actual question?
- [ ] Are my crossed-out attempts still visible?
