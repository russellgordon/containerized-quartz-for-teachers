By the end of this course, you will hold the whole family album of
functions — polynomial, rational, trigonometric, exponential,
logarithmic — and know each face well enough to recognise it in a
graph, an equation, a table, or a situation. You will also learn to
ask the question that leads to calculus: not just *where* is this
function, but *how fast is it changing?* And you will work the way
mathematicians work: investigate, conjecture, verify, defend. The
curriculum names seven processes that run through everything we do:

Put plainly, week to week that means:

- **Think on your feet** — work problems you have not been shown how to
  do, at the whiteboards, in [[Our Classroom Norms|a room built for it]].
- **Argue, don't memorise** — an identity is a claim about two
  functions being secretly the same one, and
  [[Trigonometric Identities|claims get proven here]], not recited.
- **Model the real thing** — a rollercoaster's profile, a drug's
  concentration curve, an earthquake's magnitude: each is a function,
  and [[What Makes a Model Good|a good model earns its keep]].
- **Grow visibly** — your [[Math Journal]] is where struggle turns into
  evidence; see [[How Marks Work]].
