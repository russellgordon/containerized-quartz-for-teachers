Mathematics needs less equipment than any other subject — but the little
it needs, it needs every day.

## Every class

- [ ] Your journal or binder (see [[Math Journal]] for what goes in it)
- [ ] Pencil — mathematics is written in pencil, because
  [[Mistakes Are Data|first attempts matter]] and pen makes people afraid
  of them. An eraser you barely use.
- [ ] A ruler that lives in your bag
- [ ] Your scientific calculator (the class set stays in class; having
  your own means your muscle memory is always on the same buttons —
  and this course lives on the log and trig keys. Know where your
  mode button is: from the radian unit onward, a calculator quietly
  left in degrees is the single most common source of wrong answers
  in this course)

## Sometimes, and I'll say when

- A device with a browser, for [[Using Desmos]] days — school devices
  are available if you don't have one; nobody needs to buy anything.
- Grid paper for graphing units. A stack lives at the back of the room;
  take what you need — polynomials and their asymptote-fenced cousins
  both deserve better than freehand axes.

> [!warning] The phone rule
> Phones are calculators of last resort, and they are terrible at it — the
> notification that arrives mid-problem costs more than the arithmetic
> saved. Bags, not pockets, during work time. If your phone IS your
> Desmos device for the day, it stays in front of you with the one tab
> open.

Forgot something? Borrow from the back table, return it at the bell, and
nobody will ever make it a thing. Coming to class matters more than
coming to class perfectly equipped — see [[Getting Help]] if gear is ever
genuinely hard to come by, and we will quietly sort it out.
