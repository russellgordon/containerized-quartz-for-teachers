Stuck is a normal working condition in mathematics — being stuck WELL is
a skill this course deliberately teaches (see [[Getting Unstuck]]). But
there is a difference between productive struggle and spinning, so here
is the order to try things in:

1. **Read the problem again, out loud if you can.** A surprising fraction
   of stuck is really "answered a different question".
2. **Check the site.** The class page says what we did; the
   [[Concepts/index|concept pages]] explain every idea we use; every
   [[Exercises/index|practice set]] has worked answers folded below it.
3. **Ask your table.** Explaining where exactly you got lost is half the
   diagnosis — and remember the norm: helpers ask questions, they don't
   hand over answers.
4. **Ask me in class.** Work time is what it is for. Problems raised
   during work time are usually fixed in minutes.
5. **Come to a help session.** Times are on [[Help Sessions]]. Bring the
   specific thing — "Chapter everything" is hard to help with; one
   half-finished problem is easy.

> [!tip] Name where it broke
> "I can't do logs" is hard to help. "I can solve $2^x = 32$ but I
> don't see what to do when the 10 in $2^x = 10$ refuses to be a
> power of 2" — that one is fixed in five minutes. The habit of
> locating the exact broken step is [[Showing Your Thinking]] pointed
> at yourself.

One more thing: if the trouble is bigger than mathematics — time,
workload, something at home — tell me straight away, in whatever way is
easiest. Marks can wait; see [[Our Classroom Norms]] for the room this
class tries to be.
