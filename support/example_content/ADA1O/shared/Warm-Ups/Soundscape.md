Eyes closed, the class builds a place out of nothing but layered sound — a
rainforest, a subway platform, a kitchen before dinner. One sound starts,
others join and weave around it, and within a minute everyone is standing
somewhere that does not exist. Soundscape trains two things at once: the
control of [[Voice]] as an instrument that can be wind, machinery, or crowd
murmur, and the shared imagination a group needs before it can build scenes
together — a place you have half-created is an easy place to set a drama.

## How to run it

1. Sit or stand comfortably around the room and close your eyes. Silence
   first — a full ten seconds of it.
2. The teacher names a place: *a beach at dawn*, *a hospital waiting room*,
   *a forest at night*.
3. Anyone may begin with one repeatable sound, made with voice, breath,
   or body percussion. Keep it quiet — a soundscape is built at a murmur.
4. Layer in one at a time. Listen for what the place still needs: a
   foreground sound, a distant one, a rhythm underneath.
5. The teacher conducts with simple cues — louder, softer, a storm coming
   in, fading to silence. End in the same silence you started with.

## One variation

Story soundscape: the place changes over time — the calm street, the
approaching sirens, the aftermath. Now the group is telling a wordless
story in sound, and afterwards you can ask the question that turns a
warm-up into drama: *who was there, and what happened to them?*

> [!tip] Leave room in the mix
> If everyone makes their sound constantly, the place turns to mud. Real
> places breathe — sounds come and go. Drop out for a few seconds, listen
> to what the room has become, then rejoin. Silence is a contribution too.
