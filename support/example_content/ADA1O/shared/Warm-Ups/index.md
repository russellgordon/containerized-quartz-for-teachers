Warm-ups are not time-filling before the real work starts. They *are* the
real work, compressed. A basketball team does not shoot layup drills because
layups are fun — it drills them because the skill has to be automatic before
it is useful in a game. Same here: every game in this folder trains one
specific skill that a scene will later demand of you, and by then it is too
late to learn it.

That is why we play the same games again and again. The tenth round of
[[Group Counting]] is not a repeat of the first — it is the same group,
measurably better at listening. If a warm-up ever feels pointless, look up
what it trains and watch for that one thing next round.

| Game | What it trains |
| --- | --- |
| [[Mirrors]] | Complete attention to a partner; slow, readable movement |
| [[Machine]] | Building something together, one offer at a time |
| [[Zip Zap Zop]] | Focus, and picking up cues without hesitation |
| [[Group Counting]] | Listening to the whole room at once |
| [[Status Walks]] | Putting [[Status]] into the body, not just the head |
| [[Soundscape]] | Layered [[Voice]] work and shared imagination |
| [[What Are You Doing]] | Saying yes to absurdity without flinching |
| [[Yes Lets\|Yes, Let's]] | Accepting offers — the engine of improvisation |

Every one of these asks you to look slightly silly in front of other people,
which is exactly the risk drama runs on. That only works in a room built on
[[Why Trust Comes First|trust]] — the games and [[Our Ensemble Agreement]]
hold each other up.
