One person mimes an action — brushing their teeth, say. The next person
asks, "What are you doing?" and the first person answers with anything
*except* the truth: "I'm flying a kite." The asker must immediately start
flying a kite, the next person asks *them* the question, and around the
circle it goes. The game trains the improviser's core reflex: accepting an
absurd offer and committing instantly, without the half-second of *but that
doesn't make sense* that kills scenes.

## How to run it

1. Stand in a circle. One volunteer starts a clear, committed [[Mime]] of
   an everyday action.
2. The person to their left asks, "What are you doing?"
3. The mimer keeps miming their original action while naming a completely
   different one. The further the answer is from what their body is doing,
   the better.
4. The asker instantly begins miming the named action — full commitment,
   no editing — and play passes down the circle.
5. Keep the pace brisk — hesitating, repeating an action already used, or
   accidentally telling the truth passes the spotlight along.

## One variation

Alphabet round: each named action must start with the next letter of the
alphabet — *arranging flowers*, *baking bread*, *catching pigeons*. The
constraint quiets the panicked search for a *good* answer, because any
answer that fits the letter is a win.

> [!tip] Commit before you understand
> The game punishes thinking. If you wait until you can picture "wrestling
> an octopus" before you start, you have already stalled. Start moving on
> the first word and let your body figure it out mid-action — that is
> exactly the reflex [[Improvisation Showcase]] will ask of you.
