The whole class stands in a circle passing a single spark of energy — clap
towards a person and say *zip*, they pass it on with *zap*, the next with
*zop*, and around it goes, faster and faster. Zip Zap Zop trains two things
a performer cannot fake: focus, and picking up a cue the instant it arrives.
A scene dies in the half-second a performer spends deciding whether it is
their turn — this game burns that half-second away.

## How to run it

1. Stand in a circle where everyone can see everyone.
2. One person starts: eye contact with someone across the circle, a clear
   clap-point towards them, and the word *zip*.
3. The receiver immediately passes to someone else with *zap*, the next
   with *zop*, then back to *zip* — the three words cycle forever.
4. The pass must have all three parts at once: eyes, hands, voice. A pass
   with eyes pointing one way and hands another is a broken pass.
5. Hesitate, say the wrong word, or receive a pass you did not notice —
   restart the count, or step out for one round if you are playing
   elimination.

## One variation

Silent round: eyes and gesture only, no words. Suddenly everyone discovers
how much they were relying on sound, and the quality of eye contact across
the circle doubles. This is [[Focus and Emphasis|focus]] training with the
safety net removed.

> [!tip] Send it like you mean it
> Most dropped passes are the sender's fault. A mumbled *zip* aimed vaguely
> between two people is not a cue — it is a coin flip. Commit: lock eyes
> with one person, point at that person, speak to that person. Clear
> sending is a kindness, on stage and off.
