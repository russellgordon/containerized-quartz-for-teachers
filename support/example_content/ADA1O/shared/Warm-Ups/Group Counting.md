The class counts to twenty out loud — one voice at a time, no planned order,
no signals. If two people speak at once, the count restarts at one. That is
the whole game, and it is the hardest one in this folder. Group Counting
trains listening to the entire room at once: not waiting for your turn, but
sensing when the room is about to speak and when it is leaving space for
you. It is the [[Ensemble]] skill, distilled to a single number.

## How to run it

1. Stand or sit in a circle, or scatter around the room facing outward —
   harder, and better.
2. Close your eyes, or soften your gaze at the floor. No eye contact, no
   pointing, no rhythm patterns — those are ways of *avoiding* listening.
3. Anyone may say *one*. Anyone may say the next number. One voice per
   number.
4. Two voices overlap? Back to one. No groaning, no blame — the restart is
   the game working, not the game failing.
5. Reach twenty, and sit with the small, strange pride of it for a moment
   before moving on.

## One variation

Count to a number equal to the size of the class, with the rule that every
person must speak exactly once. Now the listening includes tracking who has
not yet spoken, and the quiet students discover the group is actively
holding space for them.

> [!tip] The urge to speak is the thing to watch
> Notice what happens in your body just before you say a number — the
> breath, the lean, the almost-speaking. Everyone else's body does that
> too, and hearing it is how the group succeeds. The game is not about
> being quick. It is about being tuned in.
