One person walks to the centre and starts a repeated motion with a repeated
sound — a piston arm with a *chunk*, a swivel with a *whirr*. One at a time,
everyone else joins on, physically connected to the growing machine, each
adding a new part that fits the rhythm. Machine trains [[Ensemble]] building
at its most literal: nobody plans the machine, nobody is in charge of it,
and yet it works — because each person makes an offer that fits what is
already there.

## How to run it

1. One volunteer starts a simple, repeatable motion and sound in the centre
   of the room. Small and steady beats big and exhausting.
2. When you see where you fit, join: touch or connect to an existing part
   and add your own motion and sound, locked to the shared rhythm.
3. Keep joining one at a time until the whole class is a single machine.
4. The teacher adjusts the controls: *faster*, *slower*, *the machine is
   breaking down*, *power off*. The machine responds as one body.

## One variation

Themed machines: build the *Monday morning machine*, the *exam stress
machine*, the *cafeteria machine*. Now every part is also a tiny piece of
role play, and the machine becomes a group statement about its subject —
which is one step away from a devised scene.

> [!tip] Pick a part you can sustain
> The classic mistake is a huge, dramatic motion that dies after twenty
> seconds. Choose something you could repeat for two minutes without
> strain. A machine is interesting because of how the parts fit together,
> not because any one part is spectacular.
