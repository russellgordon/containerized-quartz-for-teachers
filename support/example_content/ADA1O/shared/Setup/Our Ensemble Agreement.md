Drama asks you to do something no other class asks: to be seen trying. That
only works in a room where trying is safe. This agreement is how we make it
safe — and it binds everyone, including your teacher.

> [!danger] The two kinds of safety
> **Physical safety:** no contact without agreement, rehearsed slowly first.
> **Emotional safety:** what someone offers in role is never used against
> them outside it.

## What we agree to

- **We start together and end together.** The circle at the start of class is
  where the ensemble forms; be in it.
- **Offers are gifts.** When someone makes a choice in a scene, we work with
  it. Mockery — in the moment or later, in person or online — breaks the room.
- **The audience works too.** Watching is a job with skills of its own; see
  [[Watching Like an Artist]].
- **Stop means stop.** Anyone can pause a scene at any time, no explanation
  owed. We reset and choose differently.
- **Costumes and props are borrowed, not owned.** They go back where they came
  from, in the state they arrived.
- **Other people's work is theirs.** When we borrow a story, a script, or an
  idea, we say so — that is [[C3.1|ethical practice]], and it is also just
  respect between artists.

## Why this is not just being nice

An ensemble that trusts itself takes bigger risks, and bigger risks make
better drama. Every professional company works this way for the same reason
we do: the work is only as brave as the room is safe. We talk about this
properly in [[Why Trust Comes First]].
