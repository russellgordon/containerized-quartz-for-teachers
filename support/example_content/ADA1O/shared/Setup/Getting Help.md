Stuck is a normal working condition in this course — [[The Creative Process]]
has "stuck" built into it. But there is a difference between usefully stuck
and spinning, so here is the order to try things in:

1. **Check the site.** The class page for the day says what we did; the task
   page says what is due and what good looks like; the Concepts and
   Conventions pages explain every term we use.
2. **Ask your group.** Most rehearsal problems are solved by saying the
   problem out loud to the people in the scene with you.
3. **Ask in the room.** Flag me during workshop time — that is what it is
   for. Problems brought up in rehearsal are usually fixed in minutes.
4. **Ask outside the room.** Come by at lunch, or message me through the
   school's usual channels — details are on the board in the studio.

> [!tip] Name the real problem
> "Our scene is boring" is hard to help with. "The middle has no
> [[Tension|tension]] because nobody wants anything" — that one solves
> itself. Naming the problem in the course's vocabulary is half the fix,
> and [[Writing About Drama]] gives you the words.

One more thing: if something in the room — not the work, the room — does not
feel right, tell me straight away, in whatever way is easiest. That is
[[Our Ensemble Agreement]] working as intended.
