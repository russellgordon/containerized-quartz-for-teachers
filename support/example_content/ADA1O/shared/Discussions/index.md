In this course we talk before we make and after we make, on purpose.
Talking first surfaces the ideas a scene will need; talking afterwards is
where the learning actually settles — a performance nobody discusses
evaporates by Friday. The pages here frame those conversations:
[[Why Trust Comes First]], [[Drama in Everyday Life]], and
[[Watching Like an Artist]].

Two norms hold every discussion together:

1. **Build on.** Before adding your own idea, connect it to someone
   else's — "adding to what Sam said" is the spoken version of "yes, and".
2. **Disagree with the idea, not the person.** "I see it differently,
   because —" keeps an argument useful. Arguments about ideas make drama
   better; arguments about people end it.

Come ready to be persuaded. A discussion where nobody changed their mind
even slightly was not a discussion — it was a set of speeches.
