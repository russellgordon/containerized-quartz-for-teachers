Every task in this course asks you to risk looking silly in front of people
you will see at lunch. That is not a side effect of drama — it is the
material. A room where it is safe to freeze mid-scene, to try an accent and
miss, to play a character nothing like yourself, produces braver work than
a room of more talented people who are protecting themselves. Safety here
means [[C3.1|emotional safety as well as physical]], and it is built or
spent in every single class. [[Our Ensemble Agreement]] is our written
version of it; this discussion is where that agreement comes from.

## Questions worth arguing about

1. Think of a time you decided *not* to try something in front of others —
   in any class, not just this one. What exactly were you protecting?
2. Laughter can be the best thing in this room or the worst. Where is the
   line between laughing *with* a performer and laughing *at* one — and
   who gets to decide which it was?
3. What could a group member do, without saying a word, that would make
   you more willing to take a risk in the next rehearsal? What could they
   do that would shut you down?
4. Is trust something a class agrees to once, or something rebuilt every
   day? What does your answer mean for the day after somebody breaks it?
5. Physical safety has clear rules — no contact without agreement, no
   surprises. Should emotional safety have rules that clear, or does
   writing them down miss the point?

Nothing we make later — not [[Tableau Story Sequence]], not the cold
scenes of the [[Improvisation Showcase]] — works without what this
conversation builds. When you propose a norm today, propose one you are
willing to be held to, because [[Our Ensemble Agreement]] will hold you to
it by Friday.
