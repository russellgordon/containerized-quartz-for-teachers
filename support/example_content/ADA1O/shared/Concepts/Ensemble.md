An **ensemble** is a group that has become more than a list of its members.
You can see the difference from across the room: five individuals wait for
their own moments and defend their own ideas; an ensemble breathes together,
passes focus without a word, and treats every idea as belonging to the
group the moment it is spoken. Drama is one of the few school subjects
where the *group* is the instrument — a scene can survive one weak
performance, but not five people performing alone in each other's vicinity.

## What actually builds it

Ensemble is not chemistry or luck. It is a set of habits, each one
trainable:

- **Listening with the whole body** — tracking the room, not waiting for a
  turn. [[Group Counting]] trains nothing else.
- **Accepting offers** — treating a partner's idea as material, not as
  competition. That is the entire lesson of [[Yes Lets\|Yes, Let's]].
- **Giving and taking focus** — knowing when the scene needs you at the
  centre and when it needs you to hold the frame for someone else; see
  [[Focus and Emphasis]].
- **Matching and building** — fitting your contribution to what already
  exists, the skill [[Mirrors]] and [[Machine]] drill from opposite ends.

Notice that every item is a warm-up. That is not a coincidence — the games
in [[Warm-Ups/index|the warm-up library]] are ensemble training with the
seriousness disguised.

## Trust is the load-bearing wall

None of those habits survives in a room where a risk can be met with a
smirk. [[Our Ensemble Agreement]] exists to make the habits safe to
practise, and [[Why Trust Comes First]] explains the order of operations:
trust, then risk, then skill. An ensemble is what you get when a group
keeps that bargain long enough for it to become invisible.

> [!tip] The ensemble question
> After any group work, there is one reflection question that measures
> ensemble better than "did it go well?" — *whose idea was that moment,
> and can anyone actually remember?* When the answer is genuinely "the
> group's," write that down in your [[Drama Journal]]. It is the thing
> worth knowing how you did.
