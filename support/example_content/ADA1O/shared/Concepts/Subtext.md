**Subtext** is the line under the line — what a character actually means,
wants, or feels while saying something else. "Nice to see you" can mean
*I missed you*, *I dreaded this*, or *you owe me money*, and the words on
the page will not tell you which. People almost never say exactly what they
mean, which is why characters who do sound flat: no gap between the said
and the meant leaves the audience nothing to read.

## Where subtext lives

If the words carry the text, everything else carries the subtext: the six
strings of the [[Voice]], the [[Movement and Gesture|body]], a pause held a
beat too long, eye contact given or withheld. Before playing a line, decide
what the character wants the other person to do — then notice how far the
words are from asking for it directly. That distance is your subtext, and
your voice and body choices are how you let the audience glimpse it.

The cleanest way to make subtext audible is [[Thought Tracking]]: freeze
the scene, tap a shoulder, and the character speaks the under-line out
loud. If the spoken thought is identical to the dialogue, the scene has no
subtext yet — which is useful to discover in rehearsal rather than in
front of an audience.

## Playing against the words

The strongest version of the skill is deliberate contradiction — playing
*against* the line. "I never want to see you again," said softly, slowly,
while stepping closer, tells the audience the character means the exact
opposite, and no rewrite could say it better. Try a plain line three ways:
once meaning it, once doubting it, once meaning its reverse. Keep the words
identical; change only what is underneath. The audience's job — and
pleasure — is decoding the difference.

> [!tip] Choose one under-line and commit
> Vague subtext reads as no subtext. "She's sort of annoyed but also sad,
> maybe?" gives your voice and body nothing to play. Write the under-line
> as one blunt sentence — *get out of my room* — and let every choice in
> the speech serve it. You can always choose a different sentence for the
> next run.
