> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual research, presented in pairs · researched during Weeks 9–10 ·
> interviews performed Week 10 · about three minutes each

## What you are making

A performance you watch is the visible tenth of an iceberg. For this task
you will research one of the roles that make the other nine tenths happen:

- director
- stage manager
- set or costume designer
- lighting or sound operator
- front-of-house manager

Then, instead of a report, you present what you learned as a
[[Hot Seating]]-style interview: you sit in role as that professional, and
a partner questions you — about your responsibilities, your working day,
the skills your job demands, and what goes wrong when you are not good at
it. You are building a character from research, which is
[[Role and Character]] work as much as it is career work — and thin
research shows the moment a question goes off script.

## How to work

1. Choose a role you genuinely wonder about. You will answer questions in
   it for three minutes, and curiosity is hard to fake.
2. Research the real work: responsibilities, vocabulary, a typical day.
   Learn the terms your professional would actually use — entrance cue,
   technical rehearsal, dress rehearsal, props list — and use them
   naturally, not as trophies.
3. Prepare your partner. Give them enough background to ask real
   questions, then ask them to write two or three you have *not* prepared
   for — the unrehearsed answers are where your understanding shows.
4. Rehearse staying in role under pressure. "I don't know" is allowed;
   breaking character to say it is not. Your professional has their own
   way of not knowing things.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Accuracy | The responsibilities described match the real job |
| Terminology | Production vocabulary is used correctly and unselfconsciously |
| Role | You answer as the professional throughout, even when surprised |
| Depth | Unprepared questions get thoughtful answers, not blank ones |
| Partnership | Questions are real questions; the interview breathes like conversation |

> [!success]- Finding good sources (click to expand)
> Job postings are underrated research: a theatre hiring a stage manager
> lists exactly what the job requires. Interviews with working
> professionals — many theatres publish them — give you the voice, not
> just the facts.
