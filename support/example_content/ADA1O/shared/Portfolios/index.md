Most of what you make in this course vanishes the moment it ends — a scene
performed is a scene gone. Your journal is the part that stays. It is where
a term of workshops, rehearsals, and performances turns into something you
can look back through and actually *see*: this is where I started, this is
what I tried, this is what changed. In this course, the journal is the
portfolio — the place growth becomes visible.

The hub is [[Drama Journal]]: what the journal is, how entries work, and
the success criteria it is collected against at the end of each unit. The
pages below support it at each stage of the course:

| Page | When you need it |
| --- | --- |
| [[Your First Entry]] | Week one, facing a blank page |
| [[What a Strong Entry Looks Like]] | Once the habit exists and you want entries worth rereading |
| [[Journal Checklist]] | Before handing in each unit's journal |
| [[Showing Growth]] | Mid-course, when earlier entries become evidence |
| [[Final Reflection]] | The last week, looking across the whole record |

> [!tip] The journal is written forwards and read backwards
> Each entry takes ten minutes and feels small. The value arrives later,
> when [[Showing Growth]] asks you to compare your September self to your
> January self — and the comparison is only possible if September wrote
> honestly. Write for the person you will be in four months.
