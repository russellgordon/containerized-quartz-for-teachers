A **convention** is a reusable way of working inside drama — not a play, not
a script, but a repeatable structure you can pick up, use, and put down.
Freeze the action. Tap a shoulder. Form two lines. Each one is a tool, and
like any tool it exists because some job comes up again and again.

Conventions are how we practise [[The Elements of Drama|the elements]]
without needing a finished play to practise on, and they are the working
vocabulary of [[The Creative Process|the creative process]]: when your
group is stuck, the right convention is usually the way out.

| When you need to… | Reach for… |
| --- | --- |
| Control exactly what the audience sees, and hold it still | [[Tableau]] |
| Hear what a character is thinking but not saying | [[Thought Tracking]] |
| Question a character directly and watch them answer | [[Hot Seating]] |
| Stage the moment of a hard decision | [[Corridor of Voices]] |
| Reorder time to build tension or irony | [[Flashback and Flash-Forward]] |
| Slow down and get private with a character | [[Writing in Role]] |
| Set one voice against many | [[Choral Speaking]] |
| Say it without words | [[Mime]] |

None of these lives alone. [[Thought Tracking]] rides on top of [[Tableau]];
[[Corridor of Voices]] ends where [[Hot Seating]] could begin. And every one
of them asks you to take a small risk in front of other people, which is why
[[Why Trust Comes First]] is the page of this course worth rereading first.
