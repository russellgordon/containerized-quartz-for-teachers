In **hot seating**, one performer sits in a chair as a character — from a
story, a devised scene, a photograph, history — and the rest of the group
interviews them. The seated actor answers in role, inventing whatever the
source material does not supply, but never contradicting what it does.

It looks like a spotlight on one person. It is not. Hot seating is really a
whole-class exercise in [[A1.3|role play]], because the questions do as much
of the work as the answers.

## How to run it

1. Choose the character and the moment — *when* in the story are we speaking
   to them? Before the decision, or after it has changed everything?
2. Give the class two minutes to prepare questions, alone or in pairs.
3. Seat the actor. From the first question, everyone is inside the fiction.
4. Keep it short — five or six minutes. Stop while it is still alive.
5. Debrief out of role: what did we learn that the story never told us?

## Interviewer skills matter as much as the actor

A flat question lets the character hide. A digging question makes them decide
who they are, in front of everyone.

| Flat | Digging |
| --- | --- |
| How old are you? | What were you like at my age? |
| Are you sad about what happened? | What do you wish you had said that day? |
| Do you like your sister? | Why did you let her take the blame? |
| What happens next? | What are you most afraid happens next? |

Digging questions are built on the story's evidence, which makes preparing
them close reading in disguise — the same skill you bring to
[[Scene Study from a Story]]. Watch for [[Status]] while you listen, too: who
speaks carefully to the seated character, and who challenges them?

Answering as someone else, at speed, without a script, is the most direct
empathy practice drama offers. You cannot answer well without working out how
the character sees the world — and that working-out tends to follow you back
out of the chair.

> [!warning] The pitfall: playing it for laughs
> The moment the seated actor winks at the audience, the fiction dies, and
> it is very hard to restart. Protect the actor by staying in role as
> questioners — this convention only works inside
> [[Our Ensemble Agreement|the agreement]] we made about taking each other's
> work seriously.

Hot seating pairs naturally with [[Thought Tracking]] — track the thoughts
first, then seat the character and ask about the gap you heard.
