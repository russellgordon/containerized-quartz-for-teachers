By the end of this course, you will be able to open a computer without
fear, build and fix real systems, wire and control simple circuits —
and reason about where all this hardware comes from and where it goes.
In the curriculum's own words, the through-lines are:

Put plainly, week to week that means:

- **Hands on hardware** — from [[Take It Apart|first teardown]] to
  [[Build a Workstation|a machine you assembled yourself]], with
  [[Safety in the Lab|the habits that keep it safe]].
- **Systems, not just parts** — [[Networking Basics|networks]] you
  crimp and configure, [[Digital Logic Gates|logic]] you can breadboard,
  [[Code Meets Hardware|code that moves the physical world]].
- **Diagnosis as a craft** — look, smell, listen, then read; symptoms
  into causes into fixes, logged like a professional.
- **The bigger picture** — [[Where Does Old Tech Go|e-waste]],
  [[Locked Down or Wide Open|security]], and
  [[Careers in Computer Technology|where these skills lead]], including
  the skilled trades.
- **Grow visibly** — your [[Tech Journal]] is where fried components
  turn into evidence; see [[How Marks Work]].
