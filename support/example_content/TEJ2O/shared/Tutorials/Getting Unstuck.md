Stuck is a normal working condition at a bench — [[Getting Help]]
says so, and working technicians live there daily. But there are two
kinds of stuck. **Stuck-and-thinking** is generating suspects and
testing them; it looks slow and is actually diagnosis. **Spinning**
is reseating the same stick and hitting the power button again,
hoping the machine changes its mind. It will not. These moves turn
spinning back into thinking:

1. **Change one thing.** One cable, one part, one setting — then
   test. Change three things and the machine boots, and you have
   learned nothing: which one fixed it? One variable per test is the
   entire science of troubleshooting.
2. **Write down what you tried.** On paper, at the bench: what you
   changed, what happened. Four lines in, patterns appear that
   memory hides — and you stop repeating a test you already ran.
   The list is also gold for your [[Tech Journal]] at tools-away.
3. **Step back and look.** Literally. Stand up and take in the whole
   bench from a metre away. The unplugged power lead, the fan cable
   pinched under the case edge — the close-up view hides exactly
   what the wide view shouts.
4. **Explain it aloud.** To a partner, or to the empty chair: "power
   comes in here, the board splits it there, so the drive should —
   wait." Saying the system out loud forces you through the step you
   were skipping, and the fault usually lives in that step.

## When to stop and ask

Twenty minutes of real stuck-and-thinking is time well spent; twenty
minutes of spinning is suffering with extra steps. Honest test: when
did you last change something *on purpose*? If the answer is "three
reboots ago", escalate up the ladder in [[Getting Help]].

> [!danger] Power questions are not twenty-minute questions
> Everything above applies to a machine that is off and unplugged.
> Anything involving mains power, the inside of a power supply, or a
> smell of heat is ask-first, every time — that is the standing
> agreement in [[Safety in the Lab]], and no diagnostic cleverness
> outranks it.
