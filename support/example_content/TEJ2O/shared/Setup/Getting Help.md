Stuck is a normal working condition in technology — half this course is
learning to be stuck WELL. The order to try things in:

1. **Look, smell, listen, then read.** Half of hardware diagnosis is
   senses: what lights, what spins, what beeps? Then read any message
   on screen, twice — [[Troubleshooting Practice]] builds the method.
2. **Check the site.** The class page says what we did; the
   [[Concepts/index|concept pages]] explain every idea; every practice
   set in [[Exercises/index|Exercises]] has worked answers folded below.
3. **Ask your bench.** Explaining the problem out loud fixes a
   startling fraction of it — see [[Getting Unstuck]], and remember
   the norm: helpers ask questions, they don't grab the screwdriver.
4. **Search like a professional.** [[Finding Answers Online]] is a
   taught skill in this course: what to type, which results to trust,
   and how to credit what you use.
5. **Ask me in class.** Bench time is what it is for — and anything
   involving power or a first-time tool, ask FIRST.
6. **Come to a help session.** Times on [[Help Sessions]]. Bring the
   specific broken thing; "my program doesn't work" is hard to help,
   one error message is easy.

> [!tip] Name where it broke
> "No display, but fans spin and it beeps three times" is a symptom
> report a technician would be proud of — and it is halfway to the fix.

One more thing: if the trouble is bigger than the course — time,
workload, something at home, or anything happening online that worries
you — tell me straight away, in whatever way is easiest. And any
safety incident, however small, gets reported the moment it happens:
see [[Safety in the Lab]]. Reporting is information, never trouble.
