A retired desktop, a screwdriver, and permission to open it up. Take
it apart down to the bare case, photographing and naming every part
as it comes out — the photos feed [[Name That Part]] — and keep
notes so good that future-you can rebuild it, because
[[Build a Workstation]] depends on exactly those notes.

> [!danger] Safety notes
> **Power off and unplugged** before the case opens, then press the
> power button once to drain leftover charge. **The power supply
> never gets opened** — its capacitors hold a dangerous charge long
> after unplugging; it leaves as one sealed unit. **Case edges are
> sheet metal** — fingers off unfinished edges. **ESD kills
> silently** — strap on first; [[Anti-Static Habits]] is the routine.

## What you need

- [ ] A retired desktop, clearly marked for teardown
- [ ] Screwdriver set, and a container or bag for every screw
- [ ] Anti-static wrist strap and mat
- [ ] Camera (a phone is fine) and your paper journal

## The work

1. **Confirm it is unplugged, then drain it** — hold the power button
   for five seconds. Only then does the side panel come off.
2. **Photograph everything before touching anything.** The untouched
   interior is your reassembly map; no note you write later beats it.
3. **Cables first, photographing both ends before unplugging each.**
   A cable's route is information — where it goes matters as much as
   what it is.
4. **Work from the outside in** — cards, then drives, then memory —
   looking for latches and levers before you pull. Anything that
   resists has a catch you have not found yet.
5. **Bag and label screws by where they came from.** "Miscellaneous
   screws" is how reassembly turns into archaeology.
6. **Look closely at the mainboard.** The capacitors near the CPU
   socket, the coin battery, the rows of connectors — small
   electronic components you will meet again in
   [[Breadboard a Circuit]], already doing real jobs here.
7. **Name every part in your photo captions**, using the precise
   terms from [[What a Computer Is]] and [[The CPU and Memory]] —
   "the green thing" will not help you next week.

## What can go wrong

- **A connector will not release.** It has a hidden latch, not a
  need for force — forced connectors break forever. Stop, look from
  another angle, ask.
- **Leftover mystery screws.** A step went unlabelled — backtrack
  through your photos now, while memory is warm.
- **A part you cannot name later.** Usually a skipped photo. The
  discipline lives in the moment: photograph, caption, set it down.

## Level up

Read the labels — wattage on the supply, capacity on the drive, size
and speed on the memory — then find this machine's age and ask what
happens to [[E-Waste and the Environment|machines like it]] when
nobody wants them.
