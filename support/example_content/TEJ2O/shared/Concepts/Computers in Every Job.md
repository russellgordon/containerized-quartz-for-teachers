[[Careers in Computer Technology]] is about jobs where the computer IS
the work. This page is about the larger group: jobs where the computer
is a tool somebody else's work depends on — which, now, is nearly all
of them.

## Twelve jobs, and what the machine does in each

| Job | What the computer does | What it means when it breaks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural engineer | Runs load calculations no one would attempt by hand | The design waits, and so does the site |
| Architect and drafter | Holds the drawings, in layers, at full precision | Nobody can build from a drawing nobody can open |
| Pilot and air traffic controller | Navigation, separation, and weather, continuously | Procedures fall back to voice and paper, and capacity collapses |
| Ship's officer | Charts, position, and cargo stability | The vessel slows to what can be navigated by hand |
| Farmer | Yield maps, guidance, irrigation and feeding schedules | A planting window closes |
| Nurse and paramedic | Records, dosing, and monitoring | Care continues on paper, more slowly and less safely |
| Machinist and welder | CNC toolpaths, tolerances, inspection records | The cell stops; skilled people stand still |
| Auto technician | Scan tools, service data, calibration after any repair | The car cannot be signed off, even when the repair is finished |
| Electrician | Load calculations, permits, panel schedules | The inspection is postponed |
| Retail and hospitality manager | Inventory, scheduling, and sales data | Ordering reverts to guessing |
| Musician and producer | Recording, editing, and distribution | The session is the loss — takes do not come back |
| Teacher | Attendance, marks, resources, and this website | Everyone remembers the day the network went down |

## What that means for you

Two things follow, and neither is "everyone should learn to code".

**First: the technician is not support staff.** When the network in the
studio goes down, the recording stops. When the shop's scan tool cannot
reach the server, the cars do not leave. The person who fixes that is
holding up somebody's whole day, which is why the documentation
standards in [[The Network Job]] are strict — you are writing for
someone in a hurry.

**Second: your bench skills travel.** Every job in that table is done
by someone who needed a machine specified, built, connected, secured,
and repaired. You can do those things now. That is why
[[The Trades Are Tech]] is a discussion rather than a lecture.

> [!question] Worth arguing about
> Pick a job from the table that nobody in the room wants. Now find the
> computing in it, and say what would have to fail for that job to stop.
> Then say who fixes it. That person is often the one nobody thinks
> about until the day they matter most.

## Where to look next

Ontario's job-market and apprenticeship sites list, for any occupation,
what it pays, what it requires, and where it is hiring. Look up one job
from the table and one from [[Careers in Computer Technology]] and
compare them honestly — including the parts that would not suit you.
[[Finding Answers Online]] applies here too: a recruiter's page and a
government dataset are not the same kind of source.
