At the end of [[Take It Apart]] there was a tray of parts that used
to be a computer. Multiply that tray by every machine ever retired
and you have one of the fastest-growing waste streams on Earth —
and, oddly, one of the richest.

## Where hardware goes when it dies

A computer's costs run before and after its useful life. Before:
mined metals, enormous volumes of ultra-pure water, and the energy
of chip fabrication. After: circuit boards and batteries carry lead,
mercury, and cadmium, which do not stay put in landfill — they leach.
Too much retired hardware is also shipped abroad to be burned and
stripped in open yards, moving the poison rather than managing it.
None of this appears on a spec sheet, but it is part of what every
machine costs.

## What is still worth something

The tray is not garbage; it is low-grade ore. Circuit boards carry
recoverable gold, silver, copper, and palladium — richer per tonne
than much of the rock actually mined for those metals.[^1] In
Ontario, regulated recycling programs run public drop-off points for
electronics and batteries, and refurbishers wipe, repair, and resell
machines whose only fault was age. Where a given device should go —
recycler, refurbisher, or donation — is exactly the question
[[Where Does Old Tech Go]] debates, and [[The Refurb Report]] has you
put one real machine through the decision.

## Design for repair

The strongest move is to keep hardware out of the stream longer.
Whether that is possible is decided at design time: screws or glue,
RAM in sockets or soldered down, batteries replaceable or entombed,
parts and manuals available or not. A machine designed for repair
dies slowly and in stages; one designed against it dies all at once,
whole. [[Repair or Replace]] argues the economics case by case.

[^1]: Metal concentrations in printed circuit boards are commonly
    measured at tens to hundreds of grams of gold per tonne, versus
    single-digit grams per tonne in typical commercial gold ore —
    which is why "urban mining" is an industry, not a metaphor.
