One component goes up on the projector or gets passed down the
bench — a resistor, a capacitor, an LED, a transistor from the
parts bin. Three things earn the point: its *name*, its *job*, and
*one place it lives* in a real machine. The routine grows straight
out of [[Take It Apart]]: parts you have already held stop being
anonymous and start being vocabulary.

## How to run it

1. Reveal the part. One quiet minute — no calling out.
2. Cold-call three answers: name, job, one place it lives.
3. The class judges each claim: precise, close, or vibes.
4. If a photo was used, finish by holding up the real part —
   scale surprises people every time.

> [!example]- A worked round (click to expand)
> On screen: a small cylinder with coloured bands and a wire at
> each end. Name: a resistor. Job: it limits current. One place
> it lives: in series with the LED in your first
> [[Breadboard a Circuit]] build, keeping that LED alive. Full
> marks — every claim is a property, not a guess.

## One variation

Run it in reverse: give only the job — "stores charge and blocks
DC" — and ask for the part and one place you have seen it. Naming
from the function is harder, and closer to how diagnosis works.

> [!tip] "It does electricity" is not a job
> Push every answer one notch more specific. A capacitor does not
> "help the circuit" — it stores charge. Precise jobs are the
> habit [[Electronics Fundamentals]] will cash in later.
