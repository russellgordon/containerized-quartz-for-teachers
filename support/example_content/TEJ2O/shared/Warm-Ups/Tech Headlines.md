One current technology story, one minute of telling, and three
columns on the board: what is *claimed*, what is actually *known*,
and *who benefits* if you believe the claim. New chips, robot
demos, network outages, battery breakthroughs — this is the shop's
version of media literacy, because technicians are exactly the
people the hype is aimed over.

## How to run it

1. One student (rotating roster) brings the story and tells it in
   about a minute, no screen required.
2. The class fills the three columns: claimed, known, benefits.
3. The gap between the first two columns is the discussion.
4. Close with one line: what would move the claim into known?

> [!example]- A worked round (click to expand)
> Story: a company announces a battery that "charges in five
> minutes and lasts a week". Claimed: the headline. Known: a lab
> prototype exists; no shipping product, no independent test, no
> price. Who benefits: the company's next funding round. Verdict:
> interesting, unproven — check back in a year.

## One variation

Bring two stories about the *same* announcement from different
outlets and fill the columns twice. Watching the known column hold
still while the claimed column swings is the whole lesson.

> [!tip] "Who benefits" is a question, not a verdict
> Sometimes the honest answer is "everyone". The habit is asking
> it every single time, so that the one time it matters, it has
> already been asked.
