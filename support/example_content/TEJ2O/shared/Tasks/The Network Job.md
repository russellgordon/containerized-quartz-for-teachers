## What you are making

A client brief lands on your bench: a two-room recording studio needs
wired networking — machines in both rooms sharing files, and a reason
in writing for why the cable runs where it runs. Your bench takes the
job end to end: plan it, cable it, build and address it, prove it,
and hand over documentation the next technician could service from.

The handover is the real test. Another bench receives your binder and
answers service questions from it alone — if the answer is not in the
binder, the job is not done, however well the network runs.

## How to work

1. Plan on paper first: sketch both rooms, count the drops, measure
   the runs, and choose your transmission medium. The client asks
   "why not wireless?" — answer with the comparison, not a shrug.
   [[Networking Basics]] and [[How Data Travels]] carry the reasoning.
2. Make every cable yourselves — [[Crimp and Test a Cable]] is the
   skill, [[Cable Habits]] the discipline. Every cable passes the
   tester and wears a label before it touches a switch.
3. Build and address the network — [[Build a Small Network]] walks the
   assembly; [[Network Addressing Practice]] is the warm-up. Record
   every address as you assign it, not afterward from memory.
4. Enable what the client asked for: file sharing between the rooms,
   working in both directions. Test it with a real file, not a wish.
5. Verify like a technician: prove with the verification utilities
   that every machine sees every other, and record the outputs.
6. Assemble the binder: room sketch, labelled cable map, address
   table, test results, honest known-issues list. Then hand it over.

Cutters and crimpers are real tools — [[Safety in the Lab]] opens
every period, [[Spot the Hazard]] eyes on all week. Bench periods are
class time; using them well counts, as [[How Marks Work]] explains.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your job |
| --- | --- |
| A plan before tools | Rooms, runs, and media argued on paper first |
| Cables that pass | Every cable tests clean and carries a label |
| Working addresses | Every machine reaches every other, proof kept |
| Services delivered | Files move between rooms in both directions |
| A serviceable binder | Another bench answers questions from it alone |
| Safe tool habits | Tools handled safely, benches clear, no reminders |

## Reflect

Your [[Tech Journal]] entry: what did the tester catch that your eyes
missed, and what changes next time you crimp? A cable that failed and
got remade is better evidence of learning than one that worked first.
