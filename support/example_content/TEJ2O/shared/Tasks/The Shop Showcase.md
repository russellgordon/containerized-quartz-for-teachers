> [!abstract] At a glance
> Solo · three bench periods in the final two weeks · showcase evening
> in the last week · one artifact, one talk, one growth statement

## What you are making

The culminating task. The lab opens its doors to invited guests —
family, staff, another class — and each student presents **one
artifact** from the course, technician-style, at their own bench: the
build sheet from [[The Build Sheet]], the network binder from
[[The Network Job]], the gadget from [[The Gadget]], or the machine
and verdict from [[The Refurb Report]].

Technician-style means four things, in order: what it is, how it
works, what went wrong on the way, and the service documentation that
proves all three. "What went wrong" is not a confession — the fault
you traced is the strongest proof you understand the system. Beside
the artifact stands a short **growth statement**: who you were at the
first bench period, who you are now, evidenced from your
[[Tech Journal]].

## How to work

1. Choose your artifact — not the one that went smoothest, but the one
   whose story you can tell best. The two are rarely the same.
2. Gather everything the artifact generated — diagrams, code, photos,
   test results, journal entries — into one organised portfolio
   folder, sensibly named and backed up in a second place. One copy
   on one machine is a failure mode you know how to diagnose.
3. Write the growth statement. [[Showing Growth]] shows how to pair a
   then-entry with a now-entry, [[Journal Checklist]] helps you mine
   the year, and [[Final Reflection]] is drafted in the same window.
4. Rehearse with a partner playing a guest who knows nothing — then
   with one playing a guest who knows everything. Both will come.
5. Showcase evening: artifact on the bench, documentation open, talk
   ready, questions welcome. Powered demos run the [[Safety in the Lab]]
   habits in front of guests exactly as on a Tuesday.

The bench periods are class time, and using them well is part of the
task — [[How Marks Work]] explains how.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like on showcase evening |
| --- | --- |
| A working narrative | What it is, how it works, what went wrong |
| Proof on the bench | Guests can check any claim in the open package |
| An organised portfolio | Files gathered, sensibly named, backed up twice |
| An honest fault story | The thing that went wrong is told with its diagnosis |
| Evidence of growth | Early journal entries paired with late ones |
| Safe habits on display | Powered demos follow lab discipline |

## Reflect

The growth statement is the reflection, and it outlives the evening:
it opens your [[Final Reflection]] and starts the portfolio the
[[Careers in Computer Technology|people who hire technicians]] will
one day ask to see.

> [!success]- If presenting makes your stomach drop (click to expand)
> You are not performing — you are giving a tour of work that already
> exists. Every question a guest can ask is about something sitting on
> your bench, built and documented by you. Rehearse the first minute
> until it is automatic; the artifact carries the rest.
