The closing entry. Roughly 500 words, written after
[[The Chemistry Showcase]] is finished and before the course ends.

## What to cover

1. **The sentence you wrote in [[Your First Entry]]** about how
   chemistry knows things. Quote it. Would you still write it? If not,
   what replaced it, and what changed it — a specific investigation, a
   discussion, an argument you lost.
2. **The investigation you would run again.** Not the one that went
   best. The one where you now know what you should have measured, or
   how many trials you actually needed.
3. **A mechanism you can explain now that you could not at the start.**
   Explain it here, briefly, to a reader who has not taken the course.
   Why ionic compounds dissolve and molecular ones often do not. Why a
   limiting reagent limits. Why a gas pushes harder when you warm it.
   One of those, properly, beats five of them in outline.
4. **What you still do not understand.** Named precisely. "Solutions"
   is not precise; "why a solution can be saturated and still have
   solid dissolving and crystallising at the same time" is.
5. **What you are taking with you** if you never take another chemistry
   course. Be honest — for some of you the answer is a habit rather than
   a concept, and a habit is a perfectly good answer.

> [!question]- Prompts, if you are staring at a blank page
> When did you first argue with a number instead of accepting it?
> Which page on this site did you actually return to more than once?
> What did you believe in Unit 1 that Unit 4 contradicted?
> When did the mole stop being a formula you looked up?
> Whose reasoning in this room changed yours, and on what?
> What is the first thing you now do when a calculated answer looks
> wrong?
> What would you tell somebody starting this course in the first week —
> not the encouraging version, the useful one?

## Who to write it for

Not for me. I was in the room; I already know what happened. Write it
for a student taking this course next year who is reading your journal
to find out what it is like and what to do differently.

That reader changes what belongs in it. They do not need "the
quantities unit was hard". They need "I spent three classes treating the
mole as a formula to substitute into, and the thing that finally worked
was drawing the balanced equation and labelling which quantity I could
actually measure."
