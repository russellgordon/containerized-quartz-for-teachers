By the end of this course you will be able to look at a reaction and say
not just what it makes, but **how much** — and defend the number. Grade 10
chemistry asked whether something new was made. This year asks how many
particles were involved, how much product you should have recovered, and
what the gap between "should have" and "did" is telling you.

Three overall expectations set the tone. The first runs through every
unit rather than living in one:

Put plainly, week to week that means:

- **Count what you cannot see** — [[The Mole]] is the hinge of the whole
  course, and everything quantitative after it runs through the same two
  conversions.
- **Design the procedure** — you will more often be handed a question and
  some glassware than a numbered list of steps.
- **Report honestly** — a yield of 94% with a stated reason beats a
  yield of 100% that quietly rounded. See
  [[Percentage Yield of a Precipitate]].
- **Carry your uncertainty** — the answer has units and the right number
  of significant figures, or it is not an answer. See
  [[Significant Figures and Units]].
- **Connect it outward** — the water in your tap and the air outside the
  window are both chemistry problems somebody is currently solving. See
  [[The Water Report]] and [[The Air We Share]].
- **Show your growth** — your [[Chemistry Journal]] is the record of how
  your thinking changed; see [[How Marks Work]].
