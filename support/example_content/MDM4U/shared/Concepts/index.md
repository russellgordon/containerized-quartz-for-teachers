Every page in this folder names an idea you met first at the boards, in
a visibly random group, marker in hand, usually while being surprised.
That order is not an accident. The surprise is the teacher — the
birthday problem, the test that is 99% accurate and still mostly
wrong, the correlation with a beautiful $r$ and nothing behind it. You
build the tool that explains the surprise, and then these pages say
the tool cleanly, for your future forgetful self.

This course has one through-line, and it is not a formula. It is
**honesty about uncertainty**. Every number below comes with a
question attached: how sure are we, how did we find out, and who is
missing from the data that told us. Hold on to that question. It is
the difference between doing statistics and being fooled by them.

## Unit 4 — Analysis, inference, and the investigation

Unit 4 adds no new pages. It re-reads the ones above with harder
eyes — margin of error, confidence, regression, and the question of
whether a published claim survives contact with its own data. The
work lives in [[Regression and Inference Practice]], in
[[The Statistical Claim Report]], and in the long thread that runs
under this whole semester, [[The Culminating Investigation]].

Most pages end by pointing at the practice set that turns the idea
into a reflex, and at the curriculum expectations the page serves.
When your investigation needs actual numbers to chew on, start at
[[Choosing a Data Set]] — a data management course without data is a
course about nothing.
