Some problems are worth standing up for. On exploration days you work
in random groups of three at the vertical whiteboards, on a problem
nobody has taught you to solve yet — that is the point, not an
oversight. The technique arrives *after* the thinking, and in this
course your conjectures are expected to survive cross-examination.
Expect to be stuck; being stuck is the work, as
[[Why Struggle Is the Point]] explains.

This course's explorations have a second habit. Most of them begin
with the room being confidently wrong about something — a birthday, a
correlation, a chart — because learning to distrust a fast answer is
half of what data management teaches.

| Exploration | The question |
| --- | --- |
| [[The Birthday Problem]] | How many people before two share a birthday? |
| [[Counting Without Counting]] | How do you count what you cannot list? |
| [[The Simulation]] | Can ten thousand trials tell you what theory will not? |
| [[The Sampling Frame]] | Who does your sampling method never reach? |
| [[The Spurious Correlation Hunt]] | Beautiful correlation, no cause — how? |
| [[The Graph Gallery]] | Every number true, every chart lying — where? |

Hints nudge; they never march. Ask, and you get a question back — see
[[Getting Unstuck]]. No solutions appear on these pages, ever: the
class builds them together, per [[Our Classroom Norms]].
