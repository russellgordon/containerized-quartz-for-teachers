The first entry in your [[Math Journal]] is the hardest, because the
page is blank and you do not yet trust what belongs on it. So for
week one only, the entry is guided: answer the three prompts below, a
few sentences each, written the same day as class. Ten minutes, done.

## The three prompts

1. **What we did** — the facts, briefly. The number talk, the
   thinking task, where your group got to before time ran out.
2. **What surprised me** — one specific moment you did not expect.
   This course opens with a problem designed to ambush you, so there
   should be something.
3. **Where I stand with math right now** — honestly. Everyone arrives
   here with a history, and a fair number of you chose this course
   because it sounded less like mathematics than the alternative.
   Naming yours is the first piece of data in the record — and this
   is a course about being honest with data.

## What honest looks like

These fragments are the register to aim for — plain, specific, true:

> "I said 180 people. The answer was 23. I did not just get it
> wrong, I got it wrong by a factor of eight, and I was *confident*.
> Apparently the thing I was counting — people — was not the thing
> that mattered, which was pairs. I want to know what else I have
> been counting wrong."

> "My group listed every outcome for three coins and got it right,
> then tried to list every outcome for ten coins and gave up at about
> forty. Somebody said 'there must be a way to count these without
> writing them', and that sentence is apparently the whole unit."

> "I picked this course because I heard there was no trigonometry.
> Today there was no trigonometry and I still could not answer the
> first question on the board. Not sure yet whether that is bad
> news."

None of these is polished, and none performs for a reader. Each names
a real moment and stays with it — which already beats a page of "math
was fine today, I learned a lot."

> [!important] Nobody reads this but me
> As [[Math Journal]] promises: entries are private. I read them;
> other students never do, and nothing you admit here is quoted or
> used as an example without your explicit okay. That is not just a
> classroom courtesy — it is the same standard this course will ask
> you to apply to every dataset you touch, and it would be strange to
> demand it of you and not of me.
