Most days, the first ten minutes of class are a number talk: one
prompt on the board, no pencils, no calculators. You think, then you
defend your reasoning aloud while we listen for paths unlike our own.
The prompts have changed shape for this course — a chart with no
labels, a probability that feels wrong, a correlation too good to be
honest — but the rhythm has not: there is no single right method, and
reasoning is defended in public. Ten minutes a day is also the
cheapest possible practice at the skill this whole course is for:
saying what a number does and does not entitle you to claim.

Why speak before you are certain? Because [[Mistakes Are Data]] — and
in a course about uncertainty, admitting it out loud is the job.
