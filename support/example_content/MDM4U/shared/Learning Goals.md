By the end of this course, you will be able to do something most adults
cannot: look at a number in the news and know what to ask. How was this
counted? Who was sampled — and who was missed? Is that difference real
or is it noise? You will also carry out a full investigation of your
own, from a question you chose to a conclusion you can defend. And you
will work the way mathematicians work: investigate, conjecture, verify,
defend. The curriculum names seven processes that run through
everything we do:

Put plainly, week to week that means:

- **Count what cannot be listed** — arrangements, selections, and the
  [[The Fundamental Counting Principle|principle]] that makes both
  countable without writing them all down.
- **Put a number on uncertainty** — from a single
  [[Probability Basics|probability]] to a whole
  [[Random Variables and Distributions|distribution]], including the
  [[The Normal Distribution|one that keeps showing up uninvited]].
- **Interrogate the data** — where it came from, who is missing from
  it, and whether the pattern survives [[Bias|honest scrutiny]].
- **Run your own investigation** — [[The Culminating Investigation]] is
  the spine of this course: your question, your data, your analysis,
  your defence.
- **Grow visibly** — your [[Math Journal]] is where struggle turns into
  evidence; see [[How Marks Work]].
