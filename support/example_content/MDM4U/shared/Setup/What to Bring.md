Mathematics needs less equipment than any other subject — but the
little it needs, it needs every day.

## Every class

- [ ] Your journal or binder (see [[Math Journal]] for what goes in
  it)
- [ ] Pencil — mathematics is written in pencil, because
  [[Mistakes Are Data|first attempts matter]] and pen makes people
  afraid of them. An eraser you barely use.
- [ ] A ruler that lives in your bag — tree diagrams, two-way tables,
  and axes are drawn, not implied
- [ ] Your scientific calculator (the class set stays in class;
  having your own means your muscle memory is always on the same
  buttons). Find its factorial key, its $_nC_r$ and $_nP_r$ keys, and
  its statistics mode *this week*, not the night before the first
  quiz — a calculator you have to explore mid-question is a
  calculator working against you.

## Sometimes, and I'll say when

- A device with a browser, for [[Using Desmos]] days and for the
  spreadsheet work in [[Using a Spreadsheet for Statistics]] — school
  devices are available if you don't have one; nobody needs to buy
  anything.
- Grid paper for the data-display units. A stack lives at the back of
  the room; take what you need — a histogram sketched freehand tells
  you almost nothing, and this course cares a great deal about
  whether a shape is symmetric.

> [!warning] The phone rule
> Phones are calculators of last resort, and they are terrible at it
> — the notification that arrives mid-problem costs more than the
> arithmetic saved. Bags, not pockets, during work time. If your
> phone IS your Desmos device for the day, it stays in front of you
> with the one tab open.

Forgot something? Borrow from the back table, return it at the bell,
and nobody will ever make it a thing. Coming to class matters more
than coming to class perfectly equipped — see [[Getting Help]] if
gear is ever genuinely hard to come by, and we will quietly sort it
out.
