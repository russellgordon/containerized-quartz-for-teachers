One page per class, newest at the top of your teacher's list: the agenda we
followed and anything to do before next time. If you were away, start here —
then try the thinking task before reading anything else about it. The page
holds the plan; the discoveries happen at the boards.

Our semester runs in four units:

- **Unit 1 — Counting and Probability**: the surprises first — a shared
  birthday, a test that is right $99\%$ of the time and still misleads
  you — then the counting that explains them, put on trial in
  [[The Fair Game Audit]].
- **Unit 2 — Probability Distributions**: from one outcome to the whole
  table of them — expected value, binomial, hypergeometric, and the
  normal curve that keeps turning up uninvited.
- **Unit 3 — Organizing Data for Analysis**: where data actually comes
  from, who it quietly leaves out, and how to summarize one and two
  variables without lying — dissected in [[The Survey Autopsy]].
- **Unit 4 — Statistical Analysis and the Investigation**: reading a
  study, a poll, and a chart with your guard up, tested against the news
  in [[The Statistical Claim Report]], and presented at
  [[The Data Symposium]].

Running underneath all four units is [[The Culminating Investigation]] —
your own question, your own data, your own defended conclusion. It is
launched in Unit 2 and fed by every class that follows, so the milestones
in "Things to do before our next class" are not optional extras: they are
the project, arriving in pieces you can carry.
