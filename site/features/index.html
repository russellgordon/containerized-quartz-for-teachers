What it does

Your course notes become a real website. Fast, searchable, readable on a phone — and yours to take with you.

The point of it

Most systems ask you for the same material twice.

Once when you write it. Again when you post it — a form here, an upload there, a dozen clicks to share a single handout.

That second pass is where your evenings go.

Plantoir removes it. Your notes are the website. Writing a file and saving it is the whole job; publishing is a button.

What you get

Files that outlive the software

Plain text, in ordinary folders. Change schools, change tools, or stop using Plantoir altogether — your material is still readable, still organised, still yours.

One course, several classes

Write the course once and publish it separately for each section. Two groups moving at different speeds each see only what they've reached.

A course that's ready on day one

Dozens of Ontario course codes arrive with a full working course already written, curriculum expectations included as pages you can link to. Every other code opens with an outline shaped for the subject.

Nothing to administer

No server, no accounts to manage, no administrator password. The app brings what it needs the first time you run it.

Private until you say otherwise

Your notes stay on your computer. Nothing leaves it until you publish a section on purpose.

Free, and open

It costs nothing and the source is public. Nothing about your course depends on a company's plans for its product.

Adding a course

A course code is enough to start.

If ready-made material exists for that code, the pages, folders and curriculum expectations arrive in one step. If it doesn't, you get an outline that suits the subject — a drama course opens with conventions and warm-ups, a chemistry course with investigations and lab safety.

It looks like your course, not like an app

Each section picks its own colours, typeface and footer, so two sections of the same course are told apart at a glance.

You choose in the app and see a live sample before anything is built.

See it before anyone else does

Preview builds the real site and shows it inside the app window.

Not an approximation — the same site your class would get, built the same way. Right down to the equations. What you see in the preview is what they see when you publish.

What your students get

  • Search across the whole course. A student looking for a definition finds it, instead of scrolling through a term of material.
  • Mathematics set properly. Equations are typeset, not pasted in as pictures — chemistry notation included.
  • Diagrams drawn from your notes. A flowchart or a timeline is a few lines of text, drawn when the site is built. Fixing it means fixing a sentence.
  • Code with syntax highlighting, alongside images, video and PDFs.
  • Pages that link to each other, so a lesson points at the concept behind it and the concept lists the lessons that used it.
  • A site that reads well on a phone, in light or dark, with no separate mobile version to maintain.

Publishing

Send a finished section to a free hosting service, to your school's own web space by way of an ordinary folder, or to a paid host — whichever your school allows.

The address doesn't change from one publish to the next. A link you hand out in September still works in June.

You can also publish on a schedule. Prepare a section on Sunday evening, and it appears Monday morning without you opening a computer.

What you need

A Mac

macOS 15 or later, with Apple silicon. A Windows version is built and will be released separately.

An internet connection, at the start

The first run downloads what the site builder needs — a few minutes. After that, writing, building and previewing all work offline. Publishing needs a connection again.

Somewhere to put the site

A free hosting account, or a folder on a school web server. Neither costs anything to try.
